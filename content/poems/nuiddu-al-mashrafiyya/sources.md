# نُعِدُّ المَشرَفِيَّةَ وَالعَوالي

## Recording and extent

Osama Al-Waaedh, «نعد المشرفية والعوالي», on his own channel, uploaded 12 August 2023. Both full-file transcriptions put the verse about the morning cloud directly after «يسر الروح فيه بالزوال» and repeat it in full. There is no spoken preface or closing credit. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QBAo9vHxZLM)

The source is 200.853 s. The edit keeps 2.38–198.24 s. Speech begins immediately after the initial half-second of room and fades before the end. A +2 dB gain gives −20 LUFS and −1.1 dBTP over 195.86 s. Al-Wahidi's bayts 1–15 and 17 are heard. His 16 («رواق العز») and 18–45 fall outside the recitation. The catalogue numbers the sixteen different bayts once each. In the repeat of 16.2 the aligner stops at 194.74 s, inside the last syllable. The final voicing dies away at about 195.33 s and the highlight ends at 195.36 s, with half a second of room left.

## Editions and commentary

- Ibn al-Iflili, *Sharh Shi'r al-Mutanabbi*, first part, ed. Mustafa Ulayyan, Mu'assasat al-Risala, Beirut, 1992, vol. 1, pp. 184–188 for the excerpt, pp. 189–196 for the continuation. His account places the poet in Antioch when news of Sayf al-Dawla's mother's death at Mayyafariqin reached Aleppo. He dates the performance before the prince in Aleppo to Jumada II 337 AH. He places «أطاب النفس» immediately after «جديداً ذكرناه», giving 44 verses in all. His explanations of the horses, the arrows, the funerary perfume, the renewed memory and the two kinds of giving inform the sharh. [Occasion and opening](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/28) · [The arrows](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/30) · [The body's decay](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/31) · [Prayer for rain](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/32)
- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, Shamela 754/195–198. The complete poem has 45 bayts here, including our 13, «وما أحد يخلد في البرايا». This is the edition counted in `abyat_in_full`. His text was collated with Ibn al-Iflili's. He preserves the indefinite وصال attributed to al-Khwarizmi at 3.2, two openings at 7.1, and Ibn Jinni's and Ibn Furraja's rival vocalizations at 8.2. His gloss at 3 explains that it is lasting union the lover cannot obtain. His comments on 10–11 distinguish the funeral covering from the shelter of her conduct in life. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/754/195) · [Variants and commentary](https://shamela.ws/book/754/196) · [The cloud](https://shamela.ws/book/754/197) · [End of poem](https://shamela.ws/book/754/198)
- Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, section «قافية اللام», the elegy for the prince's mother, text and notes 18–33 for the excerpt. His text also has «وما أحد يخلد», the extended rhyme بالي, and a masculine second-person form in 14.1. [Text and numbered notes](https://www.safahat.org/books/82959503/20/)

## Metre and readings

The metre is wafir, with lam as the rhyme consonant. In the opening sadr the feet are ⏑––– | ⏑–⏑⏑– | ⏑––, in the ajuz ⏑–⏑⏑– | ⏑–⏑⏑– | ⏑––. Bayt 8 has ⏑––– | ⏑––– | ⏑–– in the sadr, and ⏑–⏑⏑– | ⏑––– | ⏑–– in its ajuz. These are مفاعلتن with or without عصب, followed by فعولن.

In 7.1 al-Wahidi also records «وها أنا ما أبالي». Audar transcribes the printed «وهان فما أبالي» across the entire bayt, the narrow Cohere clip passes, and both complete-file backends retain the fa of «فما». In 7.2 the joined pronunciation of «ما انتفعت» leads the full-file models to divide it as «من تفعت». The latter offers no verb with the required meaning. In 12.2 ذِكرُناه is a verbal noun with two pronominal attachments, «ذِكرُنا إيّاه». Ibn al-Iflili singles out that construction. The nominative sense belongs to our remembrance, while the decaying person remains its object.

The internal half-verse ends likewise follow the sustained final vowel. For example, 2.1 carries the last syllable of «مقربات» to just before 17.7 s, and the i of the following rhyme is still voiced at 23.05 s. In the first performance of 16 the final vowels subside at about 177.91 and 183.77 s, respectively. These durations were checked against pitch and signal level before the clips were transcribed again.

## Checks against the recording

In the first check, Cohere transcribed the 34 automatic half-verse cuts from the app audio, including the repeated final bayt. It flagged ten addresses. Audar then transcribed the same passages with additional audio on either side. The two full-file transcripts provided a second way of checking words which lost their context in an isolated half.

At 3.2 al-Wahidi attributes the reading «إلى وصال» to al-Khwarizmi. The candidate scorer ranks the indefinite word 5.46 higher, but both full backends and the short Cohere pass hear the article. A text-independent phone pass in app time finds the l of إلى at 32.75 s and a second l at 33.13 s, before the rounded onset of وصال. The recording therefore has «إلى الوصال». In 7.1 the phone sequence /wa hāna fa mā/ confirms the first reading mentioned above. In 7.2 the scorer ranks the meaningless segmentation «من تفعت» 9.87 higher than the words spoken in liaison, «ما انتفعت». The isolated ما has the lowest word-alignment confidence in the poem, 0.396. In 8.2 the diphthong in مَيتة is distinct in a phone pass, and the candidate leads مِيتة by 5.21. Ibn Jinni takes the former to mean a woman who has died. Ibn Furraja takes the latter as the way someone met death. In 10.1 the genitive خالِقِنا, an apposition to the divine name, leads the nominative by 5.34.

The final address is still to Sayf al-Dawla's mother. Ibn al-Iflili vocalizes the pronouns as feminine throughout. Al-Barquqi prints masculine أنَّكَ مُتَّ in 14.1, then feminine forms in 15–16. In this recording the masculine addresses continue through the last verse. They can refer back to الشَّخص, the poet's grammatically masculine word for her person in 12.1. In 14.1 أَنَّكَ مُتَّ leads the feminine by 10.23 and the alternative first vowel مِتَّ by 3.70. The independent phones and formants at the ends of أنك and مت are open vowels. At 15.1 the masculine «وزلتَ ولم ترى» leads the feminine «وزلتِ ولم تَرَيْ» by 10.34. The two full transcripts write ترى. The scorer cannot distinguish short from long a, but the reciter sustains the open vowel before يوماً. The long syllable fills the required second position in the foot. Al-Wahidi's electronic text writes ترى without marking the preceding pronoun. For the retention of final alif with a jussive in verse, compare Nazir al-Jaysh's discussion of «كأن لم ترى قبلي». The familiar feminine reading and the extended masculine vowel are both explained in the word notes. [Grammatical parallel](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/11356/295)

Both performances of the cloud-prayer favour مَثواكَ (by 1.69 and 2.78) and كَفِّكَ (by 4.35 and 5.56). The first word alone has a small score difference. In both instances the vowel after k opens and its second formant drops towards 1,300 Hz, unlike the following clear i in the same recording. In the second half the phone pass independently gives /k a/ or /k ɛ/ on both takes, and the formants and candidate ranking support the same masculine ending. Neither whole-file transcriber supplies the short vowels on those suffixes.

On the re-cut halves, the independent Cohere transcript supplies the vowels on أَنَّكَ مُتَّ at 14.1 and وَزُلْتَ وَلَمْ تَرَى at 15.1. It gives masculine مَثْوَاكَ on the first closing bayt and masculine كَفِّكَ on both performances, although it lengthens the first vowel of كفك on the repeat. It also gives ذِكْرُنَاهُ explicitly at 12.2. The complete transcripts agree on the underlying words, while the re-cut clips and the local acoustic measurements distinguish the vowels.

The final comparison flags five of the 34 clips, with eight differences across 153 words. The extended cuts now pass at 2.1, 12.2, 13.1, 14.2, 15.2 and on the first performance of 16.1. On the remaining flags the wider Audar pass still hears «اللالي» at 2.2, restores «تكسرت» at 6.2 and gives the initial «أطاب» at 14.1. At 5.1 it recovers الدهر and حتّى but substitutes الأرزاق for الأرزاء. The complete Cohere transcript preserves the printed opening. On the repeat of 16.1 both short models misdivide «سقى مثواك». The first take and both occurrences in the complete Cohere transcript preserve the distinct words.
