## Attribution

To Abu Firas in Ibn Khalawayh's recension of his diwan (al-Dahhan; the Tunisian manuscript in Bencherifa), by al-Tha'alibi (d. 429), a near-contemporary, in his chapter on the captivity, by al-Zamakhshari («في شعر الحمداني»), Ibn al-Jawzi, Safi al-Din al-Halabi (*Uns al-Masjun*), Ibn Khallikan and al-Damamini, and by al-Taftazani as al-Kashkul quotes him. Ibn Hisham does not name the poet («بعض المحدثين»). No source read gives the poem to anyone else.

## Is it one of the Rumiyyat?

Yes, and the sources say so; it is not an inference from its theme:

- Ibn Khalawayh's heading has him a prisoner when he heard the dove: «وَهُوَ في الأَسرِ» (al-Dahhan, whose apparatus gives the words to با; al-Duwayhi), «وهو مأسور» (Bencherifa's Tunisian manuscript; al-Dahhan's ح); Ibn Khallikan has «وهو في الأسر». The headings al-Dahhan records from ب, ي and طا, and al-Tha'alibi's, have no word of captivity.
- Al-Damamini (d. 827): «وذلك في زمن أسر الروم له» (*Masabih al-Jami'*, vol. 4, p. 435); al-Baghdadi quotes him elsewhere as «قال وهو في أسر الروم». Safi al-Din al-Halabi heads it «ولأبي فراس بن حمدان في أسره» (*Uns al-Masjun*, p. 132).
- Al-Dahhan lists it among the poems of the captivity, 351–355 AH, and calls those poems the Rumiyyat (pp. 76, 91, 92).
- Al-Tha'alibi quotes it inside his chapter on the poems of the captivity (p. 85 onwards).
- Al-Taftazani, as al-Kashkul quotes him, gives it as one of the Rumiyyat: «فازدادت رومياته رقة ولطافة. فمنها ما قال وقد سمع حمامة…».
- The poem itself: «أَيَضحَكُ مَأسورٌ وَتَبكي طَليقَةٌ» (6.1).

No source dates it within the captivity or says where he was held when he wrote it (al-Tha'alibi has him held at Kharshana and then Constantinople, p. 85). The intro gives al-Dahhan's span.

On a tighter window (38.24–40.50 s, `_scratch/r2/variants_r2.log`) «هل» still leads: «ما» −2.3, «يا» −0.5, «قد» −6.3; the certain «هل» of 1.2 scored the same way gives «ما» −3.7, «يا» −2.9.
- **4.2 «تَعالي», with kasra:** the reciter says the first «تعالي» of 4.2 with kasra, the form the grammarians report for this half. In 5.1 he says it with the fatha, as al-Dahhan and al-Duwayhi print both. The text follows him (in the house style a kasra before its ي isn't written: «تَعالي»), and the note gives the editions' fatha and the grammarians. The aligner leans to the kasra by 0.3 over the half and 1.1 on the word, a coin toss. The first formant of the vowel after the lam (`_scratch/f_*.txt`) is the evidence:
  - 4.2's first «تعالي» (44.26–44.36 s): about 360 Hz, with F2 rising to about 2,400 Hz;
  - the rhyme «تعالي», certainly ī (48.82–48.91 s): about 390 Hz, F2 2,200–2,400 Hz;
  - 5.1's «تعالَي» (50.30–50.40 s): about 485 Hz, F2 1,600–1,840 Hz before the y glide;
  - the fatha of «لَدَيَّ» (52.90–52.99 s): about 500 Hz.
  - So 4.2's first vowel is a close i, like the rhyme, and 5.1's is an open a, like «لَدَيَّ». Both transcribers write «تعالي» and can't tell.
  - The second reader's own track agrees (`_scratch/r2/f42a.txt`, `f51a.txt`): after the lam of 4.2's first word (44.22–44.26 s) the vowel has F1 390–430 Hz from its first 10 ms, with F2 rising to 1,900–2,100 Hz, for 280 ms, as the rhyme's ī (from 48.82 s: F1 365–450, F2 2,070–2,280). 5.1's «تعالَي» has 80 ms of open a (50.32–50.40 s: F1 500–525, F2 1,600–1,670) before the glide, as «تَرَي» has (51.04–51.10 s). Scored on the whole word (43.50–44.58 s), the kasra leads by 1.1, the same lean as the rhyme's certain ī (1.7, 47.64–49.10 s), while 5.1's first word leans 3.8 to the fatha.
- **1.2:** the candidates differ in length, so the scores are only a guide. The full transcript and both transcribers of a wide clip (6.5–12.9 s) hear «هل تشعرين بحالي».
- **3.1 «أَتَحمِلُ», 7.2 «في»:** close scores, and the shorter candidate («وَ») is favoured by default. Both transcribers hear «أتحمل» and «في» in the half clips and in wider ones (`asr_cohere/`, `asr_audar/`: w31, w72, 004, 013).
- **Meter:** all 14 halves scan as tawil, its third form: the arud maqbuda (mafa'ilun, u-u-) and the darb mahdhuf (fa'ulun, u--). I scanned 1.1 and 4.1 by hand, and every half with `…/b4/tools/scan.py`, which flags nothing. Licences:
  - 3.1 «قَوادِمٌ»: a diptote given tanwin for the meter, as all three editions print it. Without the tanwin the arud ends «-dimu» (u-uu), which tawil's arud doesn't allow. The reciter says the tanwin: a nasal (F1 310–350 Hz) follows the vowel of «-mu» at 30.88–31.08 s.
  - The rhyme words «بِحالي، بِبالِ، عالِ، تَعالي، بالِ، سالِ، غالِ» end in -ālī: the manqus nouns are written as the editions write them and said with the rhyme's long vowel.
  - The ي of «نائي» in 3.2 is said short before the article (nā'i l-masāfati).
  - The opening is not muṣarraʿ: its sadr ends «حَمامَةٌ».

## Commentary drawn on

- **The sharh** follows the footnote on the poem in al-Kashshaf (vol. 1, p. 526):
  - 1: the dove addressed as one who understands («لتنزيلها منزلة العاقل كما في ندائها»);
  - 2: «معاذ الهوى» on the pattern of «معاذ اللَّه», «لعظمة الهوى عنده … أى ألتجئ إلى الهوى، من دعوى أنك مثلي»; parting likened to a hateful food that is tasted;
  - 1.2, «جارتا»: the ي turned to alif to lengthen the call;
  - 4: the repeated call shows regret; fate unfair «حيث أطلقك وأسرك وأسرنى وأحزننى» (so the edition; «وسرّك», "and pleased you", is the likely word behind «وأسرك»); «أقاسمك الهموم»: «فلي النصف ولك الآخر»;
  - 5: «ضعف الروح: عجز حواسها عن الإدراك»; «تردد» for «تتردد»; «بالي» is «نحيل»;
  - 6: the captive and the grieving are the poet, the free one and the carefree the dove; «الندبة: رفع الصوت بالبكاء، والمراد به النوح السابق»; «والسالى: الصابر وقليل الهموم»;
  - 7: «مقلة» an accusative of specification; «غالى» is «مرتفع وممتنع لتجلد الشامتين».
- **Bayt 3:** the commentators are silent. The footnote's text lacks the bayt, al-Duwayhi glosses only «القوادم» and «النائي», al-Tibi quotes only 4–6, and the editor of *Uns al-Masjun* glosses only «القوادم» (from al-Qamus). The sharh now says so in both languages. The sharh's reading, that a bird borne up to so high and far a branch cannot be grieving, is ours. It follows from 2 and 4 and from the grammar («مَحزونَ الفُؤادِ» object, «قَوادِمٌ» subject). Another reading is possible: he, the sad-hearted one, could not be carried up to her branch. It fits the denial in 2 and 4 less well.
