## Title

The poem has no settled name. No book on shamela calls it «مقصورة المتنبي»; al-Badi'i (*al-Subh al-Munbi*, vol. 1, p. 144, shamela 21498/143) calls it «المقصورة التي أولها: ألا كل ماشية الخيزلى», and the uploads, the editions and the poetry sites name it by its opening. So the title is the first sadr. The id `maqsurat-al-mutanabbi`, set before the work began, keeps the descriptive name the reciter's upload and al-Badi'i use.

### Enjambed abyat

Five abyat run a word across the caesura: 4 (القِمار), 7 (النِّقاب), 9 (الدَّبور), 11 (الرِّداء), 17 (الرِّماح). All three editions and the reciter's own description split the word where the meter does («ضَرَبتُ بِها التّيهَ ضَربَ القِما / رِ إِمّا لِهَذا وَإِمّا لِذا»), and so does the text, so each half scans as a half. The notes on those words say where the rest of the word is.

## Meter

Mutaqarib (المتقارب), each half four feet: فعولن or فعولُ three times, then فعَل in every ajuz (al-Ma'arri: «من المتقارب الثالث», *al-Lami' al-Azizi*, shamela 29591/28) and فعولن, فعولُ or فعَل in the sadr. Every half was scanned (the house scanner, with the feet set for mutaqarib). Three halves the scanner flagged scan correctly once its blind spots are allowed for: 3.1 «وَلَكِنَّهُنَّ» (lākinna, which it reads short), 4.2 «لِهَذا» (hādhā, the same) and 26.2 «مَهامِهُ مِن» (it lengthens the root ه of مَهامِه as if it were a pronoun).

The meter decides:
- 2.2 «بِيَ» (biya, as al-Wahidi and al-Ukbari vowel it): «بي» leaves the half a syllable short.
- 24.2 «قَدَرِ», as al-Wahidi and al-Barquqi vowel it: al-Ukbari's print has «قَدْرِ», which leaves the half a syllable short.
- 13.1 «صَوَرٌ»: the transcript's «صوار» is the stretched vowel; صُوار breaks the second foot.
- 7.2 «وادي» and 10.1 «رَوامي»: the ya is silent, as al-Wahidi says («سكّن الياء ضرورةً»).

## Commentary drawn on

- **al-Wahidi** throughout, for the meaning of each bayt and most glosses: الخيزلى and الهيذبى, النجاة, بجاوة (with Ibn Jinni's story from the poet), الخنوف, المشى, الميط, التيه as gambling, the camels' «choice» at al-Naqab (with «يشكو إليّ جملي طول السرى»), Ibn Jinni on Turban, هبّت from الهباب, روامي, Busayta, al-Jurawi (with the verse «ألا لا أرى ماء الجراوي شافيا»), صور and al-Jarmi's صوري, الشغور and «إذا وردت الشغور فقد أعرقت», الدئداء, الصوى, the dispute over 16 (Ibn Jinni, al-Qadi al-Jurjani, Ibn Furraja, whose reading al-Wahidi adopts), 17–20, 22–26, 27 (his 36); and the slaves who took a bull for a minaret and an ostrich for a palm at Busayta (pp. 698–699).
- **al-Ukbari:** التيه as the Wilderness of the Israelites between al-Qulzum and Ayla, which the poet crossed, also called بطن نخل (6.1); the horses led beside the camels (5) and the pairing of white swords and brown spears; the second reading of the camels' choice (7); ها as a particle standing for its sentence (8); Hisma and the poet's praise of it (9); والصباح in the accusative or nominative (13); التوى and the «مقابلة حسنة واستعارة جيدة» of 23; الخويدم and the common use of خادم for a eunuch (25); «وثُبنا» (18).
- **al-Barquqi:** بجاوة as a land in Nubia; the second reading of 2.2 and of 6.2; the second reading of 8 (Turban far from Iraq, the camels' speed); وادي الغضى as a badal; Busayta between Kufa and Mecca; أعكش near Kufa; العواصم with Antioch as its capital; his reading of 20 (the vow to leave Egypt); «بيت القصيد» for the last bayt; الهيذبى as a horse's pace; «مهامه» as the noun of كان, «بيننا» its predicate (26).
- **Ibn al-Iflili:** Turban «بعيد من أرض العراق» and al-Asma'i's reading of distant places made near by speed (8); الأضارع and الدنا as waters (14); الآلة as «الشدة والجرأة» (22).
- **The occasion:** al-Wahidi's heading (Kufa, Rabi' al-Awwal 351); his heading to «عيد بأية حال» for the flight from Egypt in 350 (shamela 754/343: «وقال يوم عرفة وقد خرج من مصر سنة خمسين وثلثمائة»). Al-Badi'i dates the arrival in Kufa to Rabi' al-Akhir 351; the intro gives al-Wahidi's date.
- **The intro's verdict:** Shawqi Dayf, *al-Fann wa-madhahibuhu fi al-shi'r al-'arabi*, p. 335 (shamela 8666/366, `dayf.txt`): «إذ يحشد فيها الألفاظ الغريبة حشدًا».
- **Wadi al-Qura:** Yaqut, *Mu'jam al-Buldan*, vol. 5, p. 345 (shamela 23735/2378): «واد بين المدينة والشام من أعمال المدينة كثير القرى».

## Review

The drafter checked the draft first, when no second reader could be started (it fixed 2.1's vowels, the 2.1.3 note and the wording of 16). A second reader then checked it independently (2026-11-17): every half against al-Barquqi's page images and, by script, word by word against the reciter's own description; the readings in the tables against al-Wahidi's and al-Ukbari's text and commentary; every half scanned for the mutaqarib; the title searched again on shamela; seven more vowels scored on the audio; the sharh, notes and intro against al-Wahidi, al-Ukbari, al-Barquqi's notes (page images, pp. 151–157) and Ibn al-Iflili. Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** no change. The drafter's four doubts all come out for the text: the reciter's description prints «الهَيْذَبَى», «وَكُلِّ نَجَاةٍ بُجَاوِيَّةٍ», «بِنَخْلٍ» and «بَعضَ», and al-Ma'arri and Ibn Jinni (quoted by al-Zawzani) also have ذ in 1.2. The title stays the first sadr: shamela has no «مقصورة المتنبي» or «مقصورة أبي الطيب», only al-Badi'i's «المقصورة التي أولها». Recorded above, with the new tests.
- **Meaning:** 16 opened, in both languages, with the reading it then rejects (al-Ruhayma reached in the middle of the night). It now opens with Ibn Furraja's reading, which al-Wahidi adopts: a water in the middle of A'kush, with most of the night still ahead. Ibn Jinni and al-Jurjani follow.
- **Sharh:** 2: Ibn Jinni's story of the Bajawi spear is a fact about a people, so it moved to the note, and the sharh now says what the poet does want; 11 English: "as a blade cuts a cloak" (there is no blade in the bayt or the commentaries) now "splitting it down the middle as a cloak is split"; 14 English: "fast trot" contradicted the note (دئداء is faster than a trot) and now reads "racing pace".
- **Notes:** added 20.1.4 «أَبَيتُ», 23.2.4 «قَلبَ», 25.2.4 «عَمىً» and 27.1.3 «نَفسُهُ» (27 had no note); 2.1.3 now carries the Bajawi story in both languages; 6.1.2 gives al-Ukbari's «بنحل» and his Batn Nakhl; 26.2.1 gives al-Barquqi's i'rab; English 16.1.2 and 16.1.4 fit the reading of 16.
- **English:** 14.1 "Their swift trot" now "Their racing pace".
- **Intro:** a sentence on why the poem is remembered, from the sources: al-Wahidi calls 24 a proverb, and al-Barquqi calls the last bayt «بيت القصيد».
