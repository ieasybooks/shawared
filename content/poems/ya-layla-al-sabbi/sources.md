## Editions

- **محمد المرزوقي والجيلاني بن الحاج يحيى, يا ليل الصب ومعارضاتها**, 2nd ed., enlarged (الدار العربية للكتاب, Tunis, 1986): scan archive.org `ar103anda192` (a PDF; PDF pages 11–16 are printed pp. 12–17). The whole poem, voweled where a vowel might be misread, headed «عليّ الحصري القيرواني»; the editors' introduction (pp. 7–11) says it has 99 abyat, 23 of them nasib, the turn to praise at 24, addressed to the prince of Murcia Abu Abd al-Rahman Muhammad ibn Tahir, and sets out three ways of reading the opening words. This is the edition `abyat_in_full` follows. The same editors wrote the poet's biography, «أبو الحسن الحصري القيرواني».
- **عبد الخالق جبريل أحمد جبريل, قصيدة أبي الحسن علي الحصري (ت ٤٨٨هـ) «يا ليل الصب» ومعارضاتها: دراسة بلاغية نقدية** (MA thesis, كلية اللغة العربية بالزقازيق, 2017): scan archive.org `gebreila_yahoo_488.`, printed pp. 34–35 (leaves 50–51) give the 23 abyat fully voweled, from al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani's biography of the poet, pp. 143 ff.; pp. 37–90 (leaves 53–106) read the nasib bayt by bayt. Its notes record the other readings of 1.1 (p. 38 n.) and 19.2 (p. 82 n. 1).
- **Zahr al-Adab** (Ibrahim al-Husri's anthology, Dar al-Jil edition): shamela 10768, vol. 1, pp. 9–10 (`zahr-intro.txt`). The editor's introduction, on the author's cousin Ali, quotes abyat 1–22, lightly voweled, and two mu'aradat. Several misprints (16.1 «دوايت», 16.2 «وتبده», 17.2 «فليبك؟؟؟»).
- **Partial quotations:** al-Bahai al-Amili, *al-Kashkul*, vol. 1, p. 211 (shamela 1571/209, 12 abyat); Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-A'yan*, vol. 3, p. 332 (shamela 1000/1343, abyat 1–2); al-Safadi, *Nakt al-Himyan*, p. 198 (shamela 28182/193, abyat 1–2); Shawqi Dayf, *Tarikh al-Adab al-Arabi*, vol. 9, p. 247 (shamela 11996/4420, 7 abyat); Nazik al-Mala'ika, *Qadaya al-Shi'r al-Mu'asir*, pp. 132–134 (shamela 2097/127–129, abyat 1–5 and 7–9); Abd al-Aziz Atiq, *Ilm al-Arud wa-l-Qafiya*, p. 128 (shamela 11298/129, abyat 1, 2, 12–15).

## Meter

Al-mutadarik in its khabab form: every foot فَعِلُن (u u −) or فَعْلُن (− −), four to a half. The schema has no separate khabab, and the prosody books put it under al-mutadarik: Mahmud Mustafa, *Ahda Sabil ila Ilmay al-Khalil*, p. 75 (shamela 10860/106), scans the opening bayt as eight فعلن, some with the second letter silent (تشعيث) and some with it moving (خبن); Abd al-Aziz Atiq, p. 128, gives this poem and Shawqi's «مضناك جفاه مرقده» as examples of المتدارك التام. Al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani (p. 7) and Nazik al-Mala'ika (p. 132) call the measure الخبب.

Every half was scanned against `(uu-|--){4}` (`/tmp/shw/ylas/scan_mt.py`, a copy of the batch's scanner with the foot pattern changed). All 46 halves scan. The scanner misreads two, which scan by hand: 15.1 «بِاللَّهِ هَبِ المُشتاقَ كَرىً» is `-- uu- -- uu-`; 17.2 «فَليَبكِ عَلَيهِ عُوَّدُهُ» is `-- uu- -- uu-` with the pronoun of «عَلَيهِ» lengthened, as it may be in verse. Rawiy د, with a voweled ه after it (ـدُهُ) in every half.

- **1.1:** al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani (pp. 10–11) give three ways of reading «يا ليل الصب»: «لَيلَ الصَّبِّ», a vocative with the lover's night addressed; «لَيلُ الصَّبُّ», the night addressed and «الصبّ متى غده» a sentence of its own; «لَيلُ الصَّبِّ» with يا for attention. They prefer the first two, and print the second. The reciter's vowels fit the second best, by a small margin. The word note on «لَيلُ» gives the first.
- **Meter decides:** 6.1 «قَنِصٌ» (the transcript's «قنيص» is a stretched vowel; «قَنيصٌ» breaks the fourth foot); 8.1 «صاحٍ», where «صاحِ» breaks the first foot as well as scoring worse; 17.2 «عُوَّدُهُ», plural of عائد (the transcript's «عواده» is a stretched vowel; «عُوّادُهُ» breaks the last two feet); 19.2 «مُوَرَّدُهُ», as the study prints it (al-Marzuqi prints «مَورِدُهُ», which breaks the third foot, as the study notes, p. 82 n. 1); 20.2 «تُبَعِّدُهُ», as al-Marzuqi and the study vowel it («تُبعِدُهُ» breaks the third foot); 20.1 «لِقاءَكُمُ», the mim voweled to complete the last foot; 23.1 «أَعَفُّ» (the transcript's «أعاف» is a stretched vowel).
- **20.2:** a coin toss by score, and the aligner hears ظ and ص only as z and s. The full transcript and the clip transcript both hear «وظروف», the second transcriber hears «وضروف» (ض, voiced like ظ; ص is voiceless), and al-Marzuqi prints it, so the text keeps «وَظُروفُ». The word notes give Zahr al-Adab's and the study's «وصروف», the blows of fortune.
- **Grammar decides:** 17.1 «لَم يُبقِ», with «هَواكَ» the subject and «رَمَقاً» the object, as the study prints it.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Meaning:** there is no classical commentary on the poem. The sharh follows the study's reading of each bayt (pp. 37–90): يرعاه, the lover watching the star and waiting for it to set (p. 45, after *Lisan al-Arab*, رعى); the snare of sleep that catches the phantom (p. 51); قنص as an intensive form (p. 54); the idol and the blind poet's sense of touch (p. 56, given as some scholars' view); صاح and معربد (pp. 58–59, after *Lisan*); the sword and its sheath of drowsiness (pp. 60–61); كلا as a rebuke to whoever would blame the beloved (p. 62); تورّده, the rosy flush of the lover's blood, and why "rose" rather than "red" (pp. 64–65); sleep asked for as a way of asking for union (p. 70); عُوَّد (p. 77); شرق and the play of «الشوق» and «شرق» (p. 81); مورّده as the tears, the eye or the cheek (pp. 82–83); the turn to the first person in 22 (p. 90).
- **Added here:** 10.2: the study reads «والويل لمن يتقلّده» as woe to whoever the sword strikes (p. 60). The sharh puts first the reading that «يتقلّده» means taking on the blood, or wearing the sword, so that the beloved's guilt is hinted and 11 «كلّا» takes it back, and gives the study's reading after it.
- **Glosses** follow the lexicons as the study quotes them (*Lisan al-Arab* for أرق, البين, رعى, هيف, لحظ, عربد, نضا, الصبابة, رمق, شرق and فيض; *Maqayis al-Lugha* for شرد; *al-Sihah* for جحد) and the ordinary dictionary senses elsewhere.
- **The occasion and the poem's length:** al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani, pp. 7–9: «ويظهر أن السبب في نظمه لهذا القصيد أن وشاية بلغت إلى الأمير تتهم الحصري بشتمه إياه في مجالسه». The intro says "think", as they do.
- **The fame of the prelude:** Ibn Khallikan's «وهي مشهورة فلا حاجة إلى إيرادها» (vol. 3, p. 332); his «صاحبنا» al-Qamrawi's mu'arada follows it. The study (p. 34) says the prelude's fame left many unaware that the poem goes on to praise. Shawqi's mu'arada: Atiq, p. 128; Dayf, vol. 9, p. 247, names Shawqi as the last of many who answered it.
- **The poet's life:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 4, pp. 300–301 (shamela 12286/3866–3867, `alam.txt`): name, death in Tangier in 488 (1095), blindness, Kairouan, al-Mu'tamid and «المستحسن من الأشعار», the diwan, «اقتراح القريح» on a son, the poem on the readings, the cousinship with Ibrahim al-Husri. Ibn Khallikan, vol. 3, pp. 331–333 (shamela 1000/1342–1344, `wafayat.txt`): Ibn Bassam's account of his coming to al-Andalus after Kairouan was ruined and of the Taifa kings, teaching the readings in Ceuta, the settling in Tangier, and the 500 dinars and the lines on Noah. Not to be confused with Ibrahim al-Husri (d. 453), the author of *Zahr al-Adab*.
