## Editions

- **محمد المرزوقي والجيلاني بن الحاج يحيى, يا ليل الصب ومعارضاتها**, 2nd ed., enlarged (الدار العربية للكتاب, Tunis, 1986): scan archive.org `ar103anda192` (a PDF; PDF pages 11–16 are printed pp. 12–17). ## Meter

Al-mutadarik in its khabab form: every foot فَعِلُن (u u −) or فَعْلُن (− −), four to a half. The schema has no separate khabab, and the prosody books put it under al-mutadarik: Mahmud Mustafa, *Ahda Sabil ila Ilmay al-Khalil*, p. 75 (shamela 10860/106), scans the opening bayt as eight فعلن, some with the second letter silent (تشعيث) and some with it moving (خبن); Abd al-Aziz Atiq, p. 128, gives this poem and Shawqi's «مضناك جفاه مرقده» as examples of المتدارك التام. Al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani (p. 7) and Nazik al-Mala'ika (p. 132) call the measure الخبب.

Every half was scanned against `(uu-|--){4}` (`/tmp/shw/ylas/scan_mt.py`, a copy of the batch's scanner with the foot pattern changed). All 46 halves scan. The scanner misreads two, which scan by hand: 15.1 «بِاللَّهِ هَبِ المُشتاقَ كَرىً» is `-- uu- -- uu-`; 17.2 «فَليَبكِ عَلَيهِ عُوَّدُهُ» is `-- uu- -- uu-` with the pronoun of «عَلَيهِ» lengthened, as it may be in verse. Rawiy د, with a voweled ه after it (ـدُهُ) in every half.

- **1.1:** al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani (pp. 10–11) give three ways of reading «يا ليل الصب»: «لَيلَ الصَّبِّ», a vocative with the lover's night addressed; «لَيلُ الصَّبُّ», the night addressed and «الصبّ متى غده» a sentence of its own; «لَيلُ الصَّبِّ» with يا for attention. They prefer the first two, and print the second. The study (p. 38 n. 2) adds «لَيلِ الصَّبُّ», “O my night”, and calls the printed reading the best known. The scorer (second reading, 2026-11-17, on the half's aligned times 0.54–4.86) puts «لَيلِ الصَّبُّ» first by 2.9, but it can't hear vowel quality reliably; the formants settle it. The vowel after the lam of «لَيلُ» (original 7.80–8.12 s) has F1 ≈ 550–650 Hz and F2 ≈ 950–1000 Hz, a back rounded vowel, where a kasra would put F2 near 2000 Hz like the glide of «لَيـ» just before it (2030–2100 Hz); the vowel after «الصَّبّ» (9.04–9.16 s) has F2 ≈ 800–840 Hz, again ḍamma, not kasra. So the reciter says «يا لَيلُ الصَّبُّ», the printed reading; the clip transcriber also writes «لَيْلُ الصَّابُّ». The word note on «لَيلُ» gives the other three.
- **Meter decides:** 6.1 «قَنِصٌ» (the transcript's «قنيص» is a stretched vowel; «قَنيصٌ» breaks the fourth foot); 8.1 «صاحٍ», where «صاحِ» breaks the first foot as well as scoring worse; 17.2 «عُوَّدُهُ», plural of عائد (the transcript's «عواده» is a stretched vowel; «عُوّادُهُ» breaks the last two feet); 19.2 «مُوَرَّدُهُ», as the study prints it (al-Marzuqi prints «مَورِدُهُ», which breaks the third foot, as the study notes, p. 82 n. 1); 20.2 «تُبَعِّدُهُ», as al-Marzuqi and the study vowel it («تُبعِدُهُ» breaks the third foot); 20.1 «لِقاءَكُمُ», the mim voweled to complete the last foot; 23.1 «أَعَفُّ» (the transcript's «أعاف» is a stretched vowel).
- **20.2:** a coin toss by score (the second reading's run: «وَضُروفُ» −51.7, «وَصُروفُ» −54.3, «وَظُروفُ» −55.5; the aligner hears ظ, ص and ض only as z, s and d). The sound settles it. Every ص this reciter says carries a long hiss: the share of energy at 4–11 kHz rises to 0.78–0.89 and stays above 0.35 for 45–200 ms (7.1 «صَنَمٌ», 16.2 «صَبٍّ», 1.1 «الصَّبُّ», 8.1 «صاحٍ»). The first consonant of 20.2 (original 188.35–189.0 s) has no hiss at all (peak share 0.03), like his ظ in 14.2 «وَأَظُنُّكَ» (0.06) and 18.2 «نَظَرٍ» (0.01) and his ض in 16.1 «ضَرَّكَ» (0.02). So he does not say «وصروف». The full transcript and the clip transcript hear «وظروف», the second transcriber «وضروف» (a voiced emphatic, as his ظ sounds), and al-Marzuqi prints it, so the text keeps «وَظُروفُ». The word notes give Zahr al-Adab's and the study's «وصروف», the blows of fortune, and the translation follows the text (circumstances), not «صروف».
- **22.2:** «لِفُؤادي» with kasra. The vowel of the lam (original 206.70–206.84 s) has F1 ≈ 365–390 Hz and F2 ≈ 1780–1850 Hz, an i; a fatha would have F1 near 600 Hz, as in «فَيا» just before. The grammar agrees: the lam of the one called for help (المستغاث به) takes fatha, «يا لَزيدٍ»; the lam of the one help is called for (المستغاث له) takes kasra, and here the poet calls (on no one named) for help for his heart, as the study reads it (p. 89: «يستغيث الشاعر لفؤاده»). The score agrees by 3.4.
- **Grammar decides:** 17.1 «لَم يُبقِ», with «هَواكَ» the subject and «رَمَقاً» the object, as the study prints it (al-Zuhur has «بِهِ» for «لَهُ»; the reciter says «لَهُ»).

## Commentary drawn on

- **Meaning:** there is no classical commentary on the poem. The sharh follows the study's reading of each bayt (pp. 37–90): يرعاه, the lover watching the star and waiting for it to set (p. 45, after *Lisan al-Arab*, رعى); the snare of sleep that catches the phantom, and Majnun's «وإني لأستغشي وما بي نعسة» (p. 51); قنص as an intensive form (p. 54); the idol and the blind poet's sense of touch (p. 56, the study's own suggestion, «ويبدو أن…»); صاح and معربد (pp. 58–59, after *Lisan*); the sword and its sheath of drowsiness (pp. 60–61); كلا as a rebuke to whoever would blame the beloved (p. 62); the two cheeks as the two witnesses the law requires (p. 66); تورّده, the rosy flush of the lover's blood, and why "rose" rather than "red" (pp. 64–65); sleep asked for as a way of asking for union, and the cure in 16 that only union can bring (pp. 70, 74); عُوَّد, a plural of many, and death blamed on love rather than on the beloved (p. 77); the glance in 18 as provision to stay alive (p. 78, given as «وقيل»); the addressee of 19–20 (p. 80: the beloved, addressed in the plural, since «لقاءكم» in 20 must be said to him; the study rejects the fellow lovers, whom the sharh keeps as «وقيل»); شرق and the play of «الشوق» and «شرق» (p. 81); مورّده as the tears (preferred), the eye or the cheek (pp. 82–83); time blamed for the parting out of tact (p. 85); the run-on from 21 into 22, التضمين (p. 87); the lam of «لفؤادي» and the turn to the first person in 22 (pp. 89–90); «غيري يفسده» as «I do not», after Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani (p. 91).
- **Added here:** 10.2: the study reads «والويل لمن يتقلّده» as woe to whoever the sword strikes (p. 60). The sharh puts first the reading that «يتقلّده» means taking on the blood (تقلّد الأمر: احتمله, *Lisan*, قلد), or girding on the sword, so that the beloved's guilt is hinted and 11 «كلّا» takes it back, and gives the study's reading after it. Al-Arrajani's answer to the poem (in Zaki Mubarak's introduction, vol. 1, p. 10) uses the word in that sense: «ودمي لا يحسن محمله في الناس فلم تتقلّده». 23: the turn to praise quotes bayt 24 from al-Marzuqi, p. 13: «كالدهر أجلّ بنيه أبو عبد الرحمان محمّده». 13: Shawqi's «جحدت عيناك زكيّ دمي، أكذلك خدّك يجحده» is from al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani, p. 8 n. 2.
- **Glosses** follow the lexicons as the study quotes them (*Lisan al-Arab* for أرق, البين, رعى, هيف, لحظ, عربد, نضا, الصبابة, رمق, شرق and فيض; *Maqayis al-Lugha* for شرد; *al-Sihah* for جحد) and the ordinary dictionary senses elsewhere.
- **The occasion and the poem's length:** al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani, p. 9: «ويظهر أن السبب في نظمه لهذا القصيد أن وشاية بلغت إلى الأمير تتهم الحصري بشتمه إياه في مجالسه»; they add that he was then teaching in a mosque of Murcia, and quote the poem's apology («ما لي ذنب فتعاقبني، كذب الواشي تبّت يده»). They claim no more than this; the intro says "think", as they do, and quotes that line. The 99 abyat, 23 of them nasib and the turn at 24: p. 9.
- **The fame of the poem:** Ibn Khallikan's «وهي مشهورة فلا حاجة إلى إيرادها» (vol. 3, p. 332) is said of the whole poem («القصيدة التي أولها…»), not of the prelude; his «صاحبنا» al-Qamrawi's mu'arada («وازنها») follows it. The study (p. 34) says the prelude's fame left many unaware that the poem goes on to praise. Singers: al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani, pp. 7–8. Al-Arrajani's answer: Zaki Mubarak's introduction to Zahr al-Adab, vol. 1, p. 10. Shawqi: Dayf, vol. 9, p. 247, names him as the last of many who answered it; al-Zuhur (1910) tells that a singer sang the poem at a musical evening Shawqi attended and the company asked him to answer it; Atiq, p. 128, quotes the two openings together.
- **The poet's life:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 4, pp. 300–301 (shamela 12286/3866–3867, `alam.txt`): name, death in Tangier in 488 (1095), blindness, Kairouan, al-Mu'tamid and «المستحسن من الأشعار», the diwan, «اقتراح القريح» on a son, the poem on the readings, the cousinship with Ibrahim al-Husri. Ibn Khallikan, vol. 3, pp. 331–334 (shamela 1000/1342–1345, `wafayat.txt`): Ibn Bassam's account of his coming to al-Andalus after Kairouan was ruined and of the Taifa kings, teaching the readings in Ceuta, the settling in Tangier, and the 500 dinars and the lines on Noah (p. 333–334: sent «وهو بالقيروان», after which «دخل الأندلس بعد ذلك، وامتدح المعتمد»). Ibn Khallikan and al-A'lam make him Ibrahim al-Husri's «ابن خالة», a maternal cousin; Dayf (vol. 9, p. 247) says «ابن أخت», a nephew; the bio follows al-A'lam. Not to be confused with Ibrahim al-Husri (d. 453), the author of *Zahr al-Adab*.

## Review

A second reader checked the text against al-Marzuqi and al-Jilani's page images (pp. 7–13) and the study's (pp. 34–35, 38), scanned every half, scored the doubtful readings again and measured the disputed sounds, and checked the sharh, notes, intro and bio against the study, the introductions, Ibn Khallikan and al-A'lam (2026-11-17). The verse text needed no change. Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** 1.1 «يا لَيلُ الصَّبُّ», 20.2 «وَظُروفُ» and 22.2 «لِفُؤادي» confirmed by the formants and the hiss of ص (above), where the scores alone were too close or pointed the wrong way (1.1 «لَيلِ», 20.2 «وَصُروفُ»); 13.2, 17.2 and 19.2 scored and their other readings recorded; al-Zuhur added to the sources.
- **Meaning:** 19–20 read with the study as addressed to the beloved in the plural, the fellow lovers kept as «وقيل» (the draft spoke of “loved ones” in 20 and of fellow lovers in 19); 18 no longer says he asked for union before sleep (15 asks for sleep, 16 for a cure, 18 for a glance), and the study's reading of the glance as food for life added; 23 «غيري» read, after al-Jurjani, as “I do not”, not as a charge against others; 10 the sword reading put back into the Arabic, and al-Arrajani's «فلم تتقلّده» cited for the blood reading.
- **English:** 20.2 translated from the text («ظروف», circumstances), not from «صروف»; 1.2 «قيام الساعة» rendered as the rising of the Hour, without the added “when the dead rise”; 10.2 “who must bear” (a compulsion not in the Arabic) corrected.
- **Sharh:** 6 and 8 no longer repeat their notes; 5 (the phantom shy as a gazelle; Majnun), 13 (two witnesses; Shawqi's borrowing), 16 (the cure is union), 17, 21 (التضمين) and 23 (the turn to praise, with bayt 24) deepened; English 1 (the other reading), 2, 9, 12, 19, 20, 22 and 23 deepened; Zahr al-Adab's «وصروف» moved from the sharh of 20 to its note.
- **Notes:** 1.1.2 gives all four readings of the opening and both referents of the pronoun; 2.2.1 «أَسَفٌ», 16.1.1-2 «ما ضَرَّكَ», 20.1.3 «لِقاءَكُمُ», 23.2.1 «غَيري» added (English for 16.1.1-2 and 23.2.1); 4.1.1 gives the study's «أنا كلف»; 8.1.3 and 23.1.3 no longer repeat the sharh; 12.1.3 and 20.2.1 name every source of the other reading; 22.2.1 explains the kasra.
- **Intro:** Ibn Khallikan's «وهي مشهورة» is said of the whole poem, not of the prelude, and is no longer used to show the prelude outshone it (the study says that, and is now named for it); the editors' evidence for the slander, the singers, al-Arrajani, and the story of Shawqi's answer (al-Zuhur) added.
- **Poet:** the 500 dinars put where Ibn Khallikan puts them, while he was still in Kairouan, before he crossed to al-Andalus and praised al-Mu'tamid; “maternal cousin” for «ابن خالة».
- **sources.md:** Zaki Mubarak named as Zahr al-Adab's editor; the study's view of the idol credited to the study, not to “some scholars”; page references for everything the sharh now draws on.
