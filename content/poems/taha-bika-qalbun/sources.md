# طَحا بِكَ قَلبٌ فِي الحِسانِ طَروبُ

## Introduction and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed introduces the poem with «قال علقمة بن عبدة بن النعمان بن قيس، أحد بني ربيعة بن مالك بن زيد مناة بن تميم، يمدح الحارث بن أبي شمر الغساني، وكان أسر أخاه شأسًا، فرحل إليه يطلب فيه، فأطلقه وسبعين أسيرًا من بني تميم كانوا أُسروا في ذلك اليوم، وهو يوم عين أباغ». The two full-file transcribers misrecognise the last place name to varying degrees. The video's introduction and the scholarly accounts identify عين أباغ. The genealogy in the speech and in the *Mufaddaliyyat* has النعمان. Al-Zirikli gives ناشرة in the poet's lineage. Saqr's heading calls the king الحارث بن جبلة الغساني. The headnote to the recitation names him ابن أبي شمر. The introduction reports the release of Sha's and seventy other Tamimis. The poem itself asks for the release and does not narrate the king's response. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oBU-SoD13fo) · [Al-Mufaddaliyyat, p. 390](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/364) · [Al-A'lam, IV, p. 247](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3708)

The decoded source lasts 391.430 s. The retained interval is 45.77–383.41 s (337.64 s). The recording proceeds through 39 bayts and ends after «فإني امرؤ وسط القباب غريب». The terminal silence starts at 382.813 s. All 78 halves are taken once. The descriptions of the camel's travel, the horses and the aftermath of battle each contain distinct bayts. Their order changes considerably between editions.

## Sequence in the witnesses

- Al-Sayyid Ahmad Saqr, *Sharh Diwan Alqama al-Fahl*, Cairo, first edition, 1935, قافية الباء, no. 1, pp. 9–19. Counted from «طحا بك قلب» to «فلا تحرمني», the scan contains 43 bayts. The 39 recited correspond in order to **1–12, 14–31, 33, 36–43**. Omitted are 13 «وعيس بريناها»، 32 «ولست لإنسي»، 34 «وأنت أزلت الخنزوانة» and 35 «وأنت الذي آثاره». Saqr prints 32 but says on p. 16 that he regards its attribution to another poet as correct. His last line, printed alone above the next poem on p. 19, is also the reciter's last. [Printed p. 9](https://archive.org/details/diwan-3alqama/page/n15/mode/2up) · [p. 16](https://archive.org/details/diwan-3alqama/page/n22/mode/2up)
- Al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, *al-Mufaddaliyyat*, ed. Ahmad Muhammad Shakir and Abd al-Salam Harun, no. 119, pp. 390–396, also has 43. Its numbers in **recording order** are **1–11, 15, 17, 18, 13, 19, 20, 14, 21, 22, 16, 23, 25, 27–31, 34, 33, 32, 35–39, 42, 43, 24**. The four unrecited bayts here are 12, 26, 40, 41. Thus 24 in the *Mufaddaliyyat* closes the recording, while its own close, «وما مثله …», is the penultimate recited bayt. [P. 391](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/365) · [p. 393](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/367) · [p. 394](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/368) · [p. 396](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/370)
- Al-A'lam al-Shantamari, *Ash'ar al-shu'ara' al-sitta al-jahiliyyin*, section «شرح المختار من شعر علقمة», p. 25 in the electronic edition. Of its 40 bayts, the recording follows **1–28, 30, 31, 29, 32–39**. Its final «فلست لإنسي …» is absent. Letter loss and false spacing elsewhere in the electronic text, e.g. «هو لهن» and «فويهم», should not be promoted to independent poetic readings. ## Readings and commentary

At 3.1 the reciter has «لا يستطاع كِلامها», with «لا» as in al-Shantamari. Saqr p. 9 and Shakir–Harun p. 391 give «ما يستطاع». Shakir–Harun gloss «الكِلام» as the verbal noun of «كالم» and specify «بكسر الكاف». The card at 69 s has the same kasra. The vowel after the kaf has a low first formant and a second formant around 2.0–2.2 kHz in the recording. At 5.2 the voice has the spatial «حيث تصوب» with Saqr p. 10. Shakir–Harun p. 392 gives the temporal «حين تصوب». [Shakir–Harun, printed pp. 391–392](https://archive.org/details/20240401_20240401_0413/page/n390/mode/2up)

At 6.1 the card at 96 s marks «وعارضٌ». Saqr p. 10 expands the prayer into two clauses, «سقاك سحاب يمان مركوم، وسقاك سحاب عارض». The second cloud is another subject of «سقاك». The electronic Shakir–Harun text gives «وعارضٍ». At 7.1 the card at 102 s and the **printed** Shakir–Harun p. 392 have accusative «رَبَعِيَّةً». The electronic transcription has «رَبَعِيَّةٍ». Saqr pp. 10–11 identifies the Rabi'a lineage and the well at Tharmada, then explains the speaker's claim to know the ailments of love. [Saqr, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/diwan-3alqama/page/n16/mode/2up) · [Shakir–Harun, printed p. 392](https://archive.org/details/20240401_20240401_0413/page/n391/mode/2up) · [Electronic transcription](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/366)

App 15–22 uses Saqr pp. 12–14 for the camel Qarub, the short ribs, the two guiding stars, the cairns, the discoloured well and the watering custom of *tandiya*. The conjunctions do not imply that these desert scenes occur in the same sequence in the two recensions. At 24.1 «بنو كعبِ بنِ عوف» has the regular elision of the alif of *ibn* between names. The royal fosterling left behind has competing identifications. Saqr p. 15 glosses him as Sha's. C. J. Lyall, *The Mufaddaliyat*, II, no. CXIX, pp. 327–333, discusses the royal fosterlings, including a tradition about al-Mundhir, and compares versions of the battle. The bilingual explanation retains the uncertainty. At 27 the pair «مخذم ورسوب» are the two prized swords named by the poet. At 29–32 the tribal identifications are traditional and sometimes disputed. [Saqr, printed pp. 14–15](https://archive.org/details/diwan-3alqama/page/n21/mode/2up)

At 28.1 the recording and Saqr p. 16 give «فجالدتهم», Shakir–Harun p. 395 «فقاتلتهم». At 31.2 the reciter and Saqr p. 17 have «وأنت بها يوم اللقاء تطيب», Shakir–Harun «فأنت بها عند اللقاء خصيب». At 33.1 the card has «فداحص». Shakir–Harun print «فداحض» and expressly accept both spellings. They gloss it as a man kicking the ground. Saqr p. 17 describes him lifting his legs as he dies. For the sky-calf, Saqr records the Thamud tradition, while Lyall II, pp. 332–333, argues for thunder in view of the cloud and thunderbolts in 34. At 37 the literal full bucket, «ذَنوب», gives Sha's his portion of the king's generosity. At 38 Saqr p. 18 has «قبيله / مساوٍ» as recited. Shakir–Harun p. 396 has «أسيره / مدانٍ», which admits a captive to the comparison with the king. Lyall II, p. 333, records both constructions. The metre is *tawil*, the rawiy ba. [Saqr, p. 17](https://archive.org/details/diwan-3alqama/page/n23/mode/2up) · [Shakir–Harun, pp. 395–396](https://archive.org/details/20240401_20240401_0413/page/n394/mode/2up)

The biography follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, IV, p. 247, for the name, affiliation and approximate death around 20 before the Hijra / 603 CE. Lyall II, pp. 327–328, recounts the judging of the horse poems by Umm Jundub and the historical setting of the Ghassanid victory in 554 CE. He distinguishes the narrative about the release from the language of the petition. [Al-Zirikli, IV, p. 247](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3708)

The mare in 35.1 is «شَطبة» on the card at 345 s, against Shakir–Harun's «شِطبة». In the source waveform the sibilant at about 340.70 s is followed by a short open vowel before the ṭ closure (F1/F2 approximately 575/1480 to 615/1340 Hz). The harekat are significant even though the consonantal transcripts coincide. Ibn Sida, *al-Mukhassas*, II, p. 94, gives Ibn Durayd's description of the long, lean mare, also reports Ibn al-A'rabi's kasra and calls the fatha preferable. [II, p. 94](https://shamela.ws/book/3590/571)

The opening sadr scans فعولُ مفاعيلن فعولُ فعولن. It matches the curtailed ending of the ajuz in the rhymed opening. In 19.1 the scansion is فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعلن and in 19.2 فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ فعولن. [Lyall, II, p. 328](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/n365/mode/2up) · [p. 332](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/n369/mode/2up) · [p. 333](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/n370/mode/2up)

At 19.1 the Farqadan guide Alqama on his journey. The two stars are Kochab and Pherkad in Ursa Minor. The same pair look down on generations of travellers in al-Ma'arri’s «غير مجد», verses 10–11. [Acevedo and Bénard, *The Stars of Indian Ocean Arab Navigation*, p. 21](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.14758605)
