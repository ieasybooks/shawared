# Labid ibn Rabi'a · بَلينا وَما تَبلى النُّجومُ الطَّوالِعُ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari recites the elegy for Arbad ibn Qays, Labid’s maternal half-brother. The original-clock interval is 0.06–161.26 s. There are 20 different bayts in the order printed by Ihsan Abbas, all heard once. The last two appear in brackets in the diwan. After bayt 17 the apparatus marks the end in manuscript م. The recorded poem continues with 18 and both bracketed concluding bayts. The last asks the diviners when a young man will die and when rain will fall. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iit92QcoSAg) · [Diwan, pp. 171–172](https://archive.org/details/3929pdf/page/n216/mode/2up)

## Witnesses and commentary

- *Sharh Diwan Labid ibn Rabi'a*, al-Tusi’s commentary, ed. Ihsan Abbas, no. 24, printed pp. 168–172. The opening on p. 168 names Arbad. At 4, «طريف» can mean newly acquired wealth or be understood as a man’s name. At 6 the flame returns to ash. At 7 the possessions are on loan for a lifetime. At 9 the rear of a moving herd is gathered in by its driver. The sword and its sheath are distinct at 14. The commentator treats death as a rise towards which some are drawing near and others are already climbing at 15. Those readings inform both sets of verse explanations. [pp. 168–169](https://archive.org/details/3929pdf/page/n213/mode/2up) · [pp. 170–172](https://archive.org/details/3929pdf/page/n215/mode/2up)
- Al-Jirawi, *al-Hamasa al-maghribiyya*, 2/1399–1400, no. 1010, selects our 1, 6, 8, 12, 13, 19 and 20. In particular it independently transmits the pair marked off by Abbas. It has plural «ودائع» at 8.1 where the recorded diwan reading is singular «وديعة». [2/1399](https://shamela.ws/book/6902/1341) · [2/1400](https://shamela.ws/book/6902/1342)
- Ibn Aydamur, *al-Durr al-farid*, 7/340–341. In an account of al-Mu'tasim hearing the elegy, the printed excerpt preserves the incipit, the prized neighbour at 2, the depopulated homes at 5, and other verses. The digital text has such slips as «الحبال» for the mountains and «يجور» for the flame’s returning to ash. For these the diwan, al-Jirawi and the recording agree. The anecdote is a witness to later reception. [7/340](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/3263) · [7/341](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/3264)
- Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 5/240, supplies the name, tribe, meeting with the Prophet, later residence in Kufa, old age and death date used in the poet profile. [5/240](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4678)

## Readings in the performance

The opening and the staff verse (12) are in tawil, with ayn as rawiy. At 2.1 the diwan’s «جار مضنّة» means a neighbour prized so dearly that one grudges the thought of losing him. «مظنّة» in the two recognisers confuses the emphatic consonants. Ibn Aydamur 7/341 transmits «مضنّة». At 5.2 «غَدْواً» means tomorrow in al-Tusi’s gloss. Both running transcripts and isolated Cohere give «غدوا». The word-only score favours the shorter «غداً» by 5.6, but the commentator explicitly glosses the written «غدواً» with «غداً», and Ibn Aydamur also transmits the longer form. [Diwan, pp. 168–169](https://archive.org/details/3929pdf/page/n213/mode/2up) · [Al-Durr, 7/341](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/3264)

The recording distinguishes «مُضمَرات من التقى» from the property called «مُعمَرات ودائع» at 7. Verse 8.1 then uses the singular «وديعة» of both property and family before «الودائع» at the rhyme. At 9.1 both complete recognisers and the isolated Cohere half give second-person «وتخلف بعدهم» beside the diwan’s «ونخلف بعدهم». The retained «وتُخلَفُ» addresses the one who remains behind while successive groups move forward. The change of person is also identified on the word card. At 14 «جفنه» is the sword’s scabbard and «القين» the smith. At 15.2 «عليك» is the address heard in the diwan reading, against «علينا» in Ibn Aydamur. At 16.1 «أعاذل» opens an address to a woman who finds fault. At 19–20 the diviners’ pebbles and birds set up the final test of their claims. [Diwan, pp. 170–172](https://archive.org/details/3929pdf/page/n215/mode/2up) · [Al-Durr, 7/341](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/3264)
