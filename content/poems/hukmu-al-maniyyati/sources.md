# حُكمُ المَنِيَّةِ في البَرِيَّةِ جارِ

## Recording and order

Osama al-Nahari, on his own channel, 17 June 2023. The source runs 903.442 s. Keep original 2.45–899.42 s. The first word begins at about 2.92 s. The last word, الضاري, is voiced through about 898.65 s and its echo fades by 899.33 s. A separate sound starting at 899.93 s repeats the opening studio effect. There is no spoken introduction. The 896.97 s of retained audio follow all 89 bayts of the numbered diwan in order, once each. The reciter’s on-screen text, the two original-clock transcripts and the sequence of 178 aligned halves identify the performance. The internal alignment breaks fall at app 206.58 s before 22.1, 387.45 s before 40.1 and 618.52 s before 63.1. The pauses remain in the recording. ## Editions, commentary and biography

- Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Rabi', ed., *Diwan Abi al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Tihami*, Maktabat al-Ma'arif, Riyadh, 1982, poem 47. The heading on p. 308 identifies an elegy for a son who died young. Pages 308–317 give the numbered text and lexical notes, pp. 318–319 the manuscript variants, and p. 320 an inventory of earlier quotations. This is the source of the total 89. Its notes distinguish the moonless end of the month from the full moon, explain the blade edge echoed in the word for a snatch of sleep, and identify the armour, the bubble on the water and the foster-nurses. [Opening, p. 308](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n307/mode/1up) · [Armour and water, p. 313](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n312/mode/1up) · [Last bayts, p. 317](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n316/mode/1up) · [Manuscript apparatus, p. 319](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n318/mode/1up)
- Al-Bakharzi, *Dumyat al-qasr wa-usrat ahl al-asr*, ed. Muhammad al-Tunji, I, pp. 140–149. He says he copied the elegy in his youth from the handwriting of Umar ibn Ali al-Muttawwi'i and names the son Abu al-Fadl. His text has the birthmates and the eyelids in 17, the five handspans in 25 and the near rhyme of the two senses of غِرار in 35. His order exchanges the descriptions at our 52–53 and 55–56. His version ends with the right and left hands at our 86. [Opening and provenance](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/124) · [The grave and the hidden fire](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/127) · [Armour, hospitality and the longer 57](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/130) · [Ending](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/133)
- Al-Jirawi, *al-Hamasa al-maghribiyya*, II, no. 487, pp. 867–869, selects 24 bayts, moves the submission of the soul (our 7) forward to third place and ends with the snares of death in 26. His next item is a separate elegy beginning «أبا الفضل طال الليل». Ahmad al-Hashimi, *Jawahir al-adab*, II, pp. 383–385, preserves a different selection, specifically attributing it to al-Tihami on the death of a young son. The electronic poetry corpus has a 90-bayt recension which introduces the armed defenders at its 14 and postpones the morning star until 39. Its other search hit is by Ibn al-Wardi. Neither supplies the order of this recording. The four witness extracts and their bayt-by-bayt collation are saved with the local research. [Al-Jirawi](https://shamela.ws/book/6902/809) · [End of his selection](https://shamela.ws/book/6902/811) · [Al-Hashimi](https://shamela.ws/book/21505/591)
- Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-a'yan*, no. 471, III, pp. 378–381. He quotes parts of the elegy, including the envious men at 75–76, and recounts the poet's arrest carrying letters from Hassan ibn Mufarrij to the Banu Qurra. He records his death in detention in Cairo on 9 Jumada I 416 AH and leaves open whether the poet’s nisba points specifically to Mecca or to Tihama more broadly. Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, IV, p. 327, supplies the form Ibn Nahd, the travels in Syria and Iraq, the preaching post in Ramla and the date 416 AH (1025 CE). [Ibn Khallikan on the elegy](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/1390) · [Arrest, date and nisba](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/1392) · [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3945)

Yaqut, *Mu'jam al-buldan*, under al-Ramla, III, p. 70, places the child's birth and death in Ramla during his father's tenure as preacher. He quotes the other ra elegy addressed to Abu al-Fadl and calls this one its sister. His extract gives the parallel behind the note on 15. In both poems the father hopes to see a crescent become a full moon. Thomas Bauer follows the later reception of this particular elegy in Ibn Nubata, who anthologised four of its bayts and returned to its opening rhyme in his own elegy for a son. [Yaqut on Ramla and the two elegies](https://shamela.ws/book/23735/1144) · [Bauer, “Communication and Emotion”, *Mamluk Studies Review* 7.1 (2003), pp. 54–57](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/api/records/rxt9a-8rs19/files/MSR_VII-1_2003-Bauer_pp49-95.pdf/content#page=6)

## Metre and readings

Full kamil, three feet per half. The opening runs «حُكْمُلْمَنِيْ / يَتِ فِلْبَرِيْ / يَتِ جَارِي». The first foot has idmar, the second is متفاعلن and the third is shortened to متفاعلْ to rhyme with the end of the bayt. The middle sadr at 42.1 divides «قَوْمُنْ إِذَا / لَبِسُدْدُرُو / عَ حَسِبْتَهَا», a contracted foot followed by two full متفاعلن feet. Ra is the rhyme consonant, with a preceding long alif and an audible extension of the final kasra. The divisions follow the printed halves even when the syntax runs on, as with «إن / هنّى» in 10 and the armoured men in 57. [Diwan, p. 308](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n307/mode/1up)

- **7–13, 19.** The diwan has «بأزمة المقدار» in 7, while both the recording and al-Bakharzi have «بأزمة الأقدار». Al-Bakharzi also gives «وتراكضوا» in 9. In 10, يُغِصّ is causative. Time makes a person choke even while offering pleasure. The equal-window fit favours يُغِصّ over يَغَصّ by 7.20. The reciter’s cards at 126 and 180 s both show a dotted ghayn in يُغتَبَط. So do both places in al-Rabi', who expressly glosses 19 as envy on p. 310. Al-Jirawi also gives ghayn in 13. Al-Bakharzi’s «لم يُعتبط» in 13 means that the boy might have been spared an untimely death. His tradition also has a reading of 19 about small stature inviting contempt. The full transcripts support ghayn in 13, although the local fit favours 'ayn by 5.00. The fit in 19 favours ghayn by 4.81 over يُعتبط and by 15.22 over يُحتقر. The printed and displayed text carries the reading at both places. The word notes preserve the alternatives. Ibn Manzur records both the weaker sense of admiring another's good fortune and the older association with the evil eye. [13 on screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VmY3tLrUBE&t=126s) · [19 on screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VmY3tLrUBE&t=180s) · [Al-Bakharzi, I, p. 142](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/126) · [Lisan al-arab, غبط, VII, p. 359](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/3742)
- **26–38.** In 26 the performance has «واغتال عمرك قاطع الأعمار» with al-Rabi', while al-Bakharzi has «واعتاق». The fire in 29 is hidden out of anguish, البُرَحاء, with al-Rabi' p. 311. Al-Bakharzi’s main text hides it from the observers الرُّقباء. The equal-window reading favours البرحاء by 22.85. In 31 the diwan gives نار الحزن and أورى, where the *Dumya* gives زند الحزن and وارٍ. The acoustic margins are 19.74 for نار and only 1.94 for أورى. The latter therefore rests also on the printed and displayed word. In 37–38 the take gives «تبلج الأسحار» and «الصبح يألف كفّه». Al-Bakharzi has الأنوار and «الفجر يرفع كفّه». Al-Rabi' p. 318 records both lifting and growing familiar with the tent flap. The word scores favour الأسحار by 17.72 and يألف over يرفع with the same الصبح by 19.74. [Al-Rabi', p. 311](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n310/mode/1up) · [Al-Bakharzi, I, pp. 143–144](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/127) · [The dawn card](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VmY3tLrUBE&t=375s)
- **43–56.** Al-Bakharzi’s apparatus records تكفّ for تمدّ in 43. The recorded reading leads by 18.38. In 46 راوَحوا leads his text’s زاوَجوا by 15.27. In **47.2** the studio card at 465 s has «وَعَمودَ», with fatha over an undotted 'ayn. The diwan, p. 313, reads «وعمود أنصلهم». Both continuous transcriptions and Cohere’s isolated half give the same consonant, and the equal-window fit favours عمود over غمود by 25.13. Al-Azhari quotes al-Nadr on the lengthwise ridge of a blade. Al-Bakharzi has غمود, the scabbards, giving a different companion to the armour bags. In 50 the diwan's «فتسربلوا بمتون ماء جامد» fits the take. Its first two words lead فتدرعوا بمتون by 14.77. In 56 ثاورته leads بارزته by 6.11. [47 on screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VmY3tLrUBE&t=465s) · [Al-Azhari, Tahdhib al-lugha, II, p. 150](https://shamela.ws/book/7031/454) · [Al-Bakharzi, I, p. 145](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/129)
- **57–62.** Al-Rabi' p. 314 and the performance join the rings of mail straight to the crowded host in 57. Al-Bakharzi I, p. 146, supplies an intervening comparison with bracelets on an archer’s wrist, then «ويجرّ ثمّ يجرّ صعدة رمحه». His apparatus explicitly records a witness that loses those two intervening halves. The commentary explains why the recorded construction hangs, and the English keeps that incomplete image. The editions have several different pairs of verbs at 62.2. Al-Rabi' p. 315 has n in the first verb, as does the studio card «يُداني». The two continuous transcriptions and the isolated half all render the consonant as r in both verbs. The three equal-window readings يُدارى, يُداني and يُداري lie within 1.00 log-score unit when the next word is included. Tested on its own, the first verb favours يُداري over يُداني by 2.14. The last verb, an internal control with the same speaker and melody, favours r in وَيُداري by 5.62. We retain the transcribed r and construe the first verb as passive to make sense of the repetition. That voice and the consonant cannot be established from the acoustic score. [Al-Bakharzi on the missing halves](https://shamela.ws/book/10665/130) · [Al-Rabi', p. 315](https://archive.org/details/3185pdf_202001/page/n314/mode/1up) · [62 on screen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VmY3tLrUBE&t=615s)
- **84–89.** At 84 the recording gives الوفاء over the variant الحياء by 14.41. At 86 اعتضد leads اعتصم by 11.60. Al-Rabi' p. 319 notes that 87–89 were absent from several manuscripts, transmitted as a separate piece in others, and joined to the elegy in others still. Al-Bakharzi moves to another poem after 86. Al-Nahari continues with the same voice, rhyme and sequence as the 89-bayt printed diwan, from the scour of adversity through the iron made into a sword to the lion cub in the last half. ## Per-half verification

The first Cohere pass flagged 35 of 178 halves. Replacing غمود at 47.2 brought the count down to 34. Following the last timing adjustments, 17.1, 23.1, 29.1 and 39.1 also cleared, leaving 30 flags (45 of 816 words). Audar independently transcribed wider clips for all 34 and again for the final 30. Its readings were unchanged on the 30 common halves. The middle column gives the widened readings, including the earlier results for the four that cleared. The final Cohere clip confirms 47.2. The numerical comparisons above use equal app-clock windows, preserved with the raw scores. The judgments draw on speech-model results, the reciter’s text cards and the printed pages. Direct listening was unavailable.
