# لا تَلُم قَلباً رَقَّ أَو سيقَ رِقّا

## Recording, text and occasion

Ihab al-Shabatat's own al-Diwan account, @eehabodeh, prints 61 abyat. Alqimmah Studio displays all 61 with Arabic explanatory captions. Its description dates the poem to the poet's first visit to Syria, when he stayed in Damascus in early June 2026, and quotes his earlier lament for the city. Khalid al-Sharafi's recording was published on 20 August 2026. He reads all the abyat in the poet's order and repeats 61 once. The author text and selected caption frames are in *media/research/la-talum-qalban/*, with further frames under *media/research/video/hNYzuP0RXos/*. [Author's publication](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem167395.html) · [Recording, occasion and studio commentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hNYzuP0RXos)

The retained interval is 2.65–539.30 s of 542.059 s. The 536.65 s edit removes the silent opening and tail while keeping the release of the repeated final line. A gain of −5.6 dB gives −20.0 LUFS. The opening and middle of the poem (1 and 31) scan on khafif, فاعلاتن مستفعلن فاعلاتن with the usual substitutions. The rhyme consonant is qaf with an open ending.

## Collation and commentary

- The paired words at 1.1 mean tenderness and bondage. At 15.2 «الغمد مشقاً» embeds the consonants of دمشق at the word boundary. The studio points out the pun and the sword's desire to return to its city. The Old City neighbourhood at 38 is where the studio says the visitor stayed. The poet and the studio caption both vowel it القِيمَرِيّة. Scored with the surrounding words at 319.52–322.94 s, that reading gives −35.59 against −37.64 for القَيمَرِيّة. The English uses the familiar spelling al-Qaymariyya. [Contemporary English spelling](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00104140261500259)
- **Across the caesura.** The poet and studio visibly divide the words أحمله (16), تعلو (23), أبهى (39), أمتن (54), رتقاً (57) and أندلساً (60) at their metrical breaks. Word notes give the complete forms. At 57 both displays repeat the joining qaf graphically in «رتقـ / ـقاً». The metre and continuous reading have one qaf. The corresponding halves therefore give «رَت / قاً». The allusion is to the Quran's paired words for joining and splitting. [Quran 21:30](https://quran.com/21/30)
- **46.2.** The author and studio give جَذْرُهُ “its root”. At 393.14–395.58 s the fatha scores −22.63 against −27.57 for the also current جِذْر.
- The archived word collation also records the author’s spacing «أوَ بعد» at 49.2. The edition joins the interrogative and conjunction to بعد.
- The studio's commentary provides the movement from jasmine to the young dead, the returning bee and glory (19–24), the condition of standing beneath a tree in safety (27), and the neighbouring towns addressed as little neighbours (48). It also explicitly interprets the owls and their young (52), the brief visit of personified happiness (53), and the green land as territories joined into one piece (59). These observations informed separately written Arabic and English explanations.
- The named figures in 18 span ancient Palmyra and the modern uprising. For the queen of Palmyra see *The Oxford Handbook of Palmyra*, chapter 11. For the protest singer from Homs and his death from battle wounds see the contemporary report of 8 June 2019. UNESCO's account describes Damascus as the Umayyad capital. [Oxford, Queen Zenobia](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/55821/chapter-abstract/437562572) · [Al Jazeera, Abdel-Basset al-Sarout](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/6/8/syrian-goalkeeper-who-became-rebel-icon-dies-in-hama-battle) · [UNESCO, Ancient City of Damascus](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/20/)
- The seven oft-repeated verses (33), young attendants in paradise (43), pride of Iblis (44), and the whitening of an eye (59) have Quranic antecedents. The exception made for Syria in 45 recalls al-Tirmidhi's report from Qurra ibn Iyas, no. 2192. The unfamiliar بَوق in 29 is explained by the studio as severe affliction. Ibn Manzur under بوق attests the root for calamity befalling a people. ## Audio check

The separate verification marks 47 of 124 clips, 75 of 588 words. The comparison used the full performance, both written witnesses and a second, wider Audar clip at 3.2, 14.1, 15.2, 16.2, 27.2, 38.1, 39.2, 53.2, 57.2 and both readings of 61.1. No complete bayt is missing.

- **3.2, 17.1, 27.2, 49.2, 53.2, 54.1–2, 56.1, 57.1 and 58.1.** Connected ta marbuta after a sustained vowel keeps becoming a written plural, e.g. الصبابات، حمامات، دوحات، الحريات، الزيارات and أميات. The poet and screen agree on singular forms throughout. The MMS comparison ties الصبابة and الصبابات at −41.17 (22.56–25.52 s) and دوحة and دوحات at −21.14 (229.56–231.70 s). At 27.2 the middle foot في دَوحَةٍ is long–long–short–long, as khafif requires. A long plural vowel in دَوحاتٍ would lose that short syllable. A long vowel heard amid this reciter's extended notes is not by itself evidence of a plural. At 53 the wider clip also restores the initial زغردت.
- **4.2, 7.1, 8.1, 12.1, 13.1, 14.1, 15.2, 21.1, 29.2, 31.2, 33.2, 34.2, 36.1, 38.1, 39.1, 40.1–2, 42.2, 44.2 and 52.2.** Word joins or divisions obscure ordinary connected forms (قيد شامٍ لا becomes قيد شامل, منهما becomes منه ما) as well as unfamiliar ones such as تدمشقت، الغمد مشقاً، وَدقاً، القيمرية and حوتَ ماء. Both captions and the poet give the printed forms. The wider clip retains the Damascus-like sound at 15 and the intake transcript joins الغمدِ مشقاً much as the wordplay intends. The models also divide the pronouns away from the verbs in 40 and cut the initial ذاع in 52.
- **16.1–2, 39.2, 54.1–2, 57.1–2 and 60.1.** The clips cut complete words at the caesura. Wider Audar gets أحمله at 16 and أبهى وجوهاً at 39. In the app audio the aligner finds «هى» at 332.26–332.36 s, «وجوهاً» at 332.52–333.26 s and «قاً» at 486.38–486.54 s. Short isolated clips sometimes suppress those fragments. The full transcript and both displays retain them.
- **8.2, 13.2, 17.2, 24.1, 25.1, 28.2, 32.2, 37.2, 41.1, 45.2, 48.2 and 61.1.** The model often supplies a new ending after a held rhyme (فرقه، خرقاه، خفقة، خلقاه) or a new consonant at a release (قبوره، بصحبك). Other substitutions are فرجت for خرجت, رسقاً for غسقاً, نيياً for نأياً, and أنا for أنّ. Both statements of the last bayt can be followed in the recording and both end by praising the people and the soil of Damascus. At 12.2 one isolated clip reports an initial «إن». The full track and screen already place وإن in 12.1 and start 12.2 with أمرُ ربّي.
