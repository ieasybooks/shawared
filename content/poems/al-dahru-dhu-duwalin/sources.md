## Attribution

Abu al-Atahiya's in both of Faisal's manuscripts that have it, in the Beirut diwan and in the books below; no source gives the poem to anyone else. The witnesses are not independent of one another: the diwan as it survives is Ibn Abd al-Barr's collection of his zuhd poems ((ظ) is that collection, and (ت) opens with part of Ibn Abd al-Barr's introduction), Cheikho based his print chiefly on it (Faisal, introduction, pp. 8–10), Ibn Muflih names Ibn Abd al-Barr in the next lines, and Dayf quotes the Beirut print. No earlier book quoting the poem was found (shamela searches on more than twenty of its halves). The nearest is a parallel, not a witness: al-Zubaydi, *Tabaqat al-Nahwiyyin*, p. 267 (shamela 13235/240, `_scratch/r2/zubaydi-240.txt`), has four other abyat on the same meter and rhyme, which Ibn al-Ghazi dictated in al-Andalus in 295 AH, among them «يا ذا الذي هو في لهو وفي لعب ... طوبى لعبد منيب القلب أواه» (compare 8.1 and 6.1–2); it names no poet.
- Ibn Abd al-Barr, *Bahjat al-Majalis*, p. 41 (shamela 558/41, `bahja-41.txt`): «وقال أبو العتاهية: والناس حيث يكون المال والجاه» (4.2); p. 238 (558/238, `bahja-238.txt`): «قال أبو العتاهية: إن الشقي لمن غرته دنياه» (11.2).
- Ibn Muflih, *al-Adab al-Shar'iyya*, vol. 3, p. 266 (shamela 21582/1182, `adab-shariyya.txt`): «وقال أبو العتاهية: والناس حيث يكون المال والجاه».
- Shawqi Dayf, *al-Fann wa-Madhahibuh fi al-Shi'r al-Arabi*, p. 115 (shamela 8666/123, `dayf.txt`): 8, 9, 11, 12 and 18 as Abu al-Atahiya's, «وأخذ يتغنى بالموت والفناء… على شاكلة قوله», citing «ديوان أبي العتاهية "طبعة بيروت سنة ١٨٨٦" ص٢٩٢».
- Unattributed: Ahmad Matlub, *Asalib Balaghiyya*, p. 121 (shamela 17376/113, `matlub.txt`), and Abd al-Aziz Atiq, *Ilm al-Ma'ani*, p. 101 (17670/95, `atiq.txt`, with «فاتحا فاه»), quote 8 as a question of «الاستبطاء». No source gives the poem to anyone else.

The drafter's settlements of 4.1, 5.1, 12.1, 15.2, 17.2, 18.1 and 20.1 stand, and so does the head «يَسوى» of 7.2.5: *Lisan al-Arab* lists the word as «يَسوى» and al-Layth says it has no past («ولا يقال منه سَوِيَ ولا سَوى»), while «ساوى» is another verb.

Fixed:

- **Text (credits):** 10.1 «ضِلَّةٌ» was credited to Faisal, who prints «ضِلَّةً» (the tanwin al-fath is plain on his page, the same mark as on «وَهَوًى» beside it), as al-Salman does. The reciter's nominative is confirmed (formants against «حَيّاً وَقَد», «يَوماً وَسَجّاهُ» and «تَحريكٌ وَإِنباهُ»; scores, above), so the text keeps it as a departure from both editions: the Editions list, the table, the 10.1 paragraph and `faisal.txt` corrected. The Beirut print's readings now include 10 «لَغَرورٌ».
- **Attribution:** sources.md no longer calls the books that quote the poem independent: all its witnesses go back to Ibn Abd al-Barr's collection (Faisal, pp. 8–10), as the Attribution section now says, with al-Zubaydi's four abyat on the same rhyme as a parallel.
- **Notes:** 10.1.4 gives both editions' «ضِلَّةً» (it named al-Salman alone) and says the nominative is the reciter's; the head of 18.1.3-4 voweled («مُمسى وَمُصبَح»).
- **Sharh:** 9 says that al-Mutanabbi's line came more than a century later, in both languages (the English read as if Abu al-Atahiya had borrowed it); the ellipses of 22 (Arabic «بينا… إذ», English “while … suddenly”) and 23 (English “then … then”) rewritten.
- **English:** 14.2 «بِصَوتِ النَّعيِ» translated as the voice announcing a death, not a “death-cry”, which reads as the cry of the dying, in the translation and the sharh; notes added on 14.2.4 «النَّعيِ» and 18.1.3-4 «مُمسانا وَمُصبَحُنا».
