## Title

No source gives the poem a name: shamela's full-text search finds neither «تائية ابن الخطيب» nor «تائية لسان الدين». Ibn Khaldun introduces it «ومما قال في ذلك», Ibn Hajar calls the last two abyat «الأبيات المشهورة», and Miftah heads it «وقال — حين شعر باقتراب أجله —». So the title is the first sadr.

## Attribution

Confirmed in independent lines:
- **Ibn Khaldun**, *Kitab al-Ibar*, in his account of Ibn al-Khatib's death: «وكان عفا الله عنه أيام امتحانه بالسجن يتوقع مصيبة الموت … ومما قال في ذلك» (de Slane's edition, vol. 2, p. 506, read on the page image). Ibn Hajar calls him Ibn al-Khatib's friend («صديقه شيخنا ولي الدين بن خلدون»).
- **Ibn Hajar**, *Inba' al-Ghumr*, ed. Hasan Habashi, vol. 1, p. 93 (shamela 26317/117, `inba-habashi.txt`): «واشتهر أَنه نظم - حين قدم للقتل - الأَبيات المشهورة» (the last two abyat, as «وَفَاتَ، وسُبْحَان من لا يفُوتْ» and «فمَنْ كان يَشْمت منكم بهِ / فقل: يشمتُ اليوم من لا يموت»), then «والصحيح في ذلك ما ذكره صديقه شيخنا ولي الدين بن خلدون أَنه نظم الأَبيات المذكورة وهو في السجن». The same in his *al-Durar al-Kamina* (shamela 6674/1777, `durar.txt`). This is a separate line: the lines were known to him as popular verse.
- **al-Maqqari** copies Ibn Khaldun in *Nafh al-Tib* and *Azhar al-Riyad* (below); **Ibn al-Imad**, *Shadharat al-Dhahab*, vol. 8, p. 426 (shamela 12398/4024): «هذا الصحيح كما ذكره ابن خلدون، فلا يلتفت إلى غيره».
- **Muhammad Miftah** includes it in the poet's diwan (below).

No source gives it to anyone else.

So he says a stop, ت, not ن. The note gives the other editions' «سكنت».

(al-Istiqsa, the takhmis and Durrat al-Hijal have «غربنا», "we set", the last two with «علينا».)
- **1.2 «صُموت»:** cohere writes «صُمُوتٌ», audar «صمود»; the reciter stops on a silent ت, as at every rhyme.

## Meter and rhyme

- **Mutaqarib.** Every half scans (`media/research/baduna-wa-in-jawaratna/tools/scan_mutaqarib.py`: `scan.py` with the maqsur darb added). Every ajuz ends in the maqsur darb, فَعولْ (u + a closed long syllable, -ūt), and the opening sadr ends the same way (tasri'). The rhyme is sakin (muqayyad), as Miftah and Nafh print it, with a sukun on every ت, and as the reciter says it; the text writes the rhyme letter bare. The other sadrs end in فَعولُن in 3.1 and 7.1 and in فَعو (mahdhuf) in the remaining five (2, 4, 5, 6, 8). Ibn Abd Rabbih's *al-Iqd al-Farid*, vol. 6, pp. 341–342 (shamela 23789/2152–2153, `iqd-mutaqarib.txt`), heads the full mutaqarib's 'arud «العروض التام الجائز فيه الحذف والقصر» (a bracketed heading, likely the editor's), so a sadr may end فَعو; for the maqsur darb it gives first the old shahid «ويأوى إلى نسوة بائسات / وشعث مراضيع مثل السّعالى» (printed so that it no longer shows the qasr), then «على رسم دار قفار وقفت / ومن ذكر عهد الحبيب بكيت», whose sadr also ends فَعولْ, the tasri' of 1.1. The other feet are فَعولُن or فَعولُ (qabd).
- 8.1 «لَهُ» is said short (the half ends فَعو).
- The rhyme word «البُيوت» of 1.1 comes back in 4.2, an ita' (الإيطاء). Nafh, Azhar, de Slane and Durrat al-Hijal have «السموت» there, but the reciter says «البُيوت» (−17.9), with Miftah. Lisan (vol. 1, p. 200, shamela 1687/200, `_scratch/r2/lisan-wta.txt`): «قال أبو عمرو بن العلاء: الإيطاء ليس بعيب في الشعر عند العرب، وهو إعادة القافية مرتين», while Ibn Sallam counts it a fault «إذا كثر الإيطاء في قصيدة مرات»; the note on 4.2.4 quotes Abu Amr.

## Commentary drawn on

- **No commentary on the poem** was found, and Miftah gives no glosses. The sharh rests on Lisan al-Arab for the words and on Ibn Khaldun for the occasion. Where it reads the poem, the commentators are silent, and these are the likelier readings:
  - **2:** «القنوت» after «جهر الصلاة» read as the qunut of the dawn prayer, which in the Maliki school is said silently: Khalil, *Mukhtasar*, p. 33 (shamela 11355/39, `khalil.txt`): «وقنوت سرا بصبح فقط وقبل الركوع». The bayt sets the loud part of the prayer against what follows it, and Lisan (vol. 2, p. 73, shamela 1687/877, `lisan-qnt.txt`) gives «القُنوتُ: الإِمساكُ عَنِ الْكَلَامِ، وَقِيلَ: الدعاءُ فِي الصَّلَاةِ», and on Zayd ibn Arqam's report «فأُمِرْنا بالسُّكوتِ» it says «فالقُنوتُ هَاهُنَا: الإِمساك عَنِ الْكَلَامِ فِي الصَّلَاةِ» (on al-Baqara 238, «وقوموا لله قانتين»). That the word's first sense, silence, also plays in the bayt is the second reader's reading, given in the sharh as a second sense, not as the commentators'.
  - **4:** «البيوت» as the people of the houses, the subject of «ناحت», or the houses themselves personified (the sharh gives both); «غربنَ» and «عليها» refer to the suns.
  - **5:** «الظُّبا» as the subject of «جدّلت» and «ذا الحسام» its object; «وذا البخت», in the accusative, joined to «ذا الحسام» (Miftah sets a comma after «البخت»), with «كم خذلته البخوت» as a second exclamation. That each man is undone by the kind of thing he relied on, the swordsman by swords and the lucky man by luck, is the sharh's own reading of the parallel (the swords need not be his own, so not "the very thing").
  - **6:** «فتىً» is the tamyiz of «كم», in the accusative because a verb separates them, or the na'ib fa'il of «سيق» with «كم» meaning "how many times"; the maqsur noun can't show which. Sibawayh, *al-Kitab*, ed. Abd al-Salam Harun (Cairo: al-Khanji, third edition, 1408/1988), vol. 2, p. 165 (shamela 23018/600, `_scratch/r2/sibawayh-kam.txt`), on al-Qutami's «كم نالني منهم فضلاً على عدمٍ»: «وإن شاء رفع فجعل كمِ المرار التي ناله فيها الفضل، فارتفع الفضل بنالني». The draft's note called «فتىً» the na'ib fa'il only, while its translation ("How many a youth") took the tamyiz; the note now gives both.
  - **8:** «مِنكُم» turns the address from the listener («فقل») to the enemies.
- **Glosses**, from Lisan al-Arab (shamela 1687; `lisan.txt`, `lisan2.txt`, `lisan3.txt`): وعظ «النصح والتذكير بالعواقب» (vol. 7, p. 466); صمت «صَمَتَ يَصْمُتُ صَمْتاً وصُمْتاً وصُمُوتاً» (vol. 2, p. 54); دفع (vol. 8, pp. 87–88, above); جهر «جهرت بالقول … إذا أعلنته» (vol. 4, p. 150); قنت (vol. 2, p. 73); قوت «القُوتُ: مَا يُمْسِكُ الرَّمَقَ مِنَ الرِّزْق» and «قاتَه ذَلِكَ قَوْتاً» (vol. 2, p. 74); العلا «ذو العُلا والعَلاء والمَعالي» (vol. 15, p. 85); جدل «جَدَّلَهُ … صَرَعه عَلَى الجَدَالة» (vol. 11, p. 104); حسم «والحُسامُ: السَّيْفُ القاطِعُ» (vol. 12, p. 134); ظبا «الظُّبَة: حَدُّ السَّيْفِ والسِّنانِ» (vol. 15, p. 22); بخت «والبَخْتُ: الجَدُّ، مَعْرُوفٌ، فارسيٌّ، وَقَدْ تَكَلَّمَتْ بِهِ الْعَرَبُ» (vol. 2, p. 9; Lisan has no plural, and «البُخوت» is read as its plural); خذل «ترك نصرته وعونه» (vol. 11, p. 202); خرق «والخِرْقة: القِطعة مِنْ خِرَقِ الثَّوْبِ» (vol. 10, p. 73); كسا «الكِسْوَةُ والكُسْوَةُ: اللِّبَاسُ … والكُسا: جَمْعُ الكُسْوَة» (vol. 15, p. 223); تخت «التَّخْتُ: وِعَاءٌ تُصانُ فِيهِ الثيابُ، فَارِسِيٌّ، وَقَدْ تَكَلَّمَتْ بِهِ الْعَرَبُ» (vol. 2, p. 18); عدا «وقد جاء في الشعر العِدى بمعنى الأعداء» (vol. 15, p. 35); فوت «وفاتَني الأَمرُ … ذهَب عَنِّي» (vol. 2, p. 69); شمت «الشماتة: فرح العدو؛ وقيل: الفرح ببلية العدو» (vol. 2, p. 51).
- **The occasion and the poet's life:**
  - Ibn Khaldun (de Slane, vol. 2, pp. 506–507, page images; Dar al-Fikr, vol. 7, pp. 449–454, shamela): Ibn al-Ahmar's support for Abu al-Abbas Ahmad's proclamation on condition that he hand over Ibn al-Khatib («وأن يبعثوا إليه ابن الخطيب متى قدروا عليه», p. 449; «وقد كان الشرط وقع بينه وبين السلطان ابن الأحمر … على نكبة ابن الخطيب وإسلامه إليه», p. 452); Sulayman ibn Dawud's grudge and his urging the arrest; Ibn Zamrak's arrival, the hearing on «بعض كلمات وقعت له في كتابه» (Nafh: «في كتابه في المحبة»), torture, the fatwa, the strangling at night by men Sulayman sent, the burial at Bab al-Mahruq, the burning of the body the next day; and the poem, said «أيام امتحانه بالسجن» while he «يتوقع مصيبة الموت».
  - al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 6, p. 235 (shamela 12286/5656–5658, `alam0.txt`, `alam.txt`): the name and nisbas, 713–776 AH (1313–1374 CE), born and raised in Granada, «اللوشي الأصل», vizier to Abu al-Hajjaj Yusuf and then al-Ghani billah Muhammad, his secret departure through Gibraltar and Ceuta to Tlemcen in 773, settling in Fez, the charges «الزندقة» and «سلوك مذهب الفلاسفة», the fatwa, the strangling, the burial at Bab al-Mahruq, the titles «ذي الوزارتين: القلم والسيف» and «ذو العمرين», «نحو ستين كتابا», and «وعلى اسمه صنف المقري كتابه العظيم (نفح الطيب، من غصن الأندلس الرطيب، وذكر وزيرها لسان الدين ابن الخطيب)».
  - Ibn Hajar, *Inba' al-Ghumr*, vol. 1, pp. 92–93 (above): the year of his killing, 776, and the two accounts of when he composed the poem.
  - **Ibn al-Ahmar was al-Ghani billah.** al-A'lam: «فتولى المغرب السلطان (المستنصر) أحمد بن إبراهيم، وقد ساعده (الغني باللَّه) صاحب غرناطة مشترطا عليه شروطا منها تسليمه (ابن الخطيب)», then «وكتب بذلك إلى الغني باللَّه، فأرسل هذا وزيره (ابن زمرك)». So the ruler who demanded him was the sultan he had served; the intro and both bios now say so (the draft's "Ibn al-Ahmar" alone hid it). Ibn Khaldun's «السلطان ابن الأحمر» on de Slane p. 506 is the same man.
  - **Birthplace, split:** al-A'lam «ولد ونشأ بغرناطة»; Ibn Hajar, *al-Durar al-Kamina*, first Hyderabad edition, vol. 3, p. 469 (shamela 30171/1472, `_scratch/r2/durar-hind.txt`): «ولد في خامس عشري رجب سنة ٧١٣ بلوشة», with the family's moves «قرطبي الأصل ثم نزل سلفه طليطلة ثم لوشة ثم غرناطة». The bios no longer name the town of his birth: born in 713 to a family from Loja, raised in Granada, which both sources allow. The same page gives the family name, used in the note on 7.1.4–5: «كان سلفه قديما يعرفون ببني وزير ثم صاروا يعرفون ببني الخطيب نسبة الى سعيد جده الاعلى وكان قد ولي الخطابة بها» (بها: Loja, just named).
  - The dates in Hijri are al-A'lam's; 773 AH is given in CE as 1371–72, since the departure fell in Jumada II 773 (the Ihata introduction, shamela 11682/9), which straddles the two years. Inan (vol. 5, p. 476, `inan.txt`) dates the crossing to Ceuta 772; al-A'lam, the Ihata introduction and Ibn Hajar («ودخل فاس سنة ثلاث وسبعين», `inba-habashi.txt`) have 773, which the bio keeps.
- **Consistency with the Andalusian poems:** Ibn al-Ahmar is "the ruler of Granada", as in `nuniyyat-al-rundi`, where the name belongs to the ruler of 665 AH, a century earlier; here it is al-Ghani billah Muhammad, and the intro names him. The Nasrids are "the Nasrid sultans of Granada", as `ya-arda-andalus` calls them its "Nasrid kings". The poet's name is written «لِسان الدّين اِبن الخَطيب», with the alif, as al-A'lam heads him and as the bio has it (al-Khatib is not his father).

Fixed in this reading:

- **Text:** no word or vowel changed; the readings the drafter doubted all stand, now with more evidence. 2.1 «سَكَتَت»: the consonant between the vowels is a stop closure as deep as the k of the same word, not a nasal (above). 4.2 «غَرَبنَ»: the long vowel the transcribers hear is this reciter's habit on every short syllable ending a فَعولُ foot (0.15–0.22 s, same position), so the editions' short «ـنَ» stays. 2.1 «دُفعَةً»: the reciter's own description writes the damma, besides the formants and the scores. 5.2 «وذا»: Durrat al-Hijal is a second print witness for the accusative. The spelling «العُلا» for Miftah's «العُلى» was a silent departure; it is now recorded with «الظُّبا».
- **Meaning:** 6, the note made «فتىً» the na'ib fa'il only, while the translation took it as the tamyiz of «كم»; the note now gives both, after Sibawayh. 2, the double sense of «القنوت» (the prayer's silent supplication, and silence, its first sense in Lisan) in the sharh of both languages. 4, the wailing houses are their people or the houses themselves. 5 (English), "the very thing he relied on" is "the kind of thing": the blades that fell the swordsman need not be his own.
- **Notes:** 2.2.4 rewritten in both languages (the prayer sense, Khalil, and Lisan's first sense with al-Baqara 238); 4.2.4 says that the editions' «السموت» avoids repeating the rhyme word, the ita', with Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' on it; 7.1.4–5 says why he is Ibn al-Khatib (an ancestor, Sa'id, preacher at Loja, after Ibn Hajar's al-Durar), in both languages; 1.1.1 no longer shares «بعيدين … الأحياء» with the sharh.
- **English:** 8.1 «فَمَن» is "So whoever", not "And whoever" (that is Nafh's «ومن»).
- **Intro:** Ibn al-Ahmar, who set the condition for the arrest, was al-Ghani billah, the sultan Ibn al-Khatib had served, as al-A'lam says; both intros name him (the draft's bare "Ibn al-Ahmar, the ruler of Granada" hid it, and is the same label `nuniyyat-al-rundi` uses for a ruler a century earlier). The Arabic intro writes «ابن الخطيب» with the alif, as the bio does.
- **Poet:** the bios said he was born in Granada (al-A'lam), but Ibn Hajar's al-Durar says Loja; both now say born in 713 to a family from Loja and raised in Granada. Both bios now say that al-Ghani billah made his handing over a condition. `name` written «لِسان الدّين اِبن الخَطيب», as al-A'lam heads him. Inan's 772 for the flight recorded against the 773 kept.
- **sources.md:** the recording's description does not have Miftah's «دَفعة» (it has the reciter's «دُفعة»); the sadr endings are فَعولْ in 1, فَعولُن in 3 and 7 and فَعو in the other five, not six; al-Iqd's first maqsur example added; Durrat al-Hijal and the takhmis's «وأصواتنا» added to the witnesses; Inan's page for 772 is p. 476.
