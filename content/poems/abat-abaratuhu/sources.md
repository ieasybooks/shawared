# أبت عبراته إلا انسكابا

## Recording, order and occasion

Osama al-Nahari recites fifty-five bayts of Abu Firas al-Hamdani. The keep is **5.68–414.94 s** of the source, giving 409.26 s of app audio. The first word is aligned at 0.54 s. The short spoken ending «النهاية» follows the poem. The two continuous acoustic passes, measured half-lines and the reciter's word cards establish this sequence in Sami al-Dahhan's numbering: **1–32, 34, 35, 33, 36–55**. Each half occurs once. The sequence moves from the watering place al-Furuqlus to the shower at al-Jibah, across al-Sahsahan, then to Tadmur's springs. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sc2ulpGbbZU)

Ibn Khalawayh's account connects the expedition to the killing of al-Sabbah, Sayf al-Dawla's official at Qinnasrin. Abu Firas was the prince's cousin and led a detachment. Submission and pardon at the end explain the change of voice from retaliation to forbearance. The family and tribal judgements in the poem are those of a participant on the victorious side. Al-Dahhan dates the events to Safar 344 AH, May 955 CE, in his apparatus on p. 12. [Al-Dahhan, pp. 11–12](https://archive.org/details/0453_20191224) · [Bencherifa, introductory narrative, pp. 18–21](https://archive.org/details/20240327_20240327_1433)

## Translation and meter

The paired translations and explanations follow the connections between the nasib, the mounted itinerary, retaliation for al-Sabbah, the petition and resettlement, and the final first-person claim. The descriptions of hospitality at 36 and of social standing at 37 belong to the speaker. The campaign notices and place glosses above support the historical annotations. The existing Abu Firas profile is used.

The meter is **al-wafir** with ba as rawiy. The final long a heard in the rhyme extends the ba. At 1.1 «أبت عبراته إلا انسكابا» gives *ʾa bat ʿa ba rā | tu hū ʾil lan | si kā bā*, **⏑ – ⏑ ⏑ – | ⏑ – – – | ⏑ – –**. At 7.1 «ألم ترنا أعزّ الناس جاراً» gives *ʾa lam ta ra nā | ʾa ʿaz zan nā | si jā rā*, the same feet. In each sadr the second *mufa'alatun* has the contracted form *mufa'altun*, and the final foot is *fa'ulun*. The opening ajuz, *wa nā ru ḍu lū | ʿi hī ʾil lal | ti hā bā*, has the same pattern as its sadr. At 25 the two parallel halves scan *fa mā kā nū | la nā ʾil lā | ʾu sā rā* and *wa mā kā nat | la nā ʾil lā | ni hā bā*. Each has **⏑ – – – | ⏑ – – – | ⏑ – –**.
