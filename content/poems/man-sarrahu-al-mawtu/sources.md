## Literary witnesses and historical context

- *Diwan Hassan ibn Thabit*, ed. Walid Arafat, Dar Sadir, Beirut, 1974, I, poem 20, pp. 96–97. The heading places the poem in the lament for Uthman ibn Affan. The ten verses on p. 96 give the count and order of this edition. Arafat reports that only manuscript طا among his diwan manuscripts has our verse 4, and lists further literary attestations, including *al-Iqd*, *al-Isti'ab*, Ibn al-Athir and *Lisan al-Arab*. Ibn Qutayba's *al-Ma'arif* cites the verse without attribution. The apparatus on p. 97 also identifies variants in verses 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8. [Scan of verse text, p. 96](https://archive.org/download/HSAND/HSAND01.pdf#page=144) · [Scan of apparatus, p. 97](https://archive.org/download/HSAND/HSAND01.pdf#page=145) · [Searchable text](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/14694/144?lang=ar)
- Arafat, II, pp. 92–93, reproduces the glosses on poem 20. Several manuscript glosses explain that the rings of mail pair with the helmets and that أبدان here means coats of armour. The reading with initial s describes the marks helmets leave on noses. For verse 3 Arafat quotes Ibn al-Athir on a tradition that Syrian transmitters added the question about Ali. For verse 4 he quotes Ibn Abd al-Barr’s *al-Isti'ab*, which records an attribution to Imran ibn Hittan. On the cry at verse 5 the *Lisan* allows an appeal to the avengers or a reproach addressed to the killers. A gloss at verse 9 places the call for help before the siege, despite its position after the lament in this sequence. These are attributed interpretations and reports about transmission. [Photographed glosses, II, p. 92](https://archive.org/download/HSAND/HSAND02.pdf#page=96) · [Continuation, II, p. 93](https://archive.org/download/HSAND/HSAND02.pdf#page=97)
- *Diwan Hassan*, Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, Beirut, 2nd edition, 1414 AH (1994 CE), pp. 244–245. These photographed pages place all ten in the same order. In verse 2 they give سفعت and بيضٌ, in verse 5 ديارهم. The glosses explain مأسدة as a haunt of lions, زافرة as helpers, and مغبطة as joy. The editor offers Mu'awiya or Habib ibn Maslama as possible referents of الأمير. The reader-facing text leaves the title open. [Edition scan, printed p. 244](https://data2.dawateislami.net/Data/Books/Download/ar/pdf/2020/2180-1.pdf#page=244)
- Al-Tabari, *Tarikh al-rusul wa-l-muluk*, year 35 AH, IV, p. 425, introduces seven verses with وقال حسان. Their order in diwan numbering is **1, 2, 8, 6, 7, 5, 3**. Besides the different wording shown below, this account closes with the question about Ali. Al-Maliqi, *al-Tamhid wa-l-bayan fi maqtal al-shahid Uthman*, p. 208, also includes ضحّوا بأشمط, and appends the question after describing an addition to the verses. These witnesses establish literary circulation of different sequences. Uthman was killed in Medina in 35 AH (656 CE). Verse 3 expresses the speaker's wish to know what passed between Ali and Uthman. Verse 5 imagines a future vengeance cry. Neither supplies evidence of Ali's involvement, the first use of that slogan or the motives of particular commanders. The Arabic and English commentary preserves the differences between recollection, question, threat, profession of allegiance and hope. [Al-Tabari](https://shamela.ws/book/9783/2314) · [Al-Maliqi](https://shamela.ws/book/6649/190)

The opening sadr is مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. At 6.1, وقد رضيت بأهل الشام زافرةً gives متفعلن فَعِلُن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. The metre is basit. Nun is the rhyme consonant before the released alif, heard even in عثمانا and عفّانا.

## Recording against the variants

Both whole-take recognisers give the consonants شفعت. The studio card at source second 15 and Arafat’s photographed p. 96 each mark three fathas, شَفَعَت. The rings of mail join the helmets above the men’s noses. The active verb takes بيضاً as its object. Arafat’s p. 97 assigns مستشعري … شُفِعَت … قبل to al-Tabari and Ibn al-Athir, records both active and passive forms beginning with s in the manuscript tradition, and gives nominative بيضٌ in most manuscripts. The other Beirut edition glosses سَفَعَت as “left a mark”. The recorded image, its alternatives and the active reading have been checked separately against the voice and the photographs. [Arafat, pp. 96–97](https://archive.org/download/HSAND/HSAND01.pdf#page=144) · [Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, p. 244](https://data2.dawateislami.net/Data/Books/Download/ar/pdf/2020/2180-1.pdf#page=244)

At 3.1 بل opens a turn from the armoured men to the wish for news. Al-Tabari has يا. At 5.1 direct address دياركم contrasts with his ديارهم. At 6.1 the speaker's singular رضيت and زافرة (supporters) differ from al-Tabari's plural رضينا and نافرة (a body that goes forth). At 7.2 his ما دمت حيّاً replaces حتى الممات. At 8.1 his صبراً anticipates the patience named in the second half. The recitation opens with ويهاً, a cry of encouragement.

## Flag review

In the initial half-line pass Cohere flagged two of twenty halves. At 4.1 both sliced recognisers deform بأشمط عنوان السجود by shifting consonants across word boundaries. The whole-take Audar result recovers the phrase, the source card prints it, and the individual aligned words occupy app 33.50–34.52, 34.66–35.88 and 35.94–36.96 seconds, with confidences 0.64, 0.73 and 0.69. At 9.2 both clipped recognisers join من حانا as منحانا. The measured words occupy 94.06–94.30 and 94.46–95.10 with confidences 0.90 and 0.99. The conditional relative and verb in both editions explain the grammar. The audio review used source PCM, two acoustic transcriptions, per-word alignment and the original frames. Direct playback to the editor was unavailable.

## Final timing check

The final highlight for 10.2 ends at 106.49 s on the app master. The 7 half-lines whose end times changed were transcribed again. The final per-half check flags 2 halves.

At 4.1 the context transcriptions join the end of «أشمطَ» to the beginning of «عنوانُ» and split the latter into short words. The pictured mark of prostration gives «عنوان السجود» its meaning. The two textual witnesses and the variant attribution of this bayt are discussed above.
