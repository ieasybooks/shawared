## The poet and the name

- **Poet:** Abd al-Qahir al-Tabrizi, as the video says. Al-Dhahabi heard the poem from him: «أنشدنا القاضي عبد القاهر لنفسه سنة أربع وسبعمائة» (Mu'jam, below), and again in the Takmila; Ibn Hajar's entry names him «صاحب القصيدة … التي أولها» and quotes stanzas 1–2. The video's «ابن عبد الواحد» is al-Dhahabi's; Ibn Hajar, al-Safadi and al-Zirikli give «عبد القاهر بن محمد بن عبد الواحد» (al-Zahrani: al-Dhahabi named him after his grandfather).
- **Title:** the old sources give the poem no name. It circulates today, in recordings and in lessons (Haytham al-Hamri's «شرح القصيدة الرباعية في الوعظ والسنة للتبريزي»), as «القصيدة الرباعية في الوعظ والسنة» (the title of several reciters' uploads besides this one), and also as «عينية التبريزي»; al-Zahrani's book calls it «المنظومة التبريزية». The title takes the name it is known by.
- **The form:** each stanza is four short lines of rajaz, two feet each (مستفعلن مستفعلن). Lines 1–3 rhyme together on a rhyme that changes from stanza to stanza (the first stanza keeps ع throughout); line 4 always ends in ـعِ. The format has two halves to a bayt, so a stanza is one bayt, lines 1–2 its sadr and lines 3–4 its ajuz, as al-Dhahabi's editor prints it (under «[مجزوء الرجز]») and as al-Zahrani numbers it; `rawiy` is the common ع. The Takmila sets stanza 2 as four separate lines. The intro and this file describe the form; the line breaks inside a half are not shown.

## Editions

- **al-Dhahabi, معجم الشيوخ الكبير**, ed. Muhammad al-Habib al-Hayla (Maktabat al-Siddiq), vol. 1, pp. 409–410, read from the page images (archive.org `121581`, vol. 1 PDF pp. 409–410). The whole poem, 22 lines of one stanza each, partly voweled. Shamela 12531/389–390 (`dhahabi-mujam.txt`) transcribes this page with errors (موجِعٍ, وَعُدَّ, وَنُهِّدَت, «الفضالا», «ورجاء» kept); the print has مُوجَع, وَعَدِّ, ونهدتْ. The print also vowels 17.1 «وَشِل» and 20.2 «مُونَع», where the text has وَشَل and مونِع (table below). **The base text.**
- **مرزوق بن هياس الزهراني, المنظومة التبريزية في العقيدة الصحيحة السنية، شرح وتوثيق** (Medina, 1426/2005): shamela 35312, text pp. 16–19, commentary pp. 20–92 (`tabriziyya.txt`). All 22 stanzas, numbered, with a commentary on each. Its text comes from al-Dhahabi and differs in ورامية (1), وكن (10), «ولا تؤل» (11, a slip one syllable short), وأنه (12, in the list; the commentary has وإنه), ومعنا (15), من منبع (17). Its «في آية» (10) is al-Dhahabi's too.
- **الإشارات الفؤادية في التعليق على المنظومة التبريزية** (an unsigned typed PDF, archive.org `noor-book.com_20230302`, pp. 2–3, read from the images): a fully voweled text taken from al-Dhahabi, then notes on al-Zahrani's commentary. Used for vowels: أَعيا, وَعَدِّ, دجى … وَرَجا, وَمَعنا, وَنَهَدَت.
- **al-Dhahabi, تكملة سير أعلام النبلاء**, vol. 30, p. 432 (shamela 10907/900, `dhahabi-takmila.txt`): stanzas 1–2, «إلى آخرها، وهي موعظة مذكِّرة».
- **Ibn Hajar, الدرر الكامنة**, vol. 3, pp. 195–196 (shamela 6674/1158–1159, `durar-hind2.txt`; first Hyderabad printing 30171/941): stanzas 1–2.
- **عبد الله البصيري, أبيات مختارة**, pp. 13–14 (shamela 7473/9–10, `basiri-abyat.txt`): stanzas 8–22, from al-Dhahabi.

`abyat_in_full` is 22, al-Dhahabi's count.

- **8.2 «وُفِّقتَ»:** the reciter departs from every edition: a prayer («may you be guided») set inside the prohibition, where the editions have «وقعتَ». The full transcript and the clip transcript hear «وفقت», Audar «أفقته» (f–q–t, not q–ʿ–t). The word note gives the editions' reading.
- **10.2 «آيِهِ»:** `variants.py` spells «آيَةٍ» with a bare t (`ayat`), one letter shorter than «آيِهِ» (`ayihi`), so the first coin toss favoured it by length alone. Spelled as a reciter says it, «آيَتِن» (with its tanwin) or «آيَتِ», it loses by 12.9 and 8.9 on the half and by 7.8 and 5.5 on the last three words; «آيِهي» (−1.6), «آيَهِ» (−2.7) and «آيِتِ» (−2.9) come next, so the h is heard where the t would be. Cohere hears «آيِّهِ» on the clip and «ايه» in the full transcript; Audar writes «آية» (it writes ة for a final h-sound). The text follows the recording, «in its verses», and the note gives the editions' reading. Both scan.
- **12.1:** a coin toss by score; both transcribers write «وإنه» with the hamza below, as al-Dhahabi's print has it.
- **12.2 «كَلامَ مُسمِعِ»:** the reciter says «كلامَ» without tanwin, against every source. The score alone proves nothing (the aligner marks down tanwin al-fath it certainly hears: «طويلاً» −5.4, «ليلاً» −5.2, «زماناً» −1.5, «حقّاً» −2.5), but: all four transcripts (the full one, Cohere's clip, Audar on two clips) write «جهرا كلام مسمعي», with the alif of «جهراً» and none on «كلام», where they write it on every other tanwin al-fath in the poem (طويلا، زمانا، حقا، ليلا، مسرعا، سقيا، معتضدا); the nasal murmur before «مُسمِع» lasts 0.09 s (123.47–123.56 s), like the single m's around it (0.07, 0.09 s), against 0.16 s for the doubled m of «مِن مَربَعِ» (40.28–40.43 s), so there is no «كلامَم مُسمِع». And «كلامَ مُسمِعٍ» is regular, a cognate object in idafa («he spoke to him the speech of one who makes himself heard»), where the editions' «كلاماً مُسمِعِ» needs the rhyme to excuse the case of «مسمع». Both scan (12.2 line 4: −−u− u−u− against −−u− −−u−). The note gives the editions' reading.
- **15.1 «وَمَعنا»:** al-Dhahabi's editor prints «ومعنى» with a note pointing to 2:115 («فأينما تولّوا فثمّ وجه الله»: «and the meaning of ‹wherever› is without how»); al-Zahrani and al-Ishārāt have «ومعنا», God's being with us, which al-Zahrani's commentary on the stanza expounds («إثبات العلو والمعية»). The two sound the same (مَعْنا, with مَعْ); the text follows al-Zahrani. Cohere writes «ومعنى», Audar «ومعنا».

**Spelling and vowels against al-Dhahabi's print:**
- 6.1 «دَجا»: the verb is دَجا يَدجو (root د ج و); the sources write «دجى».
- 6.1 «وَرَجا»: al-Dhahabi prints «ورجاء»; the meter and the rhyme need رجا (al-Zahrani, al-Ishārāt), and the reciter says it.
- 9.1 «أَعيا»: al-Dhahabi «أعيى»; al-Ishārāt «أعيا».
- 21.2 «وَمَفزَعي»: al-Dhahabi and al-Zahrani «ومفزع», leaving the ya of «my» to the rhyme's kasra; al-Basiri writes «ومفزعي». The sound is the same; the ya is written so the reader sees «my refuge … my shelter».
- 4.1 «الحَلي», 4.2 «العَلي»: without shadda, as al-Dhahabi prints them, rhyming with «الأَوَّلِ».
- 1.2 «سَكرانَ»: diptote, as the Takmila vowels it.
- 10.1 «مُعتَضِدا», 10.2 «أَحَدا», 13.2 «مُسرِعا»: at the end of a line inside the stanza, with the pausal alif, as al-Dhahabi prints them.

**Line ends inside a stanza:** the end of every line is a line end for the meter. Where the reciter lengthens the case vowel, it is written (الأَجرَعِ, الحَلي, جَهَنَّمُ, تَضطَرِمُ); where he stops on the consonant, the letter is bare (الحُلَل, المُقَل, تَسَل, وَرَد, وَيَد, أَحَد, الوَجَل, البَشَر, عَبَد, وَسَجَد, القَمَر). **12.1 «وَجَلّ» and 17.2 «وَقَلّ»** end in a doubled letter at such a stop: they keep the shadda with no vowel, and `content:check` warns on both. The reciter stops there (0.44 s and 0.14 s before the next word), and a vowel would add a syllable the meter doesn't have; without the shadda the reader would not see the verbs جَلَّ and قَلَّ. The second reader agrees: the two warnings stay.

**Meter:** every one of the 88 lines scans as two rajaz feet (`media/research/al-qasida-al-rubaiyya/_scratch/scanparts.py`, a version of `scan.py` that scans each line with its end as a line end). A foot is مُتَعِلُن (u u u −) in 9.2 «وَبَهَرَت», 13.1 «فَوَعى», 16.2 «وَنَصَر» and 20.1 «وَنَهَدَت», as printed. `scan.py` misreads three lines: «لِلَّهِ» in 11.1 (the hidden alif) and the two stops on a doubled letter above.

## Commentary drawn on

- **The sharh** follows al-Zahrani's commentary on every stanza: the opening as a love prelude whose love is «في المكارم والعلا» (1–3); the first home as Bukhara or Harran (4); the impossible wish turning to the use of time (5); the ships as acts of obedience and the widest space as Paradise (6); night prayer and tears from awe (7); the Quran as God's uncreated speech and its inimitability (9–10); affirming the attributes without ta'wil (11); God's real, heard speech to Moses (12–13); istiwa' in seven places, its meaning known and its manner unknown (14); God above and with His creatures, by knowledge and support (15); the Scales (17); Hell and Paradise (18–20); prayer to God alone (21).
- **Place names** (الأجرع, رامة, لعلع) as al-Zahrani gives them from Yaqut's *Mu'jam al-Buldan*.
- **Added here:** Paradise as the first home (4), given as a possibility, with Ibn al-Qayyim's «فحيّ على جنّات عدنٍ فإنّها منازلُك الأولى» (his Mimiyya, quoted in *Tariq al-Hijratayn*, shamela 251/194); the wordplay in 3; the echo of «الفضا» in «الفضاء» (5–6); the wine-party language (7); «منه بدأ وإليه يعود» (10); the Quranic echoes in 3 (12:51), 12 (4:164, 7:143), 14 (20:5, 51:58), 15 (57:4), 18 (11:44, 18:100, 27:90), 20 (56:36, 78:33), 21 (73:8); the hadith of al-Firdaws (al-Bukhari) and of the man in two rags (al-Tirmidhi) in the notes to 19; Ka'b ibn Malik's «كأنّه قطعة قمر» (al-Bukhari) in the note to 22; al-Zahrani's reading of «اعترضت» (Hell lying between the gathering and Paradise, the Sirat over it) in 18, and his «ملهم» for «أروع» (13, from *al-Nihaya*); the rags of 19 against the robes of 2.
- **Glosses:** *Lisan al-Arab* for الأجرع, الوشل, أليل and حَلِيَ (shamela 1687); the rest are the common lexicon. Al-Safadi says the poet's verse has «بعض شيء من اللحن الخفي جدًّا» (*al-Wafi*, vol. 19, p. 39); with the recited «كلامَ» (12.2) no half shown needs that excuse.
- **The intro and bio:** al-Dhahabi, *Mu'jam al-Shuyukh*, vol. 1, pp. 408–410, and the Takmila, vol. 30, pp. 431–433 (his dismissal «لكونه لم يتأوَّل بل أثبَتَ»; the poem as «موعظة مذكِّرة»); al-Safadi, *A'yan al-'Asr*, vol. 3, pp. 124–128 (shamela 131/1550–1554, `ayan-asr.txt`), and *al-Wafi*, vol. 19, pp. 37–39 (shamela 6677/5189–5191, `wafi.txt`: «ينظم نظماً عذباً منسجماً»); Ibn Shakir, *Fawat al-Wafayat*, vol. 2, pp. 367–369 (shamela 1003/791–793, `fawat.txt`); Ibn Hajar, *al-Durar*, vol. 3, pp. 194–196; al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 4, p. 49 (shamela 12286/3114, `alam.txt`) for the full name and the dates.

## Review

A second reader checked every half against al-Hayla's page images (zoomed), al-Zahrani, al-Ishārāt and al-Basiri, scanned every line, scored the disputed words again with spellings of the same length and with control words, ran Audar on nine more clips, and checked the sharh, notes, intro and bio against al-Zahrani, *Lisan al-Arab*, al-Dhahabi, al-Safadi and al-Zirikli (2026-11-17). Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** 12.2 «كَلامَ مُسمِعِ», as the reciter says it (four transcripts, the length of the m), for the editions' «كلاماً مُسمِعِ».
- **Readings:** 10.2 «آيِهِ» re-tested with «آيةٍ» spelled as said (it loses by 5–8 on the last three words), no longer a coin toss; al-Hayla's «وَشِل» (17.1) and «مُونَع» (20.2) recorded and scored; al-Zahrani's differences put right («في آية» is al-Dhahabi's too; «ولا تؤل» added).
- **Notes:** 12.2 rewritten for «كلامَ مُسمِعِ», and al-Safadi's remark on «اللحن الخفي» no longer pinned to this half; 15.1 explains al-Hayla's «ومعنى أينما» (2:115); 18.1 gives al-Zahrani's reading of «اعترضت»; 13.2 his «ملهم» for «أروع»; 10.1 «بَدا» and 22.2 «وَجهِ القَمَر» (Ka'b ibn Malik's hadith) added in Arabic, 22.2 in English.
- **Sharh:** 4 grounds the Paradise reading in Ibn al-Qayyim's «منازلك الأولى» (both languages); 18 follows the translation (Hell lies across the way) in both; 20 no longer repeats the note on «مونع».
- **English:** 12.2 retranslated; “line 2/5/7” in the sharh made “stanza”, since a line here is a quarter of a stanza.
- **Poet:** the nisba explained: the family came from Bukhara and then Tabriz (al-Safadi «البخاري ثم التبريزي»; al-Zirikli «أصله من تبريز»).
- **Settled, unchanged:** 12.1 «وَجَلّ» and 17.2 «وَقَلّ» keep the bare shadda (the two warnings); «دَجا», «أَعيا», «وَمَفزَعي», «الحَلي», «العَلي», «وَمَعنا» and the title stand as the drafter set them.
