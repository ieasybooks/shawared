I sleep soundly, unconcerned with my roaming verses, while people stay up all night over them, arguing.Al-Mutanabbi · واحرّ قلباه · verse 17 of 38

The name comes from this line, so the identity is built on its night: a dark ground, verse text in moonlight, and one saffron dot that roams. That dot is the calligrapher's nuqta, and the product uses it for exactly one job: showing where you are.

الشعارLogo directions

Three ways into the same verse. Each is drawn to work as a 16-pixel favicon and as a home-screen icon.

A · The roaming dots النقاط الشاردةRecommended

A square-Kufic ش whose three dots have left home and drift ahead in the direction of writing. The dots are the diamond nuqta that Ibn Muqla used as the unit for proportioning every letter.

Holds up best at small sizes, and the diamond becomes the one shape the whole product reuses.

B · The sleeping eye الجفن

أنامُ ملءَ جفوني: a closed eyelid that also reads as a crescent, with the verses rising off it while the poet sleeps.

The most literal telling of the story. At small sizes it can read as a generic "hide" icon.

C · The sounding bayt البيت المسموع

An audio waveform split by a caesura into sadr and 'ajuz. The last beat lifts away as a nuqta.

Says "audio" fastest, but waveforms are common in podcast and music logos.

اسم العلامةWordmark

Three scripts set from fonts, as sketches. The final wordmark should be drawn by an Arabic letterer so that its ش carries the same diamond dots as the mark.

The script of printed diwans and the same face as the verses inside the app. Classical, but the logo would blend into the content.

الأيقونةApp icon

Mark A on the night ground. The dots stay legible down to 16 pixels because they are solid diamonds, not hairlines.

الألوانColor

Four colors for a room lit by one lamp, plus saffron as the only accent. Saffron appears only where the nuqta does: the current verse, the playhead, and words that have a meaning.

شاشة الاستماعThe listening screen

A working mockup with verses 15 to 23 of واحرّ قلباه. Press play to watch the highlighting move half-verse by half-verse. There is no audio; the timing is simulated.

  • The poem is the screen. No cover art. The player is one slim bar, and the verse gets everything above it.
  • Staggered bayt. On a phone the sadr sits right and the 'ajuz sits left, the way printed diwans fit verses on a narrow page. Wide screens set them side by side.
  • Half-verse highlighting. The half being recited lights up, its partner stays half-lit, and a saffron nuqta replaces the verse number.
  • Words with a dot have a meaning. Tap شَوارِدِها to open its entry with the root and the sense in this verse. Tapping anywhere else on a line jumps the recitation there.
  • The explanation lives under the verse. The book button shows the sharh of whichever verse is playing, so reading the commentary never stops the audio.
  • Controls move by verse. Previous and next jump a whole bayt instead of ten seconds. Repeat loops one bayt, for people memorizing a poem.
  • Meter and rhyme are links. Tapping البسيط would list every poem in that meter, a way to browse that only poetry has.
  • Progress runs right to left, following the verse. This breaks the usual platform convention for media, so it is listed below as a decision for you.

قرارات معلّقةStill to decide

  • Night first or day firstBoth are drawn. The verse argues for night.
  • Progress directionRight to left as drawn, or left to right like most media players.

  • AudienceArabic only, or translations for readers who don't know Arabic.
