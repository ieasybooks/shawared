# Al-Farazdaq on Ali ibn al-Husayn

## The recording and the 27 verses

Osama al-Nahari recites from «هذا الذي تعرف البطحاء وطأته» to «ويسترب به الإحسان والنعم». The retained interval is 0.81–268.95 seconds of the 270.826-second source. The opening and closing margins fall outside the verse. The 54 halves run consecutively, once each. The upload shows a static title and portrait. The two full audio transcripts, individual half transcripts and word alignments are available under `media/work/20XkDm-kzr8/`. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20XkDm-kzr8)

Ali Fa'ur’s *Diwan al-Farazdaq* (Beirut, Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1987), pp. 511–514, prints these 27 verses in this order. The two-volume Dar Beirut *Diwan al-Farazdaq*, II, pp. 178–181, has the same beginning, order and end. Fa'ur’s marginal explanations of the stone, the lowered eyes, the cane, the forebears and the final «يستربّ» inform the commentary. These two closely related prints establish the extent of this particular recension. The 14-verse excerpt and the 41-verse compilation beginning «يا سائلي» in the electronic corpus represent different texts. [Fa'ur, p. 511](https://archive.org/details/3383pdf/page/n510/mode/2up) · [Dar Beirut, II, p. 178](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-farazdaq-2/page/n177/mode/2up)

## What the earlier accounts actually transmit

- Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani, *Kitab al-Aghani*, XXI, pp. 246–248, gives two reports. In the account traced through Mujalid to al-Sha'bi, Hisham asks who the man in the crowd is. People tell him it is Ali ibn al-Husayn, and the poet speaks. That passage quotes 20 of our verses, 1–4, 10–24 and 27. In the account through Ishaq ibn Muhammad al-Nakha'i, Hisham makes the pilgrimage in his father’s lifetime. Al-Abrash al-Kalbi professes ignorance of Ali. This version relates Hisham’s imprisonment of the poet and his release after the poet’s satire. [First report](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1701/246?lang=ar) · [Second report](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1701/247?lang=ar) · [Continuation](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1701/248?lang=ar)
- Al-Sharif al-Murtada, *Amali*, I, pp. 67–69, quotes 2, 1, 10, 13, 11, 15, 16. He explicitly says the poem was longer and the omitted verses were sufficiently familiar. In one telling, people in the crowd ask who Ali is and the poet answers. In the report from al-Ghallabi, a Syrian asks Hisham, who says he does not know. The account allows either his father Abd al-Malik’s reign or his brother al-Walid’s. It places the detention at Usfan and gives the sum Ali sent the poet as 12,000 dirhams. Abu Nu'aym’s *Hilyat al-awliya’*, III, p. 139, reports Ibn Aisha from his father, places the encounter before Hisham’s accession, and quotes 2, 1, 13, 10, 23, 3, 4, 11. The pilgrimage, Ali and the public act of recognition recur. The identity of the questioner and the claimant to ignorance vary. Neither an exact year nor a verbatim improvised performance can be recovered from these accounts. [Amali](https://shamela.ws/book/20937/96) · [Hilya](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13417/139?lang=ar)
- Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-a'yan*, ed. Ihsan Abbas, VI, pp. 95–97, also has 27. Its order against this recording is 1, 2, 10, 17, 13, 12, 11, 20, 19, 3, 14, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, «لا يخلف الوعد», 9, 21, 23, 24, 25, 26, 22, «يأبى أن يحل الذم», 15, 16. It lacks our 18 and 27. At «ما قال لا» the apparatus records differences in manuscript support. Counting to 27 alone would conceal both the different contents and the different ending. [Wafayat](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2515)
- Abu Tammam’s *Hamasa* attributes six verses to **al-Hazin al-Kinani**, including the opening. Their order is 1, 10, 13, 15, 12, 11. Al-Tabrizi’s commentary, II, pp. 284–285, names the patron as **Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Malik**. Al-Amidi records a further attribution in *al-Mu’talif wa-l-mukhtalif*, p. 222. He says Di'bil quoted our 1, 2, 10 and 13 for **Kuthayyir ibn Kuthayyir al-Sahmi**, in praise of **Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn**, Zayn al-Abidin’s son. Its fourth verse opens «وكاد». The different patrons and the selection of verses matter even where the opening is the same. [Hamasa](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/731) · [Al-Amidi, p. 222](https://shamela.ws/book/10913/216)
- Two verses also travelled as a pair, in the order 12 then 11. Mus'ab al-Zubayri, *Nasab Quraysh*, p. 164, assigns them to al-Hazin for Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Malik. Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-shu'ara’*, I, pp. 65–66, presents the pair as praise of an unnamed Umayyad and singles out its portrayal of awe. In the account of al-Hazin in *al-Aghani*, XV, p. 216, Abu al-Faraj gives the cane to Abd Allah and expressly rejects the inclusion of these two verses in the ode on Ali, although his account of al-Farazdaq in XXI quotes them there. Al-Amidi, p. 111, embeds the pair in a four-verse account of an audience with Abd Allah in Egypt. [Nasab Quraysh](https://shamela.ws/book/2922/157) · [Ibn Qutayba](https://shamela.ws/book/23785/58) · [Al-Aghani on al-Hazin](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1695/216?lang=ar) · [Al-Amidi, p. 111](https://shamela.ws/book/10913/105)
- Ibn Abd al-Barr, *al-Isti'ab*, III, p. 1305, quotes a poem addressed to Qutham ibn al-Abbas and expressly disputes al-Zubayr’s assignment of the «البطحاء» poem to him. The poems share metre, rhyme and the image of the nose. [Al-Isti'ab](https://shamela.ws/book/12288/1298)

The chronology places the pilgrimage before Hisham became caliph. Ibn Khallikan dates Ali’s death in Medina to 94 AH, with 92 and 99 as alternatives. Ibn Kathir dates Hisham’s accession to 105 AH. Even Abu Nu'aym’s written account, which has the Syrians address Hisham as Commander of the Faithful, opens by saying he had yet to become caliph. The sources permit that chronological limit while leaving the date of the encounter open. [Wafayat, III, p. 269](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/1280) · [Al-Bidaya wa-l-nihaya, XIII, p. 17](https://shamela.ws/book/4445/7685) · [Hilya, III, p. 139](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13417/139?lang=ar)

Al-Zirikli’s *al-A'lam*, VIII, p. 93, supplies the poet’s full name, Basran setting, family, death in 110 AH / 728 CE and the protection extended at his father’s grave. [Al-A'lam](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/7258)

## Metre and division

Basit, with mim as the rhyme consonant. At 1 the feet run `hādhal-ladhī / taʿriful / baṭḥāʾu waṭ / ʾatahū` and `walbaytu yaʿ / rifuhū / walḥillu wal / ḥaramū`. At 14 they run `allāhu shar / rafahū / qidman waʿaẓ / ẓamahū` and `jarā bidhā / kalahū / fī lawḥihil / qalamū`. Each second group begins at the verse caesura. The extended final vowel in the chant belongs to the rhyme.

## Half-verse verification

The final check found three differences in 54 clips. At **10.2** the narrow transcript joins «مكارم هذا» into «مكارمها». The widened Audar clip recovers «إلى مكارم هذا ينتهي الكرم». At **16.1** the narrow clip joins «أولية ذا» and the wider gives «أولي تذاه». Both early textual branches distinguish the abstract noun from the following demonstrative. At **22.2** the first transcript divides «ومختوم» into «وما اختم», while the wider gives «ومختم». The editions and the scored word support «ومختوم», paired with «مقدّم» in the first half. In the earlier pass **4.1** broke off after «من هـ» and **12.1** split «خيزران» in two. Both cleared after the timing review. The evidence here is the electronic transcriptions, alignment and printed texts.

## Readings against the audio

At **7.1** Fa'ur and Dar Beirut print «إذا افتدحوا» and gloss it «أثقلوا بالمصائب». Ihsan Abbas prints «فدحوا» in Ibn Khallikan and records «قدحوا» across the manuscript copies. Both full transcribers write «افتتحوا», retaining the interior t but losing the d of the verb that answers «أثقال». The isolated word score favours «فُدِحوا» by 5.14 over «اُفتُدِحوا». Across «إذا» with ordinary orthography, the shorter reading leads by 9.47. The model treats initial wasl as a pronounced vowel in that test. When each connected pronunciation is entered as one phonetic string, with wasl elided, /idhaftudiḥū/ leads /idhaftutiḥū/ by 3.68 and /idhā fudiḥū/ by 3.93. Both printed forms fit basit. An independent phoneme decoder with no Arabic prompt produced `f t o d e h o` in both the 0.45- and 0.90-second context windows. It detects both the t after f and the following d, strengthening the connected reading «اُفتُدِحوا». The word-only score and the full transcribers’ treatment of the medial consonant still warrant some reserve. The alternate is on the word card. Timed phoneme outputs are in `phoneme-windows.txt`.

At **8.2** Fa’ur p. 512 and Dar Beirut II, p. 179, both print «لاءَهُ» with fatḥa on the unseated hamza. Dar Beirut’s note explicitly calls the syntax an inversion and supplies the ordinary order «كانت لاؤه نعمًا». In the verse «لاءَهُ» is the predicate placed first and «نَعَمُ» the delayed subject. The accusative leads «لاءُهُ» by 6.77 in isolation and 8.45 across «كانت لاءه نعم». In both phoneme windows a second a precedes h, followed by the u of the possessive suffix. The exhibited text and both word cards follow the printed and recorded accusative. See `case-window.txt`.

Most other textual variants give a wider margin. In short audio windows «الخلائق» at **15.1** scores 15.69 above the Hamasa’s and al-Murtada’s «القبائل», «يشكر» at **16.1** 25.38 above «يعرف», «الدين» at **17.1** 11.38 above «العز», and «مغارسه» at **19.2** 31.85 above «عناصره». «ثوب الدجى» at **20.1** leads «نور الهدى» by 7.98. «بعد جودهم» at **24.1** leads «كنه جودهم» by 6.59 and «بعد غايتهم» by 21.08. At **22.2** «ومختوم» leads «ومختّم» by 13.63. The detailed candidates, romanisation and app-time windows are in `variant-windows.txt` and `variant-phrases.txt` in the local research directory. These comparisons use the repository scorer with one-second emission context and a whole-second window fitted to each clip.
