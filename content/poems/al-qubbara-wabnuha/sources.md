## Recording and extent

Chapter 10 keeps **890.12–961.36 seconds of the original compilation** in one continuous cut. The source voice measurements place the first and last pitched sound of this chapter at approximately 890.68 and 960.18 seconds. The cut admits the entry and the decay of the final rhyme. ## Printed text and interpretation

- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, part IV, «الحكايات», Dar al-Awda, Beirut, 1988. The references follow the pagination of part IV. [p. 157](https://archive.org/details/BBib-Alex-02915/page/n352/mode/1up)
- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, «الحكايات», Hindawi Foundation for Education and Culture, Cairo, deposit 19938/2012, ISBN 978-977-719-166-1. The page images and the electronic chapter were compared. [p. 888](https://archive.org/details/alshawkeyat/page/n888/mode/1up) · [Electronic text](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/143/)

The first edition of *al-Shawqiyyat* (part I, Cairo, Matbaʿat al-Adab wa-l-Muʾayyad, 1898) provides an earlier text. The opening is the last couplet on p. 181. The other nine follow on p. 182, including «وجعلت لكل نقلة زمن» in 4.2 and «ولو تأنى نال ما تمنى» in 9.1. This witness supports «زمن» against the «ثمن» printed in Arslan’s quotation, and ties the moral about time to the interval between flights. [p. 181](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA181) · [p. 182](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA182)

Dar al-Awda and Hindawi agree on the words of the ten couplets. Shakib Arslan quotes the whole work immediately after the story of the lion’s minister in *Shawqi: Sadaqat Arba'in Sana*, «أماثيل من شعر شوقي», §8. Arslan writes «ثمن» at the end of 4.2 where the two diwans have «زمن». His account of speech intelligible to children helps place the movement from an affectionate term of address to the narrator’s maxim. [Arslan’s complete quotation](https://www.safahat.org/books/96809026/24/)

Shawqi describes his audience on printed p. 9 of the 1898 preface. After finishing two or three fables he would read some to young Egyptians. They understood at once and laughed, encouraging him to write poems from which children could draw wisdom and good manners. He names La Fontaine as a model for the genre. Shukri Muhammad Ayyad later quotes the passage in *al-Madhahib al-adabiyya wa-l-naqdiyya inda al-Arab wa-l-gharbiyyin*, pp. 86–87. [Shawqi, first edition, preface p. 9](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=PA9) · [Ayyad, pp. 86–87](https://download.almohsinlibrary.com/OBooks/OB493.pdf#page=86)

The two printings transmit the same diwan tradition. The page reproductions and the word-by-word output from `scripts/collate.py` are in `media/research/al-qubbara-wabnuha/`. The studio captions are performance evidence. The glosses and bilingual commentary are editorial close readings of the cited text.

## Metre and rhyme

Full rajaz in paired rhymes. At 1, *ra-ay-tu-fī | baʿ-ḍir-ri-yā | ḍi-qub-ba-rah* gives **⏑ – ⏑ – | – – ⏑ – | ⏑ – ⏑ –**. *Tu-ṭay-yi-rub | na-hā-bi-aʿ | lash-sha-ja-rah* answers **⏑ – ⏑ – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | – ⏑ ⏑ –**. At 6, *lā-kin-na-hū | qad-khā-la-fal | i-shā-rah* gives **– – ⏑ – | – – ⏑ – | ⏑ – –**. *Lam-mā-a-rā | da-yuẓ-hi-rush | sha-ṭā-rah* gives **– – ⏑ – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | ⏑ – –**. The final feet in 6 have qaṭʿ and khabn. There is a fresh rhyme for each unit, including العشّ/الهشّ and فنن/زمن. A single rhyme letter would conceal the couplet form.

## Readings against the performance

At 3.1 both diwans, Arslan and the card at 903 seconds print «بجنب عود». The full transcripts commonly write «عودي». The lengthened final kasra of the printed form is acoustically compatible with a first-person possessive. The possessive spelling leads by only 2.12. The free phoneme trace ends in d followed by i, which both readings permit in rhyme. The text keeps the diwan’s spelling, and the word card gives the possible “my twig”. At 4.2 the two diwans, both long transcripts, the separate 898–922-second window and the 915-second card give «زمن». «زمن» leads Arslan’s «ثمن» by 7.01. At 915.41 seconds an independent phoneme decoder returns /z/ at the beginning of the word. Giving each move time explains the rest between moves in 5. Arslan’s «ثمن» would attach a price to each movement. At 5.2 the scores for «ثِقَل» and «ثِقْل» differ by 0.08. Both diwan pages put a fatḥa on qāf. The open syllable is also needed in the rajaz foot. The phrase names the strain of the air on a wing. In 7, ارتفعا and وقعا refer to one chick. Their alifs prolong the rhyme and are not dual inflections.
