# أبا الفضل طال الليل · Al-Tihami's elegy for his son

## The recitation in its two diwans

Osama al-Nahari's upload of 14 November 2022 is 505.58 s. Keep **0.46–504.15** of the source (503.69 s). Between the father's first address and «فالسلام على الدهر» there are 56 bayts, each recited once, and no spoken introduction or commentary. Source images sampled in the middle and towards the close continue to display the title, so they cannot establish what he says there. The textual tests below use the actual audio. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jeEscBTuCDc)

- **Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Rabi' (ed.)**, *Diwan Abi al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Tihami*, Riyadh, Maktabat al-Ma'arif, 1402 AH (1982), no. 53, pp. 333–341. His manuscript apparatus continues through p. 342 and his list of earlier quotations is on p. 343. His numbered main text has **81 bayts**, the denominator here. His notes supply the meanings of ذماء (remaining life, p. 335), الترائب and الرغيب (breastbones, deep open wound, p. 336), الجذع الغر (a young, inexperienced runner, p. 339) and العبق (a lingering fragrance, p. 340). Critical no. 80 («فيا معشر اللوام كفوا») is absent here but **present** as the recording's penultimate bayt. The critical apparatus identifies its supplementary witnesses and prefers الجوى to الهوى in this elegy. This print supports فدهري (2.2), the order القبر والقدر (6.2), يوهي … الكريم (18), شطره (28.1), and نجوم الشيب (54.2). - Yaqut, *Mu'jam al-buldan*, III, p. 70, under al-Ramla, records the child's birth and death in the Palestinian town, his father's preaching there, and quotes the opening with «فدهري». Al-Jirawi, *al-Hamasa al-maghribiyya*, II, pp. 870–872, no. 488, anthologises the opening and selected later bayts. Thus 25 of the 81 printed bayts are unrecited. The overlaps between these two prints and al-Jirawi were compared bayt by bayt in the working collation. The paired explanations distinguish the poet's actual loss from the opportunities he only imagines and track returning images: the crescent in 4 and 28, the borrowed share of a life in 9–14 and 42, and the perfume in 43–44.

## Metre and rhyme

The tawil opening sadr scans «أَبَلْفَضْ | لِ طَالَلْلَيْ | لُ أَمْ خَا | نَني صَبْري» (فعولن، مفاعيلن، فعولن، مفاعيلن). The opening rhymes its two halves, صبري/تسري. In 29.2 «قَضَيْتُ | بِما لِلْلَا | هِ فيهَا | مِنَشْشُكْري» the first foot is shortened to فعولُ. The final consonant held through فجرِ، حشرِ، شهرِ، شكرِ and دهرِ is **ر**. First-person endings such as صبري and عمري and the long release of final kasra can all sound in -ri. That recurring sound explains the misleading ya label in some modern indexes.

## Word readings on the audio

Each comparison uses the same word interval on the edited recording, with 0.30 s on each side. The MMS model, romanisation and CTC scoring are those of `scripts/variants.py`. The batch used a short inference window with one second of context padding. A less negative log score fits the acoustic frames better within that row. Scores from different rows have different baselines. A gap below two offers little guidance. The model often favours ز over ذ, and it is a poor judge of the length of a chanted vowel.

At 2.2 the two prints divide between «فدهري» and «فليلي». Yaqut’s quotation and both continuous transcriptions support «فدهري». A second run with the repository’s standard inference window also favoured it (−14.42 against −20.20). At 6.2 the order **grave, worth** matches Alexandria. At 10.1 the first-person possessive in أرواحنا is common to the recording and Alexandria. The recording has ولكنما before it and repeats ما at 10.2. At 11.2 the consonantal difference between فألّا and فهلّا has only modest support from MMS. Both source transcriptions start the question with an alif. The two forms convey the father’s reproach.

At 16.1 both full transcriptions of the recording give دمائه, also favoured by MMS over the printed ذمائه. The card and both explanations account for the last of the boy’s blood as the recording has it and the last of his life as the Diwan has it. At 18.1 the margins separating spoken يُهوي from Alexandria’s يُوهي are modest. Both full transcriptions give يهوي, a possible transitive reading, “brings down”. Al-Rabi' has يُوني, “wearies”, and الكرام in the second half where the recording and Alexandria have الكريم. At 20.2 the model scarcely distinguishes يُثنى from يُبنى. Alexandria, the full transcriptions and the image of eyelashes folding over the eye support يُثنى. The gemination of غطى at 21.1 receives identical MMS scores. The printed form and the metre support the light غَطى.

At 23.1 the voiced sigh is الزفير, as in al-Rabi'. Alexandria has الحديث. The figure of splitting the heart in two (28.1) follows Alexandria’s شطره. At 29.2 both prints have نهضت where the voice supplies قضيت. At 31.2 the extra ت in the printed second person would give «you went ahead of us». Both full passes and the acoustic test support تَقَدَّمَنا, “he went ahead of us”. The unit of his lead is the slight شيئاً in the voice and شيء in Alexandria, against al-Rabi'’s سبقاً. At 34.2 the short vowel in حييتُ is less certain than the consonants. The subject of أنسَ in the preceding conditional and the father’s promise favour the first person. At 36.2 the voice favours the singular الفِكر. At 42.2 the initial verb and the last noun are weak calls: both full transcripts and Alexandria give ينقل … إلى الشطر. The printed alternative «إلى شطري» also fits the rhyme. At 49.2 the choice of يَؤولَ over the critical تَؤولَ has a small acoustic margin. Alexandria and the full voice support the masculine verb referring to غنى, wealth. At 52, the father has consumed the days that consume him. Both full passes give يُفني وَيَفنى, “consumes and perishes”. The critical text reverses the verbs. At 54.2 the recording’s grey hairs are stars of الشيب. The critical text puts them in الليل, while Alexandria prints الشيب.
