# جَفا وُدُّهُ فَازوَرَّ أَو مَلَّ صاحِبُهُ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Waaedh, «جفا ودّه», Alqimmah Studio, 5 September 2026. The video credits Husayn al-Sada with language review and displays each bayt with a short paraphrase. Its 682.678 seconds include silence to 4.279 and from 679.506. The app keeps 3.75–680.15 (676.40 seconds), −4.7 dB gain, −20.1 LUFS integrated and −4.1 dBTP peak. The reciter speaks 86 distinct bayts in order, with no repeated or omitted couplet against the 86-bayt sequence below. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jzXOinJNRp4)

- **Muhammad al-Tahir ibn Ashur, ديوان بشار بن برد**, vol. 1, Cairo, Lajnat al-Ta'lif wa-l-Tarjama wa-l-Nashr, 1369 AH / 1950, pp. 305–323. The heading on p. 305 names Marwan ibn Muhammad and Qays Aylan. The poem and commentary are on pp. 306–323. This is the main edition for the words, manuscript readings and glosses. Its 85 bayts appear in the recording in the same order. For the independent collation its text and apparatus were also consulted in the Umm al-Qura University Poetry Encyclopaedia. That electronic page includes alternative versions of entire bayts as separate rows. [Columbia copy, Arabic Collections Online](https://sites.dlib.nyu.edu/viewer/books/columbia_aco002511/313) · [Umm al-Qura](https://uqu.edu.sa/App/Poetry/Poems/116624)
- **Al-Sayyid Badr al-Din al-Alawi, ديوان شعر بشار بن برد**, 1981 impression, poem 55, pp. 42–48. Unlike the continuous recension, these are 45 numbered bayts gathered from different books. Al-Alawi himself describes the difficulty of ordering them and the extent of the overlap with the Tunis recension on p. 43. He prints our 52 as his 20 (p. 45), with «رويد» and a variant «رويداً» in the apparatus. His printed text of our 64 has فراحوا فريقٌ. All 45 were transcribed from the page images and run through the bayt collator, which matches a bayt even when it has moved. The editor's sequence also incorporates separately transmitted sayings absent from the recording. [Scanned edition](https://archive.org/details/a949n/page/n41/mode/2up)
- **Abu al-Fath al-Abbasi, معاهد التنصيص على شواهد التلخيص**, vol. 2, pp. 28–30 (Shamela 6951/410–412), quotes the opening and battle passage, including the bayt about al-Dahhak's mourner at recording position 52. It identifies the patron as Ibn Hubayra. **Ibn al-Mu'tazz, طبقات الشعراء**, pp. 27–28 (Shamela 7867/11–12), preserves a different opening, جفا جفوة, and a selection of the battle verses. Both excerpts were collated. Al-Alawi's heading names Umar ibn Hubayra. Al-Abbasi's identification of Ibn Hubayra and Ibn Ashur's explanation on p. 305 lead to Yazid ibn Umar ibn Hubayra, commander of Qays under Marwan. The poem repeatedly names Marwan and does not name Ibn Hubayra. [Al-Abbasi](https://shamela.ws/book/6951/410) · [Ibn al-Mu'tazz](https://shamela.ws/book/7867/11)
- The Abu Dhabi Poetry Encyclopaedia text (old Shamela 4496, vol. 1, pp. 196–204) gives all 86 in performed order, including رويداً after وأرعن لا تبكي. It is the sequence counted in the catalogue. At 9 it transposes the consonants of مقارف, at 13 it reproduces the manuscript's erroneous ض, and at 62 it prints مثال instead of مثار. It is valuable as a continuous comparator, never enough on its own to justify those forms. [Old Shamela](https://old.shamela.ws/browse.php/book-4496)
- The nickname «جفا ودّه» in the video is from the opening. The books have no separate poem title. The relevant historical setting is the civil war before the fall of the Umayyads, especially Marwan's fighting against Thabit ibn Nu'aym of Judham and al-Dahhak ibn Qays al-Shaybani. Ibn Ashur recounts on pp. 305–306 the report in *al-Aghani* that someone attributed the friendship maxims to al-Mutalammis, and the contemporary rebuttal. The claim in that report concerns a handful of sayings within Bashshar's long poem. For the poet's name, clientage to Uqayl, blindness, time in Basra and Baghdad, death under the lash and dates, see al-Zirikli, **الأعلام**, vol. 2, p. 52. Its 167 AH / 784 CE is used for the poet record. The video's 168 AH is also reported in al-Zirikli's apparatus. [Al-A'lam](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/1081)

## Readings and commentary

The metre is tawil and the rhyme consonant is ba, before the attached ha which recurs in the rhyme. In the opening the sadr divides جَفا وُدْ | دُهُو فَزْوَرْ | رَ أَو مَلْ | لَ صاحِبُهْ, فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. The same four-foot movement carries the description of the night journey into the battle section. The Arabic and English commentaries and translations follow the edition's distinction between the rider's camel and the wild ass to which it is compared. See pp. 309–315 on the desert vocabulary and the water-hole story, pp. 316–317 on the transition to praise, and pp. 317–323 on the campaigns and proper names. Ibn Ashur pp. 318–319 quotes and discusses Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani's *Asrar al-Balagha* for the single moving comparison at 62. [Discussion in the scanned edition](https://sites.dlib.nyu.edu/viewer/books/columbia_aco002511/327)

The following scores are total log-likelihood differences on the same aligned word interval. A positive gap favours the form named first. Each pair contains the same number of words. The first eleven comparisons use the default window of the repository's variant scorer. The other comparisons use its same model, tokenisation, margins and scoring with a shorter padded inference window. Vowel stretches, dh/dad and quiet final syllables are less secure than a clearly audible separate consonant. Selected difficult bayts were also transcribed with Audar using both halves plus margins and compared with the whole-recording and per-half Cohere passes.

## Independent check of the half verses

All 172 app halves were transcribed individually. On the final text 46 had word differences (82 of 858 words, 9.6%), with one additional possible edge. The following gives every flagged address, distinguishing a difference in source wording from a failed automatic word or word division.
