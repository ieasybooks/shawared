## Attribution

- **Not in the old diwan.** محمد سعيد مولوي, ديوان عنترة، تحقيق ودراسة (al-Maktab al-Islami): scan archive.org `3562pdf` (printed page = leaf − 7 in the introduction, leaf − 5 from the poems on). It gives al-A'lam al-Shantamari's recension with al-Batalyawsi's additions (pp. 185–331) and a supplement of pieces found in other books, «زيادات شعر عنترة (صلة الديوان)» (pp. 331–340). The poem is in none of them. Its first bayt is not in the rhyme index (pp. 364–367; the lam poems are on p. 366), and none of its abyat is in the edition's poems on the same meter and rhyme, read on the page images: no. 6 «طال الثواء على رسوم المنزل» (pp. 246–253), no. 7 «عجبت عبيلة من فتى متبذّل» (pp. 253–262) and the supplement's nos. 18–20 (pp. 336–338).
- **The printings it comes from.** Mawlawi, pp. 54–56: the first printed diwan, Iskandar Agha Abkarius's «منية النفس في أشعار عنترة عبس» (Beirut, 1864), «جمع كل ما وصلت إليه يده من شعر منسوب لعنترة، سواء ما كان منه في نسخ الأعلم أو ما كان في السيرة الشعبية» (p. 54); of it he writes «ومن الواضح أننا لا نستطيع أن نعتمد على هذا الديوان في استقاء شعر عنترة لجهالة روايته، ولأنه لا يعطي المصادر التي أخذ عنها، فضلاً عن أن الأشعار التي وردت في السيرة الشعبية يصعب قبولها جملة» (p. 55). Amin al-Khuri's Matba'at al-Adab in Beirut printed «نسخة اسكندر آغا نفسها» several times, among them a third printing of 1888 and a fifth of 1898 (p. 55); the 1893 scan used below names itself the fourth printing of Matba'at al-Adab (its title page: «طبع رابعة», «امين الخوري صاحب المكتبة … مطبعة الآداب»), so it is Abkarius's text. Amin Sa'id's commentary «قد حوت كل الأبيات التي وردت في النسخ السابقة» in another order (p. 56); the Dar Sadir diwan of 1958 holds «ما صح لعنترة وما حمل عليه، وما حفلت به السيرة الشعبية» (p. 56). The three printings used below are of this line.
- **In the Sira.** سيرة عنترة بن شداد (Cairo: Maktabat al-Jumhuriyya al-Arabiyya, n.d.), vol. 3 (part 17), pp. 289–291: scan archive.org `AAlexandrina-174539`, leaves 292–294 (p. 287, leaf 290, names him «الأخيل بن عمر[و]»). Antar meets al-Akhyal, the Banu Hariqa's champion, and «طعنه في صدره أطلع السنان يلمع من ظهره»; the tribe flees to the mountains, leaving herds and families; at evening Antar «ينشد ويقول» 23 lines on this meter and rhyme. Ten are the diwan's 1–6, 13, 17, 21 and 22, in another order and with other words in places («وإذا الذليل نهاك … من الرماح الذبل», «واحمل إذا حق اللقا», «فلقد نكبت», «وجهنم في العز أفخر منزل»); four draw on the authentic «طال الثواء» (Mawlawi no. 6, abyat 17–19 and 21: «…تخوّفني الحتوف كأنني», «فأجبتها إن المنية منهل», «أني امرؤ سأموت إن لم أقتل», «والخيل ساهمة/شاخصة الوجوه كأنما») and a fifth reworks its 20th («وكذا المنية لو تمثل شخصها…»); the other eight are in neither.
- **So:** the poem belongs to the Sira tradition, not to the early transmission. The intro says what the sources say: it is not in Mawlawi's edition, and it is in the printings he describes as drawing on the Sira.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Glosses:** Tarad's notes (القسطل, الجندل, السماكان, الأجزل, الشفار والأنصل, المحجّل, نكب, الأخيل as «المتكبّر», التقلقل); the *Mawsu'at al-qaba'il* notes (الذابل والمهند, العديد الأجزل, الشفار, المحجّل); Amin Sa'id's (الأخيل «وهو أيضاً الصرد»، a bird of ill omen; المهر; «ضبع ترعرع.. أي نما وشب وهو من التفاخر الغريب»); al-A'lam's commentary in Mawlawi, p. 253, for الكريهة («إذا حملت نفسي على مكروه الحرب»); Ibn Qutayba, *al-Anwa'*, p. 62 (shamela 9093/109, `anwa.txt`) for السماك الأعزل والرامح; *Lisan al-Arab*, vol. 10, p. 137 (shamela 1687/4807) for الزبرقان «القمر». The rest are the common lexical senses.
- **Al-Akhyal and the Banu Hariqa:** the Sira, vol. 3, pp. 287–290 (above). Tarad and the *Mawsu'at* gloss الأخيل as «المتكبّر»; Amin Sa'id's footnote says the word «أيضاً» names the shrike, a bird of ill omen, and does not say which he takes it for here. The note gives the Sira's man first, since the bayt names someone speared through the heart, and credits the glosses to Tarad and the *Mawsu'at* by name (the draft had «بعض محقّقي الديوان»).
- **Allusions and verdicts:** 2, Amr ibn Kulthum's «ألا لا يجهلن أحد علينا فنجهل فوق جهل الجاهلينا» (our comparison), and Amin Sa'id's footnote «معنى هذا البيت من الحكميات» (he adds that al-Ma'arri «قد حام حول هذا المعنى» in «ولما رأيت الجهل في الناس فاشياً، تجاهلت حتى ظن أني جاهل»); 6, Quran 4:78; 16, Amin Sa'id «من التفاخر الغريب»; 17, the Sira, where the line follows «أمي زبيبة لست أنكر اسمها / سوداء يشرق جيدها في المحمل» (p. 290), which shows whose leg and hair are described; 21–22, Amin Sa'id: «هذا البيت من الأبيات الحكمية البليغة», «هذا البيت من الحكميات».
- **The father who would not own him (8, 9):** Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-shu'ara'*, vol. 1, p. 243 (shamela 23785/236, `ibnqutayba.txt`).
- **The poet's life:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 5, p. 91 (shamela 12286/4233, `alam.txt`): name, mother, Abla, Dahis and al-Ghabra', his death and «أكثر ما فيه مصنوع»; the freedom story from Ibn Qutayba. `died` is left out of `poet.json`: al-A'lam gives «نحو ٢٢ ق هـ = نحو ٦٠٠ م», and the format (a whole hijri year shown as "d. … AH") can't hold an approximate date before the Hijra; both bios give it.

## Review

A second reader checked the text letter by letter against the page images of Tarad, the 1893 Beirut printing, Amin Sa'id and the Sira, scored the doubtful vowels again on candidates of the same length and read the aligner's frames where the scores were close, scanned every half, and checked the sharh, notes, intro, bio and this file against Mawlawi, Tarad, Sa'id, the Sira, al-A'lam and Ibn Qutayba (2026-11-17). Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** 20.1 «عِزُّكُمُ», the plural mim voweled and lengthened as Tarad and Sa'id print it and as he says it, a regular مُتَفاعِلُن (the draft's «عِزُّكُم» made the half a rare waqs); 15.2 «وَالزِّبرِقانُ» in the nominative, as he says it and as the description and the old shamela text have it (the draft had Tarad's accusative); 14.2 «مُهَلهَلِ» as he says it (+5.2), with a note on Tarad's «مُهَلهِل»; 15.1 «وَمالِكٍ», his genitive, with a note that the printings' «وَمالِكاً» is the right case.
- **Meaning:** 8, he was counted a slave because his mother was one, not «لسواد لونه»; 15 rewritten in both languages for the nominative «الزبرقانُ», a new clause, and retranslated; 16.1 «سوداء الجبين» translated as a black forehead, not "black-browed" (dark eyebrows).
- **Sharh:** 2, Amin Sa'id's verdict that the bayt is a maxim; 16, his «من التفاخر الغريب»; 17, the Sira's preceding line «أمي زبيبة لست أنكر اسمها», which shows whose leg and hair are described, in place of a guess about what his enemies taunted; 17 and 18 in English no longer speak of taunts the poem doesn't mention; 1 in English reworded.
- **Notes:** 13.2 «الأخيل» glosses credited to Tarad and the *Mawsu'at* by name, and Amin Sa'id's shrike given as he gives it («وهو أيضاً الصرد»), not as his reading of the bayt; 14.2 «الهَيذُبان» and «مُهَلهَل» added; 15.1 «وَمالِكٍ» added; 15.2 «الزِّبرِقانُ» gives Tarad's accusative; 16.2 the phrase note «رُسوم المَنزِل» given the phrase as its head; English notes for 13.2 and 14.2.
- **Intro:** the last two abyat prefer the colocynth, even Hell, with honour to the water of life with disgrace, not death to life (both languages); «إنه» made clear as Abkarius; "still quoted" softened.
- **sources.md:** the 1893 scan identified, from its title page and Mawlawi p. 55, as Matba'at al-Adab's fourth printing of Abkarius's own text; Mawlawi's page offset corrected for the introduction (leaf − 7); the old shamela text, which has the printings' words, separated from the YouTube description's text, which the reciter follows in six places; al-Akhyal ibn Amr's page in the Sira (p. 287); the draft's −3.5 for «وَبَني» shown to come from the romanized alif of «وَابنَي» (on the same letters the text wins by 1.1); 3.2 «مِنَ» is a coin toss, not a reading the meter decides; 9.2 «لي» confirmed frame by frame.
