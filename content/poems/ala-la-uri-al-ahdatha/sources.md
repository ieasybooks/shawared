# أَلا لا أُري الأَحداثَ مَدحاً وَلا ذَمّا

Osama al-Nahari’s recording, published on 18 July 2024, gives all **34 bayts** of al-Mutanabbi’s elegy for his maternal grandmother in the order of al-Wahidi’s Berlin text. The continuous keep is **3.450–306.550 s** of a 308.416 s source, **303.100 s** in the app. The opening title and the studio verse captions belong to this performance. It runs from «ألا لا أري الأحداث» through «ولا صحبتني مهجة تقبل الظلما», with each half heard once. The next verses in the diwan, «يستعظمون أبياتاً», have their own rubric and numbering. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zwdFI5fNB-w) · [End of the elegy and following rubric](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n636/mode/1up)

The metre is *tawil*. Both halves of the rhyming opening scan ⏑–– | ⏑––– | ⏑–– | ⏑–––. The opening sadr takes the form of the ajuz through *tasriʿ*. At 17 the sadr scans ⏑–⏑ | ⏑––– | ⏑–– | ⏑–⏑– and the ajuz ⏑–– | ⏑––– | ⏑–– | ⏑–––. The moving rhyme letter is **mīm** with fatha, followed by the long vowel in «حِلما»، «وَصما» and «الظُّلما».

## Readings and commentary

**1.** The studio’s opening words and both continuous transcriptions give «مدحاً», where all the written witnesses give «حمداً». Both express praise, and the substitution keeps the metre. The verb in the edition is «أُري», I show or offer events praise. Ibn Furja explicitly objects to readers who sing «أَرى», I see. He argues that the following half explains the withholding of praise or blame from things that can have neither folly nor forbearance. *Muʿjiz Ahmad* explains the verb by «لا أُظهر للحوادث ولا أُريها». In **1.2** the recording agrees with al-Barquqi and *Muʿjiz Ahmad* in «ولا كفّها». Al-Wahidi prints «وما كفّها». [Ibn Furja, p. 313](https://shamela.ws/book/37025/279) · [*Muʿjiz Ahmad*, p. 148](https://shamela.ws/book/753/148) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 260](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n640/mode/1up)

**7–8.** Al-Nahari, al-Wahidi and al-Barquqi put «عرفت الليالي» immediately before «منافعها». Al-Wahidi favours taking the nights as the antecedent. They feed when *you* go hungry, an interpretation he distinguishes from Ibn Jinni’s account of the hunger of events themselves. Al-Wahidi also records «نجوع ونظمأ». Ibn Furja takes the pronoun back to the grandmother who goes hungry to feed others. In *Muʿjiz Ahmad* «منافعها» stands before «عرفت الليالي», closer to the woman. The language layers give both readings and translate the order performed. [Al-Wahidi, pp. 260–261](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n639/mode/1up) · [Ibn Furja’s conclusion, p. 315](https://shamela.ws/book/37025/281) · [*Muʿjiz Ahmad*, p. 149](https://shamela.ws/book/753/149)

**9, 11–12, 15.** Both continuous passes give «فمتّ بها غمّاً» with al-Barquqi and *Muʿjiz Ahmad*. Al-Wahidi and *al-Lamiʿ* have «همّاً». In 11 the caption and continuous first pass give «من لفظي وخطّي كأنّما». The wider second pass recovers the order but runs together «خطّي كأنّما». The printed «من خطّي ولفظي كأنّها» reverses speech and handwriting, and makes the grandmother the explicit subject of the comparison. The Berlin print also begins this line «تعجّبت». Al-Wahidi explains the white-marked raven as a rarity. She looks for such birds in the lettering, then kisses the paper until real ink marks her eyes and teeth. At 15.2 the performer follows al-Wahidi and al-Barquqi in «رضيت بها» beside «رضيت بي». *Muʿjiz Ahmad* and *al-Lamiʿ* give «رضيت لها». [Al-Wahidi, p. 261](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n639/mode/1up) · [p. 262](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n638/mode/1up) · [*Muʿjiz Ahmad*, p. 149](https://shamela.ws/book/753/149)

**20, 22–23.** The head and breast in 20 are the two things «ملئا حزماً». Al-Wahidi explains «اللذَي» as a shortened dual relative, either dialectal or with the final nūn dropped. The grandmother is «لي أمّاً» in 22, where he imagines that being his mother would confer a great father upon her. Al-Wahidi prints «أباك» and al-Barquqi «أبَكِ». Both continuous transcriptions keep «أباك» and «أمّاً». In 23 the performance combines «بيومها» with «لقد». Al-Wahidi prints «بموتها … لقد», *Muʿjiz Ahmad* «بموتها … فقد», and al-Barquqi «بيومها … فقد». Here «her day» is the day of her death. From «ولدت منّي» onwards he makes the failed reunion issue in a very different life for the grandson. [Al-Wahidi, pp. 262–263](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n637/mode/1up) · [Al-Barquqi, p. 1250](https://archive.org/details/20201020_20201020_1638/page/n1249/mode/1up) · [*Muʿjiz Ahmad*, p. 150](https://shamela.ws/book/753/150)

**27, 29, 32, 34.** The nūn after the doubled nūn in «بأنّني» is visible both on the studio card at 237 s and in al-Barquqi, p. 1251. Both continuous transcriptions give the same form. In «بذبابه» the point belongs to a sword that has yet to be named. It will serve him as a greeting in the next verse. At 32 the caption and both full passes give «نفوسهم», the souls of the people he comes from. The written witnesses give «نفوسنا», our souls. Al-Wahidi says the third person would follow «قوم» more regularly and explains the first person as the poet’s inclusion of himself in their praise. The closing «لا تعزّني» is legible in the Berlin and al-Barquqi prints. It means an hour that gives him no honour. *Muʿjiz Ahmad* also records «غبرت» for «عبرت», two verbs for time passing. [Al-Wahidi, pp. 263–264](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n636/mode/1up) · [Al-Barquqi, p. 1251](https://archive.org/details/20201020_20201020_1638/page/n1250/mode/1up) · [*Muʿjiz Ahmad*, p. 151](https://shamela.ws/book/753/151)
