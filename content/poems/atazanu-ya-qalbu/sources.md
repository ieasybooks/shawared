# أَتَظعَنُ يا قَلبُ مَع مَن ظَعَن

## Recording and extent

Osama Al-Waaedh, «أتظعن يا قلب مع من ظعن», uploaded 16 April 2024 to his own channel. The audio opens with the question to the heart. It moves from the remembered cup directly into the address to Ahmad, then closes with two complete performances of the sea and Yemen comparison. The full-file Cohere and Audar transcripts were taken before the literary text was set. Neither the visual of the fire nor the short description supplies a verse text. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qiaoPWeCOBc)

The source is 119.002 s. The app keeps 0.40–118.15 s, including room around the first syllable and after the dying voice. The gain is +11.2 dB, the result −20.3 LUFS and −1.0 dBTP. There is no spoken introduction or coda. All fourteen bayts in Diyab's text are heard in order. The last appears once in the poem and twice in the timeline. In the second performance the alignment caught the start of the final syllable at 116.74 s. Its voicing continues to about 117.18 s. The highlight ends at 117.20 s, leaving the final 0.55 s of room in the audio. At the change of subject, the final consonant of «أحمد بن الحسن» is still sounding at 62.68 s. The pause lasts from 62.70 to 63.24 s. These are the end of 8.2 and the actual onset of «ألم» at 9.1. The automatic alignment had placed that onset at 62.52 s, inside the patron's name.

## Text and commentary

- Abd al-Majid Diyab, ed., *Mu'jiz Ahmad*, appendix «زيادات من شعر المتنبي», after «شغلي عن الربع». Diyab explains that his appendix gathers material absent from the manuscripts of the commentary which he nevertheless accepts as al-Mutanabbi's. The heading names Ahmad ibn al-Hasan. All fourteen bayts are printed there in the order recited. The page numbers in this electronic text are its own. The appendix is the modern editor's work. [Appendix preface](https://shamela.ws/book/753/470) · [Poem](https://shamela.ws/book/753/471)
- Abd al-Aziz al-Maymani, «زيادات ديوان شعر المتنبي», no. 44, in *Buhuth wa-Tahqiqat*, ed. Muhammad Uzayr Shams, Dar al-Gharb al-Islami, Beirut, 1995, vol. 2, pp. 127–128. He gives twelve bayts from the Shirwani manuscript, p. 403, and reports them also in Muhibb al-Din al-Khatib's copy, pp. 357–358. His heading has Ja'far ibn al-Husayn, the actual verse Ja'far ibn al-Hasan. His notes explain the feud and the nursling who shares his milk. His witnesses omit our 9 and 11. The edition total here follows Diyab's fourteen. [Witnesses](https://shamela.ws/book/19234/570) · [Poem and readings](https://shamela.ws/book/19234/600) · [Al-Khatib's copy](https://shamela.ws/book/19234/601)
- The Abu Dhabi Department of Culture and Tourism's Poetry Encyclopedia offers a further vocalized text of the fourteen. It gives الغُصُن in 4.2, المُزَن in 7.2, and nominative قاصدُه in 13.2. Ibn Manzur and al-Zabidi explain اللِّثى, the gums, in 7.2. The editions and these lexical senses underlie the two explanations. ## Metre and readings

The metre is mutaqarib, with nun as the rhyme consonant in pause. The opening sadr scans ⏑–⏑ | ⏑–– | ⏑–– | ⏑–, and its ajuz ⏑–– | ⏑–⏑ | ⏑–– | ⏑–. This takes مَع with a still ʿayn. Both halves of 6 scan ⏑–– | ⏑–– | ⏑–– | ⏑–. In 2 the metrical break passes through the name البسوس. Both witnesses divide it البسو / س, and the word card gives the complete name. On the recording the long /u/ lasts to 11.96 s. Its final /s/ starts at 11.97 s, where the two half-verse highlights meet. After the sibilant he takes a short breath before «بين». In 8.2 بن retains the standard spelling between names, with its initial cluster joined to أَحمَدَ.

The electronic editions were collated bayt by bayt. In al-Maymani's apparatus الفَنَن is a further reading in 4.2, and his report from al-Khatib supports the reciter's لَها and لَدَيكَ against his base text in 8.1 and 10.2. The electronic transcription gives «أتظغن» in the opening and dots «الغضن» in 4.2. The other witnesses and the recorded readings give ظعن and غصن. The differences in the patron's name and the last line belong to a different textual tradition, and are also described in the word cards.

The app timings retain the full sound of the held rhymes. Where a voice trails into a pause, the release was located from its declining energy and the disappearance of a periodic pitch. Where one word runs into the next, the marked end stays just inside the old phrase. The sibilant inside البسوس and the silence before «ألم» supply unusually clear internal landmarks.

## Checks against the recording

In the first check, Cohere transcribed the thirty automatic half-verse cuts in isolation. Six addresses differed from the text. An independent Audar pass used a little of the sound on either side of each disputed clip. The two complete-file transcripts also preserve words a short clip cuts in half.

| Half | What the comparison shows |
| --- | --- |
| 1.1 | The clips substitute ض for ظ in the two forms of ظعن and attach a further consonant or vowel to the rhyme. Both literary witnesses give the same departure verb twice. The rhyme closes on nun. |
| 2.1–2.2 | Written and recited, the name البسوس straddles the metrical cut. Both short models struggle with its unattached final s. The continuous phrase and both editions preserve the name. The phone pass gives a vowel after «لا» at 9.17 s, then the sibilant of «أصاب», with no intervening t. |
| 4.1 | Short Cohere yields «الدجّال». Audar with the surrounding sound restores «بدر الدجى» as in both editions. |
| 7.2 | Short Cohere joins «اللِّثى لا» and appends a b to المزن. Audar recovers both phrases and the cloud word. The independent phone pass gives /u/ at 54.41 s, /z/ at 54.57 s, then /a/ at 54.63 s. The Encyclopedia prints this second vowel explicitly. |
| 13.2 | The two short recognizers propose «إن شارك». In the continuous recording both recognize «لشارك», the answer to «لو» in the preceding half. Both literary traditions have the same construction. |

On 1.1 the advantage of مَع over مَعَ is only 0.77, so the scansion above determines the choice. The initial consonant of أُصاب in 2.1 leads تُصاب by 3.75. بَعدَكُم in 3.1 leads بعدكما by 4.44. In 4.2 a phoneme pass with no supplied text finds /o s o n/ in the last word. The two rounded vowels agree with the Encyclopedia's الغُصُن. The candidate scorer favours a still middle consonant by 3.57. Both forms with غ and ص score well above the transmitted alternative الفَنَن. In 6.1 «بعد أن» leads «بعد ما» by 2.18. In 7.2 the recorded and printed المُزَن leads المُزُن by 4.76. Scoring the form without a written second vowel yields a near tie (0.50), resolved by the /a/ and the metre.

In 8.1 لَها leads لَهُ by 5.18. In 8.2 أَحمَدَ leads جَعفَرَ by 21.81 and أَحمَدُ by 3.63. In 10.2 لَدَيكَ leads عَلَيكَ by 12.84 and عَلَينا by 16.12. In 13.1 مِن leads في by 6.23. In 13.2 قاصِدَهُ leads قاصِدُهُ by 5.14, agreeing with al-Maymani's reading in which the child shares his milk with the visitor. Both performances of 14 favour نَداكَ over يَداكَ (5.47 and 5.34) and الباس over النّاس after في in the second half (6.89 and 6.36).

The four word alignments below 0.35 fall on the isolated س of البسوس (2.2), on مِن (11.1 and 13.1) and on اِبن in «هذا ابن من» (12.2). The joined name is explained in the word card, and the three continuous phrases are supported by the recorded context and printed text.

The check on the reviewed timings still flags six of the thirty halves. It now counts thirteen differences in 147 words. At 4.1 the longer release restores «بدر الدجى». At 1.1 the final consonant is now correct and only the two ظ spellings differ. At 2.1 the isolated clip recognises the entire name «البسوس» across the cut, but gives «وحرض». The wider Audar clip and the unbroken recording both give حرب. At 2.2 the detached s remains absent from both transcripts, although its frication from 11.97 s is distinct. At 7.2 the padded clip preserves «لا بماء المزن». At 13.2 the short clip still misses the opening lam, but independently vocalizes the object قاصِدَهُ.

The sixth flag is new at 12.1. Short Cohere appends a mim to «غَنوا». Two wider Audar cuts recover غنوا before taking in the b of the following «برؤياك». Both full-file transcripts and both print traditions give غنوا. Its complement «عن قول» in 12.2 accounts for the verb, meaning that once people see him they have no need to ask who his father is.
