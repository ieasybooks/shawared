# سَأَلوني لِمَ لَم أَرثِ أَبي

## What the text is

- **A poem of al-Shawqiyyat, not lines from a play.** It stands in the third part of al-Shawqiyyat, «في المراثي» (Dar al-Awda, Beirut, 1988, pp. 154–156), under the heading «يرثي أباه», with the note «نظم هذه القصيدة حوالى سنة ١٨٩٧ يرثى بها والده الطيب الذكر المرحوم على بك شوقى رحمه الله». It is a self-contained elegy of 31 abyat with its own opening («سألوني: لِمَ لَمْ أرثِ أبي؟»); none of Shawqi's verse plays is involved, and no source puts it in one.
- **Shawqi's, in four sources that agree:** the Dar al-Awda al-Shawqiyyat above; Ibrahim al-Abyari's الموسوعة الشوقية, vol. 5, pp. 352–354, poem 29, headed «وقال يرثي والده علياً سنة (١٨٩٧م)»; Shawqi Dayf, شوقي شاعر العصر الحديث, p. 53, who quotes our 12–15 as «قوله فى أبيه المتوفى سنة ١٨٩٥»; and Muhammad al-Majdhub, «شعر شوقي في ميزان النقد», p. 88, who quotes our 3–4 as Shawqi's elegy for his father («لنستمع إليه يرثي والده»). The upload title «أنا من مات ومن مات أنا | أحمد شوقي» and the video's description («سجلت هذه الأبيات من قصيدة لأحمد شوقي في رثاء والده») say the same. No source gives it to anyone else.
- **The father:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 1, p. 136 (shamela 12286/346, `alam.txt`) names the poet «أحمد شوقي بن علي بن أحمد شوقي»; the Dar al-Awda note calls the father «على بك شوقى»; al-Abyari «والده علياً». Shawqi Dayf (p. 14) says that the father, Ali, was made an inspector of the Khedive's estates (الخاصة الخديوية).
- **The date:** «حوالى سنة ١٨٩٧» (the Dar al-Awda note), «سنة (١٨٩٧م)» (al-Abyari), the father's death in 1895 (Shawqi Dayf, p. 53). The sources give the Christian year only, so the intro gives no Hijri year.
- **Title:** the first sadr, by the owner's rule. «يرثي أباه» is the diwan's heading, a description rather than a name the books use, and no source calls the poem by any name: Shawqi Dayf says «مرثيته في أبيه» (*al-Adab al-Arabi al-Mu'asir*, p. 120) and «قوله فى أبيه» (*Shawqi sha'ir al-'asr al-hadith*, p. 53), al-Majdhub «يرثي والده», and a shamela search for «لم أرث أبي» and «ومن مات أنا» finds no other book that names it. The upload's «أنا من مات ومن مات أنا» is the bayt the video was named after (our 3).
- **Title, the second reading (2026-11-18):** the recording starts at the edition's bayt 7, so the title is a line the listener never hears. It stays the poem's first sadr. (1) The rule is "the first sadr" of the poem, and every other title in the app that isn't a known name is its poem's opening half; in all of them the recording also starts there, so no poem yet names an excerpt by its first recited line. (2) Arabic books identify a poem by its opening (al-Badi'i's «المقصورة التي أولها», cited for `maqsurat-al-mutanabbi`), so this is the title a reader can look the poem up by in al-Shawqiyyat and al-Abyari. (3) The line is the poem's premise, which the intro explains, and the app shows "23 abyat of 31". (4) «إِنَّ لِلمَوتِ يَداً إِن ضَرَبَت» would name the poem by a half no source uses for it, and a later recording of the whole poem would then need a second title. The card's title and opening bayt differ as a result; the owner may prefer the first recited sadr for excerpts that skip the opening, which would be a new rule for the library rather than a reading of the present one.

- **1.1 «ضَرَبَت»** (second reader). Al-Abyari prints «ضَرِيَتْ» (from ضَرِيَ, of a hunting dog that has tasted the blood of its prey and craves it: «وضَرِيَ الكلْبُ بالصَّيْدِ إِذَا تَطَعَّم بلَحْمِه ودَمِه», *Lisan*, vol. 14, p. 482, `lisan/7606.txt`), "if it grows ravenous"; Dar al-Awda, Kalimat and the encyclopedia print «ضَرَبَتْ». The reciter says ضَرَبَت: the aligner prefers it by 7.8 over the half and 5.4 on the two words, where the same test separates a known ب from ي by 1.9 to 4.2 (14.1 «وَشَرِبنا» −4.2 against «وَشَرِينا», 2.2 «اللَّيثَ» −4.1 against «اللَّبثَ», 10.2 «بِهِ» −3.4 against «بِيِ», 19.1 «أَشَرِبتَ» −1.9 against «أَشَرِيتَ»), and both transcribers hear «ضربت». The text keeps it, with no note, since two of the three prints agree with him.
- **5.2 «نُلقى».** The aligner prefers «نَلقى» over the half (by 5.4) but «نُلقى» on its own two words (by 2.2), where the same test gets «كُنّا» (+1.2) and «نَلتَقي» (+4.1) right. The vowel after the nun (49.20–49.28 s) has F1 about 480 Hz and F2 1,000–1,070 Hz: far below every fatha in this voice and close to the vowel after the nun of 23.2's «أَنُلَقّى» (F1 480–530 Hz, F2 960–1,250 Hz). All three prints have «نُلقى», the passive of ألقى; the active «نَلقى جُثَّةً» would make little sense. The text keeps «نُلقى».
- **9.2 «مُستَرضَيَين».** A coin toss leaning to the active participle (by 2.5); Dar al-Awda and Kalimat vowel it as the passive (a fatha on the ḍad; al-Abyari has a fatha on the first ya only, which both forms take), «when their favour is sought», and al-Abyari's and Dar al-Awda's readings of the bayt need the passive. The vowel after the ḍad (97.00–97.26 s) runs straight into the ya and can't be measured apart from it. The text keeps the prints' vowel.
- **22.2 «ذا».** The aligner romanizes ذ as "th" and scores it badly, and the controls show it: where ذ is certain it loses to لا in the same way (14.2 «بَعدَ ذا» −4.0 against «بَعدَ لا», 6.2 «هَذا» −2.7 against «هَلا», 17.2 «تَذوقُ» −3.8 against «تَلوقُ»), and «دا» scores as well as the best of them every time (here it ties with «لا»). Neither clip transcriber hears «لا» (cohere «مره ان افتراق», audar «مره انف تراق»); the full-recording transcript has «ام لا افتراق» at a chunk boundary. Between the m of «أم» and the ā there is a voiced consonant with no frication (236.52–236.62 s), as this reciter's ذ in «بَعدَ ذا» also has none (149.86–149.92 s). The meter needs a long syllable there, «أم لا» would not parse, and all four witnesses have «ذا». The text keeps it.
- **23.1 «مُتُّ».** After the m (241.34–241.64 s; second saying 252.07–252.41 s) the vowel has F1 390–515 Hz and F2 780–960 Hz in both sayings, the damma of «مُهجَةً» (F2 700–815 Hz), not a kasra. Al-Abyari prints «مِتُّ»; Dar al-Awda and Kalimat leave the mim bare. Both forms are Arabic (*Lisan al-Arab*, vol. 2, p. 91: «وقالوا: مِتَّ تموت»), and «مُتُّ» is the regular one from ماتَ يموتُ. The text follows the reciter, and the note gives al-Abyari's «مِتُّ».
- **23.2 «أَنُلَقّى».** The meter needs four syllables (ʾa-nu-laq-qā: فَعِلاتُن), so Dar al-Awda's «أنلقَى» has lost its shadda, which Kalimat and al-Abyari print. No print vowels the nun: Kalimat has «أَنلقَّى», and al-Abyari «أُنلَقَّى», with a damma set beside the hamza (the same hamza-and-damma as his «وأُودِعْتُ» on the same line, where his «أَمْ» and «أَنْ» carry the hamza alone), the nun bare, a fatha on the lam (it stands over the lam, not the kashida after the nun, second reader's zoom `_scratch/r2/ab-analaqqa4.png`) and a shadda on the qaf. The draft's «أَنَلَقَّى» for al-Abyari misread that fatha. A damma on the hamza gives no form of the verb either; it is likely the nun's damma misplaced, which his passive gloss «أي أنودع» would support. The reciter says a damma: after the nun (245.76–245.88 s; 256.56–256.64 s) the vowel has F1 480–530 Hz and F2 960–1,250 Hz (mostly near 1,050), against 1,360–1,560 Hz for the fathas on either side of it in the same word. «أَنُلَقّى» is the passive of لَقّى («ولَقّاه الشيءَ وأَلقاه إليه», *Lisan*, vol. 15, p. 255; «وما يُلَقّاها», p. 256), and al-Abyari's own gloss, «أنلقى: أي أنودع», reads it as a passive. Since the reciter departs from no print here, the note gives the meaning and Fussilat 41:35 «وما يُلَقّاها», not al-Abyari's slip.
- **23.2 «أَو».** Every print has «أم», the «أم» that after a question's hamza asks which of two (Ibn Hisham, *Awdah al-Masalik*, vol. 3, p. 333, shamela 11825/1004, `awdah.txt`). The reciter says «أَو» in both sayings: both clip transcribers hear «او» both times; the aligner prefers it by 6.2 and 5.1 over the half (the same test prefers «أم» by 4.9 in 19.2 «أَم شَرِبتَ», where he says it); and between the a and the ḥ (248.42–248.74 s; 259.26–259.58 s) there is a vowel-loud glide with F1 about 450 Hz and F2 760–800 Hz, a w, where his m of «أم» in 19.2 (201.62–201.78 s) is a nasal murmur 6–10 dB weaker with F2 about 1,400 Hz. The text follows the reciter; the note gives the editions' «أم».
- **12.2 «وُدُّهُ».** Both transcribers hear «هده». The half opens with voicing from its first frame and F2 650–830 Hz (125.07–125.21 s), a rounded w-u, and «هَدَّهُ/هُدُّهُ الصِّدقُ» means nothing here; the aligner can't tell them apart (−0.3 to +1.8). The text keeps «وُدُّهُ», which Dar al-Awda, al-Abyari and Shawqi Dayf print and which the second half answers («وَوُدُّ النّاسِ»). Kalimat's «وَدُّه» (fatha) is a lexical variant (الوُدّ والوَدّ والوِدّ), not what he says: the vowel after the w (125.10–125.24 s) holds F1 375–450 Hz and F2 730–1,040 Hz, far from this voice's fatha (F2 1,350–1,550 Hz) (second reader).

## The meter

Ramal, its sadr ending فاعِلُن (mahdhuf) and its ajuz فاعِلانْ (maqsur), rawiy ن, a bound rhyme (قافية مقيّدة) with a ya before the nun; al-Abyari: «من الرمل، والقافية من المترادف». Every half was scanned with `media/research/ana-man-mata/tools/scan_ramal.py`, a wrapper of `/home/alifadel/.cache/shw-tmp/b4/tools/scan.py` that reads the rhyme's closing silent nun as the overlong last syllable of the maqsur foot (scan.py itself marks it X on every ajuz): all 46 halves scan. Licences: «أَنا» short at the start of 3.1 and long at its end; the pronoun ha long at the end of 2.1 «عِقبانِهِ», 6.1 «وَصفِهِ», 12.1 «فارَقتُهُ» and 15.1 «يَدِهِ» (the sadr's anceps).

The 23 rhyme words end in a silent ya and a silent nun («الفَرقَدَين», «مَين» …), as the rhyme is said in pause and as the meter needs. `tashkeel.ts` flags each as «two silent letters in a row (ين)», and `content:check` repeats the 23 warnings. They are the rhyme, not slips: written with the case vowel («الفَرقَدَينِ») the ajuz would not scan and would not be what the reciter says.
