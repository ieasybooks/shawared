- **2.2:** «تَقِرُّ» against «تَقَرُّ» is a coin toss; the text keeps al-Daya's vowel.

- **Long vowels the aligner can't hear.** In eight places the transcript hears a long vowel the editions don't have: 6.2 «لارذال», 9.2 «ورداهم», 24.1 «المالك», 25.1 «وانى», 29.2 «لاوضاعها», 35.1 «ورخام», 40.2 «كانزوا», 43.1 «ناكثوا». `variants.py` scores both forms the same in every one it was given (6.2, 9.2, 24.1, 29.2, 35.1, 40.2: gap 0.0). Each falls on the last syllable of a shortened foot (فَعولُ, u - u: li-ʾar-dha, wa-rad-da, nna-kal-ma, wa-ʾan-na, li-ʾaw-ḍa, wa-rakh-kha, fa-qad-ka, wa-qad-na), the very spot where reciters stretch a short syllable, and «كانزوا» and «رخّام» (as verbs) are not words, which shows the stretch is the reciter's. So the text keeps the editions' short vowels everywhere. 29.2 is the closest call: Inan prints «لأوضاعها», and so does Ihsan Abbas in his introduction to Ibn Hazm's *Radd* (probably from García Gómez's text, above), but al-Daya's «لِأَوضَعِها», the meanest, answers «رَفيعَ» in the sadr; the shamela text of al-Daya's first edition and the journal agree with him. Both scan (u-u or u-- in the second foot).
- **Long mim, by the meter:** 8.1 «فيهِمُ», 9.2 «وَرَدَّهُمُ», 20.1 «مِنهُمُ», 31.1 «غَيرُهُمُ» and 35.1 «قِردُهُمُ» scan only with the mim long (-humū). Al-Daya marks it in 8.1, 9.2 and 20.1, the journal in 9.2 and 35.1. Without it 9.2 has ten syllables for eleven.
- **Meter decides:** 1.1 «لِصِنهاجَةٍ» needs its tanwin (al-Daya prints it; so does Ibn Sa'id's editor); 1.2 «النَّدِيِّ», the assembly (al-Daya's gloss), where «النَّدى» breaks the second foot; 8.2 «الخيرَةِ» (khīra), where «الخِيَرَة» would break it.
- **Spelling:** 18.2 «بِئسَ», where al-Daya prints «بِيسَ» and notes the eased hamza: the house style writes the hamza (the sound is the same). 9.1 «يَستاهِلون» keeps al-Daya's eased hamza, which he notes, and which the reciter says.
- **Every half scans** as mutaqarib (فعولن فعولن فعولن فعول, with فعولُ in any of the first three feet), checked with `/tmp/shw/b3/tools/scan.py`'s syllabifier and a mutaqarib foot pattern (`scratch/mscan.py`); the long mims above and the long ā of «إِلَه» (47) are the only places the script needs telling.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Glosses** follow al-Daya's notes (pp. 108–112): النَّدِيّ (1), انتخى (4), قنت and the apes of the Sabbath in 2:65 (6), يستاهلون and «اقتباس قرآني» (9), الأخراج and 9:29 (10), قمّ (11), الأعلام (12), الهُجنة (13), الفراخ (16), استنام and بيس (18), 9:96 (19), the service meant and 3:28 (20), احتللت (26), الخضم والقضم and Abu Dharr's saying (28), الكسا (29), أمناكم (30), أكل المال (31), ناهض (32), the sorcery (33), أسواقها (34), رخّم (35), Ibn al-Naghrila's words on the Quran and Ibn Hazm's refutation (37), the wealth that is the state's (38), the sense of sacrifice (39), العلق (40), عِداهم (41), the dhimma broken (42), نكث (43), خمول (44), the vizier's harshness (45), the burdens Badis bears (46), 5:56 (47); and his notes on Sanhaja, Ibn al-Naghrila, Badis and Granada (pp. 107–108).
- **Other readers of single lines:** Shawqi Dayf on أخراج as pedlars' packs and «ملوّثة» in his prose paraphrase, not a quotation (10, 11); Ihsan Abbas on Abu Hafs al-Zakrami's complaint of a Jewish tax collector in Denia (*Tarikh al-Adab*, pp. 146–147, used in 23 to qualify the poet's claim); the verse «متى بلغ البنيان يوماً تمامه … إذا كنت تبنيه وغيرك يهدم», which al-Khatib al-Baghdadi gives to Salih ibn Abd al-Quddus (*al-Faqih wa-l-Mutafaqqih*, vol. 2, p. 189, shamela 13048/1036, `faqih.txt`; most books quote it «متى يبلغ») (17); Ihsan Abbas on the praise meant to win Badis's trust (25), the taxes going to the treasury (28, p. 146), the vizier's luxury (35), the treatise on the Quran (37) and the weight of the fatwa (42); al-Maqqari on the katib being the vizier (3).
- **Added here:** the Quranic phrases in 5 («وهم لا يشعرون»), 9 (95:5), 18 (43:38), 20.2 (2:159), 21 (16:15), 22 (2:65), 37 (2:156), 46 (74:38) and 47 (Inan's «المفلحون», 58:22); the lexical sense of القرون in 15 (the horn blown as a trumpet: «ضرب بالبوق»; the hadith «الصور قرن يُنفخ فيه»). Bayt 15.2 is the least certain line in the poem: al-Daya gives no note, and the sharh offers the reading that fits the argument (their doings are trumpeted through the land).

## History (intro, notes and sharh)

The poem is hostile to the Jews of Granada; the intro and sharh present it as a historical document, report the poet's charges as his, and draw the facts from these sources.

- **Badis, Samuel and Joseph:** al-Daya, pp. 107–108; Ibn al-Khatib, *al-Ihata*, vol. 1, pp. 238–244 (shamela 11682/270–276, `ihata.txt`), who quotes Ibn Idhari's *al-Bayan al-Mughrib* and Ibn Hayyan; Inan, vol. 2, pp. 133–137. Badis's death in 465 AH is al-Daya's (p. 108).
- **The exile:** Ibn Sa'id, *al-Mughrib*, vol. 2, p. 132: «وكان ينكر على ملكها كونه استوزر ابن نغرلة اليهودي وعلى أهل غرناطة انقيادهم له فسعى في نفيه إلى إلبيرة فقال شعره المشهور» (who «سعى» is, he doesn't say); al-Daya, p. 17 (`daya-017.png`): «ويرجح أنه قالها في المدّة التي نفي فيها عن إلبيرة؛ نفاه الأمير باديس بتحريض من وزيره ابن النغريلة», and he thinks the exile came after Buluggin's death (his «عن إلبيرة», from Elvira, sits oddly with the others' «إلى إلبيرة»; Elvira was also the name of the province whose capital was Granada, so the intro gives only the destination the others name); Inan, vol. 2, p. 135 n. 2: «سعى به الوزير يوسف بن نغرالة … لدى سلطانه باديس، فأبعده عن غرناطة فسكن إلبيرة».
- **The rising:** Abd Allah ibn Buluggin, *Kitab al-Tibyan* (Lévi-Provençal's text, reprinted by Maktabat al-Thaqafa al-Diniyya, Cairo), pp. 69–73 (archive.org `yoooooofs_gmail_20180127_1212`, leaves 69–73, read as images): Joseph's dealings with Ibn Sumadih of Almería; «يوم السبت لعشر خلون من صفر [من سنة ٤٥٩]»; a drunken slave's cry «المظفّر قد غدره اليهودي، وهذا ابن صمادح داخل في البلدة»; Joseph killed in the palace; «وأحالوا السيف على كل يهودي بالبلدة، وحصلوا على عظائم من أموالهم». His account does not mention al-Ilbiri or the poem. The CE date is Inan's (vol. 2, p. 137); the tabular calendar puts that Saturday on 9 Safar.
- **The number killed:** Ibn Bassam, *al-Dhakhira*, vol. 2, pp. 768–769 (shamela 1035/761–762, `dhakhira.txt`): «وقتل منهم يومئذ نيف على أربعة آلاف». He too puts the rising down to the plot with Ibn Sumadih and does not name al-Ilbiri. Inan gives three or four thousand, Dayf about four thousand, Rubiera Mata three thousand.
- **The poem's part in it, as the intro sets it out:**
  - Ibn Sa'id (al-Hijari): «فاشتهر هذا الشعر وثارت صنهاجة على اليهود فقتلوه»; al-Maqqari: «فثارت إذ ذاك صنهاجة على اليهود، وقتلوا منهم مقتلة عظيمة، وفيهم الوزير المذكور».
  - Ibn Idhari, quoted by Ibn al-Khatib (*al-Ihata*, vol. 1, p. 244): after Buluggin's death and Joseph's killing of a rival, «واستهدف للناس فشغلت به ألسنتهم، وملئت غيظاً عليه صدورهم، وذاعت قصيدة الزاهد أبي إسحاق الإلبيري، في الإغراء بهم، واتفق أن أغارت على غرناطة بعوث صمادحية تقول إنها باستدعائه»; then «فقتلوه … وصلبوه على باب مدينة غرناطة، وقتل من اليهود في يومه، مقتلة عظيمة، ونهبت دورهم». The same passage has «واستعمال اليهود على الأعمال» (27).
  - The Tibyan and Ibn Bassam, above: no mention of the poem.
  - Emilio García Gómez, in al-Daya's quotation (p. 18, `daya-018.png`) of his study of the poet as translated by al-Tahir Ahmad Makki (p. 118 of the translation): «وبقصيدته التي تشتعل توهجاً ضدّ اليهود، والتي استطاع بها أن يلهب غرناطة». I did not see his book (*Un alfaquí español: Abū Isḥāq de Elvira*, Madrid–Granada, 1944) or the translation.
  - García Gómez again, as María Jesús Rubiera Mata reports him in the Real Academia de la Historia's *Diccionario Biográfico Español*, «Abū Isḥāq de Elvira» (read online, `rah-rubiera.md`): «la casida no fue sino el detonante de algo que estaba en el ambiente y que la matanza de los judíos se habría producido igualmente». The same entry says some historians hold the poem caused the massacre.
  - Inan: «وكانت كالشرارة التي أضرمت الحريق»; Ihsan Abbas: «قصيدته التي ساعدت على الثورة». In his introduction to Ibn Hazm's *Radd* (vol. 3, pp. 18–19) Abbas judges that the treatise against the Quran was most likely Joseph's, that it lay at the root of the rising together with his other offences, and that «ما كانت الصيحة التحريضية التي أطلقها أبو اسحاق الألبيري إلا تتويجاً لذلك التفاعل»; on p. 15: «وكان لهذه القصيدة أثر في تحريك النفوس وازدياد الغليان». Not in the intro (it already gives the range of views); used in the sharh of 37.
  - Afif Ben Abdesselem, *La vie littéraire dans l'Espagne musulmane sous les Mulūk al-Ṭawā'if* (Ifpo), ch. X, read online through a summary, not the page: he holds that the poem contributed to the outbreak, dates it before Buluggin's death because it does not mention it, and notes that Abd Allah's memoirs and Ibn Bassam are silent on the poem and the poet. Not cited in the poem's text.

## Review

A second reader checked every half against al-Daya's page images (pp. 108–112) and his 1981 text, scanned every half as mutaqarib, rechecked the cut, the timeline and the verify.py differences, and checked the intro, sharh, translation and notes against al-Daya's notes, Ibn Sa'id, al-Maqqari, Ibn al-Khatib, Ibn Bassam, the Tibyan's page images, Inan, Ihsan Abbas, Shawqi Dayf and Rubiera Mata (2026-11-17). The text, its vowels and the audio stand; fixed after the review:

- **Intro:** the CE date no longer reads as the Tibyan's (it is Inan's conversion); the poet's objection to the vizier credited to Ibn Sa'id, and the exile given only as the others name it («إلى إلبيرة»), since al-Daya writes «عن إلبيرة»; Ibn Idhari said to list the poem's spread among the events before the rising, not to count it a cause; García Gómez's view given whole, as Rubiera Mata reports it (the massacre would have happened anyway); the poem's denial that killing them is a breach of faith added to the summary; Ibn Bassam's «نيف على أربعة آلاف» quoted as he has it.
