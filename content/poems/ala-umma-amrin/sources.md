# ألا أم عمرو أجمعت فاستقلت

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari gives 36 bayts in one pass, «ألا أم عمرو» through «في مسرّتي». Of the original 293.883 s, 1.80–293.02 s is retained. The departure of Umm Amr, the red bows, the provisions, the encounters recalled by name and the poet's final profession all occur in that order. The 72 addresses contain no second take. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nRwJ2Kabukk)

## Text, occasion and commentary

- Al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, *al-Mufaddaliyyat*, ed. Ahmad Muhammad Shakir and Abd al-Salam Muhammad Harun, Dar al-Ma'arif, no. 20, pp. 108–112. This is the 36-bayt base for the count and sequence. A circulating 38-bayt transcription adds «تراها أمام الحيّ حين تشايحوا» and «فإن تقبلوا تقبل بمن نيل منهم». Neither occurs here or in the performance. The web text supplied a search lead. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/82) · [Description of the expedition](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/85) · [Closing section](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/86)
- C. J. Lyall, ed., *The Mufaḍḍalīyāt*, vol. I, Arabic text with the commentary of Abu Muhammad al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Bashshar al-Anbari, XX, pp. 194–207. The biographical and tribal reports begin on p. 194. The Arabic edition numbers 34 bayts. Our 21 appears in the apparatus after its 20 on p. 204 and our 33 in the apparatus after its final bayt on p. 207. The p. 207 apparatus gives witness V’s «أتتني إذا بين العمودين حُمّتي» and records «إذا جاءني» for Bm in the same half. Cairo and the reciter have «إذن جاءني». The running text and these additions were checked against separate scanned pages. [Traditions and opening](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n740/mode/1up) · [First additional bayt](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n730/mode/1up) · [Final apparatus](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n727/mode/1up)
- The two original explanations draw on al-Anbari's account of «بعيني» and Umayma's conduct (pp. 200–202), the different glosses of «جُنّت» (p. 202), the walks, the rationing of provisions and the recognition of Ta'abbata Sharran behind «أم عيال» (pp. 203–205), and the feud and Dhu al-Burayqayn (pp. 205–207). At our 22 «إن لم تُبيّت» is particularly uncertain. The Arabic gloss allows for coming home from an expedition. Lyall's discussion also gives the band's dependence on the leader's decision to stop for the night. The two senses are recorded in the sharh. Lyall's English vol. II, XX, pp. 68–73, was consulted alongside the Arabic. The displayed English halves are new translations. [The address to Umayma](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n734/mode/1up) · [Food keeper and quiver](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n730/mode/1up) · [Lyall's English notes, p. 72](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/n109/mode/1up)
- Abd Allah al-Tayyib, *al-Murshid ila fahm ash'ar al-'Arab*, 3:368–370, reads the opening through the poet's restrained grief and his recollections of a woman's daily conduct. Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 5:85, gives Amr ibn Malik, records disagreement about the name and lineage, and recounts his running, his status among the outlaws, his death at the hands of Salaman and its approximate date. [Al-Tayyib](https://shamela.ws/book/150936/1284) · [Biographical entry](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4214)

The two scholarly texts were collated separately. At 5.2 the scan on p. 200 has passive third-person feminine «ذُكِرَتْ». The brief turn from addressing her to describing her is retained. The Cairo electronic surrogate misplaces a final vowel here. On the same page al-Anbari derives تقلّت from القلاء, aversion. His comment on 9 (p. 201) explains that she cuts her own speech short. At 27 the movement belongs to calves whisking their tails as they see their mothers (p. 205), and at 31 he identifies Abd Allah and Awf as men of Salaman ibn Mufrij (p. 206). At 19.2 the old printed gloss gives «أوتحت» and explains the scant measure, while a word space in the digital Cairo text splits the verb. At 23.1 both continuous recognisers give «منها», also transcribed without objection in the half-sized check. Both printed witnesses give «فيها». The reciter's preposition admits a partitive sense and has a paired word note. At 34.2 the witnesses divide twice. Cairo gives «شفاني ... غدوتي». Lyall prints «شفائي ... عدوتي» and records «شفاني» in the commentary. The recording combines the finite verb with «عدوتي», my run. ## Metre

Ṭawīl with the rhyme consonant tāʾ. The opening scans `u–– | u––– | u–– | u–u–` in both halves. In the first, ʿAmr has genitive tanwīn and its distinguishing written wāw is silent. At 18 («أمشّي على أين الغزاة») the two patterns are `u–– | u––– | u–u | u–u–` and `u–u | u––– | u–– | u–u–`. Here u is short and – is long.

## Acoustic readings and verification

Each comparison retains the same word count and uses the aligned half with 0.30 s of context on each side. Scores are summed CTC log likelihoods. Positive differences favour the alternative.

The scores use the MMS model and romanisation of the repository’s variants tool. The alternatives include readings in the editions, spellings produced by the recognisers and tests of pronunciation. A difference under two points has little discriminating value. The last column gives the reading used in the verse.

At 2.2 the text has أظلّت, which al-Anbari connects with the camel necks rising over those left behind. The two continuous recognisers divide between ظ and ض. Their competing word scores are less than one point apart. At 4.1 the recording securely gives the lament وا كبدا. An initial f is uncertain, as detailed below. At 5.2 an independent phone pass gives dhukirat followed by the w of ولا. The final t is followed directly by the conjunction. This agrees with Lyall’s third-person feminine. The stress moves from the direct address to what is said of the woman. At 7.2 the two ways of writing the neighbour or neighbours receive identical model scores. The singular has both continuous transcripts and the two main verse texts. At 12 the passive أُكمِلَت brings the description of her form to a close. At 16 the rare phrase أنشأت سُربتي means that he set his company on the road.

At 23.1 both continuous recognisers, the isolated Cohere half and the independent phone string m-i-n-h-a support منها, against فيها in the two printed texts. At 25.2 the copies, both continuous transcripts and the long vowel in the independent phone pass support رامت even though the two scores are equal. At 33.2 the same pass has an n after the dh and vowel in إذن. This is the result of the preceding supposition beginning with لو. For 34.2, the finite verb شفاني and the noun عدوتي each have the support of both continuous transcripts and of the extracted half. Lyall prints the noun and gives the verb in his commentary. His note on our 34.1 also takes لا تعُدني as a request to forgo a sick visit, which explains the cure in the second half. At 36.2 تنتحي, to incline or direct oneself, is the reading of the printed text and both continuous transcripts. The CTC model gives the same value to the version with plain h. ## Half-by-half adjudication

The release-timing Cohere pass covered 72 halves. It flagged 11, comprising 16 of 367 words under the repository’s normalisation. The table includes distinct readings from the preliminary cuts as well. Widened Audar transcriptions are shown where available. The lead’s timing review extended 4 half endings to preserve their voiced tails.

| 4.1 | فواك بداع لا اميمه بعد ما | وكبد على أميمة بعدما | The isolated Cohere clip begins with f. Continuous Cohere has أوا, continuous Audar joins وا to كبدا, and the widened clip loses the opening. The phone recogniser recovers wā kabidā but gives no reliable segment before wā. Cairo, Lyall at p. 200 and al-Tayyib support the printed connective before the cry for Umayma. Its occurrence in this take remains uncertain. |
| 4.2 | قمعت فهبها نعمه العيش زلتي | ومع تفاهبها نعمة العيش زلة | The full transcripts give طمعت. The verb recalls the speaker's hope before her departure. Neither the q of قمعت nor the unparsed first sound of the wide clip yields that construction. |
| 9.1 | كان لها في الارض نسيا تخصهم | كأن لها في الأرض نسيا تقصه | The widened clip restores تقصّه. She looks as though she were following the trail of some lost article. The object is singular. |
| 9.2 | علي امها وان تكلم كتبلتها | على أمها وإن تكلمك تبلت | The wider strip correctly separates the addressee's k in تكلّمك from the uncommon verb تبلت. The latter describes stopping or cutting one's speech short. |
| 12.2 | فلو جن انسان من الحسن جنه | فلو جن إنسان من الحسن جنة | Every half in this section rhymes in t. At the end of this particular bayt the finite feminine جُنّت completes the conditional sentence. A spelling with ة would instead make a noun. |
| 15.2 | ومن يغزو يغنم مره ويسمت | ومن يغزو يغنم مرة ويشمت | The widened clip restores the sh sound in يُشمّت. The two possible fortunes of the expedition are gaining booty and giving one's enemies cause for satisfaction. |
| 21.1 | وماء بها ضن بما في وعايها | وما إن بها ظن بما في وعائها | Widening recovers the initial negative expression وما إن بها. The apparent ظ in the wide clip does not express the intended miserliness. ضِنّ, hoarding, supplies the term explicitly denied in this added Cairo bayt. |
| 22.2 | ولا ترتزي للبيت ان لم تبيت | ولا ترتجى للبيت إن لم تبيتي | Widening restores تُرتجى and the final تبيّت. The difficulty lies in what the keeper or band can expect at night. The alternative interpretations are both given with the verse. |
| 27.1 | تراها كادني بالحسيل صوادرا | تراها كأذناب الحسيل صوادرا | The wider recognition recovers كأذناب الحسيل, “like calves' tails”. The comparison is explicitly explained on p. 205. The narrow clip moves two consonants across a word boundary. |
| 31.2 | وعوف لدي المعدي اوانستها التي | وعوف لدى المعدة أوانستهلت | Both clips run أوان استهلّت together and one supplies a separate article. The temporal noun introduces the moment when the fighting rose in clamour. The classical gloss also allows for a cloud of dust rising. |
| 34.1 | الا لا تعدني ان تسكيت خلتي | ألا لا تعدني إن تسكيت خلتي | Both clip recognisers miss the sh in تشكّيت. The printed verb means complaining of one's condition. Each continuous recitation gives this same conditional before the announced remedy. |
