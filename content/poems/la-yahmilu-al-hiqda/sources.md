## What the attribution rests on

- *Diwan Antar*, fourth printing (Beirut, 1893), pp. 11–12. The printed heading says «وقال يتوعد النعمان بن المنذر ملك العرب ويفتخر بقومه». All nineteen performed verses are present. The order agrees through 6. The print puts «إن الأفاعي» before «اليوم تعلم», the recording after it. For «غمار الحرب» (9) the print has «غبار الحرب». For «تحدو بهم» (16) it has «تعدو بهم». The contrast between the former camel-herd and defender of the tribe, the taunt about his colour and the name of Abs all belong to the received speaking persona. The heading supplies the proposed occasion. [Beirut scan](https://archive.org/details/diwan-antarah-ibn-shaddad)
- Ahmad al-Hashimi, *Jawahir al-adab*, 2, pp. 263–264, uses a closely related heading, gives eighteen verses, omits our 2, puts 4 before 3 and 8 before 7. It too has «غبار» and «تعدو». Abd Allah al-Tayyib, *al-Murshid ila fahm ash'ar al-Arab*, 4, pp. 785–786, reproduces a sixteen-verse selection. His hybrid «إن كنت تعلم يا نعمان أي فتى» is the start of our 6 joined to the question in 7. The snakes and the horses descended from A'waj are absent. He reads «غمار» and glosses the sword's striking edges, the check on the horses, the riders' spoils and the breast-strap. These books document reception and explain much of the imagery. [Al-Hashimi](https://shamela.ws/book/21505/490) · [Al-Tayyib](https://shamela.ws/book/897/3498)
- *Sharh Diwan Antara ibn Shaddad*, corrected by Amin Sa'id (Cairo, al-Maktaba al-Tijariyya al-Kubra), pp. 10–12. The preface on pp. 3–4 describes the publisher's commission to correct a reprint. Seventeen abyat follow the threat to al-Nu'man. As in al-Tayyib, our 6 and 7 form a single verse and our 8 is absent. This edition also keeps our 16, «تعدو بهم أعوجيّات مضمّرة». At 9 it reads «غمار الحرب» and explains the word through the extremity of battle. At 17 it has «حتى يصيح السرج واللبب». The note on 13 divides the dead man's flesh, bones and equipment among the recipients as the reciter does. The shared splice in 6–7 helps identify a shorter printed tradition. Sa'id's reprint provides no manuscript date for the poem. [Preface](https://archive.org/details/ar120vieux101/page/n3/mode/1up) · [Text and commentary, pp. 10–12](https://archive.org/details/ar120vieux101/page/n10/mode/2up)
- Muhammad Sa'id Mawlawi, ed., *Diwan Antara, tahqiq wa-dirasa* (al-Maktab al-Islami), title page and pp. 363–364. The edition is based on six manuscripts. Its contents distinguish al-A'lam al-Shantamari's commentary, al-Batalyawsi's additions and the supplementary poems. The full rhyme index on p. 364 lists the bāʾ pieces in these groups. It has no entry for «لا يحمل الحقد» or for a bāʾ poem opening with this line's second half. This is specific negative evidence about a manuscript-based corpus. [Mawlawi scan, rhyme index](https://archive.org/details/3562pdf/page/n369/mode/1up)
- Mishil Salim Kumayd, «الشك في الشعر الجاهلي», *Lughat al-Arab*, 6, pp. 564–574 (signature on p. 574), quotes our 19 in full, «والنقع يوم طراد الخيل يشهد لي / والضرب والطعن والأقلام والكتب», and sets beside it «الخيل والليل والبيداء تشهد لي / والسيف والرمح والقرطاس والقلم». On pp. 568–569 Kumayd quotes al-Mutanabbi with **تشهد لي**. Ibn Hisham's *Sharh Shudhur al-dhahab*, p. 20, quotes Abu al-Tayyib with **تعرفني** and the same «والسيف والرمح والقرطاس والقلم». The parallel holds even with the different verb. Both poems call on the battlefield, then give writing materials a place among the witnesses. The critic also argues from a general denial of literacy among pre-Islamic Arabs. The verbal parallel and the limits of the manuscript corpus suffice to cast doubt on this attribution independently of that historical generalisation. They establish neither the author of the later text nor a date for all nineteen lines. The catalogue retains Antarah as the traditional attribution and both introductions state the uncertainty. [Signed conclusion, p. 574](https://shamela.ws/book/36540/3448) · [Journal, p. 568](https://shamela.ws/book/36540/3442) · [p. 569](https://shamela.ws/book/36540/3443) · [Ibn Hisham](https://shamela.ws/book/6969/7)

The full count of nineteen comes from the Beirut printing. The transcriptions of the print, Sa'id’s commentary, the two anthologies and the output of `scripts/collate.py` are under `media/research/la-yahmilu-al-hiqda`. In the electronic *Jawahir*, «يومض معركة» and «ولمن لا نيوب» are evident transcription errors. The sharh treats the self-description as a speech framed by the poem. The speaker's skin becomes the ancestry visible on the day of battle (5), and at 13 four recipients divide the field. By 19 the witnesses have acquired pens and books.

## Metre

Basit. In the opening sadr «لَا يَحْمِلُلْ / حِقْدَ مَنْ / تَعْلُو بِهِرْ / رُتَبُو» scans مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. The first foot of the answering half «وَلَا يَنَا» is مخبون, متفعلن. At 9 «فَتَنْ يَخُو / ضُ غِمَا / رَلْحَرْبِ مُبْ / تَسِمَا» scans متفعلن فَعِلُن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. The second half, «وَيَنْثَنِي / وَسِنَا / نُرْرُمْحِ مُخْ / تَضِبُو», has the same feet. The rawiy is bāʾ, with a long ū in cadence. The plural endings in عتبوا and نكبوا rhyme with the singular noun endings. At 13 the article of «وللوحش» crosses the metrical break.

## Verification of the flagged halves

The narrow pass flagged eleven of the 38 halves. Each widened reading below is Audar's transcription of an independent cut. The cards also preserve what the studio intended readers to see. They are particularly valuable here because the chant lengthens short vowels inside words.

| Half | Widened transcription | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | قد كنت فيما مضى أرعا جملا هم | Widening rejoins فيما, the word originally split. The possessive in جمالهم refers to the people whose animals he used to tend. The print and card agree. |
| 5.1 | لئن يعيبوا سواد فه وليناسب | In the card the words are «سوادي فهو لي نسب». Widening still swallows the boundaries between the possessive, the pronoun and the following preposition. The second half repeats نسب explicitly. |
| 6.1 | إن كنت تعلم يا نعمان أنا يدي | Both readings stretch the vowel of أنَّ. The clause «أنّ يدي قصيرة عنك» has subject and predicate across the break. The card and the Beirut text supply that construction. |
| 7.1 | اليوم تعلم يا نعمان أيافا | The short clip loses the end of فتى. The studio spells out «أيّ فتى» and أنْ/أنَّ from 6 would give the wrong syntax here. The youth who meets the king's brother is described in 9. |
| 9.2 | وينثني واسنان الرمح مختاضب | The transcriber has split the onset of «وسِنان» as though it were أسنان and lengthened the internal vowel of مُختضب. The singular spearhead is the thing dyed. |
| 13.1 | لينفوس وللطير اللحوم وللوحد | The edge falls inside وللوحش. The narrow recogniser supplied الأرض, the broad recogniser الوحد. The complete source and paired card give «وللوحش العظام». |
| 13.2 | للوحش العظام وللخيالة السالب | The broad onset overlaps 13.1 and supplies the article. The noun is السَّلَب, the arms and belongings taken from the dead, explained by al-Tayyib. The performance lengthens its vowel. |
| 15.2 | إلا الأسنات والهندية القوضب | The lexical pair is الأسنّة and القضب, spearheads and cutting swords. Connected reading makes the tāʾ of the first noun audible. Long internal vowels occur repeatedly in this chant. Both are clear on the card and in the two complete printed passages. |
| 16.1 | تحدو بهم أعوجية مضمرة | Both engines return a singular in the fresh 158.3–195.8 s source clip. The card explicitly shows alif and tāʾ in «أعوجيّات», the form in both written witnesses, and «تحدو» as in the full audio. The plural can take the singular feminine adjective مضمرة. See the scored comparison below. |
| 16.2 | مثل السراحيين في أعناقها القابب | The widened reading restores final nūn in السراحين, wolves, and extends the short vowels of القَبَب. The written witnesses and card have the anatomical term for leanness. |
| 17.2 | طعن بطبع حتى يضج السرج واللابب | The context restores the final tack term «واللَّبَب», paired with the saddle. Al-Tayyib identifies the strap across the mount's breast. |
