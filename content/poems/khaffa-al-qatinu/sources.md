# خف القطين · Al-Akhtal before Abd al-Malik

## Recording, sequence and breaks

Osama al-Nahari's upload of 6 August 2024 runs 901.867 s. Keep **7.63–898.50** of the source, a continuous 890.87 s. The first words are «خف القطين» at about 8.13 s. The last are «بطن الراحة الشعر», with the last voiced sound at about 897.98 s. The title's «+ الشرح» refers to writing on screen. For example, at source 10, 303, 608 and 691 s the verse and the explanation occupy separate parts of the slide. Following both full-recording transcriptions from the opening to the close produces 84 bayts in succession. Neither the explanations nor their headings interrupt the verse. ## Text and commentary

- **Antun Salhani**, *Diwan al-Akhtal*, Beirut, Imprimerie Catholique, 1891, pp. 98–112. The title page identifies the St Petersburg manuscript. The transmission names al-Yazidi, al-Sukkari, Muhammad ibn Habib and Ibn al-A'rabi. All 84 bayts occur once in the recording, in this Diwan's order. The footnotes distinguish manuscript readings, quotations and geographical glosses. See especially the caravan on pp. 98–101, the Euphrates on pp. 101–102, Umayr's head and the two Ghassanid groups on p. 106, and the drinking and milking vocabulary on pp. 110–112. [Title page](https://archive.org/details/aktal1891/page/n2/mode/2up) · [Opening](https://archive.org/details/aktal1891/page/n123/mode/2up) · [Umayr](https://archive.org/details/aktal1891/page/n131/mode/2up) · [End](https://archive.org/details/aktal1891/page/n137/mode/2up)
- **Iliya Salim al-Hawi**, *Sharh Diwan al-Akhtal*, Beirut, Dar al-Thaqafa, 1968, pp. 160–162 on the poem, pp. 163–179 for its complete text and running commentary. He explains the sealed, pitch-coated jar and its «خرطوم» at 2–4, the image of the slackening bow at 10, the ploughshare as the one exception to the parched greenery at 12, the river carried over four bayts before its comparison is completed at 25, and the guest's welcome sarcastically addressed to the dead at 55. The explanations here follow the syntax from one bayt into the next and translate the two halves separately. His plant imagery at 35–36, watering-place terminology at 62 and 71, and livestock at 78–82 inform the word notes. [Introduction and opening](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-aktal/page/n160/mode/2up) · [River](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-aktal/page/n168/mode/2up) · [Ghassan and Qays](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-aktal/page/n174/mode/2up) · [Final invective](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-aktal/page/n178/mode/2up)
- **Naqa'id Jarir wa-l-Akhtal**, attributed on the title page to Abu Tammam, ed. Salhani, Beirut, Catholic Press, 1922, poem XLVI, pp. 148–165. Its manuscript is the Istanbul copy. Its 85 numbered bayts include an additional no. 26 «لا يطعم النوم إلا ريث يبعثه … هم الملوك وجد هابه الحجر», which intervenes after our 25 and is absent from the recording. The note on p. 152 says the line is absent in the St Petersburg and Leiden copies. This witness puts our 21 before 20, 30 before 29, the warning against Zufar (our 48–50) before the defence of the Umayyads (45–47), and the head of Umayr (51–55) at its 66–70. The complete counterpoem-to-recording concordance is given below. Its scholia distinguish the valley's flank at 15, the lion's posture at 29, the two senses of the water imagery at 71 and the actual tail-hair of a camel at 61. [Title](https://archive.org/details/na9aid-jarir-akhtal/page/n1/mode/2up) · [Opening and count](https://archive.org/details/na9aid-jarir-akhtal/page/n150/mode/2up) · [Extra bayt](https://archive.org/details/na9aid-jarir-akhtal/page/n154/mode/2up) · [Later order](https://archive.org/details/na9aid-jarir-akhtal/page/n163/mode/2up) · [End](https://archive.org/details/na9aid-jarir-akhtal/page/n167/mode/2up)
- Al-Marzubani's *al-Muwashshah*, pp. 187–188, transmits the caliph's objection to hearing «منك» addressed to him and the reported recasting «اليوم». It also transmits his objection when the poet claims a share in his victory (51). This is evidence for the reception of the poem. Al-Nahari recites «منك» and «بنا». Al-Jawhari, *al-Sihah*, s.v. صبر, p. 707, and Ibn Manzur, *Lisan al-Arab*, s.v. صبر, cite 55 for الصُّبر and الحَزن, two Ghassanid groups. The Arabic and English headwords use al-Subr. ## Metre and rhyme

The basit is audible across word boundaries. The opening sadr divides as «خَفْفَلْقَطِي | نُ فَرا | حُو مِنْكَ أَوْ | بَكَرُو», giving مستفعلن، فَعِلُن، مستفعلن، فَعِلُن. At 43.1 «هُمُلْلَذِي | نَ يُبا | رُونَرْرِيا | حَ إِذا» has متفعلن in the first place and the same shortened second and fourth feet. The rhyme consonant is **ر**, heard in غِيَرُ، جَدَرُ، المَدَرُ and الشَّعَرُ. The sustained final vowel is its release.

## Word readings on the audio

Each comparison uses the same word interval on the edited recording, with 0.30 s on each side. The MMS model, romanisation and CTC scoring are those of `scripts/variants.py`. The batch used a short inference window with one second of context padding. A less negative log score fits the acoustic frames better within that row. Scores from different rows have different baselines. A gap below two offers little guidance. The model often favours ز over ذ, and it is a poor judge of the length of a chanted vowel.

P. 98 has 1–3, p. 99 has 4–9, p. 100 has 10–16, p. 101 has 17–23, p. 102 has 24–27, p. 103 has 28–33, p. 104 has 34–41, p. 105 has 42–51, p. 106 has 52–55, p. 107 has 56–61, p. 108 has 62–65, p. 109 has 66–73, p. 110 has 74–76, p. 111 has 77–80 and p. 112 has 81–84. For 43.2 his p. 105 glosses العافين as people coming to ask for food and the final verb as tightening their spending from poverty. Both predicates bear on the people asking. [P. 105, commentary on the petitioners](https://archive.org/details/aktal1891/page/n130/mode/2up)

The recording’s مِنك at 1.1 is particularly clear against the celebrated substitution اليوم. In the caravan passage, the eye’s «تسفحه» in 13.1, the severed rope in 14.1 and البصر at 14.2 can all be distinguished from the alternatives. Al-Hawi gives الوطر at 14.2 but its sense here would shift attention from following the group with the eye to desire. At 18.1 Salhani has تعرّينا and al-Hawi تعدّينا. At 18.2 the final vowel of فليهنئ is less secure: the continuous Cohere suggests i, Audar is indistinct, the MMS margin is under one and the old prints vary in their writing of the hamza. At 21.2 the close-up of Salhani p. 101 and the 1968 Diwan both show the t in يغترّه. XLVI 20 rearranges the clause to «في عهده بعد توكيد له غرر».

At 23.1 the word test favours the zay of XLVI 23. Both continuous transcriptions recover the doubled ذ of the Diwan’s ذعذعته, an especially difficult distinction for this MMS romanisation. The water is ruffled by the wind in either version. At 26.2 follow يسروا and the imagery of gaming arrows over XLVI’s جزروا. At 27 the two complete voice passes give غَيرِنا as does the 299 s slide, where the Diwans and XLVI give دوننا. XLVI 28 supplies the first half’s conditional فمَن يَكُن against the Diwan’s فلم يكن. At 30 both full voices, isolated Cohere and the 331 s slide give مُقَدِّمٌ … لِمَنزِلَةٍ, with an army riding out to a position. XLVI 30, p. 153, gives لمنزلة. The two Diwan prints point مُقَدِّماً … لِمَنزِلِهِ. The test of لِمِريَةٍ came from a preliminary misreading of the scan. At 31.1, the small score advantage of يعلو (XLVI 34) is outweighed by both full transcriptions and the Diwan’s يغشى القناطر. At 34 the recitation makes the caliph «والمستقلّ بأثقال العراق», the man who shoulders Iraq’s burdens. The two Diwan texts begin ثم استقل and XLVI 35 begins حتى استقل.

At 55.1, the isolated vowel in الصُّبر is a weak call. The 594 s slide identifies the named Ghassanids and the lexicons cited above supply their vocalisation. At 62.2 the word scorer produces the identical score for تعيّا and تعايا. The clipped and continuous transcriptions support the shorter تعيّا. At 65.1 the opening connective فأصبحت is heard by both continuous passes, with a small MMS margin over وأصبحت. In 68.1 the singular ساع gives the runner who fails to catch the poet’s people. The preposition is مِنهم in Cohere and in XLVI, فيهم in the two Diwan prints. At 71 the twice-negated لا وِرد ولا صَدَر, “neither going down to water nor coming back”, is clear in both continuous decodes. XLVI’s إيراد is a different verbal noun.

At 75.1 the two source transcriptions have تَناهَتْ … كلّ فاحشة and at 75.2 وكلّ مخزية. The slide’s wording and glosses agree. Salhani p. 110 and al-Hawi p. 178 have أنابت and place مخزية before فاحشة. At 79.1 Cohere and the word test suggest تمضي. Continuous Audar has تمذي. The Diwan p. 111 and al-Hawi p. 179 gloss what the goats secrete when they grow hot. Retain تمذي, with the dental uncertain in the recording. At 80 the recording gives the feminine تفضل and the same consonant shows on the slide at 857 s. The Diwan prints يفضل. At 83.2 the slide at 887 s, continuous Cohere and MMS all give ما تستحم, “do not bathe”. The Diwan has ما يستحين, about the women’s sense of shame, and XLVI 84 has لا يستحين. At 84.1 the opening particle is قد on the recording, also recorded in Salhani’s margin on p. 112.
