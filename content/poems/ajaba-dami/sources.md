# أجاب دمعي وما الداعي سوى طلل

## Recording, occasion and verse order

Osama al-Nahari recites 48 bayts of al-Mutanabbi’s poem of reconciliation with Sayf al-Dawla. The sound of the first verse begins at about 6.22 s on the 468.915 s source, the last rhyme at «الأجل» dying away at about 459.82 s. The original-clock keep of 5.72–460.26 s yields 454.54 s. Both complete transcriptions begin with the tears answering the ruined camp and finish with the prayer for victory and long life. All 96 halves occur once. The order of 44 and 45, first «وما ثناك كلام الناس» and then «أنت الجواد», is audible in both full transcriptions and agrees with Ibn al-Iflili. Al-Wahidi has those two bayts in reverse order. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDlgQlAIFQ0) · [Ibn al-Iflili, II, pp. 81–82](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/310) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 248](https://shamela.ws/book/754/248)

The metre is basit. The opening sadr divides «أَجابَ دَمْـ | عِي وَمَدْ | داعي سِوى | طَلَلِي», giving مُتَفْعِلُن فاعلن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. The medial «خُذْ ما تَرا | هُ وَدَعْ | شَيْئَنْ سَمِعْـ | تَ بِهِي» gives مستفعلن فَعِلُن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. In that opening «الدّاعي» at 1.1.4 occupies 2.74–3.48 s on the edited clock. The rawiy is lam with final i, carried by «الإبل», «العذل» and the last «الأجل». The article which straddles bayt 18 is also divided in the early commentary.

## Editions used

- Ibn al-Iflili, *Sharh shi'r al-Mutanabbi*, ed. Mustafa Ulayyan, Mu'assasat al-Risala, 1992, II, pp. 62–83. The heading relates a return to the prince after nineteen nights, the robes of honour, the perfume and the further gifts. It dates this recitation to Sha'ban 341 AH, a month spanning December 952 and January 953 CE. This edition supplies all 48 bayts in the recorded order. It explains the answer of the camels in the opening, identifies the apparently unresponsive companion at the lovers’ meeting as the sword, and distinguishes the sword in the prince’s hand from Sayf al-Dawla, “Sword of the Dynasty”. Its explanation of each petition in 40 is particularly helpful when dividing the fourteen short verbs between the two English halves. [II, p. 62, occasion](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/291) · [Pp. 67–68, the sword](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/296) · [P. 75, the two swords](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/304) · [P. 80, the petitions](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/309) · [P. 83, close](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/312)
- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, ed. Friedrich Dieterici, *Carmina cum commentario Wâhidii*, Berlin, Mittler, 1861, pp. 487–495. The printed numbering skips 38. No. 37 («وعرّفاهم») ends p. 492, and p. 493 continues its gloss before «يا أيها المحسن», numbered 39. Those are consecutive bayts 37 and 38 in Ibn al-Iflili and in the recording. The Berlin imperatives are consequently no. 41, and «لا زلت تضرب» is numbered 49. All 48 bayts are present. Berlin places «أنت الجواد» before «وما ثناك». The numbering here follows Ibn al-Iflili. - The same commentary in the paginated Shamela text, pp. 245–248. The heading connects the apology to al-Mutanabbi’s earlier poem in mim. The commentary explains the compound of fear and hope in the opening, the two possible owners of the eyes in 8, the genealogy in 21–23 and the play on his patron’s title. Its report beneath the imperatives describes the prince answering the requests one by one. The additional lines and exercises in compressing imperatives after the closing prayer belong to the commentary’s account of the occasion. They form no part of the 48-bayt recording. The electronic texts sometimes corrupt a lemma while preserving its explanation. At 16 Ibn al-Iflili’s digital «فزاتها» is followed by an explanation of how the sword adorned the gifts. At 17 his prose explains taking up and carrying the sword even though the electronic lemma has «بجمله». At 40 the commentary expands the third command as «وأقطع المنازل والضياع», granting houses and estates, where the lemma drops the qaf. Al-Wahidi independently preserves these expressions. The same distinction matters at 24, where a whole noun has dropped from Ibn al-Iflili’s electronic verse. ## Readings

At 1.1 Ibn al-Iflili and the Berlin al-Wahidi both print the performed «وما الداعي». The electronic al-Wahidi drops the waw. At 3.2 the recording has «كانت», with the tear as subject, leading al-Wahidi’s first-person «كنتُ» by 12.04. Ibn al-Iflili and the original caption have «كانت». Al-Wahidi also records it as a variant and explains that the tear flowed even when only a curtain separated the lovers. At 6.1 Ibn al-Iflili gives «ممّن أراقبه», referring to the watchers. The two continuous passes agree. The score leads «ممّا» by only 1.57, but the personal reading makes sense with the men guarding the beloved in 5. At 9.2 the reciter and al-Wahidi have «في مشيها», which locates the imitation in her gait. The score leads Ibn al-Iflili’s «حسنها» by 15.32. [Berlin, p. 488, al-Wahidi on «كانت»](https://archive.org/download/carminacumcommen00muta/carminacumcommen00muta.pdf#page=413) · [II, pp. 64–66](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/293) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 245](https://shamela.ws/book/754/245) · [Recording at 32 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDlgQlAIFQ0&t=32s)

At 11.2 the full Cohere pass repeats «بدني» from the first half. Audar and the free phones b-a-d-a-l-i support the two editions’ «بدلي», in the young man who takes his place. The margin is 2.40. At 13.1 «ندفّعه» and al-Wahidi’s «ندافعه» score equally. Both full passes spell it without an alif after dal, and Ibn al-Iflili supplies the short form. At 16.1 both editions print «في مواهبه». Both full passes, the caption at 156 s and the original m-i-n support «من مواهبه», ahead by 5.70. Its card gives both prepositions. At 18.2 Ibn al-Iflili prints a damma on the dental in «الذُّبُل», describing the dry, slender spear shafts that move when shaken. The caption at 176.6 s gives the two rounded vowels and glosses the word as «الرماح الضامرة». Free phones register two rounded vowels here, though they misidentify the medial and final consonants. The comparison favours the rounded first vowel over a fatha by 1.22. [II, pp. 67–70](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/296) · [II, p. 70, bayt 18](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/299) · [Recording at 176 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDlgQlAIFQ0&t=176s)

At 24.2 the Berlin and electronic al-Wahidi both give «في طلعة الشمس ما يغنيك عن زحل». Ibn al-Iflili’s electronic lemma reads «في طلعة ما يغنيك» but his prose twice names the sun as the object that outshines Saturn. Both full transcriptions instead give «البدر». The video caption at 230 s prints the full moon and tells the viewer that the sun is the more familiar reading. The original phones give b-a-d-r and the full moon leads the sun by 28.84. The text, translation and paired card preserve this feature of the performance. At 25.1 both continuous passes, the pictured verse and Ibn al-Iflili give «مكان القول», ahead of al-Wahidi’s «مجال» by 6.77. [Ibn al-Iflili, II, p. 73](https://shamela.ws/book/37065/302) · [Berlin, p. 490, «الشمس»](https://archive.org/download/carminacumcommen00muta/carminacumcommen00muta.pdf#page=411) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 246](https://shamela.ws/book/754/246) · [Recording at 230 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDlgQlAIFQ0&t=230s)

At 28.2 both complete passes and al-Wahidi give «الخَلق», contrasting the make and work of the two swords. «الخَلق» leads «الفضل» by 14.20. Ibn al-Iflili’s electronic lemma has «الفضل», but the following prose explicitly contrasts the creation and the acts of the prince with the creation and the acts of the sword. At 30.1 the sandgrouse fly «مع» the Arabs, ahead of al-Wahidi’s «من» by 10.79. At 31.2 both full passes confirm «تمشي», ahead of «تمسي» by 5.58. They also preserve «عنها» in 32, referring to the fortified place, ahead of the electronic «عنهما» by 22.16. At 33.1 «فكلّما» leads al-Wahidi’s «وكلّما» by only 1.22, and has support from Ibn al-Iflili and both full passes. At 39.1 «فوق معرفتي» leads «بعد معرفتي» by 17.08. His knowledge of the prince serves as his pillow. On printed p. 493 al-Wahidi gives «فوق» in the verse and attributes «بعد» to Ibn Jinni. At 42.1 both full passes repeat the negative in «ولا سمعت ولا غيري». Berlin prints that reading on p. 494, no. 43. Ibn al-Iflili and the electronic al-Wahidi open «وما سمعت». The recorded «ولا» leads «وما» by 7.52. ## Final clip decisions

The narrow Cohere pass flags thirteen of the 96 halves. The widened Audar windows and the independent original-clock word windows resolve the following points.

| Half | Narrow Cohere | Widened Audar | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.1 | ضللت | ضلل تبين | Ibn al-Iflili prints and glosses «ظللت», continuing to wipe his tears, paired with «وظلّ يسفح» in the ajuz. The full Cohere text agrees. The score favours it by 2.65. The free onset is unclear, and the isolated transcriptions alone cannot distinguish the two emphatics. |
| 3.1 | ولا تشكن | أشكو النوى | The wider window restores the whole half. The noun «النوى», separation, completes the complaint, and the two commentaries explain the companions’ surprise at his tears. |
| 6.2 | وأنا الغريق | أنا الغريق | The wider and both complete transcriptions begin directly with the pronoun. The image of a man already drowning answers the previous comparison without another conjunction. |
| 12.2 | غير عزة | غير عزهات | The printed «عِزهاة» is a person indifferent to women. Its t is audible in liaison before «ولا غزل». The night visitor’s companion, a sword, is neither cold nor amorous. The longer lexical form leads «عزة» by 2.39. |
| 17.1 | معرفة | معرفتي | The wide and both continuous passes preserve the possessive. The poet credits the prince for his own knowledge of how to wear the sword, a phrase completed by «بحمله» across the break. |
| 17.2 | أو كعليم | أو كعلي | The wider and both full passes recover the second name. Abd Allah and his son Ali repeat the two names in 17.1. The m begins «معطي» in 18.1. |
| 18.1 | السلاه ب وال | السلاحب وال | The full passes give «السلاهب», the long-bodied horses, and both cuts also register the end article. Ibn al-Iflili prints «والْ» before the break. The noun «بيض» begins the second half. |
| 18.2 | No words returned | بض القواضب والعسالة الذبر | The wider cut recovers the two classes of gift. The full Cohere pass, the pictured verse and Ibn al-Iflili identify the keen white blades and the slender spears. The final consonant of «الذبل» is lam in the continuous Cohere text and throughout the poem’s rhyme. |
| 37.2 | الطروف … والخواليق | الطرف … والخوالي | Full Cohere and both witnesses have «الطَّرْف», his gaze, and «الخَوَل», the attendants. The free phones at 354.79–355.77 s give w-a-l-kh-a-w-a-l-i, including the audible rhyme vowel. The two last-word scores differ by only 0.60. |
| 38.1 | موسوعة النابلسي للعلوم الإسلامية | يا أيها المحسن المشكور من جهتي | The wide pass retrieves the complete address. The continuous passes also preserve the address, with a shortened possessive in their spelling of «جهتي». The unrelated proper name in the narrow pass has no counterpart in the source text or the adjacent halves. |
| 40.2 | زدها الشبشه فض الأدنس الرصل | زيد هالشبة فضل أدنى سر صلي | The decoders join several very short imperatives. The original gives z-i-d, h-a-sh-sh and b-a-sh in succession. Ibn al-Iflili quotes the seven words and comments on each in order. The remaining petitions are also distinguishable in the full Audar pass. |
| 42.2 | الأعراف الدقيقة أذب منك… | منك لزور القول عن رجل | The narrow cut invents an opening phrase and the wide cut drops «أذبّ». Both full transcripts give the complete half, and the free original phones give a-ð-a-b-b at 398.29–398.87 s. The comparative means more ready to defend someone against calumny. |
| 46.1 | ثرس | ثرس | The original onset at 434.11 s is affricated (transcribed ts). The scores for «فرس» and the clipped «ثرس» are only 0.57 apart. The full Cohere pass recognises the horse. Both clips preserve the masculine verb «يطأ» with it, while al-Wahidi gives «تطأ». The forced verb scores give the feminine only a 2.07 advantage, and Ibn al-Iflili prints the masculine. Both commentaries identify its hooves treading armour and fallen bodies. The word card retains the horse and describes the anomalous consonant. |
