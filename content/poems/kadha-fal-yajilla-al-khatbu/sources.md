## Title

No name is in use for this poem. A shamela search finds «رائية أبي تمام» twice, neither for this poem (Abdallah al-Tayyib, *al-Murshid*, vol. 4, p. 601, for the poem on al-Afshin; a grammar handbook's generic example), and «مرثية محمد بن حميد» only as a description (al-Isfara'ini, *al-Atwal*, vol. 2, p. 496; al-Dasuqi's *Hashiya*, vol. 4, p. 236). Ibn Ammar's «أمّ قلائده» (below) is a judgement, not a name. So the title is the first sadr.

## Attribution

- **Abu Tammam's, in every source:** both recensions of the diwan (al-Tibrizi's and al-Suli's, below); Ibn Dawud al-Zahiri (d. 297), *al-Zahra*, pp. 157–158 (shamela 738/157–158, `zahra.txt`), 20 abyat, «أنشد أحمد بن أبي طاهر عن أبي تمام»; al-Suli, *Akhbar Abi Tammam*, ed. Khalil Mahmud Asakir, Muhammad Abduh Azzam and Nazir al-Islam al-Hindi (Cairo: Lajnat al-Ta'lif wa-l-Tarjama wa-l-Nashr), pp. 124–125 (archive.org `0119pdf_201912`, leaves 147–148, read from the images), where Abu Dulaf quotes six abyat as «ما رثيت به محمد بن حميد»; al-Jarawi, *al-Hamasa al-Maghribiyya*, vol. 2, pp. 856–858 (shamela 6902/798–800, `jarawi.txt`), 18 abyat, «وقال أبو تمام حبيب بن أوس الطائي»; Ibn al-Athir, *al-Kamil* (below): «فرثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، منهم الطائي». Added in the second reading: his contemporary al-Jahiz (d. 255), *al-Bayan wa-l-Tabyin*, vol. 3, p. 297 (shamela 10614/848, `bayan.txt`), «وقال الطائي:» before our 6 and 7; al-Amidi, *al-Muwazana*, vol. 3, p. 523 (shamela 26183/1455, `muwazana-3.txt`), «قال أبوتمام:» before our 7–11; Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-A'yan*, vol. 2, p. 14 (shamela 1000/486, `wafayat.txt`), Abu Dulaf's words on «قصيدتك الرائية التي أولها: كذا فليجل الخطب».
- **Disputed in part, and the claim answered:** Di'bil said that Abu Tammam took lines from a long elegy by Abu Sulma al-Muzani, a descendant of Zuhayr named «مكنف» (the *Akhbar*, p. 200, prints «مَكنفٌ أبو سُلمى»; «المزني» is Ibn Ammar's; the English intro gives only the kunya), on Dhufafa al-Absi. Two chains report it (and al-Amidi, *al-Muwazana*, vol. 1, pp. 72–73, `muwazana-1.txt`, gives the same lines from Di'bil, recited to Abu Muhammad al-Yazidi):
  - al-Suli, *Akhbar*, pp. 199–201 (leaves 222–224, read from the images; shamela 515/27–28, which numbers its pages itself), from Muhammad ibn Musa ibn Hammad, who saw Di'bil bring out the notebook in 235: «سرق أبو تمام أكثر هذه القصيدة، فأدخلها في شعره». The lines Di'bil showed include our 14.1 with «بني القعقاع», our 2.1 with «بعد وفاته» and our 2.2;
  - Ibn Ammar's epistle on Abu Tammam's errors, quoted by Ibn al-Mustawfi in Nu'man's notes to al-Suli (vol. 3, pp. 293–294, from MS ن): the same story from Di'bil through Abu Bakr ibn Abi Khaythama and Muhammad ibn Musa ibn Hammad, with our 2, 3, 14 and 15 among the lines (15.2 with «المجد» for «الجود»). Azzam summarizes it in his note on al-Tibrizi, vol. 4, p. 85.
  - The answer, in the same place in the *Akhbar* (p. 201): al-Hasan ibn Wahb knew Maknaf's poem and had heard Abu Tammam recite it, «وما في قصيدته شيء مما في قصيدة أبي تمام، ولكن دعبلاً خلط القصيدتين، إذ كانتا في وزن واحد، وكانتا مرثيتين، ليكذب على أبي تمام». Ibn al-Mustawfi calls Ibn Ammar's epistle full of «الميل على أبي تمام والغضّ منه» (Nu'man, p. 294).
  - The intro gives the claim and the answer, the answer with its «ليكذب على أبي تمام» (Di'bil merged the poems on purpose, not by mistake); the text stays Abu Tammam's, as in every source.
- **al-Aghani** was not read (it isn't on shamela's current site); the *Akhbar*'s editors cite it for the same two stories (vol. 15, pp. 102–107). Abu Dulaf's is also in Ibn Khallikan (above); Di'bil's in al-Amidi (above) and in al-Marzubani, *al-Muwashshah*, pp. 408–409 (shamela 9427/408–409, `muwashshah.txt`), whose «سرق أبو تمام أكثر هذه القصيدة» al-Marzubani glosses as the poem on Muhammad ibn Humayd.

Besides the score, both clip transcripts hear fa («يَفِضْ», «يفد»). The note gives al-Suli's reading.
- **1.1 «فَليَجِلَّ»:** the comment that kasra is better (al-Tibrizi p. 79, unattributed there; given to Abu al-Ala' by Ibn al-Mustawfi, Nu'man p. 294) is about the verb's last lam, not the lam of command: the lam of command after fa is sukun in both prints, and fatha is not among its vowels, while the doubled last letter of a jussive, before a silent letter (here the article), takes «وجهان فقط: الفتح والكسر» when the verb's first letter has fatha or kasra (al-Hamalawi, *Shadha al-Arf*, p. 143, shamela 11243/159, `shadha.txt`). - **Meter decides 2.2 «السَّفَرِ السَّفرُ»:** the first is safar (travel), the second safr (travellers). With «السَّفرِ» the half's last two feet would be − − − − −; with «السَّفَرِ» they are فعولُ مفاعيلن. Both prints leave the first fa unmarked; al-Jarawi and the *Akhbar* (p. 201, in Maknaf's version) print «السَّفَرِ».
- **16.2 «اُستُشهِدا»:** passive, so the alif of wasl takes damma.

## Meter

Tawil, al-Tibrizi's «الأول من الطويل»: the first darb, ending مفاعيلن (فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن / … فعولن مفاعيلن). `python3 -I /home/alifadel/.cache/shw-tmp/b4/tools/scan.py content/poems/kadha-fal-yajilla-al-khatbu/poem.json -v` scans all 56 halves with no flag; I scanned 1, 2.2, 16 and 18.1 by hand as well. Every ajuz ends مفاعيلن, and every sadr مفاعلن (the obligatory qabd of the first 'arud) except the opening's, which rhymes with its ajuz (tasri') and ends مفاعيلن; فعولُ for فعولن (qabd) occurs often, in the first or the third foot. 18 and 19.2 need the light «وَهوَ», «فَهيَ», as the prints have them.

## Commentary drawn on

- **The commentaries are thin on this poem.** The sharh follows what they say where they say it:
  - **al-Tibrizi and Abu al-Ala'** (al-Tibrizi, pp. 79–83; Ibn al-Mustawfi in Nu'man): 1 (the vowel of «فليجلّ»), 9 (the parallels «ولو أنهم فرّوا …», al-Mazini, «وكالسيف …»; a harsh nature praised only against enemies), 17 (the two parsings of «كبراً»), 18 (some of the horses; his reading «جمالها»), 19 (المآثير, the two senses of بتر, al-Kawthar 3). The line «ولو أنهم فرّوا لكانوا أعزّة / ولكن رأوا صبراً على الموت أكرما», «قول الآخر» to Abu al-Ala', is «امرأة من كندة في أخوتها» in *al-Zahra*, p. 158, right after this poem, and «أم الصريح الكندية» in al-Amidi, vol. 3, p. 524: hence «امرأة من كندة» in the sharh of 9.
  - **al-Suli** (Nu'man): 1 (his answer to the critics, with Al Imran 21: «وقد جرت البشارة في كلام العرب بما يسوء»), 5 (الثغر), 7 («فكأن النصر عليهم على يد غيره هو له», now in the sharh), 13 («الكلام: وهي خضر من سندس»), 14 (his defence, in the edition and at length in the *Akhbar*, pp. 125ff.: «إن ذهب البدر منهم فقد بقيت فيهم كواكب»; the poet ranks him above them, where al-Khuraymi's line makes them equal; Jarir's elegy for al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, p. 300: «أمسى بنوه وقد جلّت مصيبتهم / مثلَ النجوم هوى من بينها القمر», «أفترى أن جريراً أراد أن يهجو الوليد»), 19 (as Azzam quotes him from MS م: «يريد أنه ليس أحد يفعل فعله»).
  - **al-Amidi**, *al-Muwazana* (second reading): 7–11 «وهذا غاية في حسنه وحلاوته وإنما أخذه من قول عبد يغوث: ولو شئت نجتني من الخيل نهدة … ولكنني أحمي ذمار أبيكم …», then the Kinda woman's line, «وأبيات أبي تمام أجود» (vol. 3, pp. 523–524; the sharh of 9 and 11); 14 taken from Maryam bint Tariq's «كنا كأنجم ليلٍ بينها قمرٌ … فهوى من بينها القمر» or from Jarir's line (vol. 1, p. 72; the sharh of 14). Al-Suli gives nearly the same line as Maryam's to Safiyya al-Bahiliyya (p. 300: «فهذا كلام أبي تمام ومعناه بعينه»).
  - **Ibn Ammar on 26** (Nu'man, p. 293; Azzam, p. 84 n. 3): after «بقعة» he says Ibn Abi Hakim «استعار … هذا المعنى» in an elegy on Abdallah ibn Tahir, «بلفظه وحسنه» in Nu'man's print, «بلفظة حسنة» in Azzam's quotation; the sharh says only that he took the meaning in good words.
  - **Abu Yahya, al-Kharzanji and Ibn al-Mustawfi** (al-Mubarak ibn Ahmad), as Nu'man quotes Ibn al-Mustawfi from MS ن: 7 (al-Kharzanji's martyr; Abu Yahya's «صبر على الطعن والضرب …», which Ibn al-Mustawfi prefers), 8 (Abu Yahya: the notched sword and broken spears, «فهو إذن يعذر»), 9 (Abu Yahya: «لو شاء نجا …»), 13 (Abu Yahya: «فلما قتل ألبس لباس الشهداء في الجنة»), 15 (Abu Yahya: no successor in any virtue; Ibn al-Mustawfi: الشعر is what is said about him), 16 (al-Kharzanji and Abu Yahya, quoted), 17 (Abu Yahya: «ولم يكن ذلك من ذُلّ»), 18 (al-Kharzanji's and Abu Yahya's readings, not used), 21 (Abu Yahya: «كان اصلاً فجدّ فلا فرع له», as printed), 27 (Abu Yahya: «وقد كان الثرى يثرى به ويندى لكثرة عطائه، وكان غالباً على صرف الدهر بنائله فيغمره به»).
  - **Ibn Ammar's objections** (Nu'man, pp. 292–293; Azzam, pp. 79, 80, 84): the opening (1), «انثغر» for «اثّغر» (5), 11 («ليس هذا من ألفاظ العرب ولا أوصاف الشعراء»), «بقعة» (26). The critics of 1 and 14 are reported by al-Suli.
  - **13 as tadbij:** al-Khatib al-Qazwini, *al-Idah* (with al-Sa'idi's *Bughyat al-Idah*), vol. 4, p. 577 (shamela 10281/569), among the forms of tibaq; Ibn Abd al-Haqq al-Umari, *Durar al-Fara'id al-Mustahsana*, p. 429 (shamela 147320/395): «قصد بالأول الكناية عن: القتل، وبالثاني الكناية عن: دخول الجنة» (after Ibn Abi al-Isba', *Tahrir al-Tahbir*). The Quran reference in the note on 13.2.6 is al-Insan 21.
  - **6:** no commentator explains it. The sharh gives the likelier reading (the enemy tribes weep for the men he killed while his fame shines) and the other in a clause; the note on «ضحكت عنه» follows Ahmad al-Hashimi, *Jawahir al-Balagha*, p. 281 n. 6 (shamela 9256/250): «شبه السرور والأريحية بالضحك».
- **Where the commentators are silent** (2, 3, 4, 6, 10, 11, 12, 20, 22–26, 28), the sharh gives the plain sense of the words and says so where it chooses between readings (2, 6, 23, 24, 25); 11 now gives al-Amidi's judgement beside Ibn Ammar's. The badi' the sharh points out without a commentator naming it is plain in the words: jinas in 2 (السَّفَر/السَّفر) and 27 (ثوى/الثرى, يغمر/الغمر), the repeated key word in 3, tibaq in 6, 12, 20 and 22, the grammar of 10 («نفسٌ» a third subject of «ردّه»).
- **23:** «we» read as Tayyi', the poet's own tribe, after 22, where Tayyi' wears the loss and other tribes share it; the commentators don't say. Al-Suli's print «ما تنفكّ تفقد … تشاركنا» can take Tayyi' (feminine) as the subject, or be second person; the sharh says only that it may, and no longer renders it as naming Tayyi'.
- **2:** the travellers «في شغل عن السفر» because no one is left to travel to, or because his loss keeps them from it; both in the Arabic sharh, the first in the English.

## History and people

- **Muhammad ibn Humayd al-Tusi:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 6, p. 110 (shamela 12286/5288, `alam-tusi.txt`): «محمد بن حميد الطاهري الطوسي: وال، من قواد جيش المأمون العباسي. ولاه قتال (زريق) و (بابك الخرمي)»; (… – 214 AH = … – 829 CE); «وكمن له جماعة من أصحاب بابك، فخرجوا عليه، فصمد لهم، فضربوا فرسه بمزراق فسقط إلى الأرض، فأكبوا عليه فقتلوه. وكان شجاعا ممدوحا جوادا، رثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، وعظم مقتله على المأمون». The note on 2.1.4 and the intro's dates are al-A'lam's.
- **The campaign and the battle:** al-Tabari, *Tarikh*, vol. 8, p. 619 (212 AH: al-Ma'mun sends him against Babak by way of Mosul) and p. 622 (214 AH: «مقتل محمد بن حميد الطوسي، قتله بابك بهشتادسر، يوم السبت لخمس ليال بقين من شهر ربيع الأول، ورفض عسكره») (shamela 9783/4965–4968, `tabari.txt`). Ibn al-Athir, *al-Kamil*, ed. Tadmuri, vol. 5, pp. 560–561 (shamela 21712/2925–2926, `kamil.txt`): the mountain passes, the camp at Hashtadsar, Babak's ambushers «تحت كل صخرة», the rout, «وصبر محمد بن حميد مكانه، وفرّ من كان معه غير رجل واحد، وسارا يطلبان الخلاص، فرأى جماعة وقتالاً، فقصدهم، فرأى الخرمية يقاتلون طائفة من أصحابه، فحين رآه الخرمية قصدوه لما رأوا من حسن هيئته، فقاتلهم وقاتلوه، وضربوا فرسه بمزراق», his death; «وكان محمد ممدحا جوادا، فرثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، منهم الطائي». The intro says no more than these two say; the second reading restored «يطلبان الخلاص», which the draft had left out, so that he no longer seems to ride out to fight.
- **Abu Dulaf's words:** al-Suli, *Akhbar*, pp. 124–125 (the chain: al-Hazanbal from Sa'id ibn Jabir al-Karkhi from his father, who was present), read again from the page images (leaves 147–148 are printed pages 124–125, and leaves 222–224 printed pages 199–201, so the *Akhbar* pages cited here are right): «وددتُ والله أنها لكَ فيَّ! فقال: بل أفدي الأميرَ بنفسي وأهلي، وأكونُ المقدَّمَ قبلَه، فقال له: لم يمُتْ من رُثي بمثل هذا الشعر». Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's version, «لودِدْتُ أنَّها قيلت فيَّ» (*Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 14, p. 432, shamela 23644/6790), is how the English renders the first words. «العجلي» is Ibn Khallikan's (above); «الأمير» the *Akhbar*'s.
- **Ibn Ammar's «أمّ قلائده»:** Nu'man, al-Suli vol. 3, p. 292 n. 2, read from the page image: «واستدلّوا على ذلك بمرثيته محمد بن حُميد التي هي امّ قلائده، والتي شهره الناس بها»: Ibn Ammar's own words, in his account of the scholars who held that what is admired in Abu Tammam is other men's. It is a description, not a name, so the intro has «ووصفها … بأنها «أمّ قلائده»».
- **Muhammad ibn Humayd's tribe:** the poem (14, 22) and al-Tibrizi's heading «الطائي»; al-Suli's MS ر «محمد بن حُميد الطائي»; his father is «حميد الطوسي الطائي» in al-Khatib, *Tarikh Baghdad*, ed. Bashshar, vol. 1, p. 398 (shamela 736/108, `tarikh-baghdad.txt`, «منارة حميد الطوسي الطائي»), and his brother «أصرم بن حميد الطوسي الطائي» in al-Safadi, *al-Wafi*, vol. 9, p. 167 (shamela 6677/2276, `wafi-asram.txt`). That the family is of Nabhan rests on the poem.
- **Peoples and names:** Banu Nabhan (14.1.2-3): «نبهان بن عمرو بن الغوث بن طيئ», Ibn Sa'd, *al-Tabaqat al-Kubra* (al-mutammim), vol. 2, p. 637 (shamela 12416/557); that they are the dead man's people is the poem's (14, 22) and al-Tibrizi's heading «الطائي». Tayyi' (22.1.5): Lisan, vol. 1, p. 116, «أبو قبيلة من اليمن، وهو طيئ بن أدد»; the poet's tribe (his poet page: «حبيب بن أوس الطائي»). Tamim and Bakr: Ibn Hazm, *Jamharat Ansab al-Arab*, p. 207 («بنو تميم بن مرّ بن أدّ») and p. 307 («بنو بكر بن وائل بن قاسط») (shamela 9793/207, 9793/304).
- **The poet** is the existing `abu-tammam`; nothing about him was changed.

- **Text:** no word or vowel changed. Recorded with their scores: al-Jahiz's and al-Amidi's 7.1 «الطعن والضرب», al-Amidi's 10.1 «كأنما» and 11.2 «من دون», al-Nuwayri's 5.2 «واثّغر», and al-Suli's 8.1 «مِضرَب», 25.2 «بأَسقائها» and 28.1 «فأَنّني», the last two misprints the draft didn't list; the و some clips hear before 13.1 (the end of 12.2's rhyme) and the tanwin of 22.1 settled by clip transcripts; 1.1's comment shown to be about the last lam.
