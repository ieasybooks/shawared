## Witnesses and commentary

- Nu'man Muhammad Amin Taha, *Diwan al-Hutay'a bi-riwayat wa-sharh Ibn al-Sikkit*, no. 6, printed pp. 63–68. The edition's own account distinguishes the transmissions. Its first two bayts come from Khalid ibn Kulthum. Abu Amr and Ya'qub ibn al-Sikkit did not transmit them in this position, and Ya'qub begins «وإن التي نكبتها». The p. 64 gloss explicitly identifies the camels as the subject of «سِرنَ». Abu Amr understood the feminine pronoun in 3–4 as the she-camel the poet steered away. Al-Asma'i understood it as the praise poem he took to a different household. Taha gives both explanations. The Arabic and English reader layers follow their implications through the arrival at Al Shammas. [pp. 63–64](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n87/mode/2up) · [pp. 65–66](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n89/mode/2up) · [pp. 67–68](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n91/mode/2up)
- Al-Sukkari's *Diwan al-Hutay'a*, printed pp. 19–21, supplies a complete second text of seventeen bayts. The p. 19 print has «بعد ما هجمت هند» and «وقد سرن خمساً واتلأبّ بنا نجد». The same page inserts a crossing on a tossing ship after our 2, «وهند أتى من دونها ذو غوارب». After our 10 comes «وإن غاب عن لأي بغيض كفتهم / نواشئ لم تطرر شواربهم بعد». Both are absent from the recording. The parallel edition takes the feminine in 3 as «المدحة». At our 5 its p. 20 main text has «صدورهم». Taha's p. 65 apparatus credits «رماحهم» to al-Sukkari. The distinction matters because the two words give different objects of enmity. [Al-Sukkari, opening on p. 19](https://archive.org/details/3315pdf_202001/page/n20/mode/1up) · [pp. 20–21](https://archive.org/details/3315pdf_202001/page/n21/mode/2up) · [Taha, p. 65](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n89/mode/1up)
- Al-Mubarrad, *al-Kamil*, 2/137–140 in the linked edition, supplies the rival-host narrative. Al-Zibriqan ibn Badr directs al-Hutay'a to his household. Baghid's relatives draw him to their own establishment. The poet's resulting praise and the dispute with al-Zibriqan belong to this account. Al-Mubarrad quotes our 3–10 and 15 and explains the guarded honour, help in a crisis and common idioms of the praise. Taha's glosses on the perennial well at 4, honour and forbearance at 6–10 and the alleged damage to the addressees' hide at 11 underpin the original explanations. The profile follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 2/118, including the approximate date of death. [Narrative, 2/137](https://shamela.ws/book/8505/652) · [Verses and glosses, 2/138–139](https://shamela.ws/book/8505/653) · [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/1279)

## Readings

At 1.2 both continuous recognisers and both local transcriptions hear the sound «سِرنا غورا». The word scores tie this with «سِرنَ غورا». A long note on the end of the shortened syllable is possible here, and the old commentary explicitly understands the camels to be walking. The paired reading keeps «سِرنَ». The parallel «خمساً» would replace the terrain with a number of nights. At 5.1 the recited «صدورهم» has a clear advantage over «رماحهم». At 6.2 «الحفيظة والجدّ» couples jealous care for one's honour with determination. Al-Sukkari prints «والحدّ». His 9.1 has «النعمى عليهم» against the recorded «النعماء فيهم». At 11.2 the voice describes an occasion «موطن» on which the family might have failed their critics. Al-Sukkari gives «معظم». [Taha, pp. 63–65](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n88/mode/2up) · [Al-Sukkari, pp. 19–20](https://archive.org/details/3315pdf_202001/page/n20/mode/2up)

At 7.2 the last held vowel can sound as though it ends in h. Audar similarly writes a final ه on «ودّوا»، «شدّوا» and «كدّوا» elsewhere in this performance. Running Cohere, the studio card, Taha, al-Sukkari and al-Mubarrad have «سدّوا». The preceding «المكان الذي» allows an understood object. A pronounced pronominal suffix would extend this dal-and-waw rhyme by a further consonant. The word note records the ambiguity. At 12.1 the reciter uses «مغاوير أبطال مطاعيم في الدجى», with generous hosts feeding people even in darkness. Al-Sukkari's «مطاعين في الهيجا مكاشيف للدجى» places the emphasis on daring in battle. At 13 and 15 the voice addresses the «أفناء» of Sa'd, its different branches, where al-Sukkari gives «أبناء». At 14.2 «الجدّ» means earnest effort. The «الجهد» of the parallel is a genuine lexical alternative. [Taha, pp. 66–68](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n91/mode/2up) · [Al-Sukkari, p. 21](https://archive.org/details/3315pdf_202001/page/n22/mode/1up)

The name in 4.1 is «شمّاسِ بنِ لأيٍ». The genealogical connector loses its initial alif and vowel in connected speech between the two names. This accounts for the house checker’s initial-vowel notice on «بنِ». [Taha, p. 64](https://archive.org/details/3313pdf_202001/page/n88/mode/1up)

## Metre and rhyme

Tawil, with د as rawiy. Najd, Hind and Sa'd supply nominal rhyme words with a sustained final u. The plural verbs give corresponding endings in -dū. The opening sadr rhymes with its ajuz and has the full مفاعيلن. At 8.1 the sadr ends in مفاعلن and the ajuz again has مفاعيلن. The slashes give the audible foot boundaries.
