# له الويل من ليل تطاول آخره

## Recording and historical occasion

Osama al-Nahari recites 45 bayts, the whole of Hasan Kamil al-Sayrafi’s poem 347, in order. He begins «له الويل» and ends «بين السماطين شاعره». No verse is repeated. The master retains the single interval 0.58–371.76 s of the original. The first measured voicing is at 1.08 s. The sustained last cadence reaches 371.38 s and a lighter tail is visible to 371.65 s. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X6hEtzk9xUM)

Al-Tabari places the capture in the events of 237 AH (851–852 CE). He calls Bagrat son of Ashot «بطريق البطارقة» and says that Bagrat came seeking authority, that Yusuf ibn Muhammad took him and sent him in irons to the caliph, and that other lords afterwards swore to kill Yusuf. Step'anos Taronetsi likewise records the capture of Bagrat, prince of Taron, and his removal to Ja'far before Bugha’s subsequent campaign. Step'anos credits Abu Sa'id. In note 251 his translator Tim Greenwood follows Thomas Artsruni in identifying the officer as Abu Sa'id’s son Yusuf, appointed in his father's place in 851. The poem names Yusuf in 4 and calls him Abu Ya'qub in 11. Its charges against Bagrat, especially its fifty years of hostility in 21, are the panegyrist’s account. [Al-Tabari, *Tarikh*, IX, pp. 187–188](https://shamela.ws/book/9783/5195) · [Greenwood, *The Universal History of Step'anos Taroneci*, p. 174, n. 251](https://arak29.org/muratsan-site/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2021/12/The-universal-history-of-Step%CA%BBanos-Taronec%CA%BBi.pdf#page=191)

## Printed witnesses and close reading

- Hasan Kamil al-Sayrafi, ed., *Diwan al-Buhturi*, Dar al-Ma'arif, no. 347, pp. 876–881. Page 876 has the heading «يمدح يوسف بن محمد» and the first four verses. Its apparatus discusses the attribution to Yusuf or to his father, the two opening pronouns and the alternative «بطاء أواخره». Pages 877–878 give 5–23, explaining the young wild cattle in 6, Taron in 18 and Jurzan in 23. Page 879 gives 24–31, including «تندى أظافره», and identifies Arran. Page 880 gives 32–42 and records that its 39 is missing in the Beirut edition it cites. Page 881 closes with 43–45 and explains the kinship and the taking of a tenth. [Opening and apparatus](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n940/mode/1up) · [Printed p. 879](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n943/mode/1up)
- *Diwan al-Buhturi*, first printing supervised and corrected by Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi, Cairo, 1329 AH (1911 CE), II, pp. 11–13. The scan begins with the title leaf of I. The numbering starts again in II, where this poem follows the preceding poem on p. 11 and ends before a new panegyric on p. 13. This witness too has all 45. It gives «له الويل من ليل بطاء أواخره», «الوصل القديم», «الملك الموهوب», «أسلمته جرائره», «على الخوف ناظره», «تندى أظافره», «وأحكمت», «المنصور», «وإن يك» and «محكمات مرائره». These are different configurations of the same poem. [Opening, printed p. 11](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n246/mode/1up) · [Printed pp. 12–13](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n247/mode/1up)
- Al-Amidi, *al-Muwazana bayna shi'r Abi Tammam wa-l-Buhturi*, I, p. 531, quotes 3–4 and calls the unexpected turn to praise *istitrad*. Usually the patron resembles a rain cloud. Here the poet wants rain to imitate Yusuf. Al-Amidi, II, p. 12, quotes the opening with «له … بطاء أواخره». In III, p. 359, he quotes our 20, 21, 23, 24, 25 and 31–33. His excerpt gives «أوبقته», «تدمى أظافره», «الوهي» and «فإن يك». The last two concern the unrepairable break in the enemy’s power. [Rain and praise](https://shamela.ws/book/26183/529) · [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/26183/572) · [The passage about Bagrat](https://shamela.ws/book/26183/1290)
- Ibn Tabataba, *Iyar al-shi'r*, p. 206, has «لك الويل من ليل تطاول آخره». He tells how a patron hearing the second-person opening threw the ill wish back at the poet. Ibn Sinan al-Khafaji, *Sirr al-fasaha*, p. 184, also quotes «لك الويل», attributing the account to «بعض الناس». The reports even differ over which family member heard the line. They attest an alternative opening and explain why one syllable could matter so much when a panegyric was spoken in someone’s presence. [Ibn Tabataba](https://shamela.ws/book/6911/202) · [Ibn Sinan](https://shamela.ws/book/11239/176)

The decoders and al-Nahari’s text give «له» and «تطاول آخره». Against the same stretch of sound, «له» leads «لك» by 8.93 and «تطاول آخره» leads the alternative ending by 38.38. The distant third person is retained in the English opening. The poem first transfers the lover’s distress to a watering place, a deserted settlement and its young animals. When Yusuf takes centre stage, the imagery of looking returns in other forms, from his glance towards a sword to his prisoner within a glance’s reach. The last listeners addressed are Qays. The poet finally lets us see himself standing before their two ranks.

In 4.2 the campsite is addressed in the feminine as in 3–5. The vowel of «فروّتكِ» leads the masculine alternative by only 1.31. In 6.2 «استقرّت» leads «استقلّت» by 5.94. At 9.1 «العهد» leads Cairo’s «الوصل» by 24.66, at 12.1 «المرجوّ» leads «الموهوب» by 18.84. At 15.1 the complete word «ملمّة», a calamity, leads «ملّة» by 5.96. At 20.2 «أوبقته» leads «أسلمته» by 27.19, and at 22.2 «البيض» leads «الخوف» by 37.34. The governor’s gaze in 15 and the prisoner’s inability to keep his eyes on the blades in 22 belong to opposite sides of the account.

At 24.2 both diwans print «تندى أظافره», its claws grow moist. The narrow and wide takes give «تدمى», its claws bleed, and this leads «تندى» by 10.97. Al-Amidi explicitly quotes the reading with m. Both texts yield an intelligible animal image. The recording, the medieval witness and the bilingual word card all preserve the distinction. At 27 the passive «فأُحكِمت» leads the alternative «وأَحكَمَت» by 5.69. In 29 «الميمون» leads «المنصور» by 20.93, in 32 «الوهى» leads «الوهن» by 4.61, and in 36 «محصدات» leads «محكمات» by 19.49. In 39 «سليمه» leads «سليمهم» by 9.01. In 41 both continuous passes, the video and al-Sayrafi give the plural address «عجلتم» although «عجلت» leads acoustically by 7.27. The Qays men have been addressed as «إنّكم» and are thanked as «منكم» in the next verse. At 43 «مآثره» leads «مآخره» by 6.97.

## The 90 recorded halves

The narrow Cohere check flagged nine halves and an extra word at a clip edge. The wider independent Audar cuts and the continuous takes preserve several words that disappear in isolation.
