# يا باغِيَ الإِحسانِ يَطلُبُ رَبَّهُ

## Recording and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Sharafi recites «أبيات في مدح صحابة رسول الله ﷺ» for Alqimmah Studio, released 14 February 2026. The description transcribes 24 bayts and the video displays the verses. The 196.203-second source retains the poem from 2.05 to 194.30 seconds. Only head and tail silence were trimmed. Gain −7.9 dB, measured output −20.1 LUFS, duration 192.25 seconds. All 48 halves are recited once in the order printed here. [Studio recording and transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I7s9_8ZrUf4)

## Readings

The signed differences below favour the displayed reading when positive. Inputs, clip bounds and both scores are preserved in `media/research/ya-baghiya-al-ihsan/variant-scores.txt` and `prosody-scores.txt`.

- **8.1 and 10.1.** The recitation, the Egyptian al-Fiqi printing and Shams agree on «فعلٍ سيّئٍ» and «ما شابهم». The electronic Riyadh text gives «فعلٍ منكرٍ» and «ما شانهم». The respective margins are +11.31 and +5.10. In 10 the verb returns as «شابوا الهدى» in the next verse.
- **9.2.** Both editions read «في ذي الحال». The studio, whole-recording transcription and wide Audar clip agree on «مِن ذي الحال», +3.64. Both prepositions make sense. The word note records the printed reading.
- **12.** The electronic Riyadh text has «بالضدّ في الأمرين قد / تركوا الهدى ودعوا لكلّ ضلال». The Egyptian al-Fiqi printing agrees with it in the first half (with a further variant «في أحوالهم» in n. 1) and with Shams in the second. The studio, whole recording and Shams agree on «بالضدّ حتّى إنّهم / تركوا الهدى ودعوا إلى الإضلال». Wide clips recover both halves. The same-length comparison of the second half favours «إلى الإضلال» by 7.10.
- **16.1.** Both editions and the studio’s vocalised card at 2:00 give «مَعْ تُقًى وتواضُعٍ». The short transcription renders the ending as an accusative. The ending is less distinct in the recording than the consonants, and the scores are close (genitive +2.53). The genitive coordinates with «تقى» after «مع» and continues into «ونصيحةٍ» in the next half.
- **23.2.** Shams and the studio give «ذوو إذلال». The Egyptian al-Fiqi printing (p. 237) and its electronic Riyadh counterpart have «ذوو إدلال». The scorer prefers the latter by 4.77, but it represents Arabic ذ as *th*. Both the whole transcript and the wide Audar clip recognise «إذلال», the frame at 3:00 shows the dotted letter, and the allusion is to the gentleness towards believers in Qur’an 5:54.
- **24.1.** The studio and Shams give «وبراءةٌ والحشرُ» as a fresh subject. Al-Fiqi carries over the preposition and prints both names in the genitive. The acoustic comparison gives +0.18. The vowel marks and complete new clause settle the text here.
- **Metre and rhyme.** Kamil has three feet to a half. With `–` for a long syllable and `u` for a short one, 1.1 scans `––u– | ––u– | uu–u–` and 13.1 `uu–u– | uu–u– | ––u–`. The rhyme consonant is lam, audible in «الآمال، الأفعال، الأنفال». The prolonged final vowel in «الخالي» and «الغالي» belongs to the same rhyme.

## Verification

The first pass flagged nine of 48 halves. A second transcription with Audar, the whole-recording transcript, the studio cards and the printed pages provide the following checks.

- **1.1, 12.2.** The first backend returned unrelated religious prose. Audar recovers the opening «يا باغي الإحسان يطلب ربّه». On the widened 12.2 clip it transcribes «تركوا الهدى ودعوا إلى الإضلال», matching Shams, the Egyptian al-Fiqi printing and the studio word for word.
- **4.1, 13.1, 21.1.** At the openings, «بيت الله»، «فوم» and «لوجوههم» arise from «تالله»، «فهم» and «بوجوههم». The oath and the preposition are distinct in the full transcript and both editions. Audar also recovers the guides at 13.1.
- **16.1.** The first backend joins «مع تقى» into «معتقا». The marked studio text keeps the two words separate. See the case-ending comparison above.
- **18.2.** The boundary after «انهِمالِ» is absorbed into «الوابل» when sung in liaison. The noun names the downpour to which the tears are compared. Shams and the studio give «مِثلَ», where the electronic Riyadh text has «مِثلِ». The score is close (+1.14). The accusative describes the way the tears flow.
- **21.2.** Cohere added a feminine ending to «المتلالي». Audar recovers the masculine, as in both editions and the studio text. It qualifies «نورِه».
- **24.2.** Both short transcribers run the preposition and the chapter incipit together as «وبها الأتا». The publisher actually labels the chapter «هل أتى» in the recording description. Shams prints «وبِـ{هَلْ أَتَى}» and identifies its verses in n. 5.

## Commentary and biography

The preceding 106 bayts supply the referent for «سواهم» in 9 and 12. Shams’s notes on p. 419 identify the closing scriptural passages as Qur’an 48:29, 5:54, 9:71, 59:8–10, 76:7–10 and 8:74–75. In *Madarij al-Salikin*, vol. 3, pp. 64–65, Ibn al-Qayyim discusses Qur’an 25:63 and 5:54 together. He explains the gentleness of the walk, quotes al-Hasan’s pairing of learning and forbearance, and understands lowliness towards believers as compassion. This informs 15, 16 and 23. [Shams’s notes](https://shamela.ws/book/18612/461) · [*Madarij al-Salikin*](https://ibnalqayim.net/read/madarij-al-salikin/1452)

For the poet’s full name, dates, association with Ibn Taymiyya, imprisonment in Damascus and books, see Khayr al-Din al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 6, p. 56. His father’s post at the Jawziyya school accounts for the name by which he is known.
