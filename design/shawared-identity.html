I sleep soundly, unconcerned with my roaming verses, while people stay up all night over them, arguing.Al-Mutanabbi · واحرّ قلباه · verse 17 of 38

The name comes from this line, and the identity sets it the way a diwan is printed: ink on paper, the verse at the center, and one saffron dot that roams. That dot is the calligrapher's nuqta, and the product uses it for exactly one job: showing where you are.

الشعارThe mark

Direction A, carried forward. A square-Kufic ش whose three dots have left their place and drift ahead in the direction of writing.

The mark

Construction

  • Home. The dashed diamonds are where the three dots of a written ش sit, over its teeth.
  • Roamed. Each dot drifts up and ahead, the way Arabic is written, and shrinks as it goes: 15, 13, then 11 units across. The last dot is exactly one stroke wide.
  • One stroke, square ends. The first tooth stands taller and the baseline turns up at its end, so the shape reads as ش and not as a comb.
  • Room and size. Keep one large dot of clear space on every side. The smallest use is 16 pixels, where the dots are still solid diamonds.

٣٨ بيتًا

  • Paper first. The day screen is set like a printed diwan: ink on paper, the nuqta in saffron. Night mode keeps every role and only changes the ground.
  • Staggered bayt. On a phone the sadr sits right and the 'ajuz sits left, the way printed diwans fit verses on a narrow page.
  • Half-verse highlighting. The half being recited is set in full ink, its partner stays half-lit, and a saffron nuqta replaces the verse number.
  • Words with a dot have a meaning. Tap شَوارِدِها to open its entry, with the root and the sense in this verse. Tapping anywhere else on a line jumps the recitation there.
  • The explanation lives under the verse. The book button shows the sharh of whichever verse is playing, so reading never stops the audio.
  • Controls move by verse. Previous and next jump a whole bayt instead of ten seconds. Repeat loops one bayt, for people memorizing a poem.
  • English without translating the verses. In English the verses stay in Arabic, while the commentary and word meanings switch to English. Verse translations are optional: turn them on to see each one under its verse, split at the caesura.
  • English mirrors everything but the poem. In English the whole interface runs left to right, progress bar and controls included. Arabic content keeps its own direction: the verses, the title and the glossed words.

الخطوة التاليةNext decisions

  • Final wordmarkCommission an Arabic letterer to draw شوارد with the mark's diamond dots, plus a Latin companion.
  • Verse translationsOptional. English readers already get the commentary and word meanings in English; the toggles show the screen with and without translations. Copyright is parked for later.
  • RecordingsWho recites, and who owns the recordings. This shapes the catalogue more than any design choice.
  • Sync dataEvery half-verse needs a start time. Forced alignment can make a first pass from audio and text; a person then checks it.
  • Tech stackOne codebase for iOS, Android and web, or native apps. Whatever we pick has to set Arabic with full vowel marks cleanly.
