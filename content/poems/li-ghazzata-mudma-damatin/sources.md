# لِغَزَّةَ مُدمى دَمعَةٍ في المَآقي

## Text and recording

Ihab al-Shabatat published the twenty abyat under his own account, @eehabodeh, along with signed notes on 6.2 and 14.2. The other witness is the text typeset on screen by Alqimmah Studio for Khalid al-Sharafi's recitation, released 7 August 2026. It carries an English paraphrase beneath each Arabic bayt. Both witnesses have the same twenty in the same order. Bayt 20 is recited twice. The work files preserve the author page in *media/research/li-ghazzata-mudma-damatin/author.txt* and the studio frames under *media/research/video/IXEaPxRvCuU/*. [Author's text and notes](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem118595.html) · [Studio publication](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXEaPxRvCuU)

A bayt-by-bayt collation of the author page finds only four differences in word spacing. On his page the prefixed bi or li is separated from the quoted name Gaza at 1, 2, 3 and 20. The two written witnesses contain all twenty full lines.

The source edit keeps 3.65–313.45 s of 315.339 s. It removes the silent lead and ending, retains the last delivery and its decay, and gives 309.80 s at −20.0 LUFS after a −6.2 dB gain. The text is in tawil, checked on 1 and 14 against the alternating فعولن مفاعيلن feet and the مقبوض ending مفاعلن. Qaf is the rhyme consonant. Following the author, we distinguish written possessive -i in وثاقي and فِراقي from the prolonged rhyme vowel in words such as خِناقِ.

## Readings and commentary

- At 6.2 the author expressly glosses «لم تشتهي» as poetic lengthening of the final vowel of the jussive. Both written witnesses also read «دَمَ ساقِ». The bare noun is “shin, leg”. The studio interprets the blood as the speaker's own, hence the possessive in English. The recording retains the full syllable in تشتهي.
- In 12.1 the written subject of «تُشفِق» is «حُشاشتي». The feminine singular is retained in a line addressed to two companions. The following half shortens the written دِماء to دِما before أَطفال, as the author's text does.
- The poet's note on 14.2 specifically mentions premature babies in Gaza's hospitals. The studio's paraphrase also identifies the embrace in 8 as a forced intimacy, the release in 9 as the soul leaving the body, and the verses in 18–20 as the bearers of the poet's secret. The translations and explanations here are separate editorial renderings, including the sense of a mother camel calling for her young in 20. For the seven heavens in 4 see [Quran 67:3](https://quran.com/67/3).

## Audio check

The separate Cohere transcription of all 42 half-verse clips marked ten differences, involving 14 of 215 words. The comparison included the full recording, the text on screen, and an independent Audar pass over these ten halves with 0.70 s added before and 0.75 s after each aligned phrase.

- **1.2, 6.1, 14.1 and 15.1.** The isolated clips produce الشيوف for الشيوخ and قليلي for the repeated خَليلَيَّ. At 9.76–12.52 s the MMS comparison favours the decoder’s f by 2.95 (−35.05 against −38.00 for kh). The complete recording transcribes الشيوخ, and the poet and studio both give the elders who read the healing prayers. The latter is the dual address printed throughout and recognised elsewhere in this recording. At 14.1 both models also join the boundary between بصدري and هباءة. The studio text and its image of a mote in the speaker's chest settle the wording.
- **2.2, 12.2 and 14.2.** The rhyme's release becomes a written ه or ـية in the isolated decoder's رماقه، واقية، خناقه. Audar gets خناق and also gives the rhyme of رماقي. The performer draws out the common qaf and kasra. Both written witnesses have رِماقِ، واقِ، خِناقِ.
- **15.2, 17.1 and 18.1.** Audar recovers جُبِلَت طيني بغير رقاق، أصيل سجيّة، صبابتي where the clip decoder gives جبل الطين ... لقاق، سجيتي، صبابة. The force alignment finds 15.2 in its entirety and the full-performance transcript agrees on the last two phrases.
