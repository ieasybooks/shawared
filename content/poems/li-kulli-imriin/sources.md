# لِكُلِّ اِمرِئٍ مِن دَهرِهِ ما تَعَوَّدا

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari’s chanted performance, uploaded 19 April 2024, runs 402.913 s. The edit keeps original 0.24–396.75 s, from the onset of the first sadr through the release of «موعدا». The remainder follows the final verse. Al-Nahari performs the **opening sadr twice** before «وعادة سيف الدولة». The original first full-take transcription has both occurrences, and the alignment assigns a separate interval to each. He then performs the rest of the 42-bayt poem in order, giving 85 halves. The literary text displays the opening once. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pCkvZR9TxDU)

## Witnesses and occasion

- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, pp. 264–268. He dates the ode to the Feast of Sacrifice of 342 AH (954 CE). His gloss on the invocation of custom at the opening adduces Hatim, then observes that attaching a lance-thrust to a man called “Sword” equips him with both weapons. He glosses the domestikos as the opposing commander, identifies the captured son, and distinguishes deliberate injury from the accidental harm a sea can do in 6. [Headnote](https://shamela.ws/book/754/264) · [Campaign narrative](https://shamela.ws/book/754/265) · [The poet’s claim and close](https://shamela.ws/book/754/268)
- Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, dal section, verse notes 72–113. The heading places the first recitation in the maydan at Aleppo and expressly puts **both men on horseback**. All 42 bayts here follow this sequence. The commentary traditionally attributed to al-Ukbari, *al-Tibyan fi sharh al-diwan*, I, in its numbered glosses on the same ode, explains the contrast of the two feasts, the distribution «مثنى وموحدا», and the two-edged blade in the question to the ruler at 25. [Al-Barquqi, dal section](https://adab.wiki/wiki/شرح_ديوان_المتنبي_(البرقوقي)/قافية_الدال#ftn.72) · [Opening glosses in *al-Tibyan*, I, p. 281](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/270) · [The rulers and the festival, p. 285 ff.](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/272)
- Abu al-Ala' al-Ma'arri, *al-Lami' al-Azizi*, pp. 312 ff., explicitly gives the tawil measure, quotes the plural at the opening and considers the use of shared poetic expressions. Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-a'yan*, I, pp. 124–125, attributes his account to al-Iflili. It distinguishes the performance in the maydan from the seated reprise at the emir’s residence, where the poet answers a suggestion that he should stand by repeating his opening maxim. This anecdote is distinct from the repetition on the modern recording. [Al-Ma'arri](https://shamela.ws/book/29591/308) · [Ibn Khallikan, account](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/118) · [Reprise and reply](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/119)
- Al-Tha'alibi, *Yatimat al-dahr*, I, p. 139, quotes «وما الدهر إلا من رواة قصائدي» to illustrate the transmission of the poetry in town and desert. This supplies a classical witness for the recited word «قصائدي» at 36.1. Al-Barquqi prints «قلائدي» and explicitly records «قصائدي» in his note. [Al-Tha'alibi](https://shamela.ws/book/6743/115) · [Al-Barquqi, note 107](https://adab.wiki/wiki/شرح_ديوان_المتنبي_(البرقوقي)/قافية_الدال#ftn.107)
- Al-Zirikli gives Sayf al-Dawla’s name as Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Hamdan. The ruler named by his title in 1.2 and by his given name in 19.1 is the same man. [*Al-A'lam*, IV, p. 303](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3875)

The generosity opposed to the sword in 30 is «الندى». Ibn Manzur gives «ندوت من الجود» in this sense. For «ذراك» in 41 he quotes al-Asma'i’s definition of «الذَّرى» as anything one takes shelter behind, with the particular application of entering someone’s protection. [*Lisan al-Arab*, XV, p. 315](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7926) · [XIV, p. 284](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7408)

## Readings in performance

The two 42-line transcriptions and their collation are preserved in the research directory. At 25 the electronic al-Wahidi has the opening of the question about the sword followed by the **ajuz of 26 a second time**. His own gloss explains the two edges. Al-Barquqi supplies the complete question, «أما يتوقّى شفرتَي ما تقلّدا», which the recording gives before the lion and the hawk. Other evident electronic slips include «مزيدا» at 5 for a foaming sea, «رزق» at 15 against the blue lance-heads explained in the commentary, and «الإنسان» at 41 where the bond is «الإحسان».

## Metre

Tawil, with dal as the rhyme consonant followed by alif. In 1 both halves rhyme. The ordinary final foot is مفاعلن, and the opening فعولن can undergo قبض to give فعولُ. The divisions below follow the sound across written word boundaries.

| Bayt | Sadr | Ajuz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | li-kul-lim ∣ ri-ʾin-min-dah ∣ ri-hī-mā ∣ ta-ʿaw-wa-dā | wa-ʿā-da ∣ tu-say-fid-daw ∣ la-tiṭ-ṭaʿ ∣ nu-fil-ʿi-dā |
| 22 | wa-lā-zā ∣ la-til-ʾaʿ-yā ∣ du-lub-sa ∣ ka-baʿ-da-hū | tu-sal-li ∣ mu-makh-rū-qan ∣ wa-tuʿ-ṭā ∣ mu-jad-da-dā |
| 29 | ʾi-dhā-ʾan ∣ ta-ʾak-ram-tal ∣ ka-rī-ma ∣ ma-lak-ta-hū | wa-ʾin-ʾan ∣ ta-ʾak-ram-tal ∣ la-ʾī-ma ∣ ta-mar-ra-dā |

In 29 the two types of recipient occupy the same metrical position. Honour shown to the first wins him, while the second turns against the giver. The matched halves let the reversal be heard before the final verb names it.
