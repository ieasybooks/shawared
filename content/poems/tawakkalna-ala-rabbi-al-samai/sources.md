# تَوَكَّلنا عَلى رَبِّ السَّماءِ

## Recording and extent

Osama Al-Waaedh’s recording, uploaded 17 April 2026, runs 126.862 s. On the original, «توكّلنا» begins at about 2.48 s and the final «الوفاء» ends at about 124.48 s. The intake detects the end of the opening silence at 2.512 s and the start of the closing silence at 124.444 s. The single cut keeps 2.02–124.90 s, including the attack and decay of the voice. The edited track runs 122.88 s at −20 LUFS, with a gain of +6.9 dB and a peak of −1.6 dBTP. The source, intake, cut plan and verification are under `media/work/9rFXL42DyQY/`. In the second performance of the last bayt, the highlight follows the held final vowel of «الوفاء» to 122.46 s on the app clock. There are another 0.42 s of room sound. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9rFXL42DyQY)

The reciter gives verses 1–14 of Mardam Bek’s poem 25 in order, then repeats **both halves of 14 once**. The wide clip of the two performances and the independent Audar transcript establish their extent across the intake transcriber’s segment break. The text has fourteen entries and the recitation thirty half-verse entries. Verses 15–29 are the unrecited remainder, from «توقّ الناس يا ابن أبي وأمي» through «وما حبس الخليفة لي بعار». They contain the advice to his brother, the poet’s accusations against former friends, and his closing profession of allegiance. There is no omission inside the recited passage.

## Text and occasion

- **Khalil Mardam Bek, ed., _Diwan Ali ibn al-Jahm_**, Damascus, al-Majma' al-Ilmi al-Arabi, 1949, poem 25, printed pp. 81–85. Page 81 has verses 1–3, p. 82 verses 4–11, p. 83 verses 12–20, p. 84 verses 21–26 and p. 85 verses 27–29. The next poem is numbered 26. This gives the full count of 29. Checked against images of all five printed pages in Columbia’s copy. [Scan, p. 81](https://mc.dlib.nyu.edu/files/books/columbia_aco000246/columbia_aco000246_lo.pdf#page=139)
- **Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani, _Kitab al-Aghani_**, Cairo, Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya, vol. 10, printed pp. 206–208, «أخبار علي بن الجهم ونسبه». The report of Muhammad ibn Sa'd al-Mani'i on p. 206 says Ali wrote the poem to his brother when first imprisoned by al-Mutawakkil. The anthology transmits 24 verses. Within the recorded passage it has our 1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 11, 14, 13, 12 in that order. It supplies a second early text for nine of the fourteen and a different order for the closing three. Its later account on p. 208 gives a different explanation for the caliph’s anger. The introduction follows the common ground, the imprisonment. [Scan, vol. 10, pp. 206–208](https://archive.org/download/aghani2014/10.pdf#page=214)
- **Muhammad ibn Aydamur, _al-Durr al-Farid wa-Bayt al-Qasid_**, ed. Kamil Salman al-Juburi, vol. 5, p. 450, no. 7032. Under «توقّ الناس» he attributes this letter from prison to Ali. His extracts include «محظور الثراء» as in the diwan, alongside «على غني» as in the _Aghani_. **Al-Tanukhi, _al-Faraj ba'd al-Shidda_**, vol. 5, p. 48, independently identifies «حلبنا الدهر أشطره» and «هي الأيام تكلمنا وتأسو» as verses of Ali’s poem beginning «توكلنا». He specifically questions the composite line «فلا تأسف على دنيا تولّت / ولا تفزع إلى غير الدعاء» in the report he quotes from Ibn Abi al-Dunya. It is not an omitted verse of this edition. [Ibn Aydamur, 5, p. 450](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/2430) · [Al-Tanukhi, 5, p. 48](https://shamela.ws/book/12828/1537)

- **_Al-Mahasin wa-l-Addad_**, Beirut, Dar wa-Maktabat al-Hilal, 1423 AH, p. 169. The compilation traditionally ascribed to al-Jahiz quotes 3–4 under «وقال آخر», then a different third verse ending «سوى من لا يصم عن الدعاء». It supplies another witness to 3–4 and leaves their poet unnamed. Its introduction discusses the disputed authorship of the book. [Poetry, p. 169](https://shamela.ws/book/10511/154) · [Introduction, p. 5](https://shamela.ws/book/10511/1)

## Metre, vocabulary and interpretation

The metre is **wafir**, with a hamza as rawiy and a kasra after it. The first bayt rhymes at the caesura as well as at the close. In the scans below, ⏑ is a short syllable and – a long one.

- Both halves of 1 give ⏑––– | ⏑––– | ⏑––. In connected speech the first divides «تَوَكْكَلْنَا | عَلَى رَبْبِسْ | سَمَائِي». Both of the first two feet show the customary contraction of مُفاعَلَتُن.
- The middle bayt 8 opens «وَلَا يُجْدِثْ | ثَرَاءُ عَلَى | بَخِيلِنْ», giving ⏑––– | ⏑–⏑⏑– | ⏑––. Its second half returns to ⏑––– | ⏑––– | ⏑––. The rhyme vowel has been sounded long in this notation.

Mardam’s notes on pp. 81–82 gloss «غِيَر الليالي» as changes of fortune, «أفنية» as the spaces in front of houses, and «عُقَب» as successive turns. For 5, the phrase «بثّي وحزني» recalls Jacob’s words in Qur'an 12:86. Al-Tabari’s commentary identifies the speaker and the grief he pours out to God. [Al-Tabari, _Jami' al-Bayan_, vol. 16, pp. 225–226](https://shamela.ws/book/43/8977)

Ibn Manzur’s _Lisan al-Arab_ supplies «غِيَر» as a change of state, «كَلَمَ» as wounding and «أسا الجرح» as treating a wound. The two acts give verse 6 its image. The same dictionary glosses «حلب الدهر أشطره» as experiencing good and ill and explains the camel’s four teats, two to each half of the udder. It defines «العزاء» as bearing a loss patiently. [غير, 5, p. 40](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/2624) · [كلم, 12, pp. 524–525](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/6437) · [أسا, 14, p. 34](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7158) · [The saying, 4, p. 199](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/2159) · [The udder, 4, p. 407](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/2367) · [عزاء, 15, p. 52](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7664)

For «يُجدي» in 8 the verb belongs to ج د و. The _Aghani_’s «محظور العطاء» makes explicit the withheld gift, which in Mardam’s «محظور الثراء» is left for the following bayt to name. [Ahmad Mukhtar Umar, _Mu'jam al-Lugha al-Arabiyya al-Mu'asira_, vol. 1, p. 352](https://shamela.ws/book/29511/3904)
