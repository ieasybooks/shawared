## The continuous text and its transmitters

- Abu Ali al-Qali, *al-Nawadir*, in *Dhayl al-Amali wa-l-Nawadir*, Cairo printing dated 1344 AH, pp. 158–162 (scan leaves 165–169, counting from zero). The preceding page introduces Abu Bakr ibn al-Anbari. At p. 158 he says «وقصيدة عروة هذه النونية يختلف فيها الناس في بعض الأبيات ويتفقون على بعضها» and identifies his father transmitting from Ahmad ibn Ubayd and others, Abd Allah ibn Khalaf al-Dallal from Abu Abd Allah al-Sadusi, and Abu al-Hasan ibn al-Barra' from al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar. He adds «وألفاظهم مختلط بعضها ببعض». The collation therefore follows an attested continuous arrangement whose narrator expressly combined wording from several tellers. It does not infer a single author's manuscript behind all 82. The printed pages contain our 1–15, 16–37, 38–57, 58–78 and the alternative to 78 followed by 79–82, respectively. Counting the two versions of 78 as one position gives 82. [Al-Qali, p. 158](https://archive.org/details/3625pdf1344/page/n165/mode/1up) · [pp. 161–162](https://archive.org/details/3625pdf1344/page/n168/mode/2up)
- Abd al-Qadir al-Baghdadi, *Khizanat al-adab*, 3, pp. 375–381, introduces 47 in a discussion of the exception after «ما لي». He counts a long Nuniya of **73** and a diwan version of **33**, then quotes al-Qali's account of the mixed wording. He gives «ثمانين بكرة / وما لي يا عفراء غير ثمان» and the variant «ثمانين ناقة / وما لي والرحمن غير ثمان». The former is the reciter's version. The electronic quotation loses lines and runs 73 into 74 on p. 381. The reported totals, the surviving quotation on these pages and the 82 counted directly in the scan answer different editorial questions. The witness extract in the research folder records the readable quotations. [Al-Baghdadi, pp. 375–376](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/1309) · [p. 381](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/1315)
- Ibrahim al-Samarra'i and Ahmad Matlub, eds., *Shi'r Urwa ibn Hizam*, Baghdad, *Majallat Kulliyyat al-Adab*, no. 4, June **1961**, as dated on the title page. On pp. 8–13 the editors describe comparison with a manuscript copied by al-Shinqiti in 1320 AH, and print the opening as shorter numbered pieces. Their no. 1 adds two appeals to the companions after our opening and has «في الذخر», «فذراني» and «ثمّ ذراني» where the long text has other readings. Their no. 2 begins «متى تكشفا عني القميص» (our 10). They print the Iraqi/Yemeni camel verses with our 18 ahead of 17. At p. 12 their apparatus also cites al-Mubarrad's shorter parallel assigned to a Bedouin of Kilab. ## What the Nuniya shares with the Mu'nisa

Here **44**, «إذا ما جلسنا مجلساً نستلذّه / تواشوا بنا حتى أملّ مكاني», corresponds to **14** of the existing *Mu'nisa*, «إذا ما جلسنا مجلساً نستلذّه / تواشوا بنا حتى أملّ مكانيا». The latter is in Abd al-Sattar Ahmad Farraj's *Diwan Majnun Layla*, no. 307, pp. 226–229, also in the printed al-Walibi recension. Al-Qali, p. 160, and al-Baghdadi, 3, p. 379, give Urwa's nūn cadence. The final expansion in the Majnun version fits its yāʾ rhyme. Comparing all the lines gives one shared whole bayt in these two performed editions. Their openings, beloveds, orders and endings remain separately identifiable. [Mu'nisa witness dossier](../al-muinisa/sources.md) · [Al-Baghdadi](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/1313)

There are looser parallels as well. Urwa 46 and *Mu'nisa* 22 imagine an informer in al-Yamama. Our second half fears his ill omen, «أحاذره من شؤمه لأتاني». The Majnun recording puts his own house in distant Hadramawt with «وداري بأعلى حضرموت اهتدى ليا». Dawud al-Antaki cites that parallel with a different ending, «وداري بأعلى حضرموت أتانيا», in *Tazyin al-aswaq*, p. 62. He separately compares Urwa's struggling sandgrouse (25) with a Majnun image. His judgement about who borrowed from whom is literary criticism. A shared image or a single half-line cannot demonstrate a shared 82-line poem. At **65** a third poet enters the evidence. Al-Zamakhshari quotes Ibn Muqbil, «لدهماء إذ للناس والعيش غرة / وإذ خلقانا بالصبا يسران», and glosses «سهلان متيسّران» (*Asas al-balagha*, 2, p. 390). The recorded first half names **Afra** and «الدهر والناس». These attributions identify the parallel without establishing which poet composed the words first. [Al-Antaki](https://shamela.ws/book/630/62) · [Al-Zamakhshari](https://shamela.ws/book/21568/1087)

Al-Qawwal, p. 43 n. 4, expressly identifies «إذا ما جلسنا» in Yusuf Farhat’s printed *Diwan al-Majnun*, p. 203, and records its absence from the manuscript version of the poem printed there at p. 209. On that same page of the Urwa diwan he gives «وداري بأعلى حضرموت أتاني» in the text of our 46, reserving «أحاذره من شؤمه لأتاني» for the apparatus. Another attribution concerns the very bayt the recordings share. In *Rabiʿ al-abrar*, al-Zamakhshari heads two consecutive verses «ابن الطثرية». He quotes our **45 then 44**, both in their nūn cadence. The two performances and the anthology thus preserve three distinct settings for «إذا ما جلسنا». [Al-Qawwal, p. 43 and notes 4–6](https://archive.org/details/3536pdf/page/n44/mode/1up) · [Al-Zamakhshari, 4, p. 150, no. 45](https://shamela.ws/book/10668/1543)

Other verses also circulated outside this sequence. At **16**, Ibn Aydamur gives the camel’s backward longing and the rider’s forward longing to Urwa. The apparatus to al-Mubarrad’s *al-Kamil* adds the same bayt from witness ر to a pair attributed to an unnamed Bedouin of Kilab. Al-Baha al-Amili puts it under «المجنون» in his *Kashkul*. It does not occur in the performed *Mu'nisa*. Al-Qawwal also reports parallels to our **4–5** in Ibn al-Dumayna’s diwan, pp. 31 and 28 respectively. At **49–50**, the Khalidi brothers quote substantially the same pair under «ومثله لآخر», after naming al-Farazdaq and before introducing Kuthayyir’s verses to Azza. Their arrangement distinguishes the anonymous two-verse quotation from both named sequences. [Ibn Aydamur, 11, p. 83](https://shamela.ws/book/20762/4936) · [Al-Mubarrad, 1, p. 31 n. 2](https://shamela.ws/book/8505/35) · [Al-Amili, 1, p. 206](https://shamela.ws/book/1571/204) · [Al-Qawwal, pp. 34–35](https://archive.org/details/3536pdf/page/n35/mode/2up) · [Al-Khalidiyyan, p. 75](https://shamela.ws/book/10806/62)

## Commentary, names and metre

For the poet's new profile, al-Zirikli gives «عروة بن حزام بن مهاجر الضني، من بني عذرة», his upbringing with Afra, journey to Yemen, visit to her in the Balqa, death on the return journey and grave in Wadi al-Qura. His dates are explicitly approximate, around 30 AH (650 CE). In his footnote he distinguishes a report putting the death in Uthman’s caliphate from al-Baghdadi’s account placing it under Mu'awiya. Al-Antaki reports 30 AH from al-Dhahabi and 28 AH from an anonymous work. [Al-Antaki, p. 60](https://shamela.ws/book/630/60) In this account Afra's mother asks for the unaffordable bridewealth. At 47 the speaking lover holds his uncle responsible. The poem's direct accusation and the biographical story have each been left with their own speaker. [*Al-A'lam*, 4, p. 226](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3644)

Al-Qali explains the masculine «المعرض» applied to Afra (13) through the unexpressed “person” or a comparison, and records an alternative «فيسترهما» for 15. Al-Antaki explains the sandgrouse tethered by one wing, beating the other as the lover's liver trembles (25). The crows over the abandoned dwelling (38–41) prepare the crow's wing at 82. At 62 al-Qali's note explains اليرقان as a grub found in crops. It already remarks on the differing rhyme vowel. Al-Jawhari's *Sihah*, s.v. خفق, explains «امرأة خفاقة الحشا» by «خميصة», the slim waist whose ornaments occupy 66–69. At 78 al-Qali's first reading, «ملتقى نعم وألا لا», draws al-Tusi's explanation that yes and no meet on Afra's lips. He then prints the alternative «نعام وبرك» and says they are two places. The complete source-audio passes and the studio card name the two places. [Al-Qali, pp. 160–162](https://archive.org/details/3625pdf1344/page/n167/mode/2up) · [Al-Antaki](https://shamela.ws/book/630/62) · [Al-Jawhari, 4, p. 1470](https://shamela.ws/book/23235/2905)

Tawil with **nūn** as rawiy. The long ī of the cadence can belong to a pronoun, to a word ending, or to the prolongation of kasra. In the opening «خَلِيلَيْ / يَ مِنْ عُلْيَا / هِلَالِبْ / نِ عَامِرِنْ» gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. The ajuz «بِصَنْعَا / ءَ عُوجَلْيَوْ / مَ وَنْتَ / ظِرَانِي» ends with the curtailed ضرب فعولن. At 44 «إِذَا مَا / جَلَسْنَا مَجْ / لِسَنْ نَسْ / تَلِذْذُهُو» gives the same sadr. «تَوَاشَوْ / بِنَا حَتْتَى / أَمَلْلَ / مَكَانِي» uses فعولُ in third place and فعولن at the close. In 20 the article of الفراق carries across the metrical division.

At **54** Ibn al-Anbari quotes both «زفرات الضحى» and «بزفرات العشي» as an example of suppressing the vowel of the fāʾ for the metre, «فسكّن الفاء للضرورة». Al-Ayni prints both with sukūn. The edited words accordingly read *zafrāt*, with the fāʾ unmarked in house style. The second foot of the sadr is «تُ زَفْرَاتِضْ», مفاعيلن. At **69**, al-Qawwal glosses القطار as a string of camels and الجوزاء as a black ewe with a white middle (pp. 47–48). The translation reads rain compacting the sand of the dunes. Nashwan al-Himyari quotes another description of rainfall in which he explicitly glosses «قِطار» as the plural of «قَطرة». The hip comparison and the paired dunes «مُلتبدان» supply the contextual reason for preferring the rain. [Ibn al-Anbari, *al-Mudhakkar wa-l-mu'annath*, 2, p. 142](https://shamela.ws/book/17819/691) · [Al-Ayni, 4, p. 2033](https://shamela.ws/book/16714/1989) · [Al-Qawwal, pp. 47–48](https://archive.org/details/3536pdf/page/n48/mode/2up) · [Nashwan, *Shams al-ulum*, 4, p. 2025](https://shamela.ws/book/23358/1938)
