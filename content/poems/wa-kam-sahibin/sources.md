# وكم صاحب كالرمح زاغت كعوبه

## Recording and witnesses

Osama al-Nahari recites fourteen bayts once each, including the six lines about the diseased limb that make the argument of the complete poem intelligible. The retained interval is 0.68–129.08 s of the source. Source measurements put voiced material at 1.20–128.52 s. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GbRtKtnJnrs)

- *Diwan al-Sharif al-Radi*, Beirut, Dar Sadir and Dar Bayrut, 1380 AH (1961 CE), II, pp. 329–330. The heading is «أعزّ من القلب». Page 329 gives the first four verses and identifies al-Hazn as ground belonging to Banu Yarbu' with meadows and hollows. Page 330 has 5–14 followed by a new poem. Its readings include «وأدمج», «رقت فروعه», «الطِّبّ اللبيب», «مضّ تركها» and «ولا تنجلي». [Printed p. 329](https://archive.org/details/20191017_20191017_2122/page/n1015/mode/1up) · [p. 330](https://archive.org/details/20191017_20191017_2122/page/n1016/mode/1up)
- *Diwan al-Sayyid al-Radi al-Musawi*, Matba'at Nukhbat al-Akhbar, 1306 AH, p. 476. The title leaf dates the edition. All fourteen lines stand between two separate «وقال» headings on this page. This print has «رفت فروعه» at 3.1 and «فلا تنجلي» at 11.2. It does share the diwan’s sequence and «الطب» at 7.1. An incipit identifies the poem across both headings. [Printed p. 476](https://archive.org/details/3349pdf1306/page/n478/mode/1up)
- Usama ibn Munqidh, *Lubab al-adab*, p. 385, with the adjoining item on p. 386. He attributes the extract to al-Radi and selects our 1, 2, 3, 4, 11, 5, 9 in that order. His line about the garden reads «كنور الروض رفت فروعه», the opening is «ولي صاحب», and the hand is «مضّ حملها … وإن قطمت». The electronic text’s «وأصمر» at 2.2 is visibly problematic. The modern Damascus edition of the diwan, collected by Abu Hakim al-Khabri and edited by Abd al-Fattah al-Hulw, quotes the incipit in the introduction, I, p. 115, in a discussion of the poet’s friendships. That page confirms the opening only. [Ibn Munqidh](https://shamela.ws/book/10496/415)

The two whole-recording transcripts preserve «وكم», the upland meadow and the tender shoots, the instruction of the physician, the hand that hurts to keep, and «ولا تنجلي». The camera actually prints «الطِّبّ اللبيب» and «ضنّاً به». Here *tibb* is a physician, a personal sense also required by the adjective “discerning” and the verb “orders”. At 11 the obstinate particle remains in the eye, just as the friend remains within reach. The app’s paired explanations trace what changes as one metaphor succeeds another. A crooked shaft can be set aside. Losing a hand reshapes the arm one still has. The grit in the eye leaves a third possibility, an injury that persists without destroying sight.

## The recording against the printed variants

On the same sound, «وكم» leads the anthology’s «ولي» by 11.38. At 2.2 the deliberately concealed feeling («وأدمج») leads «وأضمر» by 6.17. The poet reserves the latter verb for 3.2. At 3.1 the diwan’s «كروض الحزن رقّت» leads Ibn Munqidh’s «كنور الروض رفّت» by 55.82. Changing the single consonant of «رقّت» to the 1306 print’s «رفّت» costs 9.07. The recording’s «الطِّبّ» at 7.1 leads «الطبيب» by 12.69. At 9.1 «تركها» leads «حملها» by 23.91, and at 9.2 «قُطِعت» leads «قُطِمت» by 9.66. The choice in 9.1 is between the pain of keeping the hand and the shape the arm would have without it. At 11.2 «ولا» leads «فلا» by 7.46. All fourteen bayts are retained in sequence with no internal cut.

The comparison of all 28 independent half-line clips flagged three readings. A fourth clip picked up an extra syllable at its edge.
