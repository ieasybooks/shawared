# ثُكلُ الأُمومَةِ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Waaedh, «ما للمنية أدعوها وتبتعد», produced by Alqimmah Studio, 10 August 2024. The video identifies Badawi al-Jabal, displays the text verse by verse and runs 487.967 seconds. The opening silence lasts to 3.220 and the closing silence begins at 483.789. The app retains 2.70–484.50 seconds (481.80 seconds), with −6.6 dB gain, −20.0 LUFS integrated and −6.3 dBTP peak. Each of the 48 recited bayts is heard once, in the order of the diwan. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9XscxBi-M40)

The 1978 diwan prints 91 bayts on pp. 297–306. The counts on successive pages are 8, 10, 9, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 9 and 5. The selections in the recording follow this numbering.

## Editions and commentary

- **ديوان بدوي الجبل**, introduction by Akram Zu'aytir, Beirut, Dar al-Awda, first edition, 1978, pp. 297–306. All ten pages were checked against the 48 verse cards. The title is «ثكل الأمومة», followed by the dedication «إلى روح أخي كامل مروة صاحب جريدة الحياة». At printed 25 the note identifies الوُرُد as veins. On p. 302 the note to «عدنان عندك في النعمى» identifies Adnan as the poet's son, who died in his youth. The death wishes at the opening, the mother's searching gaze, the meeting of the departed and the return to a day without a tomorrow shape the explanations. [Page images](https://archive.org/details/20190731_20190731_1945/page/n296/mode/2up) · [Edition catalogue](https://www.qatarch.com/library/book/5753)
- **Majid Qarut, المعذب في الشعر العربي الحديث في سورية ولبنان من عام 1945 إلى عام 1985**, Damascus, Arab Writers Union, 1999. Qarut names the elegy, quotes its first three bayts in full and cites p. 297 of the diwan. He reads the poet's thirst for death as an expression of a grief he finds more bitter than dying. His discussion provides a second published witness to those three bayts. Syria's General Book Organisation published a revised 726-page diwan in 2016. Its announcement describes family corrections to the collection. The actual text of this elegy in that edition was unavailable for collation. [Qarut's passage and note](https://www.islamarchive.cc/ketab_content/25448/p-8) · [Author and book catalogue](https://catalogue.univ-mosta.dz/flaa/index.php?id=3614&lvl=author_see) · [2016 edition announcement](https://archive.thawra.sy/?p=108264)
- The first-page note identifies Abu Jamil as Kamel Mrowa, founder of the Beirut newspaper *al-Hayat*. Al-Zirikli records his death by shooting in his office on 16 May 1966. *Le Monde* reported the assassination that week. The diwan provides no date of composition. The discussion of Jerusalem and Sinai later in the poem belongs to its larger public lament. [Al-Zirikli, الأعلام, vol. 5, p. 216](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4605) · [Continuation](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4606) · [Le Monde, 19 May 1966](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1966/05/19/le-directeur-d-un-grand-journal-libanais-est-assassine-dans-son-bureau_2694052_1819218.html)
- Al-Diwan's electronic text contains 90 verse records. It drops the printed eighth bayt «لقيت في الحقّ ما لاقى به نفرٌ / من الهداة وما عانوا وما جهدوا». Konouz preserves this bayt, printing its two halves on one line, and contains all 91. Both digital texts corrupt the Jerusalem passage and share other errors. A third transcription, PoetsGate, preserves the Jerusalem half and the two halves of printed 8, but omits printed 48 «لي فيك شعر رواه العطر فازدحمت» and likewise has 90. [PoetsGate](https://poetsgate.com/poem.php?pm=15606) · [Al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem100947.html) · [Konouz](https://konouz.com/ar/الأبداع-الأدبى/الأدب-العربى/الشعر/العصر-الحديث-والمعاصر/بدوي-الجبل/ثكل-الأمومة/p.1.1.5.128.14028/)

The metre is the basit. The first half opens «ما لِلْمَنيْ | يَةِ أَدْ | عوها وَتَبْ | تَعِدُ», مستفعلن فَعِلُن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. It recurs in the central «روح الشهيد بنور الله ما همدت». The rawi is dal both in the short rhymes أجدُ، أَرِدُ and before a final long vowel in فسَدوا، انفردوا.

## Readings in the recording

References below use the app numbering.

| Half | Text and witnesses |

| 8.1 | حاملين governs the object رسالاتٍ. The accusative of the sound feminine plural ends with kasra, while the singular feminine adjective agrees in the accusative as مُقَدَّسَةً. The card places two kasras on that adjective. The final syllable of the recording is also heard as *-tin*. The app restores grammatical agreement and notes the studio's reading on the word. |
| 9.1 | The reciter enters on مُشَتَّتِينَ بعصف الريح لا وطنٌ, as in the print. Al-Diwan and Konouz add a conjunction before لا وطن. PoetsGate correctly leaves it out. |
| 15.2 | الوُرُدُ are veins, the noun glossed by the diwan itself. The scent of the roses in الوَردِ is brought up against the blood that came from the veins. |
| 23.1 | The diwan and the verse card give أُلِمُّ بِالقَبرِ أُغليهِ وَأَلثُمُهُ, «I visit the grave, hold it dear and kiss it». The objects of the last two verbs are the grave. |
| 24.2, 25.2 | The print, the cards and the recitation give لا تَبْعُدُوا and لَبِّثْ قليلاً تَرَ الظّلّامَ. The electronic comparators have تبتعدوا in the first and omit ترَ in the second. |
| 25.1 | The reciter and card read بِنُورِ اللّهِ, «by the light of God». The printed كَنُورِ اللّهِ means «like the light of God». The preposition in the recording gives a grammatical reading of the same length and is retained with a word note. |
| 29.2, 32.2 | The diwan and the cards point وَالعُدَدُ, the equipment that accompanies the men, and وَالعَمَدُ, the pillars that tower over the heavens. The latter is one of the plurals of عمود. |
| 31.2 | The burning sun's وَهْجَها, its glare, brings out the pain of the inflamed eye. The card and the diwan agree. Both electronic comparators transpose the middle letters to وجهها, «its face». |
| 36.2 | The studio has زاحَمُوا الجُلّى, «grappled with the calamity», where the diwan has زَحَمُوا الجُلّى. The continuous transcription also picks up the longer verb. The recording's form is retained with a word note. |
| 37.1–38.1 | In the print and on the studio cards the first half begins القُدسُ مَلحَمَةٌ مَيدانُها اِزدَحَمَت. The battlefield of Jerusalem fills with armed men in 37.2. The sand of Sinai becomes a grave at 38. Al-Diwan repeats the Sinai image one verse early and Konouz even starts «القدس سيناء لحد هبّ منتفضا» there. Both spoil the syntax of the following half. |

## Independent half-verse check

Cohere transcribed each of the 96 aligned halves separately. The comparison reported 24 halves containing differences and 45 differing words out of 489 (9.2%). It flagged no extra words at the edges of the clips. Audar then transcribed all 24 flagged clips, with margins. Those transcriptions, the complete recording, printed pages and studio cards were compared.

| Half | Difference and decision |
| --- | --- |
| 2.1 | The isolated decoder returned an empty string. The six words of the half align between 11.00 and 15.56 seconds of the app audio. The continuous pass transcribes all six, and Qarut quotes them as the opening of the second bayt. |
| 8.1, 14.2 | The recogniser makes the plurals رسالات and نديّات singular. The vowel-length comparison ties in both places. Audar recovers رسالات at 8 and gives ناديات with a plural ending at 14. The print and both verse cards spell out the plurals. |

| 13.2 | In لا يندحنانا the recogniser misplaces the boundary between لَيَندى and حناناً. The emphatic lam after حتى and the image of the tenderness with which the critic writes are in both the print and the card. |

The same-interval tests used the model, Latin transcription and word scoring of `scripts/variants.py`, with 0.3-second margins and shortened acoustic windows. For 24.2 لا تبعدوا beats لا تبتعدوا by 16.04. At 25.1 بنور beats كنور by 10.67. At 31.2 وهجها beats وجهها by 7.99, and at 32.2 العَمَد beats العُمُد by 19.63. At 29.2 the reading العُدَد, attested by both pointed witnesses and by the contrast with عديدك, trails العَدَد by 1.35. The scorer makes identical Latin forms of the two adjective endings at 8.1 and of زاحموا / زحموا at 36.2. The two verb forms fill the second foot of the basit, فاعلن or فَعِلُن respectively. The screen explicitly writes the alif in زاحموا, with which the continuous transcript agrees.

At 23.1 the forced scorer favours أُوليه over أُغليه by 14.82 in the three-word phrase and by 11.61 on the single word. The audible distinction is weak. As a control, at 20.1 the same scorer prefers an artificial غ-less فأودقه over the undisputed فأُغدقه by **19.33**. A lexical prediction is particularly unreliable after the rounded vowel in this voice. A separate phoneme model, XLSR-53 fine-tuned on multilingual Common Voice, produces an o-like vowel followed by l in the disputed word. It also produces an o-like vowel followed directly by d in فأغدقه and transcribes the same reciter's unambiguous أُغلينا, from «إنا محيوك يا سلمى» 6.2, as an o-like vowel followed directly by l. By contrast, the model produces an explicit uvular fricative at the onset of غامت (24.1) and غدراً (27.1). The weak articulation in 23.1 cannot distinguish the two spellings with confidence. The diwan and card agree on «أغليه», which gives the complete sense «I hold it dear». In the same poem the poet repeats the verb in the omitted printed 84 «أغليها وأفتقد». A word note preserves the audible ambiguity.
