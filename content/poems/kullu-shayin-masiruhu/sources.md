# Al-Harith ibn Ubbad · كُلُّ شَيءٍ مَصيرُهُ لِلزَوالِ

## Recording, extent and attribution

Osama al-Nahari recites 37 bayts from the long lamiyya attributed in the literature to al-Harith ibn Ubbad. The original-clock cut is 1.02–256.20 s, a single continuous passage. Fourteen successive first halves at displayed 17–30 call for the mare with the same words. Each has its own second half and its own place in the text. Bayts 31–33 continue the command in a shorter form. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psCf09dWIUM)

Anas Abd al-Hadi Abu Hilal, *Diwan al-Harith ibn Ubbad*, poem 10, printed pp. 189–209. The editor lists the witnesses and verse correspondences at pp. 189–190. The sequence copied from *Kitab Bakr wa-Taghlib* has 100 numbered bayts, with another six numbered items, 101–106, from other witnesses. This gives 106 for the full collected item. The present order follows diwan numbers **1–4, 6–13, 15, 17, 26, 30, 45, 48, 52–53, 55, 61, 64, 66–67, 73–74, 83, 85, 88–91, 93, 98–100**. None of the six supplementary lines is heard. This particular selection is already listed on p. 189 for *Shu'ara al-nasraniyya* and *Akhbar al-muraqisa*. [Sources, pp. 189–190](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n189/mode/2up) · [Text from p. 191](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n191/mode/2up) · [Ending and supplements, pp. 208–209](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n208/mode/2up)

Louis Cheikho’s *Shu'ara al-nasraniyya qabl al-Islam*, printed pp. 271–273, reproduces exactly these 37 bayts in the performed order. The opening two verses are at the foot of p. 271. Page 272 carries the lament through «للسرى والغدو والآصال». Page 273 continues the calls and closes with «فقريناه حين رام قرانا / كل ماضي الذباب عضب الصقال». The next heading introduces al-Muhalhil’s answer. Cheikho thus witnesses the whole performed selection. Abu Hilal explicitly draws on Cheikho for his correction at the close. [p. 271](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n280/mode/2up) · [p. 272](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n281/mode/2up) · [p. 273](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n282/mode/2up)

The old short witness is al-Asma'i’s *Asma'iyyat*, no. 17, p. 71. It has the pregnancy of war (our 17), the speaker’s previously clean hands (our 11), then a bayt beginning «لا بجير أغنى» which al-Nahari does not recite. Abu Hilal’s apparatus on p. 190 records the narrow authentication ascribed to Abd al-Malik al-Asma'i or his brother’s son. That evidence attaches to the small cluster, not equally to every later continuation. It does not date each enlargement. The devotional opening, the long mourning passage, the multiplied calls and the ending remain part of the literary text selected for this recording. The edition and both introductions signal that distinction. [Asma'iyyat, p. 71](https://shamela.ws/book/6905/57) · [Diwan, p. 190](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n190/mode/2up)

Al-Nuwayri, *Nihayat al-arab*, 15/402–403, relates Bujayr’s death, the price set on it and the father’s entry into the fighting. He quotes the command and calls the poem nearly a hundred bayts with the call repeated fifty times. His second quoted refrain has «وأنكرتني رجالي» against the performed «وأنكرتني القوالي» (20.2). Al-Ghundajani, *Asma' khayl al-Arab wa-fursaniha*, p. 243, identifies al-Na'ama as al-Harith’s mare. Its modern editor compares the three-bayt, four-bayt and 36-bayt transmissions and counts fourteen calls in the last. These are witness-specific counts. [Al-Nuwayri, 15/402–403](https://shamela.ws/book/10283/5248) · [Al-Ghundajani, p. 243](https://shamela.ws/book/120141/236)

The original sharh draws on Abu Hilal’s notes for Umm al-Agharr, the curtained women, the sandal strap, the mare and the equipment. The profile follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 2/156. His dating is approximate, about fifty years before the Hijra or 570 CE, and his report identifies Bujayr as al-Harith’s son. [Diwan, pp. 191–193](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n191/mode/2up) · [Al-A'lam, 2/156](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/1391)

## Readings in the performance

The opening and the refrain scan as khafif. Lam is the rawiy. At 1.1 Cheikho has «مصيره الزوال», while the performance retains the lām in «للزوال» as in Abu Hilal. At 2.2 both full recognisers and the isolated half give «بعض احتيال», also printed by Cheikho on p. 271 and recorded by Abu Hilal below his main «من احتيال». On the word alone the shorter «من» scores 6.1 points higher. The recovered consonants of «بعض» and the printed variant support the performed reading. At 3.1 the performance’s «تبكي» is in his apparatus against «تبكِ». At 6.1 the diwan corrects the manuscript to the lethal poison «سمّاً نقيعاً», and at 6.2 it identifies the curtained quarters that the fair women leave. At 9.2 the voice gives «حين تسقي الدما صدور العوالي». The text reads the blood as giving the spearheads drink, distinct from the printed passive «تُسقى» or the apparatus’s first-person «نسقي». At 11.2 the form is «لحرّها», with Cheikho. Abu Hilal prints «بحرّها», as does the early anthology, and identifies «لحرّها» in his apparatus. [Diwan, pp. 191–193](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n191/mode/2up) · [Asma'iyyat, p. 71](https://shamela.ws/book/6905/57)

At 8.2 Abu Hilal no. 9 and Cheikho read «نملأ البيد», “we fill the wastes”. Both continuous recognisers substitute «البيض» for the desert wastes, while isolated Cohere gives «تملأ البيد من رؤوس الرجال». On the individual words «تُملأَ» scores 6.4 points above «نملأَ» and «البيدُ» scores 6.7 above «البَيضُ». The text keeps the performed passive with «البيدُ» as its subject. «البيض» actually occurs in 6.2, where it refers to the women leaving their curtained quarters. [Abu Hilal, p. 192](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n192/mode/2up) · [Cheikho, p. 272](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n281/mode/2up)

At 36.1 Cheikho prints «ذي دهاء». Abu Hilal’s «ذي زهاء», imposing appearance, is the word caught by both running transcriptions. [Cheikho, p. 273](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n282/mode/2up) · [Abu Hilal, p. 208](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n208/mode/2up)

At 17.1 «قرّبا مربط النعامة مني» is the attested dual imperative. In 20.2 isolated Cohere and Cheikho p. 272 give «شاب رأسي وأنكرتني القوالي». Abu Hilal p. 200 prints «وأنكرته الغوالي», the perfumes no longer recognising his head because he gave up scent in mourning. His fifth note expressly names Cheikho’s «القوالي» and rejects it for his own text. Al-Nuwayri has «وأنكرتني رجالي». The recitation follows Cheikho, and the word card gives the printed alternative. At 31–33 «قرّباها» changes the construction. The first-person possessive in «لأمتي» at 32 names the speaker’s armour. The closing action takes up the language of hospitality in «فقريناه حين رام قرانا». [Al-Nuwayri, 15/403](https://shamela.ws/book/10283/5249) · [Diwan, p. 200, n. 5](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n200/mode/2up) · [Diwan, pp. 206–208](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n206/mode/2up)

In several refrains the short recited selection follows Cheikho against the diwan’s main text. The isolated halves recover «لكن فعالي» (18.2), «على الليالي» (22.2) and «ريح ذيل الشمال» (26.2). Abu Hilal has «لا بل فعالي»، «عن الليالي» and «ذيل ريح الشمال». At 28.2–29.2 the voice gives «لكريم» and «لا نبيع الرجال» where he prints «لحليم» and «لا يباع الرجال». At 31.2–33.2 the individual clips give «يوم القتال»، «درعاً دلاصاً»، «بمرهفات» and «لقراع الأبطال». Abu Hilal’s main text reads «يوم المجال»، «زغفاً دلاصاً»، «لمرهفات» and «لقراع الكهول». The differences between the two printed witnesses are visible in Abu Hilal’s apparatus. [pp. 200–203](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n200/mode/2up) · [pp. 205–206](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n205/mode/2up) · [Cheikho, pp. 272–273](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n281/mode/2up)

**37.2 is a verse-level segmentation failure.** Both transcribers repartition the four words following «كلّ» («ماضي الذباب عضب الصقال»), even joining the first word to «كلّ». Abu Hilal prints no. 100, «فقريناه حين رام قرانا / كلَّ ماضي الذُّبابِ عَضبِ الصِّقالِ». Cheikho prints the same continuous words at the end of his p. 273. Footnote 2 in Abu Hilal derives the correction from *Shu'ara al-nasraniyya* and explains الذباب as the striking extremity of the sword, العضب as the sharp blade and الصقال as burnishing. The second half supplies what the speaker and his men served their martial “guest”. The final video frame, inspected at original 253 s, still displays the incipit «كل شيء مصيره للزوال / غير ربي وصالح الأعمال» and the poet’s name. It supplies no last-verse wording. The full Cohere and Audar streams, both 12-second original-edge crops, isolated Cohere and widened Audar all join and divide the same sounds differently. Examples include «كلما ضد باب عض السطال», «كلما ضد باب عض بالصقال» and widened Audar’s «كلما ضد باب عبد الصالح». They agree on the position and rhythm of the ending and produce no coherent competing object of «قريناه». The edition retains the attested khafif half and its lam rhyme. [Printed no. 100 and footnote, p. 208](https://archive.org/details/diwan-al-harith-ibn-abbad/page/n208/mode/2up) · [Cheikho, p. 273](https://archive.org/details/4114pdf/page/n282/mode/2up) · [Closing frame](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psCf09dWIUM&t=253s)
