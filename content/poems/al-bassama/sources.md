## Editions

- **عبد الواحد المراكشي, المعجب في تلخيص أخبار المغرب**: shamela 8559, printed pp. 62–68 (`muajjab.txt`). The whole poem, 75 abyat, with the editor's glosses. This is the text the reciter follows, in its order and in nearly every word.
- **ابن بدرون, شرح قصيدة ابن عبدون المعروفة بالبسامة** (Cairo, 1340 AH): archive.org `AAlexandrina-137835`, read as page images. Pages are cited by their printed numbers: up to p. 287 the printed page is the scan's leaf index minus 4 (leaf 199 is p. 195), and from p. 294 the two agree. The commentary (to p. 297) quotes the abyat in its own order, each before its story, as far as bayt 42 here (al-Muqtadir), with glosses from Ibn al-Athir's abridgement in the footnotes; it then quotes the elegy (45–68) without comment (pp. 297–298). The whole poem is printed again on pp. 299–302: 69 lines, ending with the two lines of blessing the reciter says. It lacks abyat 19, 22, 34, 36, 55, 70 and 71 and has «ثلاثة ما رأى العصران مثلهم». Dozy's Leiden edition of the same commentary (1846, archive.org `3943pdf`) was checked at p. 5 and for the Habbaba line, which it also lacks.
- **ابن بسام, الذخيرة**, ed. Ihsan Abbas: shamela 1035, vol. 4, pp. 721–724 (`dhakhira.txt`). 58 lines, one of them supplied by the editor; his notes compare Ibn Badrun's text («شرح البسامة») and al-Fawat. (His note on 33.1 gives Ibn Badrun's reading as «قاصية»; the Cairo print has «قاضبة», text and comment, p. 195.)
- **الفتح بن خاقان, قلائد العقيان**: shamela 21510, printed pp. 37–40 (`qalaid.txt`). The poet's contemporary; 68 lines, in an order of its own, with Ibn Badrun's endings for 63 («لو كان دينًا على الأيام ذي عسر») and 68 («طمع»). The only source found with the reciter's «الخضر» at 62.2. Its prose before the poem has «ومزّقت ابني بدر بجفر الهباءة», used for bayt 22.
- **ابن دحية, المطرب من أشعار أهل المغرب**: shamela 93, pp. 27–33 (`mutrib.txt`). The whole poem, 76 abyat, voweled; close to al-Mu'jib in the elegy (45–71).
- **ابن شاكر الكتبي, فوات الوفيات**: shamela 1003, vol. 2, pp. 388–391 (`fawat.txt`); **لسان الدين بن الخطيب, الإحاطة**: shamela 11682, vol. 4, pp. 32–37 (`ihata.txt`). Consulted for single readings.

`abyat_in_full` is 77: al-Mu'jib's 75 and the two lines of blessing from the end of Ibn Badrun's Cairo text. The abyat of al-Mu'jib the reciter leaves out: «وفي ابن هند وفي ابن المصطفى حسن» and «فبعضنا قائل ما اغتاله أحد» (after 28), «وأعملت في لطيم الجن حيلتها» (after 32), «ثلاثة ما رأى السعدان مثلهم» (after 53).

## Commentary drawn on

- **Names and stories** follow Ibn Badrun (Cairo, printed pages): Dara and Alexander 12–23 (Alexander's six years after Dara, 23); the Sasanids and the Greeks 31–56; Tasm, Jadis and their king «عملوق» 62; Yemen and Mudar 82–102 (the poet «لم يذكر … أحدًا من أهل اليمن ولا من مضر بخصوصه», 82); Saba' 102–108; Kulayb and Muhalhil 108–117 («بين سمع الأرض والبصر», 117); Imru' al-Qays, the poisoned robe and Hujr 119; Dhubyan, Abs, the race of Qays ibn Zuhayr and Hamal ibn Badr, and al-Haba'a 122–127; Adi ibn Zayd and al-Nu'man 128–132 (the verse on the red-eyed king, which he gives to «أبو محدرة الطائي», 131); Khubayb 132–134; Ja'far and Hamza 136; Yazdajird 138–140; Rustam «الأرمني», Dhu al-Hajib (Khurrazad) and Sa'd 140–145; Uthman, Kinana ibn Bishr and al-Zubayr 145–150; Ammar and the ضيح 154; Ibn Muljam and Ali, with the hadith of «أشقاها الذي يخضب هذه من هذه», 155; Amr and Kharija 165; Abu Anas and Zufar 181–182; Ibn Ziyad and Muhalhil's «بؤ بشسع نعل كليب» 182–185; Mus'ab, al-Mukhtar and Kufa as «الشاهقة» 185–191; Ibn al-Zubayr 191; Abd al-Malik, «أبو الذبان» (his breath, or bleeding gums that drew flies), «قاضبة» and al-Latim 195–202; al-Walid ibn Yazid 202–207; al-Saffah and Marwan 207–219 (Busir, 219); Fakhkh and his objection to it 219–221; the Barmakids and the inversion in bayt 39, 222; al-Amin and the oaths in the Kaaba 242–245; al-Mutawakkil and al-Muntasir 256–259, 283; the titles of 42–43 and those who bore them, 259–297 (al-Mu'tamid, poisoned by his nephew, 294); the Abbasids and their Turks (bayt 44) 285; al-Musta'in and al-Mu'tazz 288 (its number is blurred; it follows p. 287). Shiruya as the first to kill his father is in the book's index (scan leaf 319).
- **Glosses** from Ibn al-Athir's notes in the Cairo footnotes (غرب, عضب, أُثُر, ناقض المرر, رائح ومبتكر, قارعة, الغيل والظلّام للجزر, نابية, ريق الصارم, لعا, زبّاء as a calamity) and from al-Mu'jib's editor (الغير, الضيح, الغابر, الشجا, ويب, لم يربع, لم يحر, السدر, الشرائع, الشقاشق).
- **Lines not in Ibn Badrun:** 19 and 22 from the general histories (Ibn Badrun's own story of Yazdajird's death at Merv, under bayt 20, serves 19); 34 (Zayd ibn Ali) from al-Mu'jib's editor; 36 (Habbaba) from Ibn al-Jawzi, *al-Muntazam*, vol. 7, p. 111 (shamela 12406/2338, `muntazam.txt`). Nobody consulted explains «وأحمر قطّرته نفحة القطر»; the sharh takes the red thing as the seed itself, and says so.
- **Abu Qardudah al-Ta'i** (18.2), who warned Ibn Ammar against al-Nu'man, «أحمر العينين والشعره»: al-Jahiz, *al-Bayan wa-l-Tabyin*, vol. 1, p. 190 (shamela 10614/185), and al-Zamakhshari, *Rabi' al-Abrar*, vol. 5, p. 176.
- **«أحلام عاد»** as a proverb for weighty minds (62.2): al-Tha'alibi, *Thimar al-Qulub*, p. 79 (shamela 6896/77), and *al-Shakwa wa-l-'Itab*, p. 196 (shamela 22892/189).
- **«لعلّ وعسى طمع وإشفاق»** (68.1): Ibn al-Sarraj, *al-Usul fi al-Nahw*, vol. 2, p. 218 (shamela 7365/714).
- **The proverb «كلّ أزبّ نفور»** (44.2): al-Maydani, *Majma' al-Amthal*, vol. 2, p. 133, no. 2996 (shamela 12929/3080).
- **The Aftasids and the occasion:** al-Mu'jib, printed pp. 61–62 (al-Muzaffar's anthology, al-Mutawakkil's verse and prose, his death with al-Fadl and al-Abbas «في غرة سنة ٤٨٥»).
- **The verdict quoted in the intro:** al-Mu'jib, p. 62.
- **The poet's life:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 4, p. 149 (shamela 12286/3414, `alam.txt`), which gives his death as 529 AH and notes 520 and 527 in other sources.
- **Ibn Badrun:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 4, p. 161 (shamela 12286/3449): of Silves, alive in 608 AH, when Ibn al-Abbar saw his hand; so the intro gives him no century.

## Review

A second reader checked the text against al-Mu'jib, the Cairo page images of Ibn Badrun (pp. 299–302, and his commentary for every story the notes credit to him) and the other editions, found a further one (Qala'id al-Iqyan), rescored the doubtful readings, scanned every half, and checked the sharh, notes, intro and bio against the sources (2026-11-17). Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** no change. 37.1 rescored (+0.8 for «نابية», still a coin toss; kept with al-Mu'jib and the transcripts); the tanwin of 17.1 «ذُبيانٍ» and 25.1 «عُثمانٍ» tested against the bare fatha and kept.
- **Meaning:** 62.2 «الخَضِرِ» is attested (Qala'id), and the sharh now reads «أحلام عاد» first as the proverb for weighty minds, with the dreams of long life as the other reading, in both languages; 12.1 retranslated (Tasm perished first and Jadis followed, as Ibn al-Athir's gloss has it); 49 gives both readings of «بيضهم» (their swords; the men themselves, whose necks were collared with death); 33.1 «قاضية» glossed as deadly, from قضى عليه, not as cutting.
- **Notes:** 18.2 the poet who called al-Nu'man red-eyed is Abu Qardudah al-Ta'i (Ibn Badrun's print: «أبو محدرة»), not Abu Zubayd; 33.1 Abd al-Malik's flies settled on his bleeding gums, not his lips (Ibn Badrun, p. 195); 12.1 the king of Tasm as Ibn Badrun names him, Amluq; 22.1 al-Fath's «ومزّقت ابني بدر بجفر الهباءة» added; 37.1 al-Dhakhira's «نابية» and the sense of «نبا عن»; 68.1 the grammarians' «لعلّ وعسى طمع وإشفاق» and the «طمع» reading; 62.2 both notes; the title notes in 42–43 no longer repeat the sharh; English notes for 49.1 and 62.2 added.
- **Sharh:** Arabic deepened in 24 abyat (median 102 to 122 letters); in English, dates and identities already in the notes taken out of the sharh of 22 and 30–41 in favour of what the bayt means, 5, 50, 56, 67, 69 and 71 deepened, and 15, 17, 20, 54, 61, 63 and 68 no longer repeat their notes; 40 matches its note (al-Mutawakkil killed at his son's bidding, as Ibn Badrun has it); 36 claims no more than the sources say. «Quran» spelled as in the rest of the library.
- **Intro:** Ibn Badrun is no longer dated to the 6th century AH (12th CE), which al-A'lam does not support (he was alive in 608 AH); he is named as of Silves instead.
- **sources.md:** Ibn Badrun's page numbers were the scan's leaf numbers, 4 more than the printed pages up to p. 287; all now printed pages. Qala'id al-Iqyan added; «قاصية» at 33.1 credited to al-Dhakhira's editor; al-Dhakhira added to «نابية»; the sources for 18, 62 and 68 added.
- **Poet:** checked against al-A'lam; no change.
