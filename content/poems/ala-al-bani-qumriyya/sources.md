## Attribution

The poem is al-Amjad's on every witness found; no source gives it to anyone else.

- **His diwan.** Gharib's edition (below) prints it as poem 31 with the editor's note (p. 105, n. **): «وردت هذه القصيدة في كل النسخ» and «وعدد أبياتها (٤٤) أربعة وأربعون بيتا في النسخ كلها عدا (د) فقد سقط منها بيتان» (by his notes, د lacks the edition's 35 and the halves 14.2 and 15.1). It is in all five manuscripts he used, the oldest of them copied in Damascus by Uthman ibn Ahmad on 23 Dhu al-Hijja 633 AH, five years after the poet's death (introduction, p. 11). The diwan itself is attested as his by Ibn Khallikan (*Wafayat al-A'yan*, vol. 2, p. 453, shamela 1000/925: «وله ديوان شعر»), al-Safadi (*al-Wafi bi-l-Wafayat*, vol. 10, p. 190, shamela 6677/2571: «له ديوان شعر موجود»), Ibn Shakir (*Fawat al-Wafayat*, vol. 1, p. 226, shamela 1003/224: «له ديوان شعر موجود بأيدي الناس»), Sibt ibn al-Jawzi (*Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 22, p. 310, shamela 23644/10648: «وله ديوان كبير») and al-Zirikli (*al-A'lam*, vol. 2, p. 76, shamela 12286/1154, who saw the Khalidiyya copy in Jerusalem).
- **None of the biographers quotes this poem**: Ibn Khallikan, al-Safadi, Ibn Shakir, Sibt, Ibn Wasil (*Mufarrij al-Kurub*, vol. 4, pp. 284–286, shamela 16362/1552–1554) and al-Dhahabi (*Tarikh al-Islam*, vol. 45, pp. 305–306, shamela 12397/21118–21119) each quote other pieces. **Abu Shama**'s *al-Dhayl ala al-Rawdatayn* (ed. al-Kawthari, archive.org `moharram1000_gmail_20171122_1224`, printed pp. 159–160, read from the page images) does not record al-Amjad's death in its text for 628; only the editor's note on p. 160 adds it from Ibn Kathir. *Al-Rawdatayn* names him only as one of the princes with al-Afdal (vol. 4, p. 422, shamela 6631/1745).
- **Anthologies:** shamela's full-text search finds no book that quotes any of eight rare halves of the poem («قمرية تسجع», «يرق لي المربع», «تزحزح عن برقه البرقع», «أجوب الظلام وأهواله», «عن الجزع من بينها أجزع», «يهيجني رسمها البلقع», «أبعد المشيب أروم الشفيع», «إلى أن أقض بي المضجع»); the old shamela's poetry shelf has no diwan of al-Amjad. The online poetry sites (poetsgate.com 111239, aldiwan.net 15215, madarisweb.com 79620) all give it to al-Malik al-Amjad.
- **Title:** the sources give the poem no name, so the title is the first sadr.

## Editions

- **Gharib Muhammad Ali Ahmad**, ed., ديوان الملك الأمجد بهرام شاه الأيوبي (Cairo: al-Hay'a al-Misriyya al-Amma li-l-Kitab, Markaz Tahqiq al-Turath, 1991, as its title page says, leaf 0; the colophon, leaf 363, gives Matba'at Dar al-Kutub 1991, deposit no. 8121/1991, ISBN 977-01-2819-8; Sibt's editor gives the same year, *Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 22, p. 310, n. 3): archive.org `20260226_20260226_2311`, poem 31 on printed pp. 105–108 (leaves 104–107), headed «وله رحمه الله» [المتقارب], 44 abyat. **Read from the page images**, with the doubtful places cropped and enlarged (`media/research/ala-al-bani-qumriyya/_scratch/crops/`); my transcription with every vowel that mattered and the footnotes is `gharib.txt`. The print is partly voweled. The introduction (pp. 9–15, leaves 8–14) describes the five manuscripts and their sigla: ر Manchester, John Rylands 457, copied 633 AH, the base text; م Mosul, Awqaf library; خ Jerusalem, Khalidiyya, copied 1028 AH; ت Dar al-Kutub, Taymur 1129; د Dar al-Kutub 6720 adab, copied 1306 AH. His footnotes on this poem give only manuscript variants, no glosses, so where a manuscript is not named his text has its reading. - **poetsgate.com**, poem 111239 (`poetsgate.txt`), posted by the site in 2012 and naming no source; the video's description is the same text with the same typos («أذى البينِ ن لو انّها», «وأحفِزُها ن»). 43 abyat: it lacks the edition's 29. Unconfirmed; it is treated as a second witness, not an edition.

- **17.1:** Gharib prints «أرقَلتُ» (first person). It breaks the meter (the last foot «قَلتُ» is - u where the meter needs u -) and the grammar («لَما أرقلتُ إليها بيَ الأينقُ» leaves «الأينق» with no verb), so the text has «أَرقَلَت» with the camels as subject; audar hears «أرقلت», cohere «أَرْقَلَاتُ» (the stretched end of the sadr), and the score leans the same way (`variants6.txt`).
- **Where the reciter departs from the edition,** the word note gives the edition's reading (3.1, 18.2, 19.1, 20.1 twice, 22.2, 23.2, 25.2, 33.2, 34.1, 35.1, 38.1, 38.2); 7.1 and 17.2 note the manuscripts' readings. Only 18.2 «شُقَّتَها», 22.2 «وَأَحفِرُها», 33.2 «هَجَرونا» and 38.1 «وَحَدَّثتُها» depart from both Gharib and poetsgate; 22.2 departs from every manuscript the editor reports as well.
- **35.1 «قَنَعتُ»:** in the sense «I was content» Lisan has «قَنِعَ» (vol. 8, p. 297, shamela 1687/4145), and «قَنَعَ يَقنَعُ قُنوعًا» for «to beg»; it adds Ibn al-Sikkit's remark «ومن العرب من يجيز القنوع بمعنى القناعة». The note says so.
- **22.2 «وَأَحفِرُها»:** «I dig her» makes no sense; Gharib's text «أحفزها» («I urge her on from behind», Lisan vol. 5, p. 337, shamela 1687/2921), which by his note's silence is the reading of م and د (not an emendation), is what the sharh and the translation explain, as the note says. Ms. ر has «اخفرها», خ and ت «احضرها» (Lisan, vol. 4, p. 201, shamela 1687/2161, has «أحضر الفرسُ» only of the horse that runs; the transitive is «استحضرتُه»). Lisan's حفر (vol. 4, pp. 204–205, shamela 1687/2164–2165) has nothing that could apply to a camel (digging, tooth decay, a colt losing its milk teeth). The reciter's r is the clearest score in the poem's doubtful words (the control «أحفدها» scores as badly as «أحفزها»), so the text follows him by the house rule, as at «زَردَقاً» in «لِعَينَيكِ ما يَلقى».

## Commentary drawn on

- **No commentary on this poem exists that I could find**, and Gharib's notes on it give only variants. The sharh is the drafter's reading, built word by word on the lexicons below; where it interprets (the subject of «سحّت» and «جدّدت» in 2 being «الأدمع»; «به» in 3 being the ruin; the list «حشًى … ونارُ … وطرفٌ» running from 10 to 12; «لواغبَ» and «جوانحَ» as circumstantial accusatives describing the camels; the ruin as the speaker's «it» in 9; youth as «الذي يشفع» in 30, which 32 states), that is the drafter's reading of the grammar, not a commentator's.

## The poet

Fixed:

- **Audio:** five sadrs lit up during the previous half's stretched rhyme (23.1, 25.1, 26.1, 30.1, 33.1, 0.38–0.54 s early); moved to the voice's onset (Ear check, Timeline).
- **Text:** no word or vowel changed. The drafter's readings stand: 22.2 «وَأَحفِرُها» follows the reciter by the house rule (the r scores clearly against ز and against a control, all four transcripts agree), with the editions' «أحفزها» in the note and in the translation's sense; the four places where the print breaks the meter (4.1, 17.1, 28/31/38's division) are now tied to what Gharib says of ر: its halves are not divided, and its vowels sometimes go against the grammar.
- **Meaning:** 37 is he and his beloved («وحدّثتها» in 38, and the edition's 40–41, where she signals to him), not he and "those he loves"; 18.2 «شُقَّتَها» is feminine (the camels' journey or the road to her), not "its length", the desert's, which was the edition's «شقّته»; 24.2, the camels are spent and bowed, not the riders; 25, «وحده» (alone) removed, since 24 has them riding together; 20, the claim that La'la' shows the camp is near (no source says where La'la' lies on the way) removed.
- **Sharh:** 2 gives the other possible subject of «جدّدت», the dove; 9 says the poet, not "Arabic", uses one word for both winds; 8 no longer repeats the note on «جآذر».
- **Notes:** 24.1 «طِلاحاً» is the plural of «طِلح», not of «طَليح» (Lisan, vol. 2, p. 530: «وجمع طِلْحٍ أطلاحٌ وطِلاحٌ، وجمع طليح طلائح وطَلْحى»); the head changed in both languages. 18.2 explains the feminine pronoun; 34.1 «حُبِّهِمُ» explains the construction (a verbal noun with its object).
- **English:** 7.1 read as if the complaint did the trace no good; 34.1 "in nearness to their love" made plain.
- **Poet:** al-Ashraf Musa was al-Amjad's father's cousin, not his cousin (Ibn Wasil's «ابن عمه» is loose); both bios say so.
- **sources.md:** the editor's note on this poem quoted exactly (p. 105: «في كل النسخ», with the count of 44 and د's two missing abyat); the quotation given before, «وردت هذه القصيدة في النسخ كلها», is his note on the next poem (p. 108). «أحفزها» is not Gharib's emendation but, by his silence, the reading of م and د. The edition's imprint now comes from its own title page and colophon, not from Sibt's editor. 17.2: «الظلع» is ر's reading alone, and poetsgate has «الضُّلَّعُ». The CE conversions checked, and 12 Shawwal 628 falls on a Wednesday, which supports Ibn Khallikan and Sibt against al-Safadi's 22 Shawwal.
- **Left open:** 17.2 ظ/ض/ط (the audio cannot tell; kept the oldest manuscript's «الظلع»); 22.2, where the text keeps a word that makes no sense because the reciter says it, as the house rule requires; 20.1 «نَشأى» and 18.2 «شُقَّتَها», taken from the transcripts on gaps under 5 (−3.7, −4.0); 8.1 «أُرى», a coin toss kept as the edition voweled it; the Nazim Rashid edition, still unseen, which may be poetsgate's source.
