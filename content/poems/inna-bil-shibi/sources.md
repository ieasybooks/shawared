# إن بالشعب الذي دون سلع

## The recorded transmission

Abd al-Rahman ibn Nasir al-Sa'eed first discusses why this particular metre calls for a particular tune, recounts competing attributions, then introduces the poem as the speech of Ta'abbata Sharran's sister's son. His stated witness is the transmission of Abu Muhammad al-Qasim ibn Muhammad al-Daymarti al-Asbahani, whom he places around 355 AH. The opening line quoted during the discussion belongs to Miskawayh's argument. The verse performance begins after the spoken rubric naming the uncle. The retained original-clock passage is 134.14–346.50 s of 355.138 s. It has 27 bayts, with the drinker's request to Sawad and «إن جسمي بعد خالي لخل» at the end. The full Audar transcription preserves that final half where the overlapping continuous Cohere segments trail off. The base count is the 27 the reciter identifies and performs. [Original audio and speaker's written rubric](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a3uzSeV8OOg)

Yaqut records al-Daymarti as an Isfahani grammarian and a teacher concerned with the correct reading of books. Ibrahim ibn Mas'ud al-Fayfi edited the commentary entitled *Tahdhib sharh al-Hamasa wa-ijaz lafziha*, attributed to al-Daymarti. The digitised doctoral study explicitly edits the second part. Its text starts with the love poetry and does not reproduce the elegies section. The attribution of this complete 27-bayt order to al-Daymarti is therefore specifically al-Sa'eed's statement. [Yaqut, *Mu'jam al-udaba'*, 5:2229](https://shamela.ws/book/9788/2227) · [Al-Fayfi's study, Umm al-Qura University, 1424 AH](https://quranpedia.net/book/17758)

The Süleymaniye Library also identifies a manuscript of the abridged commentary, Fatih 3944, filmed as no. 01123. Its catalogue names the copyist Ibrahim ibn Nasir al-Kindi and dates the copy to 560 AH. This identifies a manuscript for further textual comparison. The catalogue record does not reproduce the passage. [Süleymaniye microfilm catalogue, record 01123](https://www.suleymaniye.yek.gov.tr/Content/UploadFile/Doc/mikrofilm.pdf)

Miskawayh, *al-Hawamil wa-l-shawamil*, question 124, pp. 317–318 in the electronic edition, uses the opening to illustrate how a specific melodic phrasing can make an unfamiliar rhythm intelligible to a later listener. The upload's description quotes p. 283 without identifying that printing. No authorship decision follows from Miskawayh's citation. [Question](https://shamela.ws/book/4135/286) · [Example and answer](https://shamela.ws/book/4135/287)

## The competing authors

- Abu Tammam's *Hamasa* reaches us with different headings and explanations. Al-Marzuqi's *Sharh Diwan al-Hamasa*, pp. 586–595, prints «وقال تأبط شراً» and immediately prefers Khalaf al-Ahmar as the author. On p. 587 he takes the sister's son in bayt 3 to be the speaker describing himself. Al-Tabrizi's *Sharh Diwan al-Hamasa*, 1:341–347, also prefers Khalaf. He preserves al-Namari's stylistic argument from the antithesis in «جلّ حتى دقّ فيه الأجلّ» and Abu al-Nada's objection that Sal' at Medina lies away from the cave of Rakhman associated with the uncle's death. Al-Tabrizi supplies two dramatic possibilities, a nephew mourning his uncle and the endangered poet imagining his own elegy, and glosses 3 as a possible second nephew who will succeed the speaker. [Al-Marzuqi's rubric](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/565) · [His interpretation of bayt 3](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/566) · [Al-Tabrizi's two arguments](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/325)
- Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-shu'ara'*, 2:777–778, in the notice on Khalaf, gives the opening with «إلى جنب سلع» and says Khalaf assigned the composition to the nephew. This is evidence for an early report of attribution by a transmitter. Al-Farisi's heading names al-Shanfara as the nephew and he also records al-Mubarrad's claim for Khalaf. [Ibn Qutayba](https://shamela.ws/book/23785/768) · [Al-Farisi's heading](https://shamela.ws/book/16435/373) · [Al-Mubarrad's alternative](https://shamela.ws/book/16435/374)
- Al-Zabidi, *Taj al-'arus*, s.v. «سلع», 21:211, quotes al-Mubarrad on Khalaf, mentions the ascription to Ta'abbata Sharran himself, then prefers the nephew on the strength of «بعد خالي». Following Ibn Barri, he identifies this nephew as al-Shanfara. His figure of 25 verses belongs to the *Hamasa* known to him. Al-Bakri, *Simt al-la'ali*, 1:39, calls the nephew Khufaf ibn Nudba. The editorial note to al-Murtada's *Amali*, ed. Muhammad Abu al-Fadl Ibrahim, 2:185, gives Khufaf ibn Nadla. The spellings are distinct and insufficient for a secure personal identification. [Al-Zabidi](https://shamela.ws/book/7030/11146) · [Al-Bakri](https://shamela.ws/book/1586/37) · [Editorial note to *Amali*](https://shamela.ws/book/20937/853)

The poet profile uses the reciter's kinship rubric. The Arabic and English introductions preserve the competing claims of Khalaf, al-Shanfara and the uncle himself. The last bayt supports a sister's son as the speaking voice. A later poet could also have composed in that voice.

## Text and order

Al-Farisi, *Sharh Hamasat Abi Tammam*, 2:383–387, gives 26 bayts here. Its order is especially close to the recording. It includes our 24 («فأدركنا الثأر») and 25 («مطلع الشمس») on p. 387, has «يورد الألة» at 17, and cites «جاءنا» beside «نابنا» at 5. Its last pair comes as 27, 26. The recorded «فسقينا كأس حتف هذيلاً» at 23 is absent from the three continuously collated commentary texts. Both continuous transcripts and the reciter’s written text have it. A separate medieval quotation preserves the bayt with a different opening verb. [Al-Farisi on the portrait](https://shamela.ws/book/16435/375) · [On the spear and birds](https://shamela.ws/book/16435/376) · [Lihyan and the retreat](https://shamela.ws/book/16435/377)

Salama ibn Muslim al-Awtabi al-Suhari, *al-Ansab*, ed. Muhammad Ihsan al-Nass, fourth edition, 1427 AH (2006), 2:671–672, explicitly introduces al-Shanfara as avenging his uncle Ta'abbata Sharran. His eight-bayt quotation runs in the order of our 16, 17, 26, 27, 23, a further bayt about arrows, 25 and 18. On p. 672, no. 5 has «فصبّحنا كأس حتف هذيلاً» and the same second half as our 23. His no. 7 retains the sunrise and changes the verbs of retreat. The poem was therefore known with the cup-of-death bayt outside this recording. The attribution and order belong to al-Awtabi's account. [Attribution and first four bayts, p. 671](https://alsaidia.com/sites/default/files/%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%A8%D9%8A%20%D8%AC%D9%A2.pdf#page=209) · [Cup, retreat and hyena, p. 672](https://alsaidia.com/sites/default/files/%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%86%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A8%20%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%A8%D9%8A%20%D8%AC%D9%A2.pdf#page=210)

Al-Marzuqi's set has 24 bayts. It puts the band and its brief sleep before «فلئن فلّت هذيل», orders the three portrait lines at our 10–12 differently, and proceeds from the birds to the wine without our 23–25. Al-Tabrizi gives 26. He has our 24 much earlier, gives «وبما صبّحها في ذراها» where the recording has no corresponding bayt, and places the hyena and birds after the wine. At 19 his birds «تغدو» and al-Marzuqi's and al-Farisi's «تهفو». The recitation has «تمشي», making their inability to lift off particularly explicit. The written and spoken presentations of this recording have «هوّموا» at 21. Al-Marzuqi and al-Farisi give «ثملوا», continuing the image of sipping sleep. The two tastes belong, in al-Farisi’s preferred gloss, to the friend who finds honey in him and the enemy who tastes colocynth. At 11 al-Tabrizi explains أحوى as dark-lipped, while al-Farisi on 2:385 takes it of the man’s complexion. The glosses also explain the hunting animal, the wine abstention and the drinker’s shrunken body. [The lips and the animal in al-Tabrizi, 1:344](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/327) [Al-Marzuqi, pp. 589–590](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/568) · [His close](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/572) · [Al-Tabrizi's additional verse](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/329) · [His last two](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/330)

## Metre

Madīd with lām as the rhyme consonant. Short syllables are u and long ones –. Bayt 1 scans `–u–– | –u– | –u––` and `uu–– | uu– | –u––`. At 14 («فلئن فلّت هذيل شباه») the halves scan `uu–– | –u– | –u––` and `uu–– | uu– | –u––`. In 4 and 13 a word runs across the metrical division, respectively «أطـ / ـرق» and «يصـ / ـحبه». The displayed halves keep the complete word together so its word address and translation remain intelligible.

## Acoustic readings and verification

Each comparison retains the same word count and uses the aligned half with 0.30 s of context on each side. Scores are summed CTC log likelihoods. Positive differences favour the alternative.

The scores use the MMS model and romanisation of the repository’s variants tool. The alternatives include readings in the editions, spellings produced by the recognisers and tests of pronunciation. A difference under two points has little discriminating value. The last column gives the reading used in the verse.

The passages in the spoken preface do not form part of the performed sequence. In 3.1 the reciter’s written text and the two continuous transcripts put a conjunction before وراء. Both readings have nearly the same model score. At 4 the complete word أطرق has one address, and at 13 the whole of يصحبه is timed with the second half, although the metre divides it internally. At 5 the reciter says جاءنا, also expressly cited by al-Farisi as an alternative to نابنا. At 8 the waiving of the hamza in the sound of بؤس gives the competing spelling a small advantage. The sense is want or poverty, a word printed with hamza in the speaker’s text. At 14.1 the recognisers consistently represent the t before the name of Hudhayl. This and the editions support فلّت even though the shorter comparison scores almost identically.

Al-Farisi and the performance have يورد الألّة at 17. Al-Marzuqi and al-Tabrizi give ينهل الصعدة, and al-Awtabi combines the verb يورد with الصعدة. At 18 both continuous recognisers reduce لقتلى to a spelling like لقتل, but an independent phone pass has a vowel after the l before هذيل, and the complete half favours the plural قتلى. At 19 the birds walk, تمشي, because they have eaten their fill. The comparison favours the reciter’s verb over both تهفو and تغدو by more than 24 points. At 21 the reciter and al-Tabrizi have هوّموا. The other collated texts' ثملوا extends the preceding image of drinking sleep. At 22.2 the s of يُسلّ identifies the drawn blade. At 23 the widened clip recovers فسقينا كأس حتف هذيلاً, including the word division that defeats the first transcription. The recorded opening فسقينا scores 15.69 points above al-Awtabi’s فصبّحنا. At 17 the recorded يورد الألّة leads his يورد الصعدة by 20.64. The expanded verb اجفألّ describes headlong flight. The written and extended opening at 27 belongs to the personal name سواد. Full Audar preserves it, where the final Cohere segment falters as the rhyme approaches.

## Half-by-half adjudication

The release-timing Cohere pass covered 54 halves. It flagged 11, comprising 20 of 235 words under the repository’s normalisation. The table includes distinct readings from the preliminary cuts as well. Widened Audar transcriptions are shown where available. The lead’s timing review extended 2 half endings to preserve their voiced tails.

| 11.1 | مسبل في الحي احوار فل | مسبل في الحي أحوار فل | Both أحوى and رفلّ describe the man among his people, his dark colouring and his unhurried bearing. The recognition attaches the r to the preceding adjective. Al-Farisi comments on both words on p. 385. |

| 14.2 | لبما كانه ذيلا يفل | لما كان هذيل يفل | There is another هذيل here, now the object of يفلّ. Its h does not attach to كان. The reversal of who blunts whom depends on both occurrences of the name. |
| 15.1 | وبما ابركها في مناخرهم | وبما أبركها في مناخ | The half ends at مُناخ, a place where camels are made to kneel. The next half describes its rough ground and chafed feet. The narrow مناخرهم, “their nostrils”, would undo that connection. |
| 16.1 | صليت منه ذيل بخرق | صليت مني هذيل بخرق | The line turns to the speaker, منّي. It names هذيل as the tribe that now encounters him. Writing منه ذيل both changes the speaker and loses the tribal name. |
