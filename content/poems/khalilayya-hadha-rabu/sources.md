# Kuthayyir Azza · خَليلَيَّ هَذا رَبعُ عَزَّةَ فَاعقِلا

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari recites all 43 bayts of poem 3 in Ihsan Abbas’s edition. Abbas brackets 2 and 3 on p. 95 and 8 on p. 96. All three are recited in their printed positions. The single original-clock interval is 1.85–382.00 s. The two companions are addressed at the beginning and again at 29. Each verse, including the two distinct closing cloud similes, is sung once. The brief overlaps in the long automatic transcripts are recognition-window overlaps. The scrolling studio cards give the running verse text. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DouruCIupM)

## Witnesses and commentary

- *Diwan Kuthayyir Azza*, collected and explained by Ihsan Abbas, poem 3. Printed pp. 95–103 contain the poem and lexical notes, pp. 104–107 the witnesses and variants. The next poem begins on p. 108. The printed p. 95 is scan leaf 94. The commentary explains the pilgrimage oath and proper names, the struggling camel and lameness, the domestic rebuke, and the construction of the closing comparisons. [pp. 95–97](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n94/mode/2up) · [pp. 102–104](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n101/mode/2up) · [apparatus, pp. 104–107](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n103/mode/2up)
- Abu Ali al-Qali, *al-Amali*, 2/107–110, specifically says he read this poem to Ibn Durayd. He transmits the opening, gives alternate forms, and adds explanations of the places and vocabulary. His order differs, e.g. he puts our 5 near the husband episode, our 12 last after the two clouds, and passes over other bayts included by Abbas. His glosses on «أناديك»، «ميعة»، «بلّت»، «العتبى»، «طلّحت»، «أزلّت» and «اعترافه» inform the word cards and both sets of explanations. [2/107](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/391) · [2/108–109](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/392) · [2/110](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/394)
- Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-shu'ara'*, ed. Ahmad Muhammad Shakir, 1/505–507, transmits a partial sequence. Shakir marks the prefatory quotation about reading to Ibn Durayd as an addition drawn from al-Qali, so it is not independent testimony for that event. The rest of the excerpt is a comparator for such readings as «العيس» (our 13), «الغيران» (21) and the alternative turning of the soul in 40.2. [1/505–507](https://shamela.ws/book/23785/498)
- The profile takes the poet’s name, Khuza'i background, residence in Egypt, patronage and death in Medina from al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 5/219. His entry identifies Azza as the daughter of Humayl of Damra. [5/219](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4615)

## Readings in the performance

The opening and the paired-leg comparison at 18 are in tawil. The rhyme letter is ta, heard with kasra. The studio and recording give «ربع … ابكيا» (1), beside al-Qali’s alternative «رسم … انظرا». At 4 the performance has «البكا … القلب», where al-Qali has «الهوى … الحزن». At 7 «أُناديك» means to sit in company with someone. Abbas p. 96 explains that the negative is understood in the oath. Azza swears that she will never sit with him while the pilgrims continue their rites. The reciter and cards give «بفيفاء آل» here and «ذي غزال» in the distinct place-list of 8. Al-Qali puts «بفيفا غزال» in 7. [Diwan, pp. 95–96](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n94/mode/2up) · [Al-Amali, 2/108](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/392)

On p. 96 Abbas glosses «أشعرت» (8.2) as «اتخذت شعاراً», taking on a sign, and «استهلّت» as raising the voice in the pilgrim’s call. On p. 99 he records several explanations of the legs (18). Ibn Hisham al-Lakhmi prefers the one that continues the wish of 16–17. Staying where Azza is gives the lover his sound leg, losing the camel his disabled one. [Diwan, p. 96](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n95/mode/2up) · [p. 99](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n98/mode/2up)

At 11.2 the recorded «عمياء» stands beside al-Qali’s «غمّاء». At 12 the reciter uses «فيم صرمتها». The older sequence closes with «فيم هجرتها». At 13 «العُصم» are mountain goats, fitting the polished rock where even they would slip. At 14.2 the reading is «ذلك الوصل». Al-Qali also transmits «ذلك البخل». The husband is disparaged as «الخنزير» in 21, against the older «الغيران», “the jealous man”. The word «المليك» refers here to the authority of the man imposing the insult. In 24.2 the diwan, card and recording have «توالي التي تأتي المُنى قد تولّت». The sighs go on coming while his hopes have departed. [Diwan, pp. 97–100](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n96/mode/2up) · [Al-Amali, 2/108](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/392)

The knot in 26 is drawn tight by him and undone by her. The studio card at 226 s, continuous Cohere and the isolated half give «فلمّا تواثقنا», exchanging pledges. Continuous Audar and Abbas’s printed p. 100 have «توافقنا», coming to an agreement. The two word scores differ by less than 0.1. The knot being tied and untied gives the spoken pledge its concrete image. [Studio card, 226 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DouruCIupM&t=226s) · [Diwan, p. 100](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n99/mode/2up)

The recited 32.2 «فضلّت» pictures the lost attachment, while al-Qali’s «فطلّت» means it went for nothing. He gives «بالجوى» in 34 and «ما حلّ قبلها / ولا بعدها» in 37, against the recording’s «بالردى» and «ما حلّ بعدها / ولا قبلها». Continuous Cohere inserts «لا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله» around the oath at 37.1. The running Audar, studio verse and aligned words give the oath and its continuation straight through. At 39 the performance has «وحلّت» and «ولا النفس ملّت», against al-Qali’s «وأضحت» and «ولا العين ملّت». In 40 «اعترافه» is specifically the heart’s ability to bear sorrow, as al-Qali explains. In 42 the cloud’s shade disappears while the traveller is choosing where to rest. In 43 the rain finally falls, beyond the man whose land is parched. [Diwan, pp. 100–103](https://archive.org/details/3568pdf/page/n99/mode/2up) · [Al-Amali, 2/109–110](https://shamela.ws/book/9160/393)

## Per-half verification

The rows include earlier flagged cuts as well as the current verification. The last column gives the editorial decision. ∅ denotes a recogniser that returned no words.

| Half | Isolated Cohere | Widened Audar | Adjudication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 | «قل اوصيكما ثم ابكيا حيث حلت» | «قالوا صيكما ثم ابكيا حيث حلت» | Both recognisers split the first word as if it were a speech verb. «قلوصيكما» is the dual riding camel with the two friends’ possessive suffix. It is the object of the previous imperative and stands intact in Abbas p. 95, al-Qali and the card. |
| 2.2 | «وبيتا وضلا حيث باتت وضلت» | «وبيت وضلا حيث باتت وضلت» | بيتَا and ظِلّا address the two companions. باتت and ظلّت refer to Azza in the singular. The two emphatic consonants transcribed with ض belong to the daytime verb ظلّ, paired with spending the night. |
| 5.2 | «الينا واما بالنوار فظنه» | «إلينا وأما بالنوال فظنت» | «بالنوال فضنّت» means she withheld the favour he wanted from her. The narrowed recognition invents a final r, and both can confuse ظ and ض here. Both the card and Abbas read the verb of grudging something. |
| 6.2 | «قريش غدات المازمين وصلت» | «قريش غداة المأزيمين وصلت» | The technical place-name is «المأزمين», the two constricted passes on the pilgrims’ route. «غداة» takes its t sound in the construction «غداة المأزمين». The recognised variants do not change the place or the actions of Quraysh. |
| 7.1 | «اناديك ما حج الحجيج وكبرته» | «قل ذلك ما حج الحجيج وكبرت» | The recited oath opens «أناديك ما حج الحجيج وكبّرت». The feminine verb at the end has its subject «رفقة» in the next half. The card, al-Qali's lexical gloss and the diwan's discussion on p. 96 establish the uncommon first verb and the understood negative. |
| 9.2 | «والانسان انه يوم الارض» | «كناذرة نذرا وفتفأ حلت» | The narrow recogniser invents an unrelated phrase. Widened Audar restores the vow, fulfilment and release almost word for word. All three actions are present in Abbas and the studio text. Al-Qali gives «وفت فأحلت» as a variant. |
| 14.2 | «فمن مل منها ذلك الوصل مله» | «فمن مل منها ذلك الوصل ملتي» | «فمن ملّ منها ذلك الوصل ملّت» plays on the same root for the suitor and for her. Both clipped spellings stumble on the feminine tā at the rhyme and its extended kasra. The card and the running voice keep «الوصل». |
| 15.2 | «وحلت تلاعا لم تكن قبل حله» / «وحلت تلاعا لم تكن قبل حلته» | «وحلت تداعا لم تكن قبل حلت» | The objects of settlement are «تلاعاً», valleys. The verse repeats the verb for her arrival and for an area previously unoccupied. The isomorphic spoken endings of «حلّت» and passive «حُلّت» are given in the card and the diwan's syntax. |
| 16.1 | «فليت قلوصي عند عز تقيدت» | «فليتق لو صيع دعزة قيدت» | «فليت قلوصي عند عزة قيّدت» wishes for the speaker’s own riding camel to be tethered at Azza’s camp. The crop moves the boundaries of both «قلوصي» and «عزة». The pronoun, place and wish occur together in Abbas and al-Qali. |
| 19.1 | «وكنت كذات الضلع لما تحاملت» | «وكنت كذات الظلع لما تحاملت» | «ذات الظلع» is the limping beast. Widened Audar restores the emphatic consonant, which short Cohere spells as ض. Abbas p. 99 explicitly glosses it as lameness. |
| 19.2 | «علي ضلعها بعد العثار استقلت» | «على ضلعها بعد العثار استقلت» | The previous half supplies the noun repeated in «على ظلعها». Both short windows spell it with ض, which would suggest a rib. The limp and the stumble form a single comparison in the print and card. |
| 20.2 | «اذا ما اطلنا عندها المكثمه التي» | «إذا ما أطلنا عندها المكثمة التي» | The doubled m across «المكث ملّت» leads both recognisers to join the words. The noun means a long stay and «ملّت» has Azza as its subject, answering the poet’s desire to stay. Abbas p. 99 spells out the connection. |
| 29.1 | «قليلي ان الحاجبي تطلحت» | «قليليا إن الحاجب يتطلحت» | The opening dual vocative returns at «خليليّ». «الحاجبية» names the woman, and the verb «طلّحت» means to exhaust the riding camels named at 29.2. The full streams and the card preserve this three-part construction that the narrow clips divide. |
| 32.1 | «ولكن انيلي واذكري من مودتك» | «ولكن أني لي واذكري من مودة» | «من مودّة» is indefinite. The extra k in isolated Cohere has no counterpart in the wider crop, diwan or card. «أنيلي» is the imperative ‘grant me something’, directed to Azza. |
| 32.2 | «لنا خله كانت لديكم فظلت» | «لنا خلة كانت لديكم فضلت» | The card and widened Audar give «فضلّت», the old friendship went astray. The narrow «فظلّت» would make her remain and reverses the point. Al-Qali’s different «فطلّت» means the friendship came to nothing. |
| 34.1 | «فما انا بالداعي لعزه بردا» | «فما أنا بالداعي لعزة بالردى» | Widened Audar restores «بالردى», death. It is the harm he will never invoke against her, corroborated by the diwan and studio. Al-Qali records «بالجوى», consuming heartache. |
| 34.2 | «ولا شامت ان نعل عز تزلت» | «ولا شامت إن نعل عزة زلة» | The sandals belong to Azza. «زلّت» is a separate feminine verb with «نعل» as subject. Both crops attach the tā sound differently at the meeting of the name and verb. |
| 39.2 | «لفضيله الدكتور محمد راتب النابلسي» | «فلا القلب يسلاها ولا النفس ملتة» | The name and title produced by isolated Cohere are absent from the verse and the running recording. Widened Audar restores «فلا القلب يسلاها ولا النفس ملّت». The final t is the poem’s rhyme. Al-Qali’s second subject is «العين». |
| 41.1 | «واني وتهيامي بعزه بعد ما» | «وإني وتهيامي بعزة بعدما» | The flag represents a written space in «بعد ما». The conjunction is written «بعدما» in this text. Both transcribers hear the same sequence before the following «تخلّيت». |
| 42.1 | «لك المرتجي ظل الغمامه كلما» | «لك المر تجي ظل الغمامة كلما» | «لكالمرتجي» contains the emphatic lam, comparison kaf, article and participle. Both short clips split the initial syllables off as «لك». The syntax of 41–42 requires ‘I am like one hoping’, followed by the shade of the cloud. |
