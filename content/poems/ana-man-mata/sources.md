# سَأَلوني لِمَ لَم أَرثِ أَبي

## What the text is

- **A poem of al-Shawqiyyat, not lines from a play.** It stands in the third part of al-Shawqiyyat, «في المراثي» (Dar al-Awda, Beirut, 1988, pp. 154–156), under the heading «يرثي أباه», with the note «نظم هذه القصيدة حوالى سنة ١٨٩٧ يرثى بها والده الطيب الذكر المرحوم على بك شوقى رحمه الله». It is a self-contained elegy of 31 abyat with its own opening («سألوني: لِمَ لَمْ أرثِ أبي؟»); none of Shawqi's verse plays is involved, and no source puts it in one.
- **Shawqi's, in four sources that agree:** the Dar al-Awda al-Shawqiyyat above; Ibrahim al-Abyari's الموسوعة الشوقية, vol. 5, pp. 352–354, poem 29, headed «وقال يرثي والده علياً سنة (١٨٩٧م)»; Shawqi Dayf, شوقي شاعر العصر الحديث, p. 53, who quotes our 12–15 as «قوله فى أبيه المتوفى سنة ١٨٩٥»; and Muhammad al-Majdhub, «شعر شوقي في ميزان النقد», p. 88, who quotes our 3–4 as Shawqi's elegy for his father («لنستمع إليه يرثي والده»). The upload title «أنا من مات ومن مات أنا | أحمد شوقي» and the video's description («سجلت هذه الأبيات من قصيدة لأحمد شوقي في رثاء والده») say the same. No source gives it to anyone else.
- **The father:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 1, p. 136 (shamela 12286/346, `alam.txt`) names the poet «أحمد شوقي بن علي بن أحمد شوقي»; the Dar al-Awda note calls the father «على بك شوقى»; al-Abyari «والده علياً». Shawqi Dayf (p. 14) says that the father, Ali, was made an inspector of the Khedive's estates (الخاصة الخديوية).
- **The date:** «حوالى سنة ١٨٩٧» (the Dar al-Awda note), «سنة (١٨٩٧م)» (al-Abyari), the father's death in 1895 (Shawqi Dayf, p. 53). The sources give the Christian year only, so the intro gives no Hijri year.
- **Title:** the first sadr, by the owner's rule. «يرثي أباه» is the diwan's heading, a description rather than a name the books use, and no source calls the poem by any name. The upload's «أنا من مات ومن مات أنا» is the bayt the video was named after (our 3).

Between the m of «أم» and the ā there is a voiced consonant with no frication (236.52–236.62 s), as this reciter's ذ in «بَعدَ ذا» also has none (149.86–149.92 s). The meter needs a long syllable there, «أم لا» would not parse, and all four witnesses have «ذا». The text keeps it.
- **23.1 «مُتُّ».** After the m (241.34–241.64 s; second saying 252.07–252.41 s) the vowel has F1 390–515 Hz and F2 780–960 Hz in both sayings, the damma of «مُهجَةً» (F2 700–815 Hz), not a kasra. Al-Abyari prints «مِتُّ»; Dar al-Awda and Kalimat leave the mim bare. Both forms are Arabic (*Lisan al-Arab*, vol. 2, p. 91: «وقالوا: مِتَّ تموت»), and «مُتُّ» is the regular one from ماتَ يموتُ. The text follows the reciter, and the note gives al-Abyari's «مِتُّ».
- **23.2 «أَنُلَقّى».** The meter needs four syllables (ʾa-nu-laq-qā: فَعِلاتُن), so Dar al-Awda's «أنلقَى» has lost its shadda, which Kalimat and al-Abyari print. Al-Abyari vowels the nun with a fatha, «أَنَلَقَّى», which is no form of the verb (نَتَلَقّى does not drop its ta). The reciter says a damma: after the nun (245.76–245.88 s; 256.56–256.64 s) the vowel has F1 480–530 Hz and F2 960–1,250 Hz (mostly near 1,050), against 1,360–1,560 Hz for the fathas on either side of it in the same word. «أَنُلَقّى» is the passive of لَقّى («ولَقّاه الشيءَ وأَلقاه إليه», *Lisan*, vol. 15, p. 255; «وما يُلَقّاها», p. 256), and al-Abyari's own gloss, «أنلقى: أي أنودع», reads it as a passive. The note gives his «أَنَلَقَّى».
- **23.2 «أَو».** Every print has «أم», the «أم» that after a question's hamza asks which of two (Ibn Hisham, *Awdah al-Masalik*, vol. 3, p. 333, shamela 11825/1004, `awdah.txt`). The reciter says «أَو» in both sayings: both clip transcribers hear «او» both times; the aligner prefers it by 6.2 and 5.1 over the half (the same test prefers «أم» by 4.9 in 19.2 «أَم شَرِبتَ», where he says it); and between the a and the ḥ (248.42–248.74 s; 259.26–259.58 s) there is a vowel-loud glide with F1 about 450 Hz and F2 760–800 Hz, a w, where his m of «أم» in 19.2 (201.62–201.78 s) is a nasal murmur 6–10 dB weaker with F2 about 1,400 Hz. The text follows the reciter; the note gives the editions' «أم».
- **12.2 «وُدُّهُ».** Both transcribers hear «هده». The half opens with voicing from its first frame and F2 650–830 Hz (125.07–125.21 s), a rounded w-u, and «هَدَّهُ/هُدُّهُ الصِّدقُ» means nothing here; the aligner can't tell them apart (−0.3 to +1.8). The text keeps «وُدُّهُ», which every witness has and which the second half answers («وَوُدُّ النّاسِ»).

## The meter

Ramal, its sadr ending فاعِلُن (mahdhuf) and its ajuz فاعِلانْ (maqsur), rawiy ن, a bound rhyme (قافية مقيّدة) with a ya before the nun; al-Abyari: «من الرمل، والقافية من المترادف». Every half was scanned with `media/research/ana-man-mata/tools/scan_ramal.py`, a wrapper of `/home/alifadel/.cache/shw-tmp/b4/tools/scan.py` that reads the rhyme's closing silent nun as the overlong last syllable of the maqsur foot (scan.py itself marks it X on every ajuz): all 46 halves scan. Licences: «أَنا» short at the start of 3.1 and long at its end; the pronoun ha long at the end of 2.1 «عِقبانِهِ», 6.1 «وَصفِهِ», 12.1 «فارَقتُهُ» and 15.1 «يَدِهِ» (the sadr's anceps).

The 23 rhyme words end in a silent ya and a silent nun («الفَرقَدَين», «مَين» …), as the rhyme is said in pause and as the meter needs. `tashkeel.ts` flags each as «two silent letters in a row (ين)», and `content:check` repeats the 23 warnings. They are the rhyme, not slips: written with the case vowel («الفَرقَدَينِ») the ajuz would not scan and would not be what the reciter says.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Where the sharh goes beyond the commentaries** (the commentators are silent): 1, the farqadan as a first image of the parting of father and son; 5.2, the two as one corpse in two shrouds when the son dies; 10, the second reading (every child takes its parents' religion), which applies Dar al-Awda's note on 11; 14, 15 and 17–19, which the notes don't explain beyond words; 20, «عين … مني ومنك» read as one eye shared by the two, frozen in the father by death and in the son by the patience of 21; 22, the night and day set against the farqadan of 1; 23, the grave set against the shrouds of 5. 3's echo of al-Hallaj's «أنا مَن أهوى ومَن أهوى أنا / نحن روحان حللنا بدنا» is the drafter's observation from the shared form and meter; no source on this poem makes it.
