# ألا اعتزليني اليوم خولة أو غضي

Osama al-Nahari, published 30 April 2023. Keep 6.65–534.60 s of the 537.867 s original. The recording runs from the appeal to Khawla to the maxim ending ترضي. It gives one pass through all sixty bayts in al-Jundi’s sequence. The close of 51 repeats an image from 7 and 42 as part of a different bayt. The direct address أبا منذر begins six distinct verses, 46–51. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ddLW1aztDtw)

## The long recension and its authorship

- Ali al-Jundi, *al-Sha'ir al-jahili al-shabb Tarafa ibn al-Abd, tahqiq wa-dirasa li-shi'rihi wa-shakhsiyyatihi* (Dar al-Fikr al-Arabi), no. 39, pp. 197–212, continuous numbers 578–637. Number 638 on p. 212 belongs to the next poem. The note on p. 197 reports a manuscript rubric giving transmission from Abu Amr, rejection by al-Mufaddal, al-Asma'i’s refusal to authenticate the verses, and transmission by Abu Ubayda. In the same note al-Jundi identifies a collection of al-Hamasa verses credited to a man of Asad. The traditional catalogue attribution used here is to Tarafa for the long recension. The two introductions qualify it. The biographical story about the letter is also in al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam* III, 225. - Max Seligsohn, *Dîwân de Ṭarafa ibn al-ʿAbd al-Bakrî* (Paris, Émile Bouillon, 1901), Arabic appendix IV, pp. 137–143. The appendix adds manuscripts beyond those used for al-A'lam’s commentary. Its 58 numbered bayts lack recorded 33 and 51. Its no. 44 occupies the place of our 45 but names Rabiʿa and gives both halves differently. Its opening حرباء for حدباء, no. 40 على الموت and no. 55 ويلبس قوم illustrate the extent of variation in the long witnesses. At its no. 56 the reader can check the recorded names and words حد أرضه / كعب بن سهل against al-Jundi’s جو أرضه / عوف بن عمرو. - Abu Ali al-Qali, *al-Amali*, II, pp. 260–261, introduces al-Hakam ibn Abdal al-Asadi reciting before al-Hajjaj. The fourteen consecutive verses map to our 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 22, 13, 14, 16, 15, 7, 8, 9 and 21. At our 22 the second half reads «ومثل الذي أوصى به والدي أمضي», explicitly identifying Abdal as the speaker’s father. His versions of 8–9 have ودّي, سيبي and فوارع تبري العظم. The recording’s long version keeps the name عبدل in both halves of 22. Al-Hamasa al-basriyya also explicitly names al-Hakam as author of our fifth bayt (II, no. 206, p. 79). - Mahdi Muhammad Nasir al-Din, ed., *Diwan Tarafa ibn al-Abd*, third edition (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1423 AH / 2002 CE), pp. 53–54, groups nine verses under «خذوا حذركم». They correspond to our 1, 49, 46, 55, 37, 56, 57, 58 and 41. It supports على الغدر at 41.2. In 58 it gives a third set of readings, جو داره / عوف بن سعد. Treating that group as the extent would lose most of what al-Nahari recites. ## Metre and rhyme

The opening of this tawil ode is articulated *ʾalaʿta / zilīnil-yaw / ma khawla / tu ʾaw ghuḍḍī | faqad na / zalat ḥadbā / ʾu muḥka / matul-ʿaḍḍī*. Both halves give فعولُ مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعيلن, with the opening sadr echoing the ajuz. In middle bayt 29 the sadr *walākin / nanī aḥmī / dhimāra / ʿashīratī* gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعلن. The answering *wayadfa / ʿu man rakaḍ / tu dūna / humū rakḍī* gives فعولُ مفاعلن فعولُ مفاعيلن. The rawiy is ض, with a sustained kasra. Lexical and possessive final yā can both supply the same sound. The model’s completed rhyme spellings in yā or hā in isolated clips are checked against the syntax and the continuous material.

## Reading the recorded version

The collation, independent verse lists, page scans, full transcripts and equal-word MMS comparisons are preserved under `media/research/ala-itazilini` and `media/work/ddLW1aztDtw`. The following points concern places where identifications, the shape of the performance or the translation depend on the choice.

- **3.1–3.2, 11.2, 15.1, 17.1.** The Jundi reading مرزّأ denotes someone repeatedly visited by loss, against Seligsohn’s مدرأ. At 3.2 the recording has first-person بغضي. Al-Jundi prints على بُغضِ and Seligsohn على البُغضِ. The longer حيازيم, the breast of the riding beast, accompanies the saddle girth in 11.2. The recording and al-Qali give همومي in 15, where Seligsohn gives أموري. Both long printings give سَورة, the surge of anger, in 17.
- **22, 28, 33.** There is one statement about Abdal’s counsel, occupying two halves. At 28 the two full transcripts give شجي, and both long printings have the adjective شجيّ. Al-Jundi specifically glosses it as مشغول and العَدْو as running to attack, which connects the phrase to the following charge on horseback. At 33.2 both continuous transcripts give يُفضي. Al-Jundi prints the verb at no. 610 and glosses it as «يمضي ويذهب». Seligsohn omits this verse. [Al-Jundi, p. 205](https://archive.org/details/20210119_20210119_1956/page/n204/mode/1up)
- **35.1.** The full Cohere and Audar transcriptions and al-Nahari’s caption at 307 s give السُّنبُلِ الوَرثِ. Al-Jundi, p. 205, no. 612, and Seligsohn, p. 140, no. 34, both print thāʾ. The preceding verse in each scan has فاضت, which shows the two horizontally paired dots of tāʾ beside the triangular group of three in الورث. Al-Jundi glosses الورث as الطري and reports الورد in manuscript د. Al-Saghani and al-Zabidi likewise define الوَرْثُ as «الطريّ من الأشياء». The tested tāʾ spelling scored within 0.85 of the adopted thāʾ. [Al-Jundi, verse and gloss](https://archive.org/details/20210119_20210119_1956/page/n204/mode/1up) · [Seligsohn, no. 34](https://archive.org/details/3514pdf/page/n321/mode/1up) · [Al-Saghani, al-Takmila I, 393](https://shamela.ws/book/18254/391) · [Al-Zabidi, Taj al-arus V, 382](https://shamela.ws/book/7030/2563)
- **37–42.** At 37 al-Nahari’s «بني عمنا والقرض نجزيه بالقرض» restores direct first-person repayment, where al-Jundi has تجزوه. At 38.2 the closing word is رَمَضِ, the scorching heat of the cup in the message to Bakr. For 41.2 he uses على الغدر, in the short printing and the apparatus to al-Jundi no. 618, in place of the main long texts’ على الموت. For 42.1 al-Jundi’s gloss proposes al-Mutalammis for the surviving Yashkuri. The account of the royal letter and the poem’s own references to al-Nu'man are distinguished in the bilingual notes.
- **48–60.** For 48.1 al-Nahari gives نزالها. Al-Jundi prints زيالها and reports نزالها in his gloss. At 51.2 the camel image recurs with second-person حِدت, referring to the king who shrank from fidelity. At 54.2 the continuous transcripts give بالخفض, “bringing low”, supported by Seligsohn. Al-Jundi explains his الحبض as injustice and treachery. At 55.1 both long printings give لميت, while the recording gives لهالك, also in the short printing. In 57 the people of the two places receive the figurative garment of showers of death. Seligsohn supports the passive يُلبَس قوم and al-Jundi prints تُلبِس قوماً. At 58 the name, the frontier and the verb follow Seligsohn. The video identifies Taghlib as the subject of تميل. At 56.2 Nasir al-Din’s gloss gives the sense “a great army” for عرض, while al-Jundi explains the place sense “a quarter or district”. The long-form ending is attested in his last two verses and al-Jundi’s 636–637.
