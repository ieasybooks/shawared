## The stage text

Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, I, Cairo, Matba'at Misr, «أبو الهول», pp. 153–166. On p. 153 the curtain rises on a Sphinx at the opening of the Azbakiyya Garden theatre, addressed by a man. P. 166 supplies a new stage direction. A young man and a young woman emerge from the statue’s breast, stand before it and sing the following nashid. This sung passage has no separate printed title. **Twelve** two-column bayts make up the song. The preceding monologue belongs to the larger theatrical piece. The metadata counts the entire song, and the reader titles it by its opening sadr. The appeal to Karnak and the Pyramid as witnesses, the plural voice and the pairing of throne and pulpit in the commentary follow the text and stage direction. [Setting on printed p. 153](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikisource/ar/1/1a/الشوقيات1.pdf#page=155) · [Duet on printed p. 166](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikisource/ar/1/1a/الشوقيات1.pdf#page=168)

The later Dar al-Awda *Shawqiyyat*, I, p. 145, prints the same direction, twelve bayts and the same identical final halves. Two columns remain visible even where «ولنجعل مصر هي الدنيا» fills both. The 1933 Ministry of Education *Shawqiyyat lil-madaris*, pp. 83–96, identifies the 1922 opening of the theatre. Its abridgement ends the spoken address on p. 96 and starts «على قبر نابليون» on p. 97. It establishes the performance setting but supplies no textual witness to the duet. Its title page gives 1352 AH / 1933, although the Cornell catalogue says 1927. Yusuf Ilyan Sarkis, *Jamiʿ al-tasanif al-haditha*, p. 78, no. 680, lists the original 376-page Matbaʿat Misr volume of 1926 and its introduction by Muhammad Husayn Haykal. The dated cover of *al-Musawwar*, no. 80, reports the volume’s appearance on 23 April 1926. The stage direction and twelve pairs can also be read in Columbia’s copy, catalogued as 1936 with part II. Its first title leaf and that of the Wikimedia copy, catalogued as 1370 AH, carry the same press numbers without a year of impression written in full. Columbia p. 166 reproduces the same pairing down to the doubled last half. The Cairo errata pass from a correction on p. 161 to one on p. 169. ## Form, words and performance

In 6.1 «وَجِنا / نُلْخُلْ / دِ وَكَوْ / ثَرُهُو» gives ∪ ∪ – | – – | ∪ ∪ – | ∪ ∪ –. Six groups of four half-lines can be heard inside the twelve printed pairs. Each of the first five groups returns to the -ina cadence (يفدينا، مساعينا، رياحينا، أوالينا، يبنينا). Other half-lines rhyme locally, for instance «تاجا / وهّاجا / أبراجا». The last group turns to -ya and the double «الدنيا». Nun represents the recurring cadence in the single rawiy field.

The performance follows all 24 printed halves once, from the first word to the final sustained vowel, **0–60.058 s**. The final sentence is sung twice, once as 12.1 and once as 12.2. There is no extra performed reprise. The continuous Cohere run appended an ophthalmology advertisement after the song. There is no spoken advertisement in the source ending. The final phones continue the second «الدنيا». At 2.1 «يُشيد» and «يَشيد» are acoustically close (0.58-point margin). The sound has /yu/ and the accusative «العزَّ», the glory the homeland raises, supports the transitive reading. In 7.1 «نتخذ» scores 2.85 above the isolated command «اتخذوا» suggested by Audar. The first person carries through the surrounding lines. At 10.2 «الأَوّل» scores 5.05 above «الأُوَل». The editions have the same bare consonants. In context it points to the work of those who came first.
