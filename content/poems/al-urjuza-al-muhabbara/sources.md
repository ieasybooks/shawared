## Editions

- **ديوان علي بن الجهم**, ed. Khalil Mardam Bek, in its «صلة التكملة», «المحبّرة في التاريخ», pp. 228–250: archive.org `20201105_20201105_2037`, leaves 227–249, read from the page images (transcribed with every footnote in `mardam.txt`). 330 abyat, voweled in places. Mardam set the text from al-Samawi's copy («الأصل»), which al-Samawi corrected against «a manuscript copied from two old copies, and a printed copy that was confiscated and destroyed but for remnants» (his colophon, p. 250 n. 2). Mardam then collated it with the copy in al-Yamani's «كتاب الفرق», a manuscript in Abbas al-Azzawi's library in Baghdad (dated 29 January 1952, p. 250); its readings are written into the margins of this scan and marked «ع» here. The title «المحبّرة» is Mardam's: «في الأصل: (المخبرة). ولعل ما أثبتناه هو الأصح» (p. 228 n. 1).
- **al-Maqdisi, البدء والتاريخ** (as Abu Zayd al-Balkhi in Mardam's note): shamela 12318, vol. 2, pp. 85–86 (`bad.txt`). Abyat 3–24 of the Adam story, quoted with the heading «ويذكر هذه القصة ابن جهم في قصيدته»; Mardam's Takmila (p. 157, piece 68) printed these as «قصة خلق آدم».
- **Ibn Zafar, سلوان المطاع**, p. 122, as reported by al-Samawi in Mardam's note to p. 245: three abyat on Mu'awiya ibn Yazid ascribed to Ali ibn al-Jahm and missing from the diwan's text (254–256 here).
- **Shawqi Dayf, تاريخ الأدب العربي**, vol. 4, pp. 246–247: shamela 11996 (`dayf.txt`). Quotes abyat 3–8 and describes the poem; the intro quotes him.
- **Starting text:** the video's description for abyat 1–29.

`abyat_in_full` is 335: Mardam's 330, the two abyat «ع» adds and the reciter says (137, 165), and Ibn Zafar's three (254–256).

## Departures from Mardam's text

The reciter follows Mardam's text with «ع»'s readings in many places, and Mardam's own corrections in others. Each departure below is what the recording has; the word note on it gives the other reading.

- **«ع»'s readings:** 17.2 نَسلَهُما (also al-Maqdisi; Mardam أبناهما); 93.2 وَلَم يَزَل بِخَلقِهِ; 96.2 تَأمُرُ; 114 عَنها … فَرَحَلوا; 120.1 فَأَبطَنَت; 150.1 مِن خَوفِهِ; 166.1 وَسَأَلوهُ; 194.2 وَهُوَ; 201.2 الأَريافَ; 206.1 تَكثيرُ; 228.1 دَعا مَنِ اِجتَباهُ; 239.2 خاتِمَةً دَلَّت; 253.1 وَإِنَّما; 261.2 أُمَراءِ; 271.1 وَثُلثَي; 280.1 مِن بَعدِ شَهرَينِ وَبَعدِ; 306.1 وَأَشهُرا; 312 ثَمانِيَة … باقِيَة; 313.2 وَخَمسَةً … لِلحِمامِ; 332.1 فَكانَ ثاني العَشرِ (which Mardam calls right).

- **Mardam's conjectures, which the reciter says:** 42.1 توبيلُ (text يوئيل); 194.1 وَقَبلَ ذاكَ; 197.2 غازون; 202.2 شَمرُ اِبنُ رَعشٍ (text شمر بن عبس; Mardam: «والصواب شمر يرعش»); 208.2 تَفخيمِها; 214.1 يَقضي; 242.2 بَناتُ; 258.2 بِراهِطٍ; 279.1 الغافِلُ; 311.1 غافِلِ.
- **The reciter's own:** 14.1 مِنَ الجِنانِ (Mardam عن; al-Maqdisi من); 20.1 اِستَمَلّا وَأَحَبّا (Mardam استمل وأحب; the description and the second transcriber have the dual); 116.2 بِأَهلِهِ (Mardam في أهله); 157.2 مِن بَعدِهِم (Mardam من ربّهم); 163.2 وَماتَ عالي رَأسُهُم (see below); 225.2 لِأَمرِهِ (Mardam لعزّه); 277.2 فَامتَدَّت لَهُ الأَيّامُ (Mardam فاعتدّت له الأقوام; «ع» فامتدّت به الأعوام); 296.1 حَتّى (Mardam ثمّ); 305.1 قاتَلوهُ (Mardam قتلوه, which does not scan). Each of 116.2, 157.2, 225.2 and 296.1 is heard so by both transcribers and wins by score (table below).
- **Kept as the base text has it, as the reciter says it:** 90.1 زَجيلُها (Mardam: لعله رجيلها; «ع» رجيلها); 107 جُمجُما … أَنهُرَ ما (Mardam: «كذا بالأصل ولعل الصواب زمزما، يقال ماء زمزم أي كثير … وهو الصواب كما في ع»; «انهرما: كذا»). The reciter voices the first «jumjumā» (scores below; the first transcriber writes «جُمْجُمَا»), which has no sense here; the note gives Mardam's «زمزما». The text reads the second as «أَنهُرَ ما», rivers of water, which scans and makes sense of the reciter's sounds.
- **163.2:** Mardam prints «ومات الياد اسمهم من الجذع», a corruption; «ع» has «وما تعالى رأسهم من الجزع». The reciter's words sound the same either way («wa-māta ʿālī» / «wa-mā taʿālā»); the second transcriber hears «ومات على», the score favours «وَماتَ عالي», and al-Tabari's story of Eli («عيلي»), the priest who fell from his seat and died when the Ark was taken (vol. 1, p. 470, `tabari-eli.txt`), gives the line its sense. The text reads «وَماتَ عالي رَأسُهُم»; the sharh gives «ع»'s reading.

## Harakat, spelling and meter

Every half was scanned as rajaz (three feet of mustaf'ilun and its variants, the last foot also maf'ulun or fa'ulun) with a scanner adapted from `/tmp/shw/b3/tools/scan.py` for rajaz, the long vowel of the name of God, and the lengthening of a final pronoun vowel (هُمُ, ـهُ). Where the meter needs a licence the text shows it, as the reciter says it:

- **Hamzat al-qat' dropped:** 74.2 وَارفَخشَذٌ; 153.1, 200.1 بَني اِسرائيلِ, بَني اِسماعيلِ; 181.2 نَحوَ اَربَعينَ; 328.1 سِتَّةَ اَشهُر; 51.1 قَرا, 97.2 القُرانِ, 148.1 and 154.1 تَنَبّا.
- **Alif of wasl cut:** 21.1 إِبناً, 22.1 الإِبنَ, 39.1 إِبنُهُ, 162.1 إِبنُ الخُطوبِ, 260.1 إِبنُ الزُّبَيرِ.
- **«مئة» lightened:** 31.2 سَبعِ مِيَّة, 141.2 سِتُّ مِيَّة, 195.2 وَمِيَّتَينِ, 319.1 وَميتَي (Mardam prints «سبع مية», «ست مية», «ومائتين», «وميتي»; `mardam.txt` normalizes the first to «مئة»). With the standard «مِئَة» none of these halves scans; the transcript writes all four as «…ميت…».
- **Tanwin on a diptote, or none on a triptote:** 8.1 آدَمٍ; 54.1 مَتّوشَلَخٌ; 74.2 وَارفَخشَذٌ وَلاوِذٌ; 130.2 and 132.2 يوسُفٍ; 217.2 بِمَكَّةٍ; 224.1 يَثرِبٍ; 241.2 عَشرَةً; 289.2 أَفاضِلٍ; and without: 32.1 شيثَ, 76.1 وَعادُ, 230.1 أَبو بَكرِ, 265.2 مُصعَبَ, 303.1 مُحَمَّدَ.

- **88.2 does not scan:** the reciter says «وَقالوا أَخلِص» (so Mardam; both transcribers, and the score below), which gives u--- for the first foot; «ع»'s «وَقيلَ» scans. The text follows the recording.
- **House style:** `tashkeel.ts` strips the kasra of بِ and لِ before يوسُف (130.2, 132.2), treating the ي as a long vowel; the text keeps its output. Its four "two silent letters" warnings are rhyme words ending in a consonant (179, 264, 299).

## Commentary drawn on

- There is no classical commentary on the poem. The sharh draws on Mardam's notes for the readings and on al-Tabari's تاريخ الرسل والملوك (shamela 9783; the pages used are in `tabari-*.txt`) for the patriarchs, Ad and Thamud, Joshua, Elijah, Elisha, Eli, Samuel and David; on the Quran wherever the poem follows it, with sura and verse; on the Torah (Van Dyck's Arabic) where the poem's figures come from Genesis and Exodus; and on the standard dates for the caliphs.
- Where the poem's figures differ from al-Tabari's or the Torah's (31.2, 79.2, 131.2, 269.1), the sharh says so.
