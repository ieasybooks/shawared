## The poet

The poem is Baha' al-Din Zuhayr's in every source found, and no source gives it to anyone else:

- **the three printed diwans** below (Dar al-Ma'arif, Dar Sadir, Palmer) and the Abu Dhabi encyclopedia's text;
- **al-Umari, *Masalik al-Absar***, vol. 18, p. 158 (shamela 30189/6433; `masalik.txt`): abyat 4, 5, 9–12 and, after «منها», 28–29, headed «وله», inside his entry on «البهاء زهير بن محمد بن علي …» (entry 550, from vol. 18, p. 142; shamela 30189/6417, `masalik-entry.txt`). The editor's note: «من قصيدة قوامها ٣٢ بيتًا في ديوانه ٢٨٠ - ٢٨٢», Dar Sadir's pages;
- **Ibn Aydamir, *al-Durr al-Farid wa-Bayt al-Qasid***, vol. 6, pp. 327–328 (shamela 20762/2786–2787; `durr.txt`): bayt 3 under «زهير المصري», then «أبيات بهاء الدين زهير المصري، أوّلها: …», 30 abyat. The editor's note: «القصيدة في ديوان البهاء زهير: ٢١٦ - ٢١٨», Dar al-Ma'arif's pages. Bayt 21 is quoted again under «زهيرٌ المصريُّ» in vol. 7, p. 362 (shamela 20762/3285; `durr-3285.txt`), with «والخيرُ يُشكَرُ»; bayt 9 alone in vol. 8, p. 330 (shamela 20762/3759; editor: «ديوان بهاء زهير: ٢١٧»), and 30 alone under «زهيرٌ المصريُّ» in vol. 11, p. 146 (shamela 20762/4997), with «فَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ» (`_scratch/r2/wit/20762-*.txt`);
- **Ibn Darham, *Nuzhat al-Absar***, p. 150 (shamela 5375/150; `nuzhat.txt`): the whole poem, «ومن قوله وهي من فائق شعره:», in the selection that p. 140 opens «ويليه الاختيار من شعر البهاء زهير» (`_scratch/nuzhat-140.txt`). Shamela's card gives the compiler as عبد الرحمن بن عبد الله بن أحمد بن درهم (ت ١٣٦٢هـ), Dar al-Ibad, Beirut: a twentieth-century anthology, most likely drawn from a printed diwan, so not an independent witness. The attribution rests on al-Umari and Ibn Aydamir beside the diwans.

Books that quote abyat without a name: al-Baha' al-Amili, *al-Kashkul*, vol. 1, p. 39 (shamela 1571/37; `kashkul.txt`), abyat 15–19 and 21 under «ولبعضهم»; *Law'at al-Shaki* (shamela 37494/43, p. 46; `lawat.txt`), the same abyat inside a prose tale; Ibn Iyas, *Bada'i' al-Zuhur*, ed. Muhammad Mustafa, vol. 3, p. 303 and vol. 5, pp. 272–273 (shamela 30059/2221, 3166–3167), and *Uqud al-Juman*, p. 527 (shamela 30069/515), abyat 30–31 with «فلا يضرّك», each time after «كما يقال» against a prediction that failed (the sultan's fall because the feast fell on a Friday; the astrologers' word that Selim would not re-enter Istanbul); and Mar'i al-Karmi, *Ma Yaf'aluhu al-Atibba' wa-l-Da'un bi-Daf' Sharr al-Ta'un*, p. 53 (shamela 17414/20), abyat 30–32 with «يضرّك» and «والشرع أصدق» (`_scratch/r2/wit/`). The modern *Sayd al-Afkar* (shamela 8372/80, vol. 1, p. 84) quotes 21 under «بهاء الدين زهير», with «يُذكَرُ».

## Title

No source gives the poem a name. Dar al-Ma'arif's editors head it «وارحمتاه !» and Dar Sadir's «قلب محمّل بالمحبة», each edition's own headings for every poem; the upload calls it «دعوا الوشاة». The title is the first sadr.

## Editions

- **ديوان البهاء زهير**, ed. محمد أبو الفضل إبراهيم and محمد طاهر الجبلاوي (Dar al-Ma'arif): scan archive.org `3303pdf_202001`, leaves 215–217, printed pp. 216–218 (`col/maarif.txt`, typed from the page images). Heading «وقال من البسيط قافية المتواتر». 32 abyat, partly voweled, with variants from the editors' sources (ل, the Alexandria copy of 996 AH; ح, the Cairo lithograph of 1277 AH; Palmer) and one gloss (p. 218 n. 2, «الجدي والحمل: برجان من أبراج السماء»).
- **ديوان بهاء الدين زهير** (Dar Sadir, Beirut): scan archive.org `3439pdf_202001`, leaves 279–281, printed pp. 280–282 (`col/sadir.txt`). Heading «وقال من البسيط قافية المتراكب». 32 abyat, fully voweled, with variants from two copies, أ and ب, and two glosses (p. 281 n. 4 on الجدي والحمل; p. 282 n. 1, «المريخ وزحل: كوكبان مشهوران»).
- **E. H. Palmer's edition of the Arabic text** (Cambridge, 1876): scan archive.org `3440pdf12921876`, leaves 128, 127, 126, printed pp. 206–208 (`col/palmer.txt`). Heading «وقال من البسيط والقافية المتواتر». 32 abyat, voweled. His English verse translation, "A Reverie", is in vol. 2 (1877), pp. 234–238 (archive.org `poeticalworksofb02zuha`; `palmer-translation-ocr.txt`, read against the page images).
- **The old shamela's diwan** (book 4508, the Abu Dhabi encyclopedia's text), pp. 414–417 (`old-shamela-4508-p414-417.txt`, `col/mawsua.txt`): 32 abyat, partly voweled. One source, not an edition. It agrees with the text word for word.
- **Witnesses:** *Nuzhat al-Absar* (32 abyat), *al-Durr al-Farid* (30), *Masalik al-Absar* (8), *al-Kashkul* and *Law'at al-Shaki* (6 each), above. `scripts/collate.py` output: `_scratch/` (run `python3 -I scripts/collate.py content/poems/daw-al-wushata/poem.json media/research/daw-al-wushata/col/*.txt`).
- **Starting text:** Dar Sadir's voweled text, put in the house style and checked word by word against the page images of Dar al-Ma'arif and Palmer.

`abyat_in_full` is 32, the count in all three editions and the encyclopedia.

**The rhyme:** Dar Sadir names the qafiya المتراكب, Dar al-Ma'arif and Palmer المتواتر. Every rhyme here (يَنفَصِلُ, الرُّسُلُ, …) has three voweled letters between its last two silent ones, which is المتراكب; المتواتر has one. Al-Tanukhi, *al-Qawafi*, p. 70 (shamela 10911/12; `_scratch/r2/wit/tanukhi-qawafi.txt`): «المتراكب. وذلك أن يجتمع ثلاثة حروف متحركة بعدها ساكن» and «المتواتر وهو حرف واحد متحرك بعده ساكن». Every ajuz ends in fa'ilun with khabn (u u –), so Dar Sadir is right and the other two are wrong.

## Where the editions differ

The reciter follows Dar Sadir and the encyclopedia wherever Dar al-Ma'arif differs from them (20.1, 21.2), and Dar al-Ma'arif and Dar Sadir wherever Palmer differs (2.2, 11.1, 21.2).

- **20.1** «في أَمرٍ» (Dar Sadir, Palmer, encyclopedia, Durr; Dar al-Ma'arif's ل): Dar al-Ma'arif and Nuzhat have «لي أَمرٌ» (also Dar Sadir's ب).
- **21.2** «يُذكَرُ» (Dar Sadir, encyclopedia, Nuzhat, Kashkul, Law'at al-Shaki): Dar al-Ma'arif and Palmer print «يُشكَرُ» (Palmer «يُشْكَرُ», read on the page image). Dar al-Ma'arif's note (p. 217 n. 7) says «بلمر: يذكر», but Palmer's page has «يشكر», the same as their text; the note misreports him. Dar Sadir's أ has «يشكر»; Durr «والحُرُّ يُشكَرُ» in the poem and «والخيرُ يُشكَرُ» where it quotes the bayt alone.
- **2.2** «تَنفَعُني»: Palmer «تَقنَعُني», also Dar Sadir's أ; Durr «تعنيني».
- **11.1** «يا غائِبينَ وَفي قَلبي»: Palmer «يا راحِلينَ وَفي ذِكري»; Dar Sadir's أ and Masalik «يا راحلين وفي فكري»; Durr «يا راحلين وفي قلبي».
- **26.2** «سابِقٌ»: on Palmer's page the letter after the alif has a kasra and no visible dot («سايِق»), which could be «سائِقٌ»; the other editions have «سابق» and the audio settles it (below).
- **Variants the editions report** from their manuscripts: 5.2 «من عندكم», 8.2 «منكم», 13.2 «على حالي», 18.1 «وذاك», 22.2 «وطالما نفعت», 27.1 «إن حزنت له» (Dar al-Ma'arif's ل); 16.1 «وبلّغ» (its ح); 27.1 «حزنت» and 31.2 «يضرك» (Dar Sadir's ب).
- **Durr**, a modern print of an anthology, has many small differences, all tested or noted below; it lacks 3 (quoted just before as a single bayt) and 24, puts 25 after 10, runs 18.1 into 19.2, and gives 18 whole after the last bayt.
- **Found by the second reader:** Durr's «فَاللَّهُ يَحْكُمُ» in 30.2 (vol. 11, p. 146), and «يضرّك» (31.2) and «والشرع أصدق» (32.2) in Ibn Iyas and al-Karmi; scored below.

## Meter

Basit, rawiy ل. Every half scans with `scan.py` as basit (mustaf'ilun fa'ilun mustaf'ilun fa'ilun, with khabn), apart from 8.1, where the scanner doesn't know the particle «وا»: by hand, wā-raḥ-ma-tā | hu-li-ṣab | bin-qal-la-nā | ṣi-ru-hū, regular, but only with a licence: the ha of «وا رَحمَتاهُ» is the ha of pause (هاء السكت), which belongs to a pause and drops when the next word follows; here it is kept and voweled hu, as in al-Mutanabbi's «واحرَّ قلباهُ ممّن قلبُه شبِمُ». Al-Ghalayini, *Jami' al-Durus al-Arabiyya*, vol. 3, p. 163 (shamela 3284/714; `_scratch/r2/wit/ghalayini.txt`): «وأكثر ما تزاد الهاء في الوقف فان وصلت حذفتها، إلا في الضرورة، كقول المتنبي … ولك حينئذ ان تضمها». Without it the first foot is a syllable short. The word note says so. Opening bayt by hand: da-ʿul-wu-šā | ta-wa-mā | qā-lū-wa-mā | na-qa-lū / bay-nī-wa-bay | na-ku-mū | mā-lay-sa-yan | fa-ṣi-lū. The plural mim is long in 1.2 «وَبَينَكُمُ», 3.2 «إِلَيكُمُ», 5.1 «دِيارِكُمُ» and 12.2 «كَأَنَّهُمُ»; 27.2 «عَنهُ» may be read long (mustaf'ilun) or short (musta'ilun).

## Ear check

- **Alignment:** 12 weak words, all short (مِن, وا, لا, بِهِ, إِن, فَإِن, فَلا); the weakest is 26.1 «بِهِ» (0.21). The halves pass by ear.
- **`scripts/verify.py`:** 4 of 66 clips differ from the text, all the transcriber's (`_scratch/verify1.txt`):
  - 4.2 «كأنما» heard as «كان ما»;
  - 6.2 the rhyme stretched: «فيحتمله»;
  - 16.1 «وبالغ» heard as «و بالغ» (the same word);

## Commentary drawn on

There is no classical commentary on the diwan. The sharh is our own reading, checked against:

- **Palmer's verse translation** (vol. 2, pp. 234–238), which reads 23 as addressed to the listener ("Thou to whom these words I sing, / If thou wilt but list to me") and 25 as a figure for the poem: a fair maiden "May with beauty rest content, / How much fairer if she wear / Gems and ornament!" Where the sharh says who is meant in 23 (most likely the beloved, or any listener), what 22 implies (advice to the messenger, whose every attempt, «تُحاوِلُهُ» in 20, shares the root of «يَحتالُ … الحِيَلُ»; or the messenger as the poet's own stratagem), why letters and messengers fail in 2 (bayt 3 gives the size; secrecy is the other reason), and how 14.2 parses («خُلقي» the subject of «عنه لستُ أنتقل», or of «هيهات», far from betrayal), it goes beyond any source; each gives both readings.
- **The editions' glosses:** Dar al-Ma'arif p. 218 n. 2 and Dar Sadir p. 281 n. 4 (الجدي والحمل), Dar Sadir p. 282 n. 1 (المريخ وزحل).
- **Ibn Manzur, *Lisan al-Arab*** (shamela 1687; `lisan.txt`): ودع, vol. 8, pp. 383–384 («وقولهم: دع هذا أي اتركه», 1.1); وشى, vol. 15, p. 392 (1.1); نشر, vol. 5, p. 206, with Abu al-Duqaysh's definition (5.2); عذل, vol. 11, p. 437 (7); صبب, vol. 1, p. 518 («صَبِبْتُ إليه صبابة، فأنا صبٌّ أي عاشقٌ مشتاق», 8.1); غزل, vol. 11, p. 492 («الغَزَلُ: حديث الفتيان والفتيات», 10.2); شيم, vol. 12, p. 329 (14.1); هيه, vol. 13, p. 553 (14.2); بوح, vol. 2, p. 640 (15.1); همم, vol. 12, p. 620 («والمُهِمّات من الأمور: الشدائد المُحرِقة», 15.2; «همّ بالشيء: نواه وأراده وعزم عليه», 26.1); ملل, vol. 11, p. 628 (17.2); كفأ, vol. 1, p. 139 (21.1); بلغ, vol. 8, p. 420 («بالَغَ … إذا اجتهد في الأمر», 16.1); خلب, vol. 1, p. 364 (24.1); سوا, vol. 14, p. 411 (لا سيّما, 25.2); حلي, vol. 14, p. 194, and حلل, vol. 5, p. 94 (25.2); ريب, vol. 1, p. 442 («ريب الدهر: صروفه وحوادثه», 26.2); وني, vol. 15, p. 415 (26.1); ريث, vol. 2, p. 157 (29.2); جدي, vol. 14, p. 135, and حمل, vol. 11, p. 181 (30.2); خنس, vol. 6, p. 72, for Saturn and Mars among the five planets (31.2).

The intro says only that the father's line ends with this poem's half, not that he took it from Zuhayr.

## Review

Checked by the drafter, then read again independently on 2026-11-18: every half letter by letter against the page images of Dar Sadir (pp. 280–282), Dar al-Ma'arif (pp. 216–218) and Palmer (pp. 206–208), cut into strips and read as images (`_scratch/r2/img/`), with Dar Sadir's 31.2 and its notes zoomed; shamela searched again on rare halves for witnesses the draft lacked (four found, `_scratch/r2/wit/`); the new readings scored on the audio (`_scratch/r2/tests-r2.txt`, `vbatch-r2.txt`); every half scanned with `scan.py` and 1–3, 5, 10, 12, 14 and 8.1 by hand; every boundary of the timeline checked against the level (`_scratch/r2/edges2.py`); the cut against the transcript; every sharh, translation and note against the grammar of its bayt, the lexicons and the other reading's evidence; the intro and every credit against its page; the poet's other two poems for names and editions. The text, all 66 halves, stands: it is Dar Sadir's word for word, and every departure of the other editions is in the table with its score. Fixed:

- **Audio:** 13.1, 13.2 and 14.1 lit 0.4–0.6 s early, during the previous half's rhyme; the timeline now follows the voice (Ear check).
- **Intro:** Ibn Darham, whom the draft put first as if a classical critic, is a compiler who died in 1362 AH (1943 CE), now named as such and last; the draft had him cast aside the slanderers' talk about his loved ones («يطرح كلام الوشاة فيهم», "whose slanderers he dismisses"), where 1 asks the loved ones themselves to ignore what the slanderers said and passed on; Ibn Iyas's two uses of 30–31 against failed predictions added.
- **Poet:** Nuzhat al-Absar no longer counted as an independent witness of the attribution; Ibn Iyas, al-Karmi, Durr vol. 8 and 11 and Sayd al-Afkar added.
- **Meaning:** the English sharh of 31 said Mars and Saturn "were supposed to bring" good fortune; they are the two unlucky planets (as the Arabic says). 2 no longer says messengers can't be trusted, which jars with the trusted messenger of 15–20: the reason the next bayt gives (too much to carry) comes first, secrecy second. 22 now also gives the reading the text points to (the maxim urges the messenger, praised in 20 for every «تُحاوِلُهُ», to use «الحِيَل»), 14 the parse of «خُلقي» with «هيهات», 23 the wider addressee; all three marked as readings.
- **Notes:** 8.1 «وا رَحمَتاهُ» now names the ha as the ha of pause kept and voweled for the meter (al-Ghalayini), the licence the draft's meter section missed; a note on 21.2 «يُذكَرُ» gives the «يُشكَرُ» of Dar al-Ma'arif and Palmer, in both languages; 31.2 gives the «يضرّك» of Dar Sadir's ب and Ibn Iyas.
- **English:** the title and 1.1 translate «وَما نَقَلوا» ("what they passed on"), which the title dropped and the translation gave as "carried".
- **Sources:** 30.2 «يَحكُمُ» (Durr vol. 11, −33.9) and 32.2 «أَصدَقُ» (Durr, al-Karmi; −9.7 and −6.1) scored; earlier evidence for 16.2 (Miskawayh, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Khallikan) and 31.2 (al-Qazwini) beside the draft's; al-Tanukhi cited for the rhyme name.
- **Left as the drafter had them, rechecked:** 21.2 «يُذكَرُ» (Dar Sadir, the encyclopedia, Nuzhat, Kashkul, Law'at al-Shaki and Sayd al-Afkar; both transcribers; the scorer prefers it although it scores ذ badly; Palmer's page has «يُشْكَرُ», so Dar al-Ma'arif's note «بلمر: يذكر» is wrong, confirmed on the image); the coin tosses (3.2, 5.1, 10.1, 14.2, 16.1, 24.1, 25.1, 26.2, 31.2), where the text has every edition or the voweled majority and the transcribers; المتراكب; the plural mim long in 1.2, 3.2, 5.1, 12.2 and the silent ت of «الكُتبُ» in 2.2, which the meter needs (mustaf'ilun lal-kut-bu-tan).
