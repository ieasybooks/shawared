## Attribution

The poem is al-Amjad's on every witness found; no source gives it to anyone else.

- **His diwan.** Gharib's edition (below) prints it as poem 31 with the editor's note «وردت هذه القصيدة في النسخ كلها»: it is in all five manuscripts he used, the oldest of them copied in Damascus by Uthman ibn Ahmad on 23 Dhu al-Hijja 633 AH, five years after the poet's death (introduction, p. 11). The diwan itself is attested as his by Ibn Khallikan (*Wafayat al-A'yan*, vol. 2, p. 453, shamela 1000/925: «وله ديوان شعر»), al-Safadi (*al-Wafi bi-l-Wafayat*, vol. 10, p. 190, shamela 6677/2571: «له ديوان شعر موجود»), Ibn Shakir (*Fawat al-Wafayat*, vol. 1, p. 226, shamela 1003/224: «له ديوان شعر موجود بأيدي الناس»), Sibt ibn al-Jawzi (*Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 22, p. 310, shamela 23644/10648: «وله ديوان كبير») and al-Zirikli (*al-A'lam*, vol. 2, p. 76, shamela 12286/1154, who saw the Khalidiyya copy in Jerusalem).
- **None of the biographers quotes this poem**: Ibn Khallikan, al-Safadi, Ibn Shakir, Sibt, Ibn Wasil (*Mufarrij al-Kurub*, vol. 4, pp. 284–286, shamela 16362/1552–1554) and al-Dhahabi (*Tarikh al-Islam*, vol. 45, pp. 305–306, shamela 12397/21118–21119) each quote other pieces. **Abu Shama**'s *al-Dhayl ala al-Rawdatayn* (ed. al-Kawthari, archive.org `moharram1000_gmail_20171122_1224`, printed pp. 159–160, read from the page images) does not record al-Amjad's death in its text for 628; only the editor's note on p. 160 adds it from Ibn Kathir. *Al-Rawdatayn* names him only as one of the princes with al-Afdal (vol. 4, p. 422, shamela 6631/1745).
- **Anthologies:** shamela's full-text search finds no book that quotes any of eight rare halves of the poem («قمرية تسجع», «يرق لي المربع», «تزحزح عن برقه البرقع», «أجوب الظلام وأهواله», «عن الجزع من بينها أجزع», «يهيجني رسمها البلقع», «أبعد المشيب أروم الشفيع», «إلى أن أقض بي المضجع»); the old shamela's poetry shelf has no diwan of al-Amjad. The online poetry sites (poetsgate.com 111239, aldiwan.net 15215, madarisweb.com 79620) all give it to al-Malik al-Amjad.
- **Title:** the sources give the poem no name, so the title is the first sadr.

## Editions

- **Gharib Muhammad Ali Ahmad**, ed., ديوان الملك الأمجد بهرام شاه الأيوبي (Egypt, 1991, as Sibt's editor gives it: *Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 22, p. 310, n. 3): archive.org `20260226_20260226_2311`, poem 31 on printed pp. 105–108 (leaves 104–107), headed «وله رحمه الله» [المتقارب], 44 abyat. **Read from the page images**, with the doubtful places cropped and enlarged (`media/research/ala-al-bani-qumriyya/_scratch/crops/`); my transcription with every vowel that mattered and the footnotes is `gharib.txt`. The print is partly voweled. The introduction (pp. 9–15, leaves 8–14) describes the five manuscripts and their sigla: ر Manchester, John Rylands 457, copied 633 AH, the base text; م Mosul, Awqaf library; خ Jerusalem, Khalidiyya, copied 1028 AH; ت Dar al-Kutub, Taymur 1129; د Dar al-Kutub 6720 adab, copied 1306 AH. His footnotes on this poem give only manuscript variants, no glosses.
- **poetsgate.com**, poem 111239 (`poetsgate.txt`), posted by the site in 2012 and naming no source; the video's description is the same text with the same typos («أذى البينِ ن لو انّها», «وأحفِزُها ن»). 43 abyat: it lacks the edition's 29. - **17.1:** Gharib prints «أرقَلتُ» (first person). It breaks the meter (the last foot «قَلتُ» is - u where the meter needs u -) and the grammar («لَما أرقلتُ إليها بيَ الأينقُ» leaves «الأينق» with no verb), so the text has «أَرقَلَت» with the camels as subject; audar hears «أرقلت», cohere «أَرْقَلَاتُ» (the stretched end of the sadr), and the score leans the same way (`variants6.txt`).
- **Where the reciter departs from the edition,** the word note gives the edition's reading (3.1, 18.2, 19.1, 20.1 twice, 22.2, 23.2, 25.2, 33.2, 34.1, 35.1, 38.1, 38.2); 7.1 and 17.2 note the manuscripts' readings. Only 18.2 «شُقَّتَها», 22.2 «وَأَحفِرُها», 33.2 «هَجَرونا» and 38.1 «وَحَدَّثتُها» depart from both Gharib and poetsgate; 22.2 departs from every manuscript the editor reports as well.
- **35.1 «قَنَعتُ»:** in the sense «I was content» Lisan has «قَنِعَ» (vol. 8, p. 297, shamela 1687/4145), and «قَنَعَ يَقنَعُ قُنوعًا» for «to beg»; it adds Ibn al-Sikkit's remark «ومن العرب من يجيز القنوع بمعنى القناعة». The note says so.
- **22.2 «وَأَحفِرُها»:** «I dig her» makes no sense; Gharib's emendation «أحفزها» («I urge her on from behind», Lisan vol. 5, p. 337, shamela 1687/2921) is what the sharh explains, as the note says. Ms. ر has «اخفرها», خ and ت «احضرها».

## Commentary drawn on

- **No commentary on this poem exists that I could find**, and Gharib's notes on it give only variants. The sharh is the drafter's reading, built word by word on the lexicons below; where it interprets (the subject of «سحّت» and «جدّدت» in 2 being «الأدمع»; «به» in 3 being the ruin; the list «حشًى … ونارُ … وطرفٌ» running from 10 to 12; «لواغبَ» and «جوانحَ» as circumstantial accusatives describing the camels; the ruin as the speaker's «it» in 9; youth as «الذي يشفع» in 30, which 32 states), that is the drafter's reading of the grammar, not a commentator's.
