# يا دارُ ما فَعَلَت بِكِ الأَيّامُ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari, uploaded 12 March 2025. The 170.086-second source opens with silence and ends with the decay of «الأيّام». The edit keeps 2.800–167.900 s, giving 165.100 s at −21.4 LUFS. He recites all twenty bayts once and in the order of the two complete printed witnesses. The two versions of the first ajuz in Wagner occupy the same place in the poem. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H4tyElRsIAk)

## Witnesses, commentary and occasion

- Abu Nuwas, *Diwan Abi Nuwas*, ed. and comm. Ahmad Abd al-Majid al-Ghazali, Cairo, 1953, pp. 407–409, under al-Amin and the heading «ملك أغرّ». Page 407 gives bayts 1–4, p. 408 gives 5–14 and p. 409 gives 15–20 before the next poem. This is the recension al-Nahari performs. Al-Ghazali explains the well bucket and herd at 4, the promise to the mounts at 8 and the posture, belt and court assembly at 15. [Title page](https://archive.org/details/20200914_20200914_1734/page/n1/mode/1up) · [Opening](https://archive.org/details/20200914_20200914_1734/page/n446/mode/1up) · [Journey and audience](https://archive.org/details/20200914_20200914_1734/page/n447/mode/1up) · [Ending](https://archive.org/details/20200914_20200914_1734/page/n448/mode/1up)
- Abu Nuwas, *Diwan Abi Nuwas al-Hasan ibn Hani' al-Hakami*, ed. Ewald Wagner, enlarged second edition, Beirut, 2001, vol. I, poem 4, pp. 125–129. The heading on p. 125 names al-Amin. Page 126 prints the two openings. Page 127 gives the guest’s enduring reverence at 13 and explains the reading «فرع» at 15. An account on p. 128 says that al-Fadl ibn al-Rabi' described the poet to al-Amin, that Abu Nuwas recited this poem on admission and received a thousand dinars. We attribute the report to that account. Page 129 places the release of the camels beside the differing rewards for a mount in al-Shammakh and al-Farazdaq. [Title and edition](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n2/mode/1up) · [Poem heading](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n140/mode/1up) · [Opening](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n141/mode/1up) · [Audience scene and apparatus](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n142/mode/1up) · [Concluding prayer and report](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n143/mode/1up) · [The mounts](https://archive.org/details/20211008_20211008_1501/page/n144/mode/1up)
- Ibn al-Mu'tazz, *Tabaqat al-shu'ara'*, pp. 211–212, calls the mim-rhymed poem known to connoisseurs and quotes sixteen bayts. His sequence is our 1–10, 14–15, 17–20. His text gives independent early readings of the unveiling, the hall and the healing of the hearts. Ibn al-Athir, *al-Mathal al-sa'ir*, III, p. 101, quotes «لم تبق فيك بشاشة تستام». He admires the poem but discusses the ill omen of opening praise of a ruler by recalling a ruined home. [Ibn al-Mu'tazz, p. 211](https://shamela.ws/book/7867/195) · [p. 212](https://shamela.ws/book/7867/196) · [Ibn al-Athir](https://shamela.ws/book/10551/763)
- The poet profile follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, II, p. 225, for the full name, birth in Ahwaz, upbringing in Basra, visits to Baghdad and Egypt, and death in Baghdad. The main entry dates his death to 198 AH (814 CE). Its continuation also records 195 and 196 AH and quotes al-Jahiz on his knowledge of Arabic. ## Metre and rhyme

Kamil, with mim as rawiy. In bayt 1 the sadr scans ––⏑– / ⏑⏑–⏑– / ––– and the ajuz ––⏑– / ––⏑– / ⏑⏑––. At bayt 10 the sadr has three full ⏑⏑–⏑– feet. The ajuz has two full feet and a cut, contracted close (–––). The mim of «الأيامُ» carries the same prolonged final vowel as «الأوهامُ».
