# تفدّيك النفوس ولا تفادى

Osama al-Nahari recites all 56 bayts of *Saqt al-Zand* no. 33. The two printed sequences and both continuous transcriptions agree on the progress of the poem. The family speaks to Ali by name in 2, imagines the return of his camels and horse in 14–17, follows him from rivers to ships in 23–44, and asks him to stay among his kin in 45. The conditional «فإن تقبل … وإن تردد» closes both the book text and the recording. The 112 halves occur once each.

## Editions and commentary

- *Shuruh Saqt al-Zand*, the running commentaries of al-Tabrizi, Ibn al-Sid al-Batalyawsi and al-Khwarazmi, II, no. 33, printed pp. 770–809. The heading names the poet’s maternal uncle Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Sabika and his journey west. At the incipit the three commentaries preserve more than one analysis of «تفادى». The unsuffixed verb can stand for «تتفادى», with the first of the two tāʾs dropped. The apparatus gives «فأدن القرب» for the lemma «فأدن الوصل». Pp. 773–774 explain wind racing the traveller and «جلّى» as unveiling a bride. P. 783 identifies the “first surveyor” with Alexander in al-Tabrizi’s explanation. Al-Khwarazmi gives Jesus and reports Alexander as another identification. Pp. 785–789 identify the Sarat and Quwayq, explain what silver looks like through clear water and name the paired Dog Stars. [II, pp. 770–774](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n796/mode/2up) · [pp. 783–789](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n809/mode/2up)
- The same collection, pp. 794–796, supplies the change of subject from traveller to mounts in 35. Al-Tabrizi says «في توهم ضمير عائد إلى العيس» and al-Batalyawsi explicitly supplies «الناجيات». On pp. 797–798 al-Batalyawsi identifies the dry land of 39 with Egypt. Al-Khwarazmi pictures empty desert. The other land in 40 leads to the sea and its Byzantine sailors. Pp. 797–801 explain riding and sailing as counterparts, the comparison of dark ships with haircloth tents, the hunter who is hunted in turn, and the bright steel and piercing eyes. Pp. 805–809 explain the rain shared by both high ground and low, listening to friendly counsel and moderation just short of one’s object. The Arabic and English sharh draw on the three explanations at the individual bayts. [pp. 794–801](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n820/mode/2up) · [pp. 805–809](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n831/mode/2up)
- Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri, *Saqt al-Zand*, Dar Bayrut and Dar Sadir, Beirut, 1957, pp. 212–216. This separately printed text and its notes were compared across the whole ode. P. 212 opens «فأدن القرب» and has «ليالي الشهر» in 8. P. 213 explains the crow’s name and the horse’s leftover drink, and ends with Alexander and the untouched pillow. P. 214 divides «الفرات» and «الغريب» at the same metrical points as the running commentaries. Its 25.2 has the feminine pronoun and the feminine passive. P. 215 prints the collective «السفين» in 41 and «أن يُصادا» in 43. P. 216 ends at «فلم نأل اجتهادا». [pp. 212–216](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n211/mode/2up) · [p. 214](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n213/mode/1up)
- For 10.1 Ibn Manzur, *Lisan al-Arab*, s.v. موم, includes «الموماة», a waterless expanse, with the plural «الموامي». Beirut p. 213 also gives the singular in its footnote. [*Lisan*, موم](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/122/8015/موم) · [Beirut, p. 213](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n212/mode/1up)

## Wording and performance

At 1.2 the recorded «القرب» has the independent support of Beirut’s text and the *Shuruh* apparatus. It leads «الوصل» by more than eleven points. In 5.2 the two full machine passes divide between «فقد» and «وقد». Both printed lemmas have «فقد» and the scores are very close. The wording «هوج الريح» in 7.1 appears in both books. The commentators explain how the singular wind can stand for the winds and also discuss a collective. Neither a long-vowel decoder result nor the close score against «الرياح» outweighs the written form and the foot. At 8.1 «ليالي الشهر» is the object of «جلّى». Travel personified unveils the nights as a bridegroom might unveil a bride. The variant «سفر» is in the *Shuruh* apparatus. The isolated and continuous evidence supports «سير», the subject at the end of the half. [*Shuruh*, pp. 770–774](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n796/mode/2up) · [Beirut, p. 212](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n211/mode/1up)

In 21.2 both printed texts point the plural verb «يَخُضْنَ» from خاض يخوض. The camels of 21.1 wade through night’s curly hair. The free-phone pass gives x-o-ð-n-a across 170.87–171.27 s, supporting the ḍamma of «يَخُضنَ». In 24 and 26 both books leave the article at the end of the sadr. Read continuously the words are «الفرات» and «الغريب». Their division in the app preserves the metrical halves. At 26.1 the *Shuruh* lemma begins «وإن تجد» and its apparatus cites «فإن تجد» from al-Batalyawsi. Beirut has the latter and both continuous passes retain the fāʾ. [*Shuruh*, pp. 784–788](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n810/mode/2up) · [Beirut, p. 214](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n213/mode/1up)

At 25 the comparison is between silver and a lookalike in water clear enough for assaying. Both prints point «وَمُشبِهَها لَمُيِّزَتِ انتقاداً». Al-Tabrizi explicitly treats «لجين» as feminine by reference to «الفضة». Al-Khwarazmi explains why both the pronoun in «مشبهها» and the one implicit in «لَمُيِّزَت» agree with it. He also reports a reading of the image in which the pronoun refers to the water. Isolated Cohere and original-clock Cohere recover «ومشبهها». The independent free-phone trace gives h-a-h-a at the end of the first word, followed by l-a-m-u-j-iː-z-a-t-i. The first word therefore has the feminine suffix, and the second is a feminine passive with a linking kasra before «انتقادا». The final alignment places the passive at 204.38–205.42 s on the edited clock. It leads the active «لَمَيَّزتَ» by 6.44 points. The feminine suffix on the preceding word leads the masculine by 8.36 points on the same release. The translation keeps the assayer unnamed. [*Shuruh*, pp. 786–788](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n812/mode/2up) · [Beirut, p. 214](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n213/mode/1up)

At 28.2 Audar’s «تواخى» would mean professing brotherhood. Both printed texts have «توافى», feigning loyalty in speech, and the recording favours it. At 35 the mounts imagine the dawn is near, only to see the fire of their rider’s resolve. «تَوَهَّمُ» is the shortened present «تتوهّم», feminine singular with the nonhuman plural as its subject. Beirut has the performed «فلم تقدح» and the *Shuruh* lemma «ولم تقدح». At 37.2 the commentaries also record «تعلّلت» for «تعاللت». The acoustic comparison ties. Both printed lemmas give the latter and al-Tabrizi explains exhausting the last of the mounts’ and ships’ strength. [*Shuruh*, pp. 790, 795–796](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n821/mode/2up) · [Beirut, pp. 214–215](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n213/mode/2up)

In 41.1 both clip decoders suggest «السفينة». The free-phone sequence across 331.17–332.75 s is s-s-a-f-i-n-a-t-o-x-a-l-o. It has one t between «السفينَ» and the vowel of «تخال». The singular in continuous inflected speech would require the tāʾ of «السفينةَ» as well as the initial tāʾ of the verb, and would add a syllable to the foot. The edition retains the collective «السفين» of both texts. At 42.1 both books and the stronger acoustic result give «تسري وتجري», houses that move with the sailors. At 43.2 «يُصادا» reverses the traveller’s role from hunter to quarry. The commentaries also know an active «يَصادا» in which his goal is a successful catch. The score scarcely distinguishes the two. The pointed lemmas and the commentators’ preference support the passive. In 48.1 both printed lemmas give «فما», with nearly tied conjunctions in the audio comparison. At 52.1 the rare «للأصادق» is explained as a plural of an already plural word for friends. It leads the more familiar «للأصدقاء». Both editions and the stronger score support «تقول» at 53.2. [*Shuruh*, pp. 799–801](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n825/mode/2up) · [pp. 806–807](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n832/mode/2up) · [Beirut, pp. 215–216](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n214/mode/2up)

## Meter and rhyme

Wafir, with dāl as rawiy. The rhyme vowel is open and sustained in such endings as «البعادا», «السهادا» and «اجتهادا». At 1.1 «تُفَدْدِيكَنْ / نُفُوسُ وَلَا / تَفَادَى» gives مفاعيلن مفاعلتن فعولن. At 27.1 «إِذَشْشِعْرَلْ / يَمَانِيَتُسْ / تَنَارَتْ» gives the same sequence. The first foot of each is the contracted form of مفاعلتن.
