- **Audio:** kept 2.4–599.2 s of the download (the silence before the first word and the tail after the last cut), gain −7.3 dB, giving −20.1 LUFS with a peak of −7 dBTP.
- **Abyat recited:** 64 of the diwan's 73, in its order: 1–41, 44–64, 66 and 67 in the diwan's numbering. He leaves out 42–43 («تستاف بالعنبر الهنديّ», «ظنّي بكم حسنٌ»), 65 (an obscene line about al-Akhtal's mother) and the closing abuse of al-Akhtal and Taghlib (68–73). He says the last bayt twice, and the timeline plays it twice.

## Editions

- **Starting text:** al-Sawi's voweled text, put in the house style; where the reciter departs from it, the text follows him (below).
- **ديوان جرير بشرح محمد بن حبيب**, ed. Nu'man Muhammad Amin Taha (Dar al-Ma'arif): shamela 133412, vol. 1, pp. 160–167 (`diwan.txt`), headed «وقال جرير يهجو الأخطل». 73 abyat, numbered, lightly voweled, with Ibn Habib's glosses and the variants he records («ويروى»).
- **محمد إسماعيل الصاوي, شرح ديوان جرير** (Cairo, 1353/1934), pp. 593–598: scan archive.org `AAlexandrina-146341`, leaves 603–608. The poem fully voweled, with short glosses; it lacks the diwan's 43 («ظنّي بكم حسن»). Most of the reciter's readings where the two differ are al-Sawi's, and the vowelling follows him.
- **Anthologies:** Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-Shu'ara*, vol. 1, p. 68 (shamela 23785/61), quotes abyat 1 and 36 as his example of verse whose words are beautiful and sweet though the meaning is slight; al-Jarawi, *al-Hamasa al-Maghribiyya*, vol. 2, p. 1091 (shamela 6902/1033), and al-Basri, *al-Hamasa al-Basriyya*, vol. 2, p. 87 (shamela 6901/370), give bayt 36 with «حَوَرٌ».

`abyat_in_full` is 73, the diwan's count. The bayt numbers below are the recording's; from 42 on they are the diwan's minus two, and 63–64 are the diwan's 66–67.

- **10.1, 46.2:** the reciter departs from both editions; the transcript hears «تهدي» and «هل يا ترى». Notes give the editions' readings.
- **33.1:** the aligner prefers Taha's «ما» by 3.6, but «ما تعلمين» breaks the basit foot and the transcript hears «مما»; the text keeps al-Sawi's «مِمّا».
- **36.1, 37.1:** the reciter says the lines as the anthologies quote them, not as the diwan prints them.
- **13.1:** al-Sawi prints «عُلاقَتَها»; the text has «عَلاقَتَها», the lexicons' word for love that clings to the heart.
- **Vowels:** 16 «يا طَيبَ» is Tayba with the end dropped in the vocative (Ibn Habib: «أراد: طيبة»); 22 «خُنتِ … كُنتِ» are addressed to the beloved; 40.2–41.1 «طَيِّبَةَ … مَثلوجَةَ … واضِعَةً» in the accusative, carried on from the rhyme «مِبدانا», as al-Sawi prints them (Taha marks only «واضعةً»; for 40.2 `variants.py` also prefers «طَيِّبَةَ», by 2.8); 21.2 «غَدرَ», the object of «تَأمَنَنَّ», where al-Sawi has «غَدرُ» (+4.9 for the text); 45.2 «ظَعّانا», a man always travelling, agreeing with «مُغتَرِباً», where al-Sawi has «ظُعّانا», plural of ظاعن (a coin toss, −0.2); 50.2 «السَّلَوطَحِ», as al-Sawi prints it.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Glosses** follow Ibn Habib in Taha's edition (أويت, الحيران, ملح, الأعطان, السلوان, الدجن والأردان, القرحان, أميرها, الصدى, العرق والسلان, الحيران, دير اللج, دنّك والدين, مثانيها, الأحداج, ملهم وقرّان, الخدي والمناسم والنقل والحزابي والحزّان, السلوطح والروحان والصوّان, الريّان, اليمانية, حوران, الادّراء والمخدر وخفّان, المرس, الوسن, المناوأة والإدهان) and al-Sawi's notes (المحزان, حملان, بيدان, لوى حوّاء, مبدان, and «وهو مأسدة», the lions of Khaffan in 57).
- **Khindif and Qays Aylan** as the two branches of Mudar are the standard genealogy.
- **The frame:** Taha's heading makes it a satire on al-Akhtal; the poem's own turn at 56 from love to threats is plain in the text.
- **The poet's life:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 2, p. 119 (shamela 12286/1280–1281, `alam.txt`); his clan, Kulayb ibn Yarbu', from the entry on his son Bilal, vol. 2, p. 72 (shamela 12286/1142).

## Review

A second reader checked the text against al-Sawi's page images and Taha's edition, scanned every half, and checked the sharh, notes, intro and bio against the sources (2026-11-17). Fixed after the review:

- **Text:** 62.1 «نابِيَ» for the meter, confirmed by the second transcriber; 40.2 «طَيِّبَةَ» in the accusative; 50.2 «السَّلَوطَحِ»; the alif of wasl voweled in 28.1, 54.2, 60.2 and 63.1.
- **Meaning:** 57 «يَدَّري», stalking game, not knowing: the poets are hunters creeping up on a lion; 26 (whoever trusted her), 56 (a statement, and God or perhaps the caliph) and 64 (Jarir is of Khindif through Tamim) corrected in both languages; 36.1 retranslated.
- **Intro:** Ibn Qutayba's verdict quoted whole: fine words with little meaning beneath them.
- **Sharh:** the English sharh deepened to say what each bayt means (median 12 words to 28); notes no longer repeated in the sharh (3, 12, 19, 36); al-Sawi's readings in 51 and 58 added.
- **Notes:** 10.1 «تُهدي» against the editions' «نُهدي»; 17.1, 34.2, 53.1 and 58.1 added; English notes for the place names, «يَدَّري» and «قَرَن»; heads and the 29.1 phrase note put right.
- **sources.md:** the starting text named; the departures from al-Sawi recorded (21.2, 40.2, 45.2) and his notes credited correctly.
- **Poet:** «بن بدر» restored to the full name.
