## Editions

- **Ali al-Jarim and Muhammad Shafiq Ma'ruf**, ديوان البارودي, «حققه وصححه وضبطه وشرحه», with an introduction by Jabir Asfur (al-Hay'a al-Misriyya al-Amma li-l-Kitab with the Babtain Foundation, Cairo, 1992), vol. 1 («قافية الهمزة – قافية الكاف»), pp. 626–632: archive.org `AAlexandrina-126053`, leaves 659–665 (title page leaf 4), **read from the page images** (verse lines zoomed, `_scratch/p626*.png`, `_scratch/n660*.png`, `_scratch/n661*.png`). Fully voweled, 30 abyat, a note on every bayt (glosses, then a paraphrase); headed «وقال وهو بسرنديب يتشوّق إلى وطنه», with a footnote on the heading: «سرنديب (سيلان): جزيرة من أراضي الهند في جنوبيها، وكان الشاعر قد نُفي إليها عقب الثورة العرابية في صفر سنة ١٣٠٠ هـ (ديسمبر سنة ١٨٨٢ م)». The next leaf (666, p. 633) starts another poem, so 30 is the whole poem. Ma'ruf's preface to the third part (Dar al-Ma'arif, 1972; archive.org `AAlexandrina-100446`, leaf 4) says he and al-Jarim edited the first two parts together, part 1 in 1940 (hamza to dhal) and part 2 in 1942 (ra' to kaf), so this qaf poem is their joint work. **This is the text the recording is checked against, and the starting text**, put in the house style.
- **The same edition reset by Dar al-Awda**, Beirut, 1998 (copyright leaf 3), pp. 370–373: archive.org `20191210_20191210_1135`, leaves 369–372, read from the page images. Same heading, same 30 abyat in the same order, abridged notes; in the eight recited abyat its words and vowels are al-Jarim and Ma'ruf's throughout. Esat Ayyıldız (below) cites this print as their critical edition.
- **The Abu Dhabi encyclopedia's text** (old shamela 4423, «ديوان محمود سامي البارودي», pp. 620–622; `shamela-old-4423.txt`): 29 abyat, without bayt 9 («يا روضة النيل»). In 1–8 it has the same words; it runs «أو راقي» together as «أوراقى» and prints «نَفسِى» without the fatha on the ya.
- **Quotations:** al-Zayn (1–2; same words) and Abu Bakr Ibrahim (1–6), as above. Abu Bakr Ibrahim's text runs «أوراقي» together and has «يشفى غليلا» and «وإبراق» in 1.2 and «سر نديب» in 5.1: misprints of dots and spacing, tested below where they make words.
- **Hindawi's reset** (Mu'assasat Hindawi, Cairo, «ديوان محمود سامي البارودي»; archive.org `DewanAlbarody`, leaf 198 = printed p. 198, read from the page image; found by the second reader): the same 30 abyat under the same heading, a modern text without notes that follows the editions above, so a witness to their text, not an independent one. In 1–8 it has the same words and differs from al-Jarim and Ma'ruf in three vowels, none of them the reciter's: 3.2 «نَفسي» (the ya unvoweled, a syllable short), 7.1 «تَقَلَّدتُ» (first person; «تَ قَل لَد تُ», u − − u, is no basit foot, and the aligner puts it 7.3 below «تَقَلَّدَت») and 7.2 «غَيرَ مِقلاقِ» (see the coin tosses below). Its 5.2 is «وَلا أَنيسٌ», as in every print.

`abyat_in_full` is 30, as in both prints of al-Jarim and Ma'ruf.

## The meter

Basit (مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فاعلن), rawiy ق with kasra, its vowel lengthened into a ya: the sadr ends in فَعِلُن (u u −) and the ajuz in فَعْلُن (− −); bayt 1 is musarra', its sadr ending like an ajuz («أَو راقي»). All 16 halves scan with `/home/alifadel/.cache/shw-tmp/b4/tools/scan.py` (the default basit), none flagged; 1.1 and 6.2 were also scanned by hand. Where the meter decides:

- 3.2 «نَفسِيَ», with the ya voweled as both prints have it: «سِ يَ مِن» is the فَعِلُن of the second foot; «نَفسي» leaves the half a syllable short;
- 4.1 «صَبراً», not the «صابرا» both transcribers hear (the stretched first syllable): «صابِراً» breaks the third foot;
- 4.1 «وَهيَ» and 8.1 «وَهوَ» with a silent ha;
- 1.2 «أَخا», not the «أخاه» both transcribers hear: «أَخاهُ» adds a syllable, and the aligner scores «أَخاهُ حُزنٍ» 12.3 below «أَخا حُزنٍ»; what they hear as ه is the breath before the ḥ of «حُزنٍ», which follows a 2 s stretch of the alif.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Sharh, notes and intro** follow al-Jarim and Ma'ruf's notes on every bayt (pp. 626–632): their readings of 1 (the love is longing for home and family; the question is a wish, or a cry of pain), 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 (sleepless, leaning on his elbow, on a peak hard to climb), 7 («تقلّدت» used for «انتطق»; the summit wears the stars as belt and sash; «وفي البيت إشارة إلى ضخامة القنّة، وشدّة ارتفاعها») and 8 (the crescent's ring round the Pleiades like the niche round the lamp); and their glosses of راقٍ, إيراق (both senses), المهجة, الرمق, البين, استولى, براني, رعت, ويح, جازعة, أعيا, الخِلّ, الأنيس, الإطراق, أرعى نجوم الليل (with al-Khansa's half-line), مرتفقاً, القُنّة, المرقى and الراقي, تقلّد, جمان الشهب, المنطقة, الوشاح, غير مقلاق, الثريا, مضطرب, دون, الطاق. Their notes on 9 (Rawdat al-Nil: «روضة المقياس … وقد يراد بها الأراضي المصرية») and 18 (al-Miqyas, «روضة المنيل … وفي جنوبيها مقياس قديم كانت تقاس به مياه النيل») and the last bayt give the intro's account of the rest of the poem.
- **Two things the commentary doesn't say, and the sharh does:** that the rhyme word of 6.2 «الرّاقي» repeats that of the matla' in another sense (al-Jarim and Ma'ruf gloss the two separately: 1.1 «اسم فاعل من رقاه يرقيه (من باب رمى) أي عوّذه»; 6.2 «اسم فاعل منه», from «رقيت الجبل … (كرضي)»), and, in 7, the contrast with the slender woman whose sash slips, which comes from the lexicons' «امرأة مقلاق الوشاح» that the editors quote in their note. The editors themselves call the like pair at the end of 19, «ساق» (a dove) and «ساق» (a trunk), «جناس تامّ» (p. 630, note 19), and the rhyme word «ساق» comes back again in 20.

## The poet

`content/poets/mahmud-sami-al-barudi/` is new with this poem.

- **Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam***, vol. 7, p. 171 (shamela 12286/6445–6446, `alam.txt`): the dates «(١٢٥٥ - ١٣٢٢ هـ = ١٨٣٩ - ١٩٠٤ م)», the full name «محمود سامي (باشا) ابن حسن حسني بن عبد الله البارودي المصري» (`full_name` without the title «باشا», keeping the nisba «المصري» as the other poets' full names keep theirs), «أول ناهض بالشعر العربيّ من كبوته، في عصرنا»; «جركسي الأصل»; «نسبته إلى (إيتاي البارود) بمصر، وكانت لأحد أجداده في عهد الالتزام»; born and died in Cairo; the military school; a commander of the two Egyptian expeditions to help the Ottomans, «الأولى في ثورة (كريد) سنة ١٨٦٨ والثانية في الحرب الروسية سنة ١٨٧٧»; «تقلب في المناصب انتهت به إلى رئاسة النظار»; with the rebels in the Urabi revolt; arrested, sentenced to death, the sentence changed to exile in Ceylon, «حيث أقام سبعة عشر عاما أكثرها في (كندي) تعلم الإنجليزية في خلالها … وكفّ بصره. وعفي عنه سنة ١٣١٧ هـ (١٨٩٩) فعاد إلى مصر». His footnote quotes Yacoub Artin's other account of the name (a forebear who made gunpowder at Bulaq), so the bio says "al-Zirikli traces".

The English "which studies in English call neoclassical": Esat Ayyıldız, "The Neoclassical Elements in Maḥmūd Sāmī al-Bārūdī's Poetry", *Journal of Islamic Review* 13/2 (2023), pp. 761–784 (archive.org `mahmud-sami-al-barudi`, read in its OCR text): he names the movement al-Barudi led al-Ihya' wa-l-Ba'th, revival and resurgence, and ties the name to neoclassicism.
- **Where the sources differ:** the pardon is 1317 AH (1899 CE) in al-A'lam, 1900 in Dayf (p. 86) and Ayyıldız (p. 766, read in the OCR text), while a study Ayyıldız cites (p. 769) dates the exile in its title, “al-Bārūdī en Ceilán (1883-1899)” (Isaac Donoso Jiménez, 2019); the bio follows al-A'lam. Al-A'lam dates the Cretan expedition 1868, Dayf the outbreak of the revolt 1866; the bio gives no year for it.
- **Consistency with Ahmad Shawqi and Hafiz Ibrahim** (`content/poets/ahmad-shawqi/`; Hafiz's files in the `sakana-al-zalamu` worktree): no fact overlaps except Dayf's grouping of the three, which neither of their bios contradicts; the names are written as theirs are (Shawqi, Hafiz Ibrahim), and the dates come from al-A'lam, as theirs do.
