# البَيانُ في تَحزيبِ القُرآنِ

## Recording and written text

Khalid al-Sharafi recites Muhammad ibn Muhsin Abu Raqaba’s poem for Alqimmah Studio, 22 March 2025. The full-recording transcript gives fourteen different abyat. The final prayer occurs twice. The app retains 3.32–120.40 s of the original 122.645 s. The studio identifies the author on the opening card and prints the verses on screen. Full lines can be checked at 9, 15, 24, 30, 39, 45, 54, 60, 69, 75, 84, 90, 99 and 105 s of the original. The card for the prayer stays on screen for both renditions. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkjJ00QJK70)

The captions are the written witness to this contemporary text. The following sources substantiate its subject and its allusions.

- Abu Amr al-Dani, *al-Bayan fi 'add ay al-Qur'an*, pp. 78–82. The totals he transmits for the recognised regional traditions illustrate 3–4. On p. 177 he specifies that Kufan readers count «لِلأَذقانِ سُجَّداً» as a verse ending in Surat al-Isra' and the others do not. That example underlies the explanation of 4. [Totals](https://shamela.ws/book/5542/60) · [Surat al-Isra'](https://shamela.ws/book/5542/159)
- Ibrahim al-Abyari, *al-Mawsu'a al-Qur'aniyya*, I, pp. 380–381, describes al-Hajjaj’s request for counts by letter and the subsequent development of divisions into thirty parts. The poet assigns the sixty hizbs directly to al-Hajjaj in 5. The translation keeps that attribution and the sharh sets it beside the historical account. [Al-Abyari](https://shamela.ws/book/23610/377)
- Al-Bukhari, no. 5054, VI, pp. 196–197 in the Sultaniyya text. Abd Allah ibn Amr is told to read the Quran in a month and, when he says he has the strength, in seven days. Abu Dawud, no. 1393, II, p. 55, reports that Aws ibn Hudhayfa asked the Companions how they divided their readings. They answered by numbers of whole suras. Bayt 11 uses *hizb* in this earlier sense. [Al-Bukhari](https://shamela.ws/book/1681/7531) · [Abu Dawud](https://shamela.ws/book/1726/1874)
- The echoes in 12–13 are Quran 25:32 («وَرَتَّلناهُ تَرتيلاً») and 73:4 («وَرَتِّلِ القُرآنَ تَرتيلاً»). The written «السَّما» in 13.2 matches the shortened form in the rhyme with «كما».

- Bayt 14 brings together the Quran’s intercession for its readers in *Sahih Muslim*, no. 804, and the prayer for relief from sorrow reported by Ibn Masud in *Musnad Ahmad*, al-Risala ed., VI, p. 246, no. 3712. The latter asks for the Quran to be «ربيع قلبي». [Muslim](https://sunna.alifta.gov.sa/BookToc/ViewMatnPage?MainID=13750) · [Ahmad](https://dorar.net/h/FxgA2Q8v/osoul)

The unit is the rajaz couplet. The opening begins «إِنْ قِيلَ قُلْ / لي يا فَتَلْـ» and the middle has «وَالجُزْءُ ما / كانَ مِنَلْـ». The two halves share a rhyme, which changes between couplets. The first transcriber dropped the unstressed «لي» at the opening and the end of 4.2 after «لوقف». Both are printed on screen.

## Audio verification

The 117.08 s master measures −20.1 LUFS after a −7.7 dB gain. The first half starts at 0.50 s and the repeated final prayer ends at 114.30 s, before the studio echo. The 30 isolated halves account for 137 sung words including the repeat. Two clips produced differences.

- In 1.1 the isolated Cohere transcript joined «لي يا» as «ليا». The full recording supplies the vocative «يا», and the wider Audar clip supplies «لي». The card prints both. The aligner places «لي» at 1.72–1.84 s and «يا» at 2.00–2.12 s.
- In 5.1 the short-clip recogniser supplied an unrelated phrase. The full track and Audar both give «هذا وقد حزّبه الحجّاج», as on screen. The four aligned words have confidences of 0.57–0.72.
- The weaker individual alignments on «إن» (1.1), «خير» (9.2), «ابن» (10.2), «قد» (11.2) and «ربّ» (14.1) retain the printed wording. Both performances of 14 were recovered in their own clips.
