# عُلُوٌّ في الحَياةِ وَفي المَماتِ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari, uploaded 8 August 2023. Of the 150.895 s original the edit keeps 7.02–149.48 s, from just before «علوّ» through the decay of «رائحات». This removes the opening lead-in and the tail after the prayer. The two full transcriptions, the scrolling verse cards and the 42 aligned halves give all 21 bayts of al-Anbari’s elegy once, in the order preserved by Ibn Khallikan. The later verses about the burial printed after it are a separate poem. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LBU1NdOova4)

## Text, occasion and attribution

- Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-a'yan*, ed. Ihsan Abbas, V, pp. 119–121, life of Abu Tahir Muhammad ibn Baqiyya. Ibn Baqiyya had served Izz al-Dawla. Ibn Khallikan places his execution in Shawwal 367 AH (978 CE), describes the public display of the body in Baghdad, names the elegist and supplies 1–15 on p. 120 and 16–21 on p. 121. His account of written copies in the streets is introduced on the authority of Ibn Asakir. He distinguishes the later burial and the poem on that occasion by its new rhyme. [Execution date](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2117) · [Elegy](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2118) · [Close and subsequent history](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2119)
- Al-Tha'alibi, *Yatimat al-dahr*, II, pp. 439–440, gives the same sequence, interrupting it to compare lines by Ibn al-Mu'tazz and Ibn al-Rumi. His comparison at 2 brings out the double reading of the crowd as witnesses and a deputation. His comparison after 16 links the attack of time to the dead minister’s former protection of his people. The interpolated quotations belong to those other poets. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/6743/941) · [Continuation](https://shamela.ws/book/6743/942)
- Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani, *Asrar al-balagha*, ed. Mahmud Muhammad Shakir, pp. 346–347. Al-Jurjani singles out the feat of making this very posture signify honour. The outstretched arms, upright crowd, night fires and open air all remain visible. Their new meanings explain how the elegist can make a claim about the minister’s living character from what the authorities did to his body. [Argument](https://shamela.ws/book/8187/344) · [Quoted verses](https://shamela.ws/book/8187/345)
- Al-Damiri, *Hayat al-hayawan al-kubra*, I, p. 136, quotes the elegy in his account of Buyid Baghdad. Ahmad al-Hashimi, *Jawahir al-adab*, II, pp. 389–390, supplies the reading family closest to several recorded phrases. His heading repeats the confusion over the poet’s date. Al-Zirikli identifies the elegist as the secretary, preacher and Baghdad judicial witness Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Ya'qub, Abu al-Hasan ibn al-Anbari. He explicitly corrects al-Tha'alibi’s confusion with Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn al-Qasim, who died in 328 AH, before the execution. The separate poet record follows al-Zirikli, VI, p. 312. Both biographies preserve his lower bound «بعد ٣٩٠هـ / بعد ١٠٠٠م». [Al-Damiri](https://shamela.ws/book/10664/134) · [Al-Hashimi, opening](https://shamela.ws/book/21505/597) · [Conclusion](https://shamela.ws/book/21505/598) · [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/5886)

Ibn Khallikan gives two landmarks for the public display. On p. 119 he names the Adudi hospital. On p. 120 the account accompanying the poem places the body by the vizier’s residence at Bab al-Taq. The introduction follows the latter account. On p. 121 Ibn Khallikan credits Ibn Asakir with the story that the poet himself wrote out the elegy and scattered it in the streets of Baghdad. [P. 119](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2117) · [P. 120](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2118) · [P. 121](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2119)

The Zayd in 9 is Zayd ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn, killed in the Kufa uprising in 122 AH (740 CE). Al-Zirikli records his burial and the subsequent public display of his body at al-Kunasa. This sequence explains how a dead man could have ridden the same “mount” as the vizier. [*Al-A'lam*, III, p. 59](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/2103) · [Account of the display](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/2104)

At 16.2 Ibn Khallikan’s and al-Hashimi’s «يخفّف» suits the burning thirst relieved by tears. Al-Tha'alibi gives «حقيق بالدموع». At 6.2 Ibn Manzur distinguishes the wind which scatters dust and the dust carried by it. His verb «سفت الريح التراب تسفيه» gives the root س ف ي. The word card identifies the winds and the translation gives the material they supply for the shroud. [*Lisan al-Arab*, XIV, p. 389](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7513)

## Metre

Wafir, with ta as the rhyme consonant. The vowel of the rhyme can be prolonged as ī. The full middle foot مُفاعَلَتُن is shortened by ʿasb to مُفاعَلْتُن, equivalent to مفاعيلن. The ending is فعولن. A vertical stroke separates feet.

| Bayt | Sadr | Ajuz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ʿu-luw-wun-fil ∣ ḥa-yā-ti-wa-fil ∣ ma-mā-tī | la-ḥaq-qun-ʾan ∣ ta-ʾiḥ-dal-muʿ ∣ ji-zā-tī |
| 2 | ka-ʾan-nan-nā ∣ sa-ḥaw-la-ka-ḥī ∣ na-qā-mū | wu-fū-du-na-dā ∣ ka-ʾay-yā-maṣ ∣ ṣi-lā-tī |
| 11 | wa-lam-ʾa-ra-qab ∣ la-jidh-ʿi-ka-qaṭ ∣ ṭu-jidh-ʿā | ta-mak-ka-na-min ∣ ʿi-nā-qil-mak ∣ ru-mā-tī |

In 2 the sadr ends with the crowd on its feet. Its imagined identity arrives after the caesura. The final «الصِّلات» has a pronounced ta in liaison, just like «الصلاة» in the next bayt, although the two nouns mean gifts and prayer respectively.

## Per-half verification

Four of the 42 narrow clips differed. One further clip had an edge flag.

| Half | Narrow output | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.2 | الصلاة | Widened Audar has «الصلات». This is the plural of صلة, the gifts that drew the delegations. Prayer belongs to 3.2. |
| 10.1 | تاسر | Widened Audar restores «تأس». Zayd’s death on a similar mounting is a precedent that can offer consolation, «تأسٍّ». |
| 13.2 | بالتراث | Widened Audar restores «بالترات». Time demands redress for the injuries the protector dealt it. The stem descends from وِتر, a wrong for which redress is claimed. The word for inheritance is a different formation. |
| 17.1 edge | final الجنه | The card and continuous take finish the half at «قيام». The last word occupies 108.60–109.12 s on the edited clock and «بفرضك» begins at 109.78 s. There is no intervening noun in either continuous witness. |
