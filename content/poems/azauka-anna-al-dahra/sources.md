# Ibn al-Rumi’s consolation on a mother’s death

## Occasion, attribution and extent

Husayn Nassar’s third, revised edition of *Diwan Ibn al-Rumi* (Cairo, 2003), I, no. 291, pp. 374–377, prints 61 numbered verses. Ahmad Hasan Bissij’s third edition (Beirut, Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 2002), I, pp. 261–263, transmits the same 61 in the same order. Both headings name **عبيد الله بن عبد الله**, Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah. The second part of the heading, «عن والدته», identifies the deceased as his mother without giving her personal name. The address «أميري» and the poem’s appeal to the example of the house of Mus'ab place this mourning within a ruling family’s history. The funeral is pictured from the surviving son’s point of view. [Nassar](https://archive.org/details/ibn-al-rumi-1/page/n374/mode/2up) · [Bissij](https://archive.org/details/Diwan_Ibn_Rumi/01_74345/page/n260/mode/2up)

Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-a'yan*, III, p. 120, gives the addressee’s lineage as Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Tahir ibn al-Husayn ibn Mus'ab. He describes him as a poet and a prince who held charge of the Baghdad police. This supplies the family connection behind the appeal to «آل مصعب» at 30. [Ibn Khallikan](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/1131)

Both headings preserve the qualification «وهي مما نحل الدمشقي». The text is transmitted in Ibn al-Rumi’s diwan with that explicit attribution notice. The words alone do not establish al-Dimashqi’s identity. Nassar’s apparatus at p. 374 records a reader who objected that the poet changed the consonant immediately before the recurrent alif and tāʾ, and a reply defending the poem and its maker. At p. 375 Nassar reports that *al-Mukhtar* formed one verse from portions of his 29 and 30. The recording recites both complete. The form of the ending matters to this dispute in the old reception of the poem.

Al-Raghib al-Isfahani, *Muhadarat al-udaba’*, II, p. 525, explicitly names Ibn al-Rumi above an excerpt corresponding to our 43, 45, 47, 48 and 49. Thus the voice and several consecutive stages of the consolation circulated under his name beyond the full diwan. At 45 the excerpt has «أتته», at 47 «أتاني». Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi reproduces a related group in *al-Dhakha’ir wa-l-abqariyyat*, I, p. 231, adding that he had not found the poem in his copy of the diwan. The comments in that late anthology describe the copy available to its author. Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, IV, p. 297, gives the poet’s Baghdad dates, 221–283 AH / 836–896 CE, his family and differing reports about his death. [Al-Raghib](https://shamela.ws/book/9078/1307) · [Al-Barquqi](https://shamela.ws/book/36072/242) · [Al-A'lam](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3856)

## Recording and metre

Osama al-Nahari recites all 61, beginning «عزاؤك أن الدهر ذو فجعات» and closing «وحان غروب صاحب الصلوات». The single source keep is 2.46–578.03 seconds of 580.870. Only the head and tail margins are removed. The 122 halves occur once each. The marker 289.26 at 32.1 in the edition manifest is an app-time break for the aligner, within the continuous audio. The studio cards present the two halves of each verse together. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jza57iUqpfk)

Tawil, with tāʾ as the rhyme consonant. In 1 the feet are `ʿazāʾu / ka annad-dah / ra dhū fa / jaʿātī` and `wakullu / jamīʿin ṣā / ʾirun li / shatātī`. At 30 the invocation of the family occupies the first half, `taʿazza / bimawtiṣ-ṣī / di min ā / li muṣʿabin`, then `tajidhum / usan in shiʾ / ta aw qu / duwātī`. A recogniser often writes the sustained final kasra as a full yāʾ.

## Textual choices and the limits of the evidence

At **24.2** both printed diwans and the studio have «ثبات» after «مغاداة». The word score leads «بيات» by only 0.96. The raids can hold their ground as well as attack at dawn. At **41.1** the written card gives «تُرَدّى سريرها». The recorded word scores 30.37 above the competing transcript «يردني». The previous verse has the mother pray to be survived by her child, which explains the wish about her own bier. At **44.1** both full transcripts and Bissij give «بلى», a direct answer to «ألم تر». Nassar’s p. 376 reads «بل». The word score gives «بلى» a margin of only 1.02, so the printed and continuous evidence matters. At **45.2** the full diwans, the two full transcripts and the card at 417 seconds support «أبته», which leads «أتته» by 5.20. He had thought himself safe because the arrows had shunned him. Al-Raghib and al-Barquqi have «أتته», in which the arrows come upon him. The word note records both.

At **47.1** the two diwans and the studio card at 435 seconds give the consonants «ألمت». The print and studio do not fully vowel the verb. Both full transcriptions return that spelling. The isolated «أَلَمَّت» leads «أَلِمتُ» by 9.70 and «أتاني» by 11.14. In the connected «مكروه ألمت فجاءة» the score for «أَلَمَّت» leads first-person «أَلِمتُ» by 11.51 and the excerpt’s «أتاني» by 17.23. Both «أَلَمَّت» and «أَلِمتُ» fit the third foot of tawil. The first allows an understood feminine calamity (compare the preceding «الفاجعات»). The second would put the sufferer directly in the first person. Al-Raghib’s explicit first-person object explains the sense of the intervention, but cannot supply the verb in this performance. An independent phoneme decoder without an Arabic text prompt returns `a l a m m a t` in both context windows, followed immediately by f from «فجاءة». The doubled m and the absence of a final u strengthen «أَلَمَّت» over the first-person alternative. The feminine subject still has to be supplied from the preceding sense. Timed outputs are in `phoneme-windows.txt`.

At **28.2** Bissij drops «إلا» from «وهل هن إلا منجزات عدات». Both full audio transcripts give the particle and the studio text also reads it. It occupies 258.00–258.44 seconds in the app alignment (word confidence 0.67). The fuller words give the point of the paradox: the days fulfil precisely the threat of treachery they had made.

## Half-verse verification

- **4.2 and 9.2.** Both narrow and wider clips render «النخرات» with an article. In connected speech the tanwin of «أعظمٍ» meets the nūn of «نخرات», yielding the gemination the recognisers represent with the article. Both prints and the card give the agreeing indefinite adjective. At 9.2 the narrower «وهاته» extends the final demand, the wider «بها كوهات» repartitions the first imperative. «بهاك وهات» pairs “take” with “give”.
- **10.2 and 13.1.** The short «بأقصاصها من» and widened «بأقصاها من» both cut across «بأقصى سهامٍ». The full diwan and caption read the arrow noun, completed by its sharp point in «حمات». At 13.1 the wider clip restores singular «إنه» where the narrow one had «إنهم». Its predicate «معاذ» opens the next half.
- **24.2 and 41.2.** The t in «مغاداةٍ» is heard in liaison and the narrow transcript writes «مغادات». The wider gives «مغاداة وبين ثبات». The short clip at 41.2 supplies r for the rhyme, «مواتر». The wider restores «مواتي», which completes the mother’s wish in the preceding half.
- **50.2, 51.2 and 57.1.** The first recogniser joins «ولا بد» and «فلا بد», then joins «تك طوبى». Widened Audar recovers all three boundaries. It also substitutes «للإيقاظ» for «للأيقاظ» in 50 and «للنوم» for «للنوام» in 51. The reciprocal images require people awake and people asleep. Both diwan texts and the studio preserve those plurals.
- The initial **23.1** returned an unrelated lecture title in a narrow clip. The revised clip recovers the question «هل المرء في الدنيا الدنية ناظر». At **58.1** the first clip added a possessive to «حفرة» and the final one clears it. The final check has nine differences in 122 halves.

The evidence used here consists of the full and widened transcripts, studio text, metrical and grammatical readings and candidate alignment against the sound file. The raw flags, original times, score windows and witnesses remain in the recording and poem research directories.
