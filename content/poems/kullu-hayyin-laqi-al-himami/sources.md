# كل حي لاقي الحمام · Ibn Munadhir for Abd al-Majid

## Recording and the extent of the elegy

Osama al-Nahari's upload of 26 June 2025 runs 348.992 s. Keep the continuous source span **2.74–343.80** (341.06 s) from «كل حي» through «لامتداح القصيد». Its 46 different bayts occur once apiece. The written verses change during the video. A displayed word may be complete across the very syllable where the music of the metre divides it. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SQ3BXJMO_oo)

**The recording gives 46 of the 47 bayts** in Abd al-Aziz Ibrahim’s printed reconstruction. Recorded 1–38 match printed 1–38. At 282.08 s the voice finishes «لريب دهر كنود» and at 282.38 s begins «حين تمت آدابه». It passes over printed 39, «كان عبد المجيد سم الأعادي … ملء عين الصديق رغم الحسود». Recorded 39–46 then match printed 40–47. Both full transcriptions make that transition. A separate Audar clip from the source also joins «لريب دهر كنود» directly to «حين تمت آدابه». Two electronic indexes give the recorded sequence of 46. [Printed order, p. 74](https://archive.org/download/almaoreed/%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A9%202004%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%AF%D8%AF%203-4.pdf#page=74) · [Modern sequence](https://madarisweb.com/ur/deewan/poem/53899) · [Second index](https://mizanalarab.com/poem/101706)

The historical collection is lost. Ibn al-Nadim lists the poet’s work at seventy leaves in *al-Fihrist*, ed. Rida Tajaddud, p. 186, with ninety in the apparatus. Al-Zamakhshari, *Rabi' al-abrar*, V, p. 147, no. 144, calls the elegy about three hundred bayts long and quotes our 18. Abd al-Aziz Ibrahim explains on p. 76 how he ordered the surviving quotations and distinguishes them from that estimate. The al-Babtain catalogue, p. 96, describes a later reconstruction by Muhammad Gharib, *Shi'r Ibn Munadhir* (Alexandria, 2009). Only its catalogue entry was available for comparison. ## Metre, divided words and rhyme

The khafif opening scans «كُلْلُ حَيْيِنْ | لَاقِلْحِمَا | مِ فَمُودِي» (فاعلاتن، مستفع لن، فَعِلاتن). In the middle, 36.1 «كُنْتَ لِي عِصْ | مَتَنْ وَكُنْ | تَ سَمَاءَا» gives فاعلاتن، متفع لن، فَعِلاتن. The rhyme consonant is **د**. خلود، الصيخود، حديد، المجيد and القصيد keep the dal with either waw or ya before it. In first-person forms such as تليدي and مجهودي the final possessive vowel follows the same dal.

The common feature of these divided bayts is *tadwir*: one half ends inside the word continued by the next half. For example وَالأَي / يام (4) represents the two consonantal positions of the geminated ya, and فَاهتَز / زَ (25) those of the doubled zay. النّي / بُ (33) preserves the long vowel of النيب in the sadr and its final consonant in the ajuz. The doubled ra in الحَر / رى (43) divides similarly. Hindawi prints actual divisions of this kind at III, pp. 256–258. The aligned addresses follow the metrical halves even when a narrow recogniser completes the word early.

In particular, the recording places «حين تمت آدابه» at 39 and separates it from «وسقاه ماء الشبيبة» at 25. Al-Kamil sets these next to one another. At 21, أنوء in Abu al-Fadl Ibrahim’s edition of *al-Kamil* describes bearing a heavy load with the friend’s help. أبوء in Hindawi and *al-Ta'azi* describes returning to the friend as a secure support. At 37 Ibn al-Mu'tazz preserves تبلس, the act of silencing or dismaying an adversary. At 40 the interrogative «أما أراك» has the support of both the printed *Kamil* and al-Tayyib, while *al-Zahra* has «ألّا أراك وهل عندك». At 43–45 *al-Ta'azi*, the cited *Aghani* passage and Hindawi help distinguish a heart sick with love (**العميد**) from Ibrahim's **السعيد** and a pail **exhausted** (أنفدت) from one **sent forth** (أنفذت).

## Word readings on the audio

Each comparison uses the same word interval on the edited recording, with 0.30 s on each side. The MMS model, romanisation and CTC scoring are those of `scripts/variants.py`. The batch used a short inference window with one second of context padding. A less negative log score fits the acoustic frames better within that row. Scores from different rows have different baselines. A gap below two offers little guidance. The model often favours ز over ذ, and it is a poor judge of the length of a chanted vowel.

At 1.1 the source-audio Audar clip gives لاقي. The score favours it by 5.74 over the printing error لارقي on p. 73 of al-Mawrid. The long vowel of فمودي is the reciter’s rhyme at the first caesura. Both full transcriptions give «لا تهاب المنون شيئاً» at 2.1. Death fears nothing. The narrower word window nevertheless scores al-Ta'azi’s حيّاً higher. The complete decodes, both half-clip transcriptions and al-Kamil IV, p. 53, give شيئاً. At 3.2 both the place-name and the recurrent final dal support هبّود. At 10.2 the two important distinctions are the order of the consonants in بيرود and the barely distinguishable final consonant of the name. The footnote in Hindawi III, p. 257, separates the historical places behind the variants. At 11 the mounted warriors appear behind the king (خلفه in the voice and al-Kamil) or around him (حوله in al-Ta'azi). At 16 the full voice has الأيام against Ibn al-Mu'tazz’s المنون. At 19, the force of the opponent’s opposition comes from العنيد. On the burial in 20 the voice and the Kamil have دفنته, while Ibn al-Mu'tazz gives غيبته. Abd al-Aziz Ibrahim, p. 74, adds وَ before ما. Cohere’s clipped 20.2 and a fresh Audar window from the downloaded source give «دفنته ما غيبت». The word alignment places ما at 149.20–149.32 s (confidence 0.991). The acoustic comparison favours ما over وَما by 10.42. [Kamil, apparatus at III, p. 257](https://archive.org/details/alkamil1/alkamil3/page/n256/mode/2up)

At 21 the act of turning for support is أبوء. Ibn al-Mu'tazz p. 123 and al-Ta'azi p. 291 preserve this verb with a different word order. At 22 the monitor text, both continuous decoders and the two clip passes all give ربطة. Abd al-Aziz Ibrahim also prints ربطة with a clear single dot in no. 22, p. 74. The first comparison of رِبْطة with رَيْطة was inconclusive. Including the dictionary’s رَبْطة in the same interval gave −8.10, against −13.69 for رِبْطة and −13.47 for رَيْطة. Al-Mu'jam al-wasit defines the first as a bundle. The verse printed with Ibn Aqil’s commentary has the single woven wrapping رَيْطة. The reader’s card glosses both. At 26.1 the recording has eyes lifting towards him, سمَت, rather than the electronic Tabaqat transcription’s سمعت. Cohere and a widened Audar clip from the source recover the pronoun at 29.2. Its aligned interval is 216.06–216.40 s. At 31.2 the assembly is المحفل in both continuous recognitions and in al-Kamil; al-Zahra has المشهد. The two halves of 35 pair رغمي with كرهي in that order and end on indefinite ملحود. At 37.1 the producer’s 277 s slide has تُبلس, the order of consonants confirmed by Ibn al-Mu'tazz. At 37.2 the slide and the four voice transcriptions agree with Abd al-Aziz Ibrahim’s print in omitting وَ before تجزي. Dropping the conjunction costs a short syllable from the first foot, so the metrical text follows the Tabaqat and its word card gives the heard reading. At 40.1 the model has a small preference for أما over ألّا, but the second alternative («وهل» instead of «وما») is clearly disfavoured. The uninterrupted voice and the Kamil read أما أراك وما عندك. At 43.2 the voice, the 320 s slide, Hindawi and al-Aghani agree on العميد, the heart worn sick with love. At 45.1 دال and ذال are close in the short window. The full recognition, the semantics of spending a pail of tears and al-Ta'azi p. 292 support أنفدت.
