## Text, witnesses and commentary

- *Diwan Jarir bi-sharh Muhammad ibn Habib*, ed. Nu'man Muhammad Amin Taha, 3rd ed. (Cairo, Dar al-Ma'arif), no. 12, 1, pp. 140–143, introduces it with «وقال جرير يهجو الأخطل». This is the base for the full count and order. On p. 140 he glosses أجدّك as «أحقّاً منك هذا» and records Umara's المعذّل, “blamed”, in place of المعلّل. He distinguishes love on the tongue from actual desire at 3. On pp. 141–142 he identifies Dawbal as al-Akhtal's childhood nickname, draws a black army and shining weapon-lamps out of the night of 9, and reads the bay horse with white markings at 10 as al-Jahhaf leading the squadrons. At 13 he identifies Ibn Khallas and Azhal as men of Qays. At 14 he explains المغزل by flirtation and play. The mounted eagle at 16 is the standard in his gloss, and the red mingling with the Tigris water is his gloss on أشكل at 18. [Ibn Habib, pp. 140–143](https://shamela.ws/book/133412/95)
- Abd al-Qadir al-Baghdadi, *Sharh abyat Mughni al-labib*, ed. Abd al-Aziz Rabah and Ahmad Yusuf Daqqaq (Beirut, Dar al-Ma'mun, 2nd ed. of vol. 3, 1989), 3, pp. 114–117, no. 194. The passage cites the Tigris line as evidence for a new nominal clause after حتى. He then quotes nineteen lines, explicitly saying «وتركنا منها ثلاثة أبيات لا غير». The omitted verses are our 4, 5 and 21, all present in the recording and Ibn Habib. At 18 his grammatical citation has «فما زالت القتلى تمجّ دماءها» and the quotation of the poem has «وما زالت القتلى تمور دماؤها». He explains both. At 7 he attests two transmission routes and two different insults. Ibn Habib has **الفلس**, a small coin, understood as a sexual insult or in another gloss linked with payment of the poll-tax. Al-Baghdadi's other route has **القلس**, a stout rope, taken as a Christian girdle. The recording, verse card and both full transcriptions support the latter. The translation frames the religious taunt as Jarir addressing his Christian rival. [Al-Baghdadi, p. 114](https://shamela.ws/book/150966/914) · [pp. 116–117](https://shamela.ws/book/150966/916)
- Al-Suyuti, *Sharh shawahid al-Mughni*, 1, pp. 377–378, independently records «ورأيت في ديوان جرير بدله: تمور دماؤها، أي تجري». He explains that the lām in «لكم ... أفضل» in 20 has comparative force, effectively مِن. Together these grammatical selections explain why isolated citations sometimes change the order or wording. Ibn Habib's electronic text also has obvious blemishes at 8 («لئكت», immediately glossed as delay) and 12 («ذات فعل»). Al-Baghdadi's corresponding lines read «المكث» and «ذات بعل», both supported by the voice and card. [Al-Suyuti](https://shamela.ws/book/17724/375)

The performed sequence passes from Orion and a long night at al-Majaza to the army that resembles another starry night. Dawn separates the mass into recognisable squadrons. Jarir places a widow's accusation amid the lists of casualties, follows the fighting into the river, and ends by recalling the protection extended by the Marwanids. These are claims made in an Umayyad satire about the raid on Taghlib. The identification of the occasion with al-Bishr follows al-Akhtal's own line «لقد أوقع الجحّاف بالبشر وقعة», quoted by both commentators. The bilingual commentary distinguishes the satirist's accusations from the glosses of the transmitters.

At **13** the recorded sadr is «وقد قتل الجحّاف أولاد نسوة». Both commentaries have **أزواج نسوة**. The first word changes what the women have lost, children in the performance and husbands in the commentaries. The two full passes and the verse card independently read **أولاد**. It fits the syntax and metre and follows the babies and bereaved women of 11–12. The app text, translation, sharh and word card follow the recording. The diwan's complete twenty-two are performed. The full electronic pages, witness transcriptions and output of `scripts/collate.py` are in `media/research/ajiddaka-la-yashu`.

## Metre

Tawil, rawiy lām with final u prolonged in the cadence. In 1 the sadr divides «أَجِدْدَ / كَ لَا يَصْحُلْ / فُؤَادُلْ / مُعَلْلَلُو», فعولُ مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. The ajuz «وَقَدْ لَا / حَ مِنْ شَيْبِنْ / عِذَارُنْ / وَمِسْحَلُو» has a full first foot. At 10 «فَمَنْشَقْ / قَ ضَوْءُصْصُبْ / حِ حَتْتَى / تَعَرْرَفُو» gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن, as does «كَرَادِي / سَ يَهْدِيهِنْ / نَ وَرْدُنْ / مُحَجْجَلُو». The metrical spelling here shows the doubled consonants and the sound of connected reading.
