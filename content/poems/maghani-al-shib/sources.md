# مَغاني الشِّعبِ طيباً في المَغاني

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari’s recording, uploaded 7 November 2024, lasts 344.277 s. He recites all forty-eight bayts, in the order of the two commentaries. The edit keeps 2.100–342.100 s, from the lead-in to «مغاني الشعب» through the decay of «بلا معاني». It lasts 340.000 s at −20.0 LUFS. All 96 halves are performed once. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l_7NlkEPTk0)

## Witnesses and interpretation

- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, ed. Friedrich Dieterici, Berlin, 1861, printed pp. 766–773. The Berlin scan runs backwards in digital leaf numbers. The opening compares the valley to spring. The last page reaches the sword and its watered pattern at 47 and the words emptied of their meanings at 48. The explanations of the Damascene host, the guest’s safety and the arm of the state draw on al-Wahidi’s discussion of Ibn Jinni, Ibn Furraja and al-Arudi. [Opening](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n134/mode/1up) · [Valley and host](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n132/mode/1up) · [The arm and the name](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n130/mode/1up) · [Ending](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n127/mode/1up) · [Searchable commentary](https://shamela.ws/book/754/381)
- *Al-Tibyan fi sharh al-diwan*, attributed to al-Ukbari, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi, vol. IV, poem 278, pp. 251–262. Its heading names the Buyid ruler Adud al-Dawla, his sons Abu al-Fawaris and Abu Dulaf, and the poet’s route through Shi'b Bawwan. Numbering runs from 1 on p. 251 to 48 on p. 262. The commentary explains the Arabic speaker’s isolation in Fars, the words for perfume and musicians, the name Fannakhusraw and the ruler’s kunya Abu Shuja'. At 45 the two added letters of the diminutive «أُنيسيان» turn numerical increase into an insult. [Heading and opening](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1455/mode/1up) · [Song and journey](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1459/mode/1up) · [Names and titles](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1461/mode/1up) · [Diminutive and prayer](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1465/mode/1up) · [Ending](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1466/mode/1up) · [Searchable commentary](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/1379)

Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari, *Masalik al-absar fi mamalik al-amsar*, III, pp. 177–178, places the pass on the journey to Adud al-Dawla, then quotes 1–8 and the horse’s question at 17. His quotation has «الشمس» at 6 and «منها» at 8. For the silver beads shaken from the trees at 5, Ibn Manzur, *Lisan al-Arab*, XIII, p. 92, identifies «الجمان» as a Persian loanword. The *Tibyan* pictures beads of dew, while al-Wahidi sees sunlight on the horses’ manes. [Al-Umari, route](https://shamela.ws/book/30189/961) · [Quoted bayts](https://shamela.ws/book/30189/962) · [Ibn Manzur](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/6655)

The setting shifts within the valley passage. At 10 the traveller imagines a host who would have turned his bridle had he been in Damascus. At 12 Ibn Furraja takes the arriving and departing hearts as the guest’s confidence while under protection and the fear of losing it. Al-Wahidi also gives a reading in which the host is brave about giving and afraid of parting. The horse speaks at 17–18. From the rider’s answer about Abu Shuja' the poem turns to the ruler’s protection of travellers, then to his sons. [Al-Wahidi, pp. 767–769](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n133/mode/1up) · [Al-Tibyan, pp. 254–256](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1458/mode/1up)

## Readings

The two continuous transcriptions, the Cohere half-line clips, their wider Audar checks and the original verse cards give different kinds of evidence. Each score below compares candidates over the same aligned word span. For unstressed vowels, pronouns in chant and uncommon names, agreement between sources and grammatical fit can carry more weight than a small numerical difference.

The isolated scores, −10.85 and −10.19, scarcely distinguish them. At 5 al-Wahidi’s «فيها» scores −14.03 against −16.28 for the *Tibyan*’s «فيه». Both full passes give the feminine. |

## Verification of the halves

The initial pass flagged 24 of 96 halves. After the changes to «تصلّ» at 9.1 and «رثاء» at 46.1, the same check flagged 22. The wider Audar clips for those two earlier flags are transcribed in the saved first-pass file. The remaining 22 were checked against the wider clips for the revised text.

| Address | What accounts for the flag |
| --- | --- |
| 8.1–2, 9.1 | For «تشير» and «تصلّ», see the comparisons above. The recognisers represent «أواني» as «أوان» at the rhyme. The sustained ending and both printed verses support the final ya. At 9 the wider clip’s «أموال» would give the pebbles to money. The voice is describing water, «أمواه», in both continuous readings. |
| 11.1, 14.1–2 | Both clipped recognisers break the name for aloeswood, «يلنجوجي», into short familiar words. The complete Cohere reading has the intact word. Wide Audar restores «فيها» at 14.1 and «أغاني القيان» at 14.2, where Cohere had «القيام». |
| 22.1, 23.2 | «بعض الدولة» swallows the last dal of the ruler’s title. «عضد» recurs in the same bayt with its literal sense of upper arm. At 23.2 Audar recovers «حطّ» and approximates «اللِّدان» with a lengthened vowel. The commentary explains the supple brown lances. |
| 25.1–2, 27.1, 28.1 | The endings of the two verbs and following kaf blend into «فناخسر», which the printed name and parallel syntax identify. «أروض», the rare plural of «أرض», became «قروض» or singular «أرض» in the transcripts. The two printed texts and the contrast with the ruler’s one land give the plural. The wide 28.1 clip recovers «يُذمّ» from «يدم» and expands the collective «تَجر» to «تاجر». |
| 29.1, 30.1, 31.2 | Wide Audar restores «ثقات» for «في قرات», and the three words «أصمّ صِلّ أفعوان» for the compressed snake epithets. At 30.1 the short clip joins the spelling of «بلا صِحاب». The continuous Cohere reading preserves the phrase and the contrast with goods left unguarded. |
| 35.1–2, 37.1–2 | The recognisers substitute «العناصر» for loose locks, «العناصي». Full Cohere gives the locks. Wide Audar joins the francolin’s name «الحيقطان», which Cohere splits. At 37 both models redivide «شِبلَي هِزَبر» and «مُهرَي رِهان» at the dual endings. The comparison moves from two cubs of one lion to two racing colts. |
| 45.1–2, 46.1 | The short recogniser adds a mim to «كاثراه», which independent Audar restores. The subject «ابنا عدوّ» is dual. Both recognisers split «ياءَي» into «يا أي» at 45.2. The two sons are the two letters added in the diminutive «أُنيسيان». All passes support the change to «رثاء» at 46.1. |
| 47.2, 48.2 | Wide Audar recovers «وأصبح منك في عضب يمان». In the last half, the clips give «قراء» or fragments. Full Cohere gives «هراء كالكلام بلا معاني», the same comparison of men to empty talk as the two editions. |

## Metre and rhyme

Wafir, with nun as rawiy. At bayt 1 the sadr scans مفاعيلن / مفاعيلن / فعولن and the ajuz مفاعلتن / مفاعلتن / فعولن. At bayt 24 the sadr scans مفاعلتن / مفاعيلن / فعولن and the ajuz مفاعيلن / مفاعيلن / فعولن. The long i after the rhyme consonant accounts for the sustained endings of «أواني», «جاني» and «يماني» alongside «الزمانِ» and «العيانِ».
