# ألم تغتمض عيناك ليلة أرمدا

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari, original duration 207.946 s. The retained range is 0.90–204.17 s, enclosing the whole poem with natural margins. The first words are «ألم تغتمض» and the end is «أو تأبّدا». Both continuous transcripts and the 48 addressed halves trace a single passage through the 24 bayts. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LBbmBCVjQLw)

## Text and commentary

- Muhammad Muhammad Husayn, ed., *Diwan al-A'sha al-Kabir, Maymun ibn Qays*, no. 17, pp. 135–137. The first fourteen bayts are on p. 135, p. 136 continues their explanations, and 15–24 are on p. 137. All 24 are present in the recorded order. The glosses supply the sleepless patient and the snake-bitten man, the journey from al-Nujayr to Sarkhad, the stars guiding the camel and the verb for catching a camel's blood for food. At 22 the scan clearly reads «ولا السائل المحروم لا تتركنّه». [Opening and geographical glosses](https://archive.org/details/diwan-ala3cha/page/n165/mode/1up) · [Camel and journey](https://archive.org/details/diwan-ala3cha/page/n166/mode/1up) · [Counsel and close](https://archive.org/details/diwan-ala3cha/page/n167/mode/1up)
- Badr al-Din al-'Ayni, *al-Maqasid al-nahwiyya fi sharh shawahid shuruh al-Alfiyya*, 3:1049–1053, gives all 24 in his discussion of the opening as a grammatical witness. He explains the question as addressed to the speaker himself and discusses «ليلة أرمد» as indicating the quality of the closing of the eyes. In his sequence the prophet's fame precedes the arrival at the Hashimite's door. His text has «وبت كما بات»، «بعد اليوم»، «المراسيل تعتدي»، «وقوله» and «بعد اليوم» in places where the performance follows Husayn. His account preserves more than one story of the intervention by Quraysh. [Narrative and opening](https://shamela.ws/book/16714/1019) · [Variant poem text](https://shamela.ws/book/16714/1020) · [Syntax of the opening](https://shamela.ws/book/16714/1023)
- Ibn Hisham, *al-Sira al-nabawiyya*, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi, 1:386–388, «أمر أعشى بني قيس بن ثعلبة». Ibn Hisham introduces the account himself and names authorities from Bakr ibn Wa'il. The story of Quraysh deterring al-A'sha and of his death before a return is presented as their account. There are 23 bayts. The star-watch at our 9 is absent. The prayer, the captive and the forbidden woman come in another order. One bayt has its second half in common with our 22 but begins «وذا الرحم القربى فلا تقطعنّه». The old biography of the Prophet thus preserves a distinct version of both journey and advice. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/409) · [Visit and provisions](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/410) · [Closing counsel and the account of his return](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/411)
- Khayr al-Din al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 7:341–342, «الأعشى», gives Maymun's lineage, kunya, Manfuha, the patronage of Arab and Persian kings, the two epithets and 7 AH / 629 CE. [Biography](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/6954) · [Conclusion](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/6955)

The local collation uses the diwan transcribed from the scan, al-'Ayni's numbered poem and the text within Ibn Hisham's account. The count of 24 follows Husayn. For 24.2 the unvowelled ending in one transcript can represent the light emphatic nūn at pause, pronounced as alif. Both the marked diwan text and the connected grammar support «فانكِحَن». The following «أو» opens a second possible course of action.

## Metre

Ṭawīl with dāl as the rhyme consonant. The two halves of 1 scan `u–– | u––– | u–u | u–u–` and `u–u | u––– | u–– | u–u–`. At 11 («أجدّت برجليها») each half gives `u–– | u––– | u–– | u–u–`. Here and below u denotes a short syllable and – a long one.

## Acoustic readings and verification

Each comparison retains the same word count and uses the aligned half with 0.30 s of context on each side. Scores are summed CTC log likelihoods. Positive differences favour the alternative.

The scores use the MMS model and romanisation of the repository’s variants tool. The alternatives include readings in the editions, spellings produced by the recognisers and tests of pronunciation. A difference under two points has little discriminating value. The last column gives the reading used in the verse.

The first two halves belong to one question. Al-'Ayni and Ibn Hisham use “you spent the night as the snake-bitten man did”. In this take the repeated verb is عاد and the patient is definite, السليم المسهّد. Both continuous transcriptions and Husayn preserve the article on the adjective. At 5–14 the reciter’s camel runs the route in Husayn’s order and in his wording, including the two star-watchers and the “hand” received at the door. The noun نبي at 14 starts a fresh description in this order. Ibn Hisham's variation in the second half of 18 has a different number of words. The recitation clearly includes لم and كان, and the independent half check accepts the diwan's sequence. The more extensive change at 20.2 in al-'Ayni also has no equal-length word test.

At 20.1 the MMS comparison favours initial ولا, a genuine reading recorded in the Sira apparatus. Both continuous transcripts give وذا. An independent phone pass produces w-a-ð-a before the article, then an a after the b in النُّصُبَ and another after the b in المنصوبَ. The first noun and its adjective are accusative after demonstrative ذا. The case-vowel test favours النُّصُبَ by 5.46. For 22.1 the negative لا immediately before تتركنه has alignment confidence 0.987, in addition to the continuous transcripts and the diwan scan. At 23.1 the light emphatic nūn may be written differently in the sources. At 24.2 both analysis and Husayn’s vocalised text favour that same nūn in فانكحَن, and independent phone recognition places an n before أو at edited 200.03 s. ## Half-by-half adjudication

The release-timing Cohere pass covered 48 halves. It flagged 12, comprising 17 of 254 words under the repository’s normalisation. The table includes distinct readings from the preliminary cuts as well. Widened Audar transcriptions are shown where available. The lead’s timing review extended 9 half endings to preserve their voiced tails.

| Half | Isolated Cohere, including earlier cuts | Widened Audar | Editorial decision |

| 1.2 | وعاد كما عاد السليم المسهدا | وعاد كما عاد السليم المسهدا | Both short clips divide the consonant k with كما. The diwan has عادك ما عاد, “what visited him has visited you”. The k is the addressee. The alternative وبِتّ كما بات is a genuine reading in the Sira and al-'Ayni. |
| 2.2 | تناسيت قبل اليوم خل تمهددا | تناسيت قبل اليوم خلة مهددا | The first clip shifts the t of the construct خُلّة into the name Mahdad. The wider check restores the noun and the name separately. |
| 6.1 | وابتذل العيس المراقي لتغتلي | وأبتذل العيس المراق لتغتلي | The l of المراقيل is assigned to the next verb in the narrow clips. المراقيل describes swift camels and تغتلي is their movement across the distance. Both are in the diwan and Ibn Hisham. |
| 10.1 | وفيها اذا ما هجرت عجرثيه | وفيها إذا ما هجرت عجرثية | Both clips replace f by th in the uncommon عجرفية. The word designates a headstrong burst of speed in the midday heat. Husayn and al-'Ayni independently gloss the same consonants. |
| 12.2 | ولا منحفا حتي تزور محمدا | ولا منحفا حتى تزور محمدا | Both clip recognisers join the preposition من to حفى, soreness of the feet. The first member of the paired phrase also uses من. The endpoint محمد is present in both. |
| 13.2 | تريحي وتلقي من فواضره يدا | تريحي وتلقي من فواضله يدا | The wider reading restores فواضله, the benefits flowing from the host's generosity. They are the source from which the traveller receives the يد, a favour. |
| 17.1 | اذا انت لم ترحل بزاد من التقام | إذا أنت لم ترحل بزاد من التقى | The widened clip recovers التقى, piety. The apparent final m in the narrow recognition is unsupported. The genitive names what the metaphorical provisions consist of. |
| 17.2 | ولا قيت بعد الموت من قد تزودا | ولا لقيت بعد الموت من قد تزودا | The past verb لاقيت follows the coordinating و. The narrow recognitions place a separate negative لا ahead of قيت or لقيت. The sentence describes actually meeting someone who made provision. |
| 18.1 | ندمت علي الا تكون كمثله | ندمت على ألا تكون كمثله | Before lām, the n of أن is assimilated. The clipped recognisers' ألا transcribes that pronunciation. The diwan and the Sira write أن لا with its two grammatical parts. |
| 20.2 | ولا تعبد الاوثان والله فاعبده | ولا تعبد الأوثان والله فاعبد | An inflection on the final command and the dāl rhyme yield several machine spellings. The direct object الله is already expressed and emphatically fronted. Husayn and Ibn Hisham print the rhyming فاعبدا. |
| 21.2 | ولا تحمد الشيطان والله فاحمده | ولا تحمل الشيطان والله فاحمدا | The wide clip recovers فاحمدا. Its تحمل for تحمد breaks the direct pairing between praising the devil and praising God. The continuous text and editions preserve that pairing. |
| 24.2 | عليك حرام فانكها او تابدا | عليك حرام فانكحا أو تأبدا | Cohere hears an attached h and widened Audar hears alif. The light emphatic nūn of فانكِحَن can surface as alif in pausal pronunciation. The fully vocalised diwan and the connected construction select فانكِحَن. The distinct alternative تأبّدا follows in both clips. |
