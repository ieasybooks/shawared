# صَبَرتُ وَمِثلي صَبرُهُ لَيسَ يُنكَرُ

Osama al-Nahari's recording, published 3 July 2024, gives all **17 bayts**, once each, in the order of Khalil Mardam Bek's edition. The first word is «صبرت» and the last three verbs are «تفني وتغني وتفقر». The keep is **1.650–174.549 s** of the 174.549 s source, a continuous 172.899 s. The last syllable runs right up to the end of the original file. The lead's two source-end excerpts, 163–174.549 and 170–174.549 s, both recover the three verbs. There is no further silence to trim there. The caption sequence belongs to this same recording. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o9meWinky7A)

## Text and occasion

- Khalil Mardam Bek, *Diwan Ali ibn al-Jahm*, **Takmilat al-diwan**, no. **39**, pp. **131–132**. The first page gives the encounter in the caravan and bayts 1–7. The second gives 8–17. Mardam explicitly cites the Dar al-Kutub edition of *al-Aghani*, X, p. 216, for this supplement. His first note, citing Yaqut, places Khusaf in open country between Balis and Aleppo. [p. 131](https://archive.org/details/20201105_20201105_2037/page/n130/mode/1up) · [p. 132](https://archive.org/details/20201105_20201105_2037/page/n131/mode/1up)
- Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani, *Kitab al-Aghani*, **X, pp. 172–173 in the scan examined**, under «قتاله أعراباً قطعوا على قافلتهم الطريق في الشام». Abu al-Faraj transmits the report from his uncle on the authority of Muhammad ibn Sa'd. The caravan's fighting men fled, Ali held his ground, the others rallied to him and the raiders got nothing. Page 172 gives bayts 1–15 under «من الطويل». Page 173 gives 16–17 before a new report on Ali's childhood. These two full texts belong to the same line of transmission. The encounter has sometimes been classed as a reproach poem online. Its defining verb here is the first-person «صبرت». [p. 172](https://archive.org/details/al-aghani-10/page/n170/mode/1up) · [p. 173](https://archive.org/details/al-aghani-10/page/n171/mode/1up)
- Ibn Waki' al-Tinnisi, *al-Munsif li-l-sariq wa-l-masruq minhu*, p. **646**, independently quotes bayt **7**, names Ibn al-Jahm and comments that the host seems small to him «لكبر قلبه». This early witness also supplies the singular «عزيمة قلب». Its one bayt cannot establish the length of the poem. The full count rests on the two complete texts above. [Ibn Waki', p. 646](https://shamela.ws/book/31373/526)

The two printed pages of each full witness were read against the studio captions. Their consonantal texts collate throughout. The work files include the two scans, the verse transcription for each, the separate Ibn Waki' quotation and a contact sheet with the caption for each bayt.

The opening scans in *tawil*. In each sequence below ⏑ is short and – is long. The sadr of 1 gives ⏑–⏑ | ⏑––– | ⏑–– | ⏑–⏑–, its ajuz ⏑–⏑ | ⏑––– | ⏑–⏑ | ⏑–⏑–. At 9 the halves both give ⏑–– | ⏑––– | ⏑–⏑ | ⏑–⏑–. The recurring rhyme is **rāʾ** with damma, from «يُعذَرُ» to «تُفقِرُ».

## Readings and commentary

At **5** the printed distinction between the man «مُشَمِّر» and the horse «مُشَمَّر» joins two descriptions through the sound of their last word. Mardam glosses «المشيح» as the man who presses on and guards what is behind him. The horse is narrow-waisted. At **7** the subject «عزيمةُ قلبٍ» arrives only in the ajuz. Its scale determines how large the host looks. At **11–12** the sentence is likewise incomplete until «عسكرُ». Horse, heart, spear and blade have supplied one man with an army. At **13** a nail clipping measures what the attackers obtained. At **16–17** a broken bone, then a shoulder towering over other shoulders, turns ancestry into something the reader can picture. [Mardam, pp. 131–132](https://archive.org/details/20201105_20201105_2037/page/n131/mode/2up) · [Ibn Waki', p. 646](https://shamela.ws/book/31373/526)

**8.2 «بالمشرفيّة».** Both printed witnesses and the studio caption at source 78 s use the singular, denoting Mashraf swords collectively. Both continuous transcriptions write «بالمشرفيات». The same singer stretches the short vowel immediately before the linked tāʾ in «عزيمة» (7.2) and «الكريهة» (10.1), also transcribed as plurals. The two readings of «بالمشرفيّة» receive identical scores because the transliteration maps them to the same sequence. The comparable feet and the caption support the collective singular. At **6.1** Mardam vowels the place-name «خُساف». At **16.1** the bāʾ of «بن» in «فهر بن مالك» has no vowel in liaison between the two names. At **8.1** Death takes the field in many bareheaded figures. «حواسر» describes «المنايا» in both books and on the caption.
