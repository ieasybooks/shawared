## Title

No name is in use for this poem. A shamela search finds «رائية أبي تمام» twice, neither for this poem (Abdallah al-Tayyib, *al-Murshid*, vol. 4, p. 601, for the poem on al-Afshin; a grammar handbook's generic example), and «مرثية محمد بن حميد» only as a description (al-Isfara'ini, *al-Atwal*, vol. 2, p. 496; al-Dasuqi's *Hashiya*, vol. 4, p. 236). Ibn Ammar's «أمّ قلائده» (below) is a judgement, not a name. So the title is the first sadr.

## Attribution

- **Abu Tammam's, in every source:** both recensions of the diwan (al-Tibrizi's and al-Suli's, below); Ibn Dawud al-Zahiri (d. 297), *al-Zahra*, pp. 157–158 (shamela 738/157–158, `zahra.txt`), 20 abyat, «أنشد أحمد بن أبي طاهر عن أبي تمام»; al-Suli, *Akhbar Abi Tammam*, ed. Khalil Mahmud Asakir, Muhammad Abduh Azzam and Nazir al-Islam al-Hindi (Cairo: Lajnat al-Ta'lif wa-l-Tarjama wa-l-Nashr), pp. 124–125 (archive.org `0119pdf_201912`, leaves 147–148, read from the images), where Abu Dulaf quotes six abyat as «ما رثيت به محمد بن حميد»; al-Jarawi, *al-Hamasa al-Maghribiyya*, vol. 2, pp. 856–858 (shamela 6902/798–800, `jarawi.txt`), 18 abyat, «وقال أبو تمام حبيب بن أوس الطائي»; Ibn al-Athir, *al-Kamil* (below): «فرثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، منهم الطائي».
- **Disputed in part, and the claim answered:** Di'bil said that Abu Tammam took lines from a long elegy by Abu Sulma al-Muzani, a descendant of Zuhayr named «مكنف» (the *Akhbar* prints «مَكنف», the rest unvoweled, so the English intro gives only his kunya), on Dhufafa al-Absi. Two chains report it:
  - al-Suli, *Akhbar*, pp. 199–201 (leaves 222–224, read from the images; shamela 515/27–28, which numbers its pages itself), from Muhammad ibn Musa ibn Hammad, who saw Di'bil bring out the notebook in 235: «سرق أبو تمام أكثر هذه القصيدة، فأدخلها في شعره». The lines Di'bil showed include our 14.1 with «بني القعقاع», our 2.1 with «بعد وفاته» and our 2.2;
  - Ibn Ammar's epistle on Abu Tammam's errors, quoted by Ibn al-Mustawfi in Nu'man's notes to al-Suli (vol. 3, pp. 293–294, from MS ن): the same story from Di'bil through Abu Bakr ibn Abi Khaythama and Muhammad ibn Musa ibn Hammad, with our 2, 3, 14 and 15 among the lines (15.2 with «المجد» for «الجود»). Azzam summarizes it in his note on al-Tibrizi, vol. 4, p. 85.
  - The answer, in the same place in the *Akhbar* (p. 201): al-Hasan ibn Wahb knew Maknaf's poem and had heard Abu Tammam recite it, «وما في قصيدته شيء مما في قصيدة أبي تمام، ولكن دعبلاً خلط القصيدتين، إذ كانتا في وزن واحد، وكانتا مرثيتين، ليكذب على أبي تمام». Ibn al-Mustawfi calls Ibn Ammar's epistle full of «الميل على أبي تمام والغضّ منه» (Nu'man, p. 294).
  - The intro gives the claim and the answer; the text stays Abu Tammam's, as in every source.

## Editions

- **al-Tibrizi**, شرح ديوان أبي تمام, ed. Muhammad Abduh Azzam (Dar al-Ma'arif), vol. 4, poem 192, pp. 79–85: archive.org `3901pdf` (all four volumes in one scan), leaves 1395–1401, read from the page images (`tibrizi-notes.md`). Every bayt, voweled, under «قافية الراء» and the heading «وقال يرثي محمد بن حميد الطائي»; meter «في الأول من الطويل والقافية متواتر». The commentary here is short: al-Tibrizi's note on «فليجلّ» (1) and the comments of Abu al-Ala [ع] on 9, 17, 18 and 19, with Azzam's apparatus of manuscript readings (م، ل، س، ظ) and notes from Ibn al-Mustawfi, al-Suli's *Akhbar* and Ibn Ammar. **The reciter says al-Tibrizi's text word for word**, and it is the base text, put in the house style.
- **al-Suli**, شرح الصولي لديوان أبي تمام, ed. Khalaf Rashid Nu'man (Baghdad), vol. 3, poem 269, pp. 291–305: archive.org `diwan-abo-tamam-shareh-alsouly`, the file «Diwan Abo Tamam Shareh Alsouly 3», leaf n = printed page n, read from the page images (`suli-notes.md`; `scripts/archive.py page` fetches only the item's first file, so the pages were fetched from `…/Diwan%20Abo%20Tamam%20Shareh%20Alsouly%203/page/n<N>.jpg`). Al-Suli's own comments on 1, 5, 7, 13, 14 and 19, and Nu'man's notes quoting Ibn al-Mustawfi, who quotes Abu al-Ala, al-Kharzanji, Abu Yahya and Ibn Ammar.
- **Other witnesses:** al-Jarawi (above), 18 abyat in another order (our 3 before 2), with «من دونها» in 14.2; Ibn Dawud, *al-Zahra* (above), 20 abyat, with the readings marked below; al-Suli's *Akhbar*, pp. 124–125, six abyat, with «حشو» for «نسج» (12.1); the old shamela diwan (old.shamela 4387, the Abu Dhabi encyclopedia's text; one source, not an edition), pp. 494–496 (`diwan-old.txt`), the 30 abyat as al-Tibrizi has them; al-Nuwayri, *Nihayat al-Arab*, vol. 5, pp. 208–209 (shamela 10283/1758–1759, `nuwayri.txt`), a selection whose editor adds from «الديوان» a bayt neither edition has («فتى دهره شطران …»); the reciter doesn't say it.
- **The extra opening bayt:** «حرام لعمري أن يجف لها قطر / وأن تطعم التغميض ما بقي الدهر» stands after bayt 1 in al-Tibrizi's base manuscript and before it, as the opening, in his MSS ل، ظ (Azzam, p. 79 n. 1), in al-Suli's MS ن (Nu'man, p. 291 n. 2) and, among Ibn al-Mustawfi's copies, only in Abu Zakariya's (Nu'man, p. 294). Both editors leave it out as made up to answer those who faulted the opening (Azzam: «وأحسبه في أغلب الظن موضوع»). The reciter starts at «كذا فليجلّ».

`abyat_in_full` is 30, as in both editions. Collated half by half against the draft: al-Tibrizi differs nowhere; al-Suli at 1.2, 7.2, 23, 26.1 and 28.1 (below), and in two misprints (23.2 «البَدْوُ الحَضَرُ», dropping the و; 24.1 «غُيْثاً»).

- **Meter decides 2.2 «السَّفَرِ السَّفرُ»:** the first is safar (travel), the second safr (travellers). ## Meter

Tawil, al-Tibrizi's «الأول من الطويل»: the first darb, ending مفاعيلن (فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن / … فعولن مفاعيلن). `python3 -I /home/alifadel/.cache/shw-tmp/b4/tools/scan.py content/poems/kadha-fal-yajilla-al-khatbu/poem.json -v` scans all 56 halves with no flag; I scanned 1, 2.2, 16 and 18.1 by hand as well. Every ajuz ends مفاعيلن, and every sadr مفاعلن (the obligatory qabd of the first 'arud) except the opening's, which rhymes with its ajuz (tasri') and ends مفاعيلن; فعولُ for فعولن (qabd) occurs often, in the first or the third foot. 18 and 19.2 need the light «وَهوَ», «فَهيَ», as the prints have them.

## Commentary drawn on

- **The commentaries are thin on this poem.** The sharh follows what they say where they say it:
  - **al-Tibrizi and Abu al-Ala** (al-Tibrizi, pp. 79–83; Ibn al-Mustawfi in Nu'man): 1 (the vowel of «فليجلّ»), 9 (the parallels «ولو أنهم فرّوا …», al-Mazini, «وكالسيف …»; a harsh nature praised only against enemies), 17 (the two parsings of «كبراً»), 18 (some of the horses; his reading «جمالها»), 19 (المآثير, the two senses of بتر, al-Kawthar 3).
  - **al-Suli** (Nu'man): 1 (his answer to the critics, with Al Imran 21), 5 (الثغر), 7, 13 («الكلام: وهي خضر من سندس»), 14 (his defence, in the edition and at length in the *Akhbar*, pp. 125ff.: «إن ذهب البدر منهم فقد بقيت فيهم كواكب»; the poet ranks him above them, where al-Khuraymi's line makes them equal), 19 (as Azzam quotes him from MS م: «يريد أنه ليس أحد يفعل فعله»).
  - **Abu Yahya, al-Kharzanji and Ibn al-Mustawfi** (al-Mubarak ibn Ahmad), as Nu'man quotes Ibn al-Mustawfi from MS ن: 7 (al-Kharzanji's martyr; Abu Yahya's «صبر على الطعن والضرب …», which Ibn al-Mustawfi prefers), 8 (Abu Yahya: the notched sword and broken spears, «فهو إذن يعذر»), 9 (Abu Yahya: «لو شاء نجا …»), 13 (Abu Yahya: «فلما قتل ألبس لباس الشهداء في الجنة»), 15 (Abu Yahya: no successor in any virtue; Ibn al-Mustawfi: الشعر is what is said about him), 16 (al-Kharzanji and Abu Yahya, quoted), 17 (Abu Yahya: «ولم يكن ذلك من ذُلّ»), 18 (al-Kharzanji's and Abu Yahya's readings, not used), 21 (Abu Yahya: «كان اصلاً فجدّ فلا فرع له», as printed), 27 (Abu Yahya: «وقد كان الثرى يثرى به ويندى لكثرة عطائه، وكان غالباً على صرف الدهر بنائله فيغمره به»).
  - **Ibn Ammar's objections** (Nu'man, pp. 292–293; Azzam, pp. 79, 80, 84): the opening (1), «انثغر» for «اثّغر» (5), 11 («ليس هذا من ألفاظ العرب ولا أوصاف الشعراء»), «بقعة» (26). The critics of 1 and 14 are reported by al-Suli.
  - **13 as tadbij:** al-Khatib al-Qazwini, *al-Idah* (with al-Sa'idi's *Bughyat al-Idah*), vol. 4, p. 577 (shamela 10281/569), among the forms of tibaq; Ibn Abd al-Haqq al-Umari, *Durar al-Fara'id al-Mustahsana*, p. 429 (shamela 147320/395): «قصد بالأول الكناية عن: القتل، وبالثاني الكناية عن: دخول الجنة» (after Ibn Abi al-Isba', *Tahrir al-Tahbir*). The Quran reference in the note on 13.2.6 is al-Insan 21.
  - **6:** no commentator explains it. The sharh gives the likelier reading (the enemy tribes weep for the men he killed while his fame shines) and the other in a clause; the note on «ضحكت عنه» follows Ahmad al-Hashimi, *Jawahir al-Balagha*, p. 281 n. 6 (shamela 9256/250): «شبه السرور والأريحية بالضحك».
- **Where the commentators are silent** (2, 3, 4, 6, 10, 11, 12, 20, 22–26, 28), the sharh gives the plain sense of the words and says so where it chooses between readings (2, 6, 11, 23, 24, 25). The badi' the sharh points out without a commentator naming it is plain in the words: jinas in 2 (السَّفَر/السَّفر) and 27 (ثوى/الثرى, يغمر/الغمر), the repeated key word in 3, tibaq in 6, 12, 20 and 22, the grammar of 10 («نفسٌ» a third subject of «ردّه»).
- **23:** «we» read as Tayyi', the poet's own tribe, on the strength of al-Suli's «ما تنفكّ تفقد» (feminine: Tayyi'); the commentators don't say.

## History and people

- **Muhammad ibn Humayd al-Tusi:** al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 6, p. 110 (shamela 12286/5288, `alam-tusi.txt`): «محمد بن حميد الطاهري الطوسي: وال، من قواد جيش المأمون العباسي. ولاه قتال (زريق) و (بابك الخرمي)»; (… – 214 AH = … – 829 CE); «وكمن له جماعة من أصحاب بابك، فخرجوا عليه، فصمد لهم، فضربوا فرسه بمزراق فسقط إلى الأرض، فأكبوا عليه فقتلوه. وكان شجاعا ممدوحا جوادا، رثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، وعظم مقتله على المأمون». The note on 2.1.4 and the intro's dates are al-A'lam's.
- **The campaign and the battle:** al-Tabari, *Tarikh*, vol. 8, p. 619 (212 AH: al-Ma'mun sends him against Babak by way of Mosul) and p. 622 (214 AH: «مقتل محمد بن حميد الطوسي، قتله بابك بهشتادسر، يوم السبت لخمس ليال بقين من شهر ربيع الأول، ورفض عسكره») (shamela 9783/4965–4968, `tabari.txt`). Ibn al-Athir, *al-Kamil*, ed. Tadmuri, vol. 5, pp. 560–561 (shamela 21712/2925–2926, `kamil.txt`): the mountain passes, the camp at Hashtadsar, Babak's ambushers «تحت كل صخرة», the rout, «وصبر محمد بن حميد مكانه», his ride with one companion to a band of his men still fighting, the javelin, his death; «وكان محمد ممدحا جوادا، فرثاه الشعراء وأكثروا، منهم الطائي». The intro says no more than these two say.
- **Abu Dulaf's words:** al-Suli, *Akhbar*, pp. 124–125 (the chain: al-Hazanbal from Sa'id ibn Jabir al-Karkhi from his father, who was present).
- **Ibn Ammar's «أمّ قلائده»:** Nu'man, al-Suli vol. 3, p. 292 n. 2: «بمرثيته محمد بن حُميد التي هي امّ قلائده، والتي شهره الناس بها».
- **Peoples and names:** Banu Nabhan (14.1.2-3): «نبهان بن عمرو بن الغوث بن طيئ», Ibn Sa'd, *al-Tabaqat al-Kubra* (al-mutammim), vol. 2, p. 637 (shamela 12416/557); that they are the dead man's people is the poem's (14, 22) and al-Tibrizi's heading «الطائي». Tayyi' (22.1.5): Lisan, vol. 1, p. 116, «أبو قبيلة من اليمن، وهو طيئ بن أدد»; the poet's tribe (his poet page: «حبيب بن أوس الطائي»). Tamim and Bakr: Ibn Hazm, *Jamharat Ansab al-Arab*, p. 207 («بنو تميم بن مرّ بن أدّ») and p. 307 («بنو بكر بن وائل بن قاسط») (shamela 9793/207, 9793/304).
- **The poet** is the existing `abu-tammam`; nothing about him was changed.
