# أَسماءُ خَيلِ النَّبِيِّ

## Recording and text

Khalid al-Sharafi, Alqimmah Studio, 24 July 2025. The opening credit and the description name محمد بن محسن أبورقبه as the author. The whole-track transcription gives ten rajaz couplets in one uninterrupted sequence. All ten appear on screen, the first half appearing before the second. The displayed verses can be checked at 6, 15, 21, 30, 39, 45, 54, 60, 69 and 78 s of the original. For this contemporary poem those captions are the studio’s written witness. The works below can corroborate the names and stories independently. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XVKLG3sx308)

- The seven names, and the distinction between seven agreed names and other reported ones, occur together in Ibn al-Qayyim, *Zad al-ma'ad*, ed. Ata'at al-Ilm, I, pp. 129–130. Even accounts that include the same horse can describe it differently. Ibn al-Qayyim calls al-Murtajiz grey, while the poet follows the tradition that it was white. [Ibn al-Qayyim](https://shamela.ws/book/197/278)
- Al-Salihi al-Shami, *Subul al-huda wa-l-rashad*, VII, pp. 396–399, 402. The entry on al-Sakb explains its name by the flow of water and relates the ride at Uhud. The entries on Sabha, al-Murtajiz, al-Lahif and al-Ward supply the race, the sale, the long tail and Tamim’s gift. Compare al-Dhahabi’s discussion of the names and their competing vocalisations, *Tarikh al-Islam*, ed. Tadmuri, I, p. 518. [Subul, p. 396](https://shamela.ws/book/1693/3217) · [P. 397](https://shamela.ws/book/1693/3218) · [P. 398](https://shamela.ws/book/1693/3219) · [P. 402](https://shamela.ws/book/1693/3223) · [Al-Dhahabi](https://shamela.ws/book/12397/508)
- Al-Maqrizi, *Imta' al-asma'*, ed. Muhammad Abd al-Hamid al-Numaysi, VII, p. 207, quotes al-Waqidi’s account of a race at al-Naqi' on the return from al-Muraysi'. Abu Usayd rode al-Zarib to victory, while Bilal rode the winning camel. This identifies what «لَهُ التَّقديمُ» refers to in 8.2. [Al-Maqrizi](https://shamela.ws/book/1524/2640)

The opening scans «أَسْماْءُ خَيْ / لِلْمُصْطَفى / كَما تَرى», and 6.1 «وَمِنْ جُهَيْ / نَتَشْتَرا / هُ أَشْقَرا». The feet are rajaz, with khabn in the latter. The two halves rhyme with each other and the rhyme changes from one couplet to the next. There is no single rawiy.

## Performance

The retained interval is 2.63–81.91 s of the 84.288 s original. At 3.2 and 4.2 the full-track transcriber mistook «شاهِدُهُ» and «شاهِدَ» for forms of «شهيد». The second transcriber, Audar, on a continuous 16-second clip from the original, recovered «شاهده» and «شاهد بيعه» as displayed. Both full-clip backends had difficulty with the last word of 4.2. The text gives the studio’s «الثابت», which echoes the witness’s father’s name in 4.1. A sentence can run over a paired rhyme, as the identification of the witness does in 3–4.

The 79.28 s master measures −20.0 LUFS after −6.9 dB of gain. The first word starts at 0.50 s and the last ends at 76.86 s, before the echo. All 20 halves align and no word falls below the 0.35 confidence threshold. Independent transcription of the 20 clips flagged three halves (six of 82 words). At 4.1 the short cut split the name Khuzayma ibn Thabit into ordinary words. A broader Audar clip recovered the name. At 6.1 both isolated readings misdivided the liaison between «جُهَينَةَ» and «اِشتَراهُ». The continuous transcript, captions and account of the purchase agree. At 9.2 the first recogniser attached a final m to «وَانتَقاهُ». The continuous transcript and Audar return the singular object heard in the paired rhyme. The short cut of 2.1 also produced «قال» before «أوّلها». The continuous track and verse card start directly with «أوّلها». The unvowelled first letter of «بنَ» in 4.1 represents the dropped initial alif between the two proper names.

At 3.2 the acoustic scores for «شاهِدُهُ» (−21.19) and «شَهيدُهُ» (−22.33) are close. Audar, the caption and 4.2 make the witness reading intelligible. At 4.2 «الثّابِتِ» scores −33.41, ahead of both «الثّاني» (−38.96) and «الثّامِنِ» (−41.12). At 8.1 the studio and the biographical sources name the horse «الظَّرِبُ». The acoustic test alone scarcely distinguishes it (−35.49) from the recogniser’s «الضَّريبُ» (−36.08).
