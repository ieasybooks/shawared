## The elegy and its witnesses

Marwan ibn Abi Hafsa, *Shi'r Marwan ibn Abi Hafsa*, collected and edited by Husayn Atwan (Cairo, Dar al-Ma'arif), poem **50**, pp. **79–83**. The rubric names Ma'n ibn Za'ida al-Shaybani as the man mourned and names the metre. The five pages carry respectively verses 1–11, 12–21, 22–33, 34–43 and 44–54. The recording gives all 54 once, in this order. In the following poem, p. 84, the rubric explicitly connects this lam elegy with a later poem for Ja'far ibn Yahya al-Barmaki. That poem falls outside the recording. [Text and rubric, p. 79](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n78/mode/1up) · [Closing page, p. 83](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n82/mode/1up) · [Following rubric](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n83/mode/1up)

Sadr al-Din al-Basri, *al-Hamasa al-basriyya*, ed. Mukhtar al-Din Ahmad, I, pp. 208–209, no. 25, names Marwan and anthologises sixteen bayts. Its order corresponds to our 1, 3, 8, 11, 10, 12, 24, 25, 2, 34, 37, 36, 42, 44, 45, 14. It puts the darkened sun much later. It has «محامد» and «هوى الجبل» in the opening, «فيض راحته» in 12, and «زمالا» in its excerpt of 44. It does not quote the final saddle oath. Atwan's notes on pp. 79–83 support the sharh of the horse equipment, charity, campaign vocabulary, the shroud on the sun, and the chain of laments. The identity of the woman who speaks at 29–32 remains open. [Basri, p. 208](https://shamela.ws/book/6901/208) · [p. 209](https://shamela.ws/book/6901/209)

Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-aʿyan*, ed. Ihsan Abbas, V, pp. 249–251, names Marwan as Maʿn’s poet and recounts his patron’s murder at Bust in Sistan. He gives several proposed years for the death. His selection includes the pools in 15, the excuses in 37 and the closing oath in 54. On p. 251 he also transmits Ibn al-Muʿtazz’s account of Jaʿfar al-Barmaki asking to hear the elegy. [Attribution and occasion, p. 249](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2247) · [p. 250](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2248) · [Closing verses and Jaʿfar, p. 251](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2249)

The poet’s full name, his family in al-Yamama, his court patrons and his death in Baghdad in 182 AH (798 CE) follow al-Zirikli, *al-Aʿlam*, VII, p. 208. The reward in the biography belongs to a different poem. Al-Tanukhi gives its incipit «طرقتك زائرة» on p. 378 of *al-Faraj baʿd al-shidda* and the hundred-thousand-dirham award on p. 379. [Al-Zirikli, VII, p. 208](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/6556) · [Al-Tanukhi, p. 378](https://shamela.ws/book/12828/303) · [p. 379](https://shamela.ws/book/12828/304)

## The closing pronoun and other readings

At **54.2** Atwan, p. 83, prints «يميناً لا يشدّ **له** حبالا». «رحله» immediately precedes it. Ibn Khallikan, V, p. 251, also transmits «له» in this half. The masculine pronoun refers to the riding saddle or travelling gear whose straps he used to fasten. The reciter says **«لَها»**. The whole-recording Cohere and Audar passes and the lead’s two twelve-second closing clips agree. Original 468.71–469.06 s gives /l a h a/ before «حبالا», with a clear final a in the local phone evidence. The same-span comparison from 465.95–471.80 gives −26.71 for لها and −34.77 for له, a margin of 8.06. A feminine «يمين» immediately precedes the pronoun. “Under that oath” is one possible construction. The source does not explicitly tell us how the performer construed it. The translation leaves the possessor of the straps open. The word card gives both the recorded and printed pronouns. [Atwan’s final bayt](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n82/mode/1up)

At 1.2 «مكارم» scores 19.25 above Basri’s «محامد». At 3.1 «هو الجبل» scores 2.58 above «هوى الجبل», and widened Audar gives «هو». At 22.1 the enlarged first row of Atwan’s p. 81 gives «تُصابُ بِهِ». The final ba has a dot below the bowl. The two continuous transcripts and the /b/ at original 184.84 s agree with the print. The alternative «تُصال» scored 5.97 points lower. At 37.2 the main type on Atwan’s p. 82 gives «واغتلالا» with a dot on the ghayn. His own note 37 reads «الاعتلال: المماطلة». Basri and Ibn Khallikan transmit the undotted ʿayn in «واعتلالا». The recorded candidate with ʿayn leads by 2.29. The reading fits the petitioner put off with excuses, and both language cards record the difference within Atwan’s page. [Atwan, p. 81, first row](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n80/mode/1up) · [Atwan, p. 82, verse and note 37](https://archive.org/details/4099pdf/page/n81/mode/1up) · [Ibn Khallikan, p. 251](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/2249) At 49.2 the print has «في الأمور», supported by the articulated preposition. Comparing isolated «في» to the whole word «بالأمور» is not a meaningful quantitative test.

Wafir, three feet to a half. At 1.1 «مَضَى لِسَبِي / لِهِي مَعْنُنْ / وَأَبْقَى» gives ∪ – ∪ ∪ – | ∪ – – – | ∪ – –. At 27.1 «كَأَنَّ اللَّيْـ / لَ وَاصَلَ بَعْـ / دَ مَعْنٍ» gives ∪ – – – | ∪ – ∪ ∪ – | ∪ – –. The repeated lam followed by a long a runs from «تُنالَا» to «حبالا». The repeated cries «لهف أبي» introduce different verses. There is no reprise. Keep **2.95–472.25 s** retains the first /m/ around 3.56 s and the long final a with its decay.

## Earlier verification pass

**15.1** was flagged in both earlier passes. The initial log and `verification-final/verify-before.log` give «وما كانت تجف له حياضه», adding a possessive to the last noun. The half originally ended at 127.29 s. On the release window, 123.69–127.67 s, isolated Cohere gives «وَمَا كَانَتْ تَجِفُّ لَهُ حِيَاضٌ». Both continuous passes have «له حياض من المعروف». The local phone trace gives /d/ at 127.30 s, /o/ at 127.36 s and /n/ at 127.50 s, followed by the /m/ of «من» at 128.01 s. This places the audible nunation beyond the early clip’s nominal endpoint. Atwan prints indefinite «حِياضٌ» on p. 80 and glosses it as pools of water. They are the subject of «تجفّ», and «من المعروف مترعة» in 15.2 describes the bounty that fills them. «له» already identifies their giver. **Clip edges.** The isolated 1.2 prefixes «يا» to «مكارم». The preceding «وأبقى» has a long final vowel. The local source identifies /m/ at 7.60 s and gives «مكارم» directly. At 34.2 «أن» appears before «أبى». The preceding «دهرٍ» supplies an audible nasal ending. Both the printed syntax and the longer audio begin the actual ajuz at «أبى».

Full Audar loses part of 9.1–9.2, merges the words of 41.2, drops parts of 42.2–43.2 and collapses 53.2. Full Cohere, the timed contiguous halves and the print retain these passages. In particular, 41.2 has the pregnant animals casting their young and 43.2 has the confidant’s request for a way out.
