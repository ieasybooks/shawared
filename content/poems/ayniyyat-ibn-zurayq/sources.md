## Title

«عينية ابن زريق» is the name the books use: al-Tanahi's article is headed so, and al-Safadi calls it «عينيّة ابن زريق المشهورة» (*al-Wafi*, vol. 6, p. 177). The video calls it «فراقية ابن زريق», as does the editor's heading in *Masari' al-Ushshaq*. I found no source for the name «اليتيمة», so it is not used.

## Editions and sources

The poem has no diwan; it survives in biographies and anthologies, and their texts differ in readings, in the order of abyat 6–9 and in how many abyat they have (38 to 41 in the fuller texts). Every text below is in `media/research/ayniyyat-ibn-zurayq/` (`<name>.txt` as fetched; `ed-<name>.txt` the poem alone, for `scripts/collate.py`; `collation.txt` the full collation against the draft).

- **al-Subki, طبقات الشافعية الكبرى**, ed. Mahmud al-Tanahi and Abd al-Fattah al-Hilw, vol. 1, pp. 308–311: read on the page images (archive.org `Tabaghat_Shafiya_Subki`, leaves 308–311) and as text on shamela 6739/302–306 (`subki.txt`). 38 abyat, partly voweled, with notes giving Ibn Hijja's readings. The edition the vowels of 7.1 «حِلٍّ», 24.1 «لِيَ ذُقتَ», 34.1 «أَصبَحتَ», 35.1 «لِيَ» and 38.2 «فَكَّرتَ» come from. Its own readings, as the page images vowel them, include 2.1 «يُضِرُّ», 6.2 «بِالعَزمِ يُجمِعُهُ», 8.1 «الزِّماعُ», 10.1 «واصِلَةً», 24.2 «لَستُ أَرقَعُهُ» (from its manuscript ج; the printed source and manuscript د have «أوقعه»), 27.1 «أُبتُ لَهُ» and 39.2 «تُجَمِّعُني». Al-Subki gives the poem with his own isnad through al-Humaydi to the amir Abu al-Hayja' ibn Shahin: «أنشدني عليّ بن زريق أبو الحسن الكاتب البغدادي لنفسه»; Ibn Hazm's saying from Abu Sa'd (al-Sam'ani) in his *Dhayl*; and the story of the man found dead, «وذكر ابن السمعاني لهذه القصيدة قصة عجيبة فروى بسنده…».
- **al-Safadi, الوافي بالوفيات**, vol. 21, pp. 76–78: shamela 6677/5822–5824 (`wafi.txt`). 40 abyat. Its entry says the poem praised «العميد أبا نصر وزير طغرلبك» (al-Kunduri, d. 456). Al-Tanahi (pp. 119–121) sets this against Abu Hayyan al-Tawhidi (d. about 400), who already quotes abyat 15–16 without naming a poet; finding nothing to refute al-Safadi, he doubts instead that those two abyat are Ibn Zurayq's, since al-Tha'alibi (*Yatimat al-Dahr* 1:293) gives them, with the two after them, to al-Wa'wa' al-Dimashqi (d. about 385). He also notes that Ibn Khallikan (*Wafayat* 4:420) gives bayt 15 to «محمد بن زريق الكاتب الكوفي», a fourth-century poet whom al-Tha'alibi calls Abu Muhammad ibn Zurayq al-Kufi. The bio reports both.
- **al-Suyuti, المحاضرات والمحاورات** (critical edition with variants from its manuscripts, al-Subki and Ibn Hijja), pp. 289–292: shamela 9099/265–268 (`suyuti.txt`). 40 abyat.
- **Ibn Hijja al-Hamawi, ثمرات الأوراق**, vol. 2, pp. 210–213: shamela 11056/452–455 (`thamarat.txt`). 40 abyat; shamela's text shuffles halves at 12–13. Al-Subki's and al-Suyuti's notes give its readings too.
- **al-Baha' al-Amili, الكشكول**, ed. al-Namari (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1998), vol. 1, pp. 93–95: shamela 1571/91–93 (`kashkul.txt`). 40 abyat. The text closest to the recording: it alone of the older sources has 11 «قد وزّع الله بين الخلق رزقهم … من خلق», 14.2 «إرثاً», 32.1 «منزل العيش» and 31.1 «قد كنت».
- **Ibn Aydamir, الدر الفريد وبيت القصيد**, vol. 6, pp. 324–326: shamela 20762/2783–2785 (`durr.txt`). 38 abyat, voweled (shamela's text has misprints). The only older source with 5.1 «التشتيت»; with al-Kashkul, the only ones with 29.1 «أنّ الدهر».
- **Usama ibn Munqidh, المنازل والديار**, pp. 8–9: shamela 5416/8–9 (`manazil.txt`). Ten abyat (32–36, 15–19), with 19.1 «ثوب الصبر».
- **al-Sarraj al-Qari, مصارع العشاق**, vol. 1, pp. 23–24: shamela 26549/12–13 (`masari.txt`, `ed-masari.txt`). The story of the man found dead, with its isnad (heard at Kufa in 441 AH), and 13 abyat.
- **Mahmud al-Tanahi, «عينية ابن زريق»** (مجلة الشعر, January 1976), in مقالات الطناحي, vol. 1, pp. 118–123: shamela 148533/104–109 (`tanahi-a.txt`, `tanahi.txt`). What is known of the poet, the poem's sources and the doubts about its date, and a text of 40 abyat with glosses.
- **Late anthologies:** Shakir Shuqayr al-Batluni, نفح الأزهار, pp. 5–7, shamela 28175/3–5 (`nafh.txt`), 41 lines, the only source with 40.2 and 41 as recited; Abd al-Rahman ibn Darham, نزهة الأبصار, pp. 131–132, shamela 5375/131–132 (`nuzha.txt`), 39 abyat, ending with 41; Ahmad al-Hashimi, جواهر الأدب, vol. 2, pp. 371–373, shamela 21505/579–581 (`jawahir.txt`), 39 abyat, ending with the older closing bayt. Ibn Darham and al-Hashimi print 10.1 «توصله» and 4.2 «فضيّقت».

`abyat_in_full` is 41: al-Batluni's text, the longest, has 41 lines, and so does the recording; but al-Batluni lacks 26 and 27 and has two lines the reciter doesn't say («لو أنني لم تقع عيني على بلد», also in al-Sarraj, and «يا من أقطّع أيامي وأنفدها», also in Ibn Aydamir). The older sources close with one bayt where the recording has 40 and 41: «وإن تنل أحدًا منّا منيّته فما الذي بقضاء الله يصنعه».

**Order.** The recording has 6 «ما آب», 7 «كأنّما», 8 «إذا الزمان», 9 «تأبى المطامع», as al-Subki, al-Tanahi, al-Batluni and the later anthologies do; al-Safadi, al-Suyuti, al-Kashkul and Ibn Aydamir put «تأبى» before «كأنّما».

## Commentary drawn on

- There is no classical commentary on the poem. The sharh rests on the lexicons and on al-Tanahi's glosses (ضلعت، التفنيد، الحَلّ، الزماع، الظرف).
- **Parallels named in the sharh:** Abu Nuwas's «دع عنك لومي فإنّ اللوم إغراء» beside bayt 1, as al-Abbasi sets them (*Ma'ahid al-Tansis*, vol. 2, p. 256, shamela 6951/634, `maahid.txt`); Ibn al-Mu'tazz's «وجيد الظبي في أزراره» as the source of 15's «الأزرار», after Muhyi al-Din Darwish (*I'rab al-Qur'an wa-Bayanuh*, vol. 6, p. 380, shamela 2163/3193); Hud 11:6 (11), Ibrahim 14:7 (22), al-Sharh 94:5 (38).
- **On single abyat:** 15 is quoted by Abu Hayyan with 16 and by Ibn Khallikan alone (al-Tanahi, pp. 119, 121); 21 is the line Ibn Aydamir picks out from the poem as his entry no. 8401 (*al-Durr al-Farid*, vol. 6, p. 324) before giving the rest.
- **Intro and bio:** the story from al-Sarraj (*Masari'*, vol. 1, pp. 23–24) and al-Subki (vol. 1, pp. 311–312); Ibn Hazm's saying as al-Humaydi gives it (al-Safadi, vol. 21, p. 77; al-Suyuti, p. 289; al-Tanahi, p. 119); the poet's name from al-Subki and al-Tanahi; "travelled to al-Andalus and died there", the lack of a dated biography and Brockelmann's "about 420" from al-Tanahi (p. 119); the elegy for a rooster from al-Safadi (vol. 21, pp. 78–79). Al-Zirikli's *al-A'lam* has no entry for him (searched on shamela 12286), so `poet.json` has no `died`: the only date is Brockelmann's estimate.
- **Not used:** the later anthologies' story that he left a cousin he loved (al-Batluni) and that the poem was found «تحت وساده» (Ibn Darham), which the older sources don't have; and the story of a singing girl who sang bayt 15 before Tamim ibn al-Mu'izz (d. 368 by Sibt ibn al-Jawzi's placing, 374 by Ibn Khallikan's, as the *Mir'at*'s editor notes), told by al-Tanukhi (*Nishwar al-Muhadara*, vol. 7, p. 267) and Sibt ibn al-Jawzi (*Mir'at al-Zaman*, vol. 17, p. 522, `mirat.txt`). Like Abu Hayyan's quotation, which al-Tanahi discusses, it would put at least that bayt well before 420. Neither is used here.
