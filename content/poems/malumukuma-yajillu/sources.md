# مَلومُكُما يَجِلُّ عَنِ المَلامِ

## Recording and sequence

Osama al-Nahari, uploaded 19 November 2025. Of the source’s 377.348 seconds, 5.500–371.500 are retained. Cutting the quiet head and tail leaves 366.000 s at −20.6 LUFS. All 82 recorded halves are heard once. The sequence is printed bayts 1–23, then 25–42. App bayts 24–41 therefore correspond to printed 25–42. After «فتوسعه بأنواع السقام» the voice goes straight to «كأنّ الصبح يطردها». There is no internal cut. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cf4FQOeRJCU)

## Editions, occasion and interpretation

- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, ed. Friedrich Dieterici, Berlin, 1861, pp. 675–680. The heading on p. 675 places the fever in Egypt in Dhu al-Hijja 348 AH (960 CE). The final numbered bayt 42 and the next poem’s heading share p. 680. [Heading and opening](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n225/mode/1up) · [The fever’s visit, p. 678](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n222/mode/1up) · [Ending](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n220/mode/1up) · [Electronic text](https://shamela.ws/book/754/335)
- *Al-Tibyan fi sharh al-diwan*, attributed to al-Ukbari, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi, vol. IV, poem 253, pp. 142–149. Its numbered text establishes the full count of 42. Printed bayt 24 reads «إذا ما فارقتني غسلتني / كأنّا عاكفان على حرام». In al-Wahidi’s explanation the sweat at the visitor’s departure resembles a ritual washing after an illicit encounter. The recording omits that stage of the extended figure. [Opening](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1346/mode/1up) · [Printed 20–25](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1350/mode/1up) · [Printed 40–42](https://archive.org/details/20240104_20240104_1855/page/n1353/mode/1up) · [Electronic commentary](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/1270)

The two commentaries inform the poem’s movement from desert travel to confinement in Egypt. Al-Wahidi explains the proverbial ostrich marrow and also records the ostrich egg’s yolk, «مُحّ», at 7. The four streams at printed 25 are the inner and outer corners of both eyes. The terms of the doctor’s diagnosis give way to an account of a horse weakened by rest. The contrast between waking, sleeping and dying in the last two verses follows al-Wahidi’s explicit gloss of death as a third state. [Marrow and yolk](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n224/mode/1up) · [The eyes](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n222/mode/1up) · [The horse and the third state](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n220/mode/1up)

## Metre

Wafir, with mim as rawiy. In 1.1 the feet are مفاعلتن / مفاعلتن / فعولن, and in 1.2 مفاعلتن / مفاعيلن / فعولن. The return of the fever at 21.1 and 21.2 repeats those respective patterns. The alternation in the second foot is the regular عصب of wafir.
