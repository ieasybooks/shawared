# Hatim al-Ta'i at the deserted camp

## Performance and basis of numbering

Osama al-Nahari starts with the camp's perimeter trench and finishes with the good name that survives the second poor rider. The sound begins at about 0.92 seconds and dies away at about 359.55. The master keeps 0.41–360.19 on the source clock. The 41 bayts follow Ibn al-Shajari's sequence once each. The opening and «متى ترق أضغان العشيرة بالأنى» follow tawil, with alternating فعولن and مفاعيلن and a shortened fourth foot. Mim is the rhyme consonant. The following alif releases the rhyme. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-_B_z_fM54I)

## Editions, other recensions and glosses

- Ibn al-Shajari, *Mukhtarat shuʿaraʾ al-Arab*, ed. and glossed by Mahmud Hasan Zanati, Matbaʿat al-Iʿtimad, Cairo, 1925, I, pp. 11–14. All 41 performed bayts occur in this order. P. 11 explains «أذاعت به» as the winds scattering what remains, the completed year, the silver table and the light of the tent. P. 12 glosses the setting star, inherited wealth, the two senses of شرى, the cut in the earth and the hurtful remark. Pp. 13–14 explain covering another person's fault, the blade's grooves and the saddle that fits its horse. The next item on p. 14 is by Bashama ibn Amr. [P. 11](https://shamela.ws/book/740/12) · [P. 12](https://shamela.ws/book/740/13) · [P. 13](https://shamela.ws/book/740/14) · [P. 14](https://shamela.ws/book/740/15)
- *Diwan Hatim al-Taʾi*, Dar Sadir, Beirut, pp. 79–83, under «حاتم يتصعلك». This is a different recension. It has «تهادى عليها حليها» in the woman's portrait, omits the bayt on her hand and wrist, moves the advice to practise forbearance until after the inheritance passage, and prints additional advice at p. 81. Its poem closes on the saddle and horse, our 38. The following page opens a different poem. Neither its extra lines nor its different ordering describe omissions from Ibn al-Shajari's 41. Its glosses on the tent trench, rustling jewellery, burial, hunger and the sword's grooves help recover the material details. [P. 79](https://archive.org/details/20191019_20191019_1551/page/n78/mode/1up) · [P. 81](https://archive.org/details/20191019_20191019_1551/page/n80/mode/1up) · [Pp. 82–83](https://archive.org/details/20191019_20191019_1551/page/n81/mode/2up)
- Al-Marzuqi, *Amali*, Shamela electronic pages 53–54, has the hand and wrist, «تهادى عليها حليها» and «فبانت لطيّات لها». Its saddle verse corresponds to 38. It passes directly to the close corresponding to 41 and reads «وإن يحيَ» where the recording has «وإن عاش». Ibn al-Shajari also gives the intervening encounter with the spears and the battle baring its teeth (39–40). Ibn Aydamur al-Mustaʿsimi, *al-Durr al-farid wa-bayt al-qasid*, ed. Kamil Salman al-Juburi, VIII, pp. 124–126, item 11412, offers a further recension. Its variants include «وغيرت الأيام» at 3.2 and «الإتلاف في الحمد» at 11.2. - Al-Zirikli, *al-Aʿlam*, II, p. 151, gives Hatim's lineage, the journey to Syria, the marriage to Mawiyya and his death at Awarid. He dates it to 46 years before the Hijra (578 CE). Both profiles attribute that chronology to him. [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/1378)

## Readings

The original recording and the anthology give «وبدّلت الأنواء» at 3.2, «كفاثور اللجين» at 6.1, «فتضرّما» at 7.2 and «الإنفاق في الحق» at 11.2. At 16.2 «عليك» directly addresses the owner of the self, as in the Dar Sadir scan p. 80. At 19.2 the pauper's final possession is a «خطّ من الأرض», the opened grave. At 38.1 one full transcript writes «قاتل». Ibn al-Shajari defines «قاتر» as a saddle that grips the horse's back well. At 41.2 both Ibn al-Shajari and the performance close on «وإن عاش لم يقعد ضعيفاً مذمماً».

At 15.1 the anthology, Dar Sadir p. 81 and the original video at 127 seconds read «تحلّم عن الأدنين». Both full transcriptions and both clip transcriptions instead give «على». The initial word alignment gave عن confidence 0.142. In an equal-length comparison of the two prepositions, the original window scored على at −5.77 and عن at −13.07. The text therefore follows the performance, with the witnesses' preposition in the paired note. At 18.1 the caption writes تشقين, al-Marzuqi expressly has a light final nun, and Ibn al-Shajari writes «تشقياً», a spelling of the light emphatic ending with alif. Dar Sadir p. 80 also writes the verb with nun. The light ending fits the tawil here. The first alignment scored the light ending −11.77, the heavy ending −15.07 and the alif reading −16.87. Cohere transcribes تشقيا while Audar transcribes تشقين. The distinction matters for the sound of the warning, with little effect on the English. At 36.2 the panel divides the continuous utterance as «ثمّ تصمّما». Ibn al-Shajari, al-Marzuqi and Ibn Aydamur attach the t to the conjunction. Dar Sadir p. 83 explicitly glosses ثمت as ثم and صمّم as holding to a resolution. The two divisions sound alike in connected speech. The word note explains the printed division. ## Clip verification

The final Cohere check has 82 clips, eighteen marked halves and 23 differing words out of 421. Each marked half has a second Audar transcription with a 0.35-second margin at both edges of the clip. The first run also marked the preposition at 15.1, which passed after the text and alignment were corrected.
