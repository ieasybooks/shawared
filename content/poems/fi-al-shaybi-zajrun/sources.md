# في الشيب زجر له لو كان ينزجر

## Performance

Osama al-Nahari recites all 41 bayts of al-Buhturi’s praise of Ali ibn Murr. Both full transcriptions follow the diwan from the grey-haired lover to the final claim that poetry itself takes pride in its patron. Each of the 82 halves is performed once. The video displays the opening and the patron’s name on a static title card. The first voice enters at about 1.91 s and the last vowel ends about 445.72 s into a source lasting 449.515 s. The single keep, 1.38–446.23 s on the source clock, makes a master of 444.85 s. The pauses at verse boundaries, including the pause after «في» before «أضعافه», belong to the delivery. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WNsByEa-Kxs)

The metre is basit. In the opening sadr «فِشْشَيْبِ زَجْ | رُنْ لَهُو | لَوْ كَانَ يَنْـ | زَجِرُو» gives مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. In 21.1 «يُهابُ في | نا وَما | في لَحْظِهِـي | شَرَرُنْ» shortens the first foot to مُتَفْعِلُن. The rhyme consonant is ra with final u. Words as different as «حَجَرُ», «صَعَرُ» and «تفتخرُ» preserve it.

## Editions and occasion

- *Diwan al-Buhturi*, ed. Hasan Kamil al-Sayrafi, Dar al-Ma'arif, no. 379, printed pp. 953–958. Page 953 distinguishes the patron named in 15, Ali ibn Murr of Tayy, from Ali ibn Yahya al-Armani. Al-Sayrafi connects this poem with the praises of Murr and his son and tentatively dates the group to 222 AH. The date in the introduction has that status. His notes on the naked bow-tree in 5, the bitter plants and the name Murr in 15, and the two tribal branches and their halter in 36 inform the sharh and word cards. The final page sets all the last nine bayts in the recited order. - *Diwan al-Buhturi*, corrected and prepared for the press by Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi, Cairo, Matba'at Hindiyya, 1329 AH (1911 CE), II, pp. 43–44. The title page identifies a vocalised Tabriz manuscript and a further collation with a manuscript at the Khedivial Library. Page 43 begins this poem under «علي بن مر الأرمني». Page 44 completes it. The two scans give 41 bayts and supply an independent check on the sequence, including the join from 41.1 to 41.2. The patron’s genealogy in the poem and al-Sayrafi’s detailed heading favour al-Ta'i. [Title page](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n0/mode/1up) · [II, p. 43](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n278/mode/1up) · [II, p. 44](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n279/mode/1up)
- Al-Amidi quotes the opening three bayts in *al-Muwazana*, II, p. 224, and discusses the returning light of dawn in 2. He gives «وواعظ» but «في العيش» in 3. The passage in Ibn Dawud’s *al-Zahra* has «وبالغ», «في الحب» and «رحت». It follows a quotation attributed to Ibn Harma and introduces the verses with «وقال أيضاً». Al-Sayrafi notes this competing attribution. For the poem as a whole the two diwans and its internal address support al-Buhturi. ## Recorded variants

At 1.2 the early editions divide between «وواعظ» and «وبالغ». The word meaning a preacher occurs in both full transcriptions and leads by 10.00 in the aligned comparison. At 3.1 «الحب» leads al-Amidi’s «العيش» by 19.88. Al-Sayrafi and the performance give «رحت» at 4.1, ahead of the Cairo «أنت» by 7.45. At 8.2 «الوهم» leads «الفهم» by 6.50. At 9.1 «بخل وجهل» leads Cairo’s reversed «جهل وبخل» by 26.54. The order matters because the poet has just spoken about the distribution of wealth. [Al-Sayrafi, pp. 953–954](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n1018/mode/1up) · [Al-Barquqi, II, p. 43](https://archive.org/details/3302pdf1329/page/n278/mode/1up)

At 7.2 al-Sayrafi vowels the word for ease «اليُسُر». The original phones give y-u-s-u-r-u (the recogniser transcribes the vowels as o) at 75.09–75.75 s. This reading leads «اليَسَر» by 3.13. At 10.1 both printed texts have the plural relative «اللاتي». Continuous Cohere gives «التي» and Audar writes the same relative attached to the preceding word. The comparison favours «التي» by 3.80. Free phones do register a long a before t, and the metre admits either reading. The text follows the full transcriptions with the difference in the paired card. [P. 954, bayts 7 and 10](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n1018/mode/1up)

At 12.1 «من مقاطعها» leads Cairo’s «عن مقاطعها» by 6.75. Both printed texts give «وما عليّ لهم أن تفهم البقر» in 12.2. Both continuous passes and the onset i-ð in the source at 128.63–128.85 s give the conditional «إذا لم تفهم». In the same two-word window «إذا لم» leads «لهم أن» by 13.83. Al-Sayrafi records exactly that wording in the apparatus on p. 955 and discusses the extent to which this line circulated apart from the poem. In the name of Samarra at 13.2 both texts print «سُرّ من راء». Al-Sayrafi explicitly admits «سُرّ من رأى» below the line. The familiar spelling is used in the first full pass, and «رأى» leads «راء» by 4.81. At 15.2 «العيش» and Cairo’s «الدهر» score only 0.05 apart. Both continuous passes and al-Sayrafi support «العيش». [P. 955, apparatus to 12–13](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n1019/mode/1up) · [P. 956](https://archive.org/details/20230507_20230507_1209/page/n1020/mode/1up) · [Recording at 130 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WNsByEa-Kxs&t=130s)

At 20.2 the well that needs no digging gives «يحتفر», ahead of «يحتقر» by 4.35. At 25.2 the passive «يُزرى به» means that an extreme of height would diminish the man’s standing. It leads the active alternative by only 0.93, and al-Sayrafi vowels the passive. At 26.1 the two scores for «مجرَّب» and «مجرِّب» differ by only 1.86 in favour of the active. The printed passive makes experience something life has given him, in contrast to the youth at the end of the bayt. At 34.1 Cohere gives «اللؤم» and Audar «اللوم». The scores favour «اللؤم» by 0.82, and the free phones do not separate the hamza. Both printed editions have «اللؤم», baseness, the quality of those who count on escaping punishment. At 41.1 «يُمدح» leads «يذكر» by 18.94. In 41.2 both lemmas give «أصنافه», its kinds. Both continuous passes have «أضعافه», its folds. This is a manuscript variant on p. 958 and leads by 3.61. ## Final clip decisions

The final narrow pass flags ten of 82 halves. Two earlier flags at 5.1 («صجال») and 11.1 («دوي وسنير») cleared after the clip edges were revised. The edition readings there are «سجال», successive heavy draws of poverty, and «ذوي وسن», people drowsy with ignorance.

| 10.2 | فقلي | فقل لي | The wider and both continuous passes recover two words, the imperative and its indirect object. The poet asks the woman to tell him how he can apologise. |
| 14.1 | فيرابي | فيرى | The wider cut stops at the passive «فيُرى». The b belongs to «به انصراف» at the beginning of 14.2. The two editions and the full transcriptions place the transition there. |
| 21.2 | واسطندي | وسطى الندي | A gathering, «النَّدِيّ», is where he inspires respect. The previous half ends «شرر» and «وسط الندي» supplies the place. The full Cohere pass keeps both words separately. |
| 22.1 | الحشاوهجير | الحشا وهجير | The wide window separates the coolness within him from the noon heat of fear, the two sides of the image. The original conjunction is retained. |
| 26.2 | الحجاوه وغر | الحجى وهو غر | The wider cut separates judgement («الحجى») from the pronoun beginning the new clause. Both continuous passes recover the same construction and al-Sayrafi prints it. |
| 30.2 | منه هو مر | من هو مر | Both full passes untangle «منه ومُرٌّ». The first word finishes the preceding sense and the conjunction introduces the bitter taste he has when provoked. The printed wording supplies both parts of the contrast with sweetness. |
| 36.1 | خطامت | خطامة | The original phones give kh-u-t-a-m-a-t-u at 383.89–384.97 s. The tribal name «خطامةُ» has an audible t in liaison. Al-Sayrafi identifies the group and explains the play on «خِطام» in the next half. |
