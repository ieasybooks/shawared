## Attribution

The poem is one of the most disputed in the Hamasa. Every claim found, with where it was read (the second reader added al-Jahiz, *Uyun al-Akhbar*, Ibn Jinni, *Taj al-Arus*, al-Shantamari's Abu Makhzum and the Bulaq print of al-Tibrizi):

- **«بعض بني قيس بن ثعلبة»** (a man of Qays ibn Tha'laba, of Bakr ibn Wa'il): Abu Tammam's heading, «قال بعض بني قيس بن ثعلبة», in al-Marzuqi (p. 100), al-Tibrizi (p. 78; Bulaq print, vol. 1, p. 50), al-Farisi (vol. 2, p. 100), al-Ma'arri (attributed; p. 80, «رجل من بني قيس») and al-Shantamari (p. 366). Naqsha (p. 80, note 1) reports the bare heading, without Bashama, from al-Jurjani and Ibn Qazghali. Al-Namari gave it to «رجل من بني قيس بن ثعلبة» (as al-Tibrizi quotes him, p. 84).
- **Bashama ibn Hazn al-Nahshali**, of Nahshal ibn Darim of Tamim:
  - the Hamasa commentaries' second name: «ويقال إنها لبشامة بن جزء النهشلي» (al-Marzuqi, p. 100; the editors' note: «جزء» in both their manuscripts, «حزن» in al-Tibrizi, al-Mubarrad and al-Baghdadi, and in Ibn Jinni's *al-Mubhij*); «ويقال: إنها لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي» (al-Tibrizi p. 78, al-Farisi p. 100, al-Ma'arri p. 80, which adds «جاهلي»). Al-Marzuqi himself calls bayt 8 «بيت بشامة» (p. 108);
  - al-Shantamari (p. 366) lists the names, «ويقال هي لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي؛ وقيل بشامة بن حرّي، وقيل ابن جُزَيء، والأصحّ ابن حزن»: the last words settle the form of the name, not the attribution, and his list puts Abu Makhzum first (below);
  - al-Amidi, *al-Mu'talif wa-l-Mukhtalif*, pp. 80–81 (shamela 10913/74–75): «ومنهم بشامة بن حزن النهشلي بن دارم وهو القائل», with five abyat (our 3, 4, 6, the omitted Hamasa 8, and our 8), «وهي الأبيات المشهورة»;
  - Ibn Qutayba, *Uyun al-Akhbar*, vol. 1, p. 287 (shamela 23790/281): «وقال بشامة», four abyat (our 3, 4, the omitted Hamasa 8, our 8 with «من فارس»). One page earlier (p. 286, 23790/280) our 1, 6 and 7 stand apart under «وقال آخر», with «إنّا محيّون يا سلمى». Al-Marzuqi's editors (p. 100, note 1) and Naqsha (p. 83) both point to it; the draft had only Ibn Qutayba's other book;
  - Ibn Jinni, *al-Mubhij fi Tafsir Asma' Shu'ara' Diwan al-Hamasa*, p. 80 (shamela 9244/35): an entry «بشامة بن حزن النهشلي» explaining the three names;
  - *Lisan al-Arab* quotes bayt 2 as «قول بشامة بن حزن النهشلي» (ج ل ل, vol. 11, p. 116) and bayt 9 for «بشامة بن حرى النهشلي» (ظ ب ا, vol. 15, p. 22); *Taj al-Arus* quotes bayt 9 as «قول بشامة بن حزن» (ظ ب و, vol. 38, p. 521, shamela 7030/20398);
  - other verses under his name: al-Jahiz, *al-Bayan wa-l-Tabyin*, vol. 3, p. 36 (shamela 10614/587), «قول بشامة بن حزن النهشلي: فدى لرعاء بالنّحيرة ذبّبوا / بأعصيهم…», a different poem; so the name was a known poet's in the 3rd century AH, not only a commentators' guess;
  - al-Baghdadi, *Khizanat al-Adab*, vol. 8, p. 311 (shamela 12732/3849): «وهذه الأبيات قد اختلف في قائلها والصحيح أنها لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي. وعليه الآمدي في كتابه المؤتلف والمختلف»; and on p. 312, after a quoted list of opinions: Ibn al-Sayyid al-Batalyawsi on *al-Kamil* gives them to Bashama ibn Hazn, al-Sukkari calls him «بشامة بن حري», «والأول قول أبي رياش».
- **A man of Nahshal, unnamed:** al-Jahiz, *al-Bayan wa-l-Tabyin*, vol. 3, p. 221 (shamela 10614/772): «وقال رجل من بني نهشل», three abyat: the omitted Hamasa 8 («أنا لمن معشر … قول الكماة لهم أين المحامونا»), our 8 («من عاطف») and our 5 («وليس يذهب»). The oldest source read here, and it names the clan, not the man. (Naqsha, p. 84, also lists *al-Bayan* 1/663 for bayt 1 «لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي»; shamela's *al-Bayan* has neither «محيوك», «فحيينا» nor «فاسقينا», and his next line gives the same 1/663 for *Lisan* on bayt 2, so it is probably a slip and is not used.)
- **Abu Makhzum al-Nahshali:** al-Mubarrad, *al-Kamil*, vol. 1, pp. 95–98 (shamela 8505/124–127): «وقال رجل يكنى أبا مخزوم، من بني نهشل بن دارم», ten abyat: our 3, 4, 5, the omitted Hamasa 8, our 8, 10, 6, 9 and two more («فرض على مكثرينا…», «إني ومن كأبي يحيى…»). Al-Shantamari (p. 366) gives him as the first alternative: «ويقال هي لأبي مخزوم النهشليّ، ونهشل من بني تميم، ومن جعلها له روى «إنّا بني نهشل»». The *Kamil* editor's note: «زيادات ر: هو بشامة بن حزن النهشلى»; Naqsha (al-Ma'arri's editor, p. 84) cites the same gloss from another print of *al-Kamil* (1/66) with «عن أبي رياش». So Abu Makhzum and Bashama are one man for Abu Riyash; al-Shantamari and al-Baghdadi list the two names side by side without saying so, and al-Baghdadi calls each of them, separately, apparently Islamic.
- **Nahshal ibn Harri al-Darimi** (a *mukhadram* who fought for Ali at Siffin and died about 45 AH (665 CE): al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 8, p. 49, which does not mention this poem): Ibn Qutayba, *al-Shi'r wa-l-Shu'ara'*, vol. 2, pp. 622–623 (shamela 23785/613–614), six abyat (our 3, 4, 7, the omitted Hamasa 8, our 8 with «من عاطف», 5), though his *Uyun al-Akhbar* gives four of them to Bashama (above); Ibn Tabataba, *Iyar al-Shi'r*, pp. 104–105 (shamela 6911/100–101), all twelve in the Hamasa's order, «نهشل بن حرّي المازني»; Ibn Sa'id, *Nashwat al-Tarab*, p. 456 (shamela 28174/399), who adds «ورواه الآمدي لبشامة النهشلي»; al-Shantamari's «ويقال».
- **al-Muraqqish al-Akbar** (of Qays ibn Tha'laba, died about 75 BH (550 CE): *al-A'lam*, vol. 5, p. 95): al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, *al-Mufaddaliyyat*, no. 128, p. 431 (shamela 6904/405), four abyat: «يا ذاتَ أَجوارِنا قومي فحيّينا» with our 1.2, our 2 with «سَراةَ خِيارِ الناس», «شعث مقادمنا نهبى مراجلنا» and «المطعمون إذا هبت شآمية». Al-Baghdadi (vol. 8, p. 301) adds Ibn al-A'rabi's *Nawadir* and Abu Muhammad al-A'rabi for this; al-Farisi (p. 100): «وقال البرقي: إنها للمرقش».
- **Two poems run together:** Abu Muhammad al-A'rabi (al-Ghundijani, *Islah ma Ghalita fihi al-Namari*, pp. 35–38 as Naqsha cites it), answering al-Namari (al-Tibrizi, pp. 84–85, and the Bulaq print, vol. 1, p. 55; al-Baghdadi, vol. 8, p. 312): al-Namari did not tell Nahshal (Mudar) from Qays ibn Tha'laba (Rabi'a), «فلزّهما في قرن»; «والبيت الذي فيه «إنّا بني نهشل» لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي، والأبيات الأخر لمرقش الأكبر» (al-Tibrizi), «والأبيات الأُخَر الأربعة للمرقش الأكبر» (al-Baghdadi). The four are the *Mufaddaliyyat*'s, as «نظام الأبيات» at the end of the passage shows: our 1, 2, «شعث مقادمنا…» and «المطعمون…». He says nothing of our 4–6 and 8–11. He also reports Abu al-Nada's verdict that «بيض مفارقنا تغلي مراجلنا» is a weak reading of «شُعثٌ مقادمُنا نُهبى مراجلُنا». Al-Muraqqish is of Qays ibn Tha'laba («عمرو بن سعد بن مالك بن ضبيعة بن قيس بن ثعلبة», al-Tibrizi p. 84), and so is Hujr ibn Khalid (al-Shantamari's editor, p. 367, note): Abu Tammam's heading fits either.
- **Others:** Hujr ibn Khalid ibn Mahmud of Qays ibn Tha'laba (Ibn al-A'rabi, as quoted by al-Baghdadi, vol. 8, p. 312; al-Shantamari, p. 366); «ابن غلفاء التميمي», which the same quoted list ascribes to Ibn Qutayba and al-Baghdadi corrects to Nahshal ibn Harri. Al-Tibrizi (p. 79) and al-Shantamari (p. 368) note that those who gave it to the Qaysi read «إنّا بني مالك».
- **The person:** al-Zirikli's *al-A'lam* has no entry for Bashama ibn Hazn or for Abu Makhzum (shamela full-text search of book 12286; its only Bashamas are Ibn Amr and Ibn al-Ghadir). Al-Baghdadi (vol. 8, p. 314): al-Amidi gives no more lineage than «بشامة بن حزن النهشلي نهشل بن دارم»; «ولم أر له ترجمة»; «والظاهر أنه إسلامي. وكذا أبو مخزوم النهشلي كما يظهر من شرح المبرد لأبياته». Al-Marzuqi's editors explain this as al-Mubarrad's showing that some of the ideas come from Islamic poets (p. 100, note 1); al-Mubarrad names Abu al-Tamahan al-Qayni (bayt 5), Tarafa and Mutammim ibn Nuwayra (bayt 8), Ka'b ibn Malik (bayt 9) and al-Ajda' al-Hamdani and al-Qattal al-Kilabi (bayt 6). Al-Ma'arri's heading reads «ويقال: إنها لبشامة بن حزن النهشلي - جاهلي»: the word follows his name after a dash, and may describe him or the poem. Nothing read dates him; the bio gives both opinions and no date.

**Weighing it.** For a Nahshali author: the poem names the clan (3.1 «بني نهشل», which only the Qaysi's partisans change to «بني مالك»), and every source that names the author of abyat 3–11 but one names a Nahshali of Darim: al-Jahiz (a man of Nahshal), al-Mubarrad (Abu Makhzum), Ibn Qutayba (Nahshal ibn Harri in *al-Shi'r*, Bashama in *Uyun*), Ibn Tabataba (Nahshal ibn Harri), and al-Amidi, Ibn Jinni, the four Hamasa commentaries' second name, Abu Riyash, *Lisan*, *Taj* and al-Baghdadi (Bashama). Bashama is the name most of them give, from the 3rd century AH (*Uyun*; al-Jahiz knew other verses of his) to al-Baghdadi's «والصحيح». Against: Abu Tammam names no one and gives the poem to a Rabi'a tribe, al-Muraqqish's and Hujr's, and the one exception above is Ibn al-A'rabi's Hujr ibn Khalid of that tribe (in the list al-Baghdadi quotes, vol. 8, p. 312); the opening two abyat, the first the listener hears, are al-Muraqqish's in the oldest anthology (al-Mufaddaliyyat, before Abu Tammam), for Ibn al-A'rabi, al-Barqi and Abu Muhammad al-A'rabi; al-Jahiz, al-Mubarrad, al-Amidi and Ibn Qutayba's *al-Shi'r* do not have them, and his *Uyun* sets them apart from the Bashama lines under «آخر». Before the Hamasa commentaries only Ibn Tabataba gives the opening to a Nahshali, and to Nahshal ibn Harri, not Bashama.

**Decision.** The poet stays `bashama-ibn-hazn`: the app needs a poet page, and his is the best-supported name for nine of the eleven abyat recited (3–11), with sources from the 3rd to the 11th century AH. The planned `abu-makhzum-al-nahshali` rests on al-Mubarrad and al-Shantamari's first alternative, and the *Kamil* additions make him Bashama. The weak point is the opening (1–2), which has the stronger claim for al-Muraqqish the Elder: the intro now opens on the dispute and gives that claim a sentence of its own, and the bio calls the poem disputed. The owner may prefer an anonymous credit after Abu Tammam ("a man of Qays ibn Tha'laba"), which would need a poet page for an unnamed poet. Of the eleven abyat recited, 3–10 are in one or more of the Nahshali versions (al-Jahiz, al-Mubarrad, al-Amidi, Ibn Qutayba); 1, 2 and a different form of 7 are in al-Muraqqish's Mufaddaliyya; 11 is in the Hamasa tradition only (its commentaries, Ibn Tabataba, al-Baghdadi).

Collated half by half; the text is set from al-Marzuqi (Amin–Harun), the critical edition, read as page images, and departs from it only where the reciter does (5.1 «وَلَيسَ», 9.1 «يُصيبَهُمُ»), in each case with another commentary.
