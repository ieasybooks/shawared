# عفا ذو حسى من فرتنى فالفوارع

Osama al-Nahari, published 1 October 2023. Keep 4.70–365.03 s of the 368.106 s original, from عفا ذو حسى through المسك كانع. This is a single performance of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani’s 33-bayt ʿayn ode, every half once. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTXVGUYI0Vc)

## Identity, witnesses and extent

- Muhammad Abu al-Fadl Ibrahim, ed., *Diwan al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani*, second edition, Dhakha'ir al-Arab 52 (Cairo, Dar al-Ma'arif), no. 2, pp. 29–39. P. 29 introduces reports about al-Nabigha, the king and the accusations. P. 30 begins the main sequence with the remains of Fartana’s camp, p. 34 places the shared half «أتاني أبيت اللعن أنك لمتني» at no. 14, p. 38 has the night, hooks and sword, and p. 39 concludes with nos. 32–33 and the musk-scented cup. No. 3 begins on p. 40. No verse from this numbered sequence is omitted. [Occasion](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n28/mode/1up) · [Opening and place-names](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n29/mode/1up) · [Shared half](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n33/mode/1up) · [Close](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n38/mode/1up)
- Al-A'lam al-Shantamari, *Ash'ar al-shu'ara' al-sitta al-jahiliyyin*, al-Nabigha no. 2, «وقال يعتذر إلى النعمان بن المنذر». The edition as digitised on Shamela has automatic section numbers 33–34. All 33 verses appear in the recorded order. Its defective digital forms (for example الراميات for the winds, شائع for “helper”, and شبت for “wished”) were checked against Ibrahim’s scan and the complete recitation. Al-Baghdadi, *Khizanat al-adab* II, p. 451 and following, calls the ode 35 bayts and quotes it with grammatical and lexical commentary. His order moves the two servants (our 30) up after the report of the threat (15), adds «إلى خير دين» in the account of the pilgrims and «سيبلغ عذراً» after the hooks, and puts the cup before «أبى الله». These features distinguish his 35-bayt account from the numbered text performed here. [Al-Shantamari, first section](https://shamela.ws/book/5449/33) · [Last nine verses](https://shamela.ws/book/5449/34) · [Al-Baghdadi II, 451](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/919) · [Supplement at II, 466](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/934) · [Different close, II, 467–468](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/935)
- Ibn Rashiq explicitly lists the distinct apologies in *al-Umda*, II, pp. 177–178. He introduces this incipit as the third and quotes its comparison with the camel that escapes cautery and the night that overtakes a fugitive. His second apology is the longer bāʾ recension beginning «أرسماً جديداً من سعاد تجنّب». Ibrahim prints the independent twelve-bayt bāʾ version as no. 8, pp. 72–74. In al-Nahari’s earlier recording all twelve occur once, in that printed order. Here 14.1 equals the bāʾ ode’s 1.1, but our 14.2 ends in المسامع. In the other poem it ends in أنصب. Our 21.1 («حلفت فلم أترك لنفسك ريبة») recurs as its 3.1, with a different continuation. Neither coincidence is a repeated half within this recording. The opening at the deserted camp, the oath by the pilgrims and the closing cup all belong to the ʿayn sequence. ## Metre, rhyme and interpretation

Tawil. At 1, *ʿafā dhū / ḥusan min far / tanā fal / fawāriʿū | fajanbā / arīkin fat / tilāʿud / dawāfiʿū* gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن on both sides. At 16, *laʿamrī / wamā ʿamrī / ʿalayya / bihayyinin | laqad na / ṭaqat buṭlan / ʿalayyal / aqāriʿū* gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعلن in the sadr and فعولُ مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن in the ajuz. The rawiy is ع. The reciter voices its damma and sustains it at many line ends.

The half-translations and commentary follow Ibrahim’s glosses for the eroded rim (4), patterned mat and leather spread in the market (5–6), snakebite and the women’s jewellery used to keep the patient awake (11–13), the pillory-like fetters and oath (20–21), the pilgrims and exhausted animals left along their route (22–24), the healthy beast cauterised in another’s stead (25), and the bucket hooks and drinking cup (29, 33). His note on 25 also preserves Abu Ubayda’s objection to reading the veterinary comparison literally. Al-Baghdadi on 28 reports Abu Ali’s two constructions of إن. The translation takes it conditionally. ## Acoustic comparisons

Each comparison uses equal word counts across the complete aligned half, with 0.30 s of context on either side. A higher MMS score indicates a closer fit. In 6.2 the third foot ends *laṭīma* and in 31.2 it ends *maniyya*. Both scan فعولُ. Lengthening the final short syllable before the feminine tāʾ produces the apparent plurals in the transcriptions. The singular and plural candidates tie exactly at both addresses. For 6.2, Abu Amr explains اللطيمة as the cloth and perfume market. Abu Ubayda understands the caravan carrying valuable wares to market. At 31.2 Ibrahim glosses the image as the sword that Death borrowed. [Ibrahim, p. 31](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n30/mode/1up) · [Al-Baghdadi II, p. 455](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/923) · [Ibrahim, p. 38](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n37/mode/1up) · [Al-Baghdadi II, p. 467](https://shamela.ws/book/12732/935)

Ibrahim prints and explains the singular مقالة at 15.1. Al-Baghdadi attributes the nominative to al-Asmaʿi and discusses two analyses of the accusative. At 30.2 Ibrahim glosses both the crooked, unjust man (ضالع) and the word with ẓāʾ originally used of a limping camel. The two words score within 0.11 of one another.
