# رأيت الناس قد مالوا

## Recording, authorship and extent

Osama al-Waaedh published this performance on 26 December 2025. In the description and his pinned comment he attributes the first three pairs to al-Shafi'i and explicitly claims the rest as his own. The upload runs 207.807 s. It has no spoken introduction. The master keeps 1.04–207.16 s, with about half a second on either side of the voiced passage (about 1.52–206.64 s). The video shows an outdoor shoreline and carries no verse captions. The speech transcriptions and a public listener’s complete transcription can be compared directly with the author’s performance. Two early-modern and modern witnesses establish the circulation of the opening wordplay. Neither supplies a secure attribution to al-Shafi'i. Ahmad al-Shirwani, *Nafhat al-Yaman fima yazulu bi-dhikrihi al-shajan*, p. 217, combines «قد مالوا» with the silver couplet, then attributes the gold couplet to another, unnamed poet. The explicitly attributed lines of al-Shafi'i on the same page begin only after these examples. In the question reproduced in A'id al-Qarni’s lectures, vol. 57, p. 37, the gold, silver and money pairs appear in that order. Al-Qarni replies that he does not know the author. Abd al-Aziz al-Salman transmits the money pair anonymously in *Mawarid al-zam'an*, vol. 2, p. 181. The app credits al-Waaedh for his composed and performed continuation and gives his own attribution of the opening in both introductions. [Al-Shirwani](https://shamela.ws/book/35888/208) · [Al-Qarni](https://shamela.ws/book/7708/1352) · [Al-Salman](https://shamela.ws/book/7480/782)

The complete performed text consists of twenty paired units, each comprising two short metrical bayts. The inherited part is numbered 1–6, the modern continuation 7–40. The performance retains all eighty halves. The full count is for al-Waaedh’s version. Other continuations of the circulating opening have no bearing on it. The testimony for the modern portion is the author’s recorded recitation. The listener transcripts provide checks on word division without constituting a separate authorial edition.

## Metre, rhyme and wordplay

The opening «رَأَيْتُنْنَا | سَ قَدْ مالُو» gives two bound feet that sound like مفاعيلن مفاعيلن. The unbound feet elsewhere distinguish the piece as *majzu' al-wafir*. Compare «رَأَيْتُنْنَا | سَ قَدْ ذَهَبُو» (3.1) and «رَأَيْتُنْنَا | سَ قَدْ وَرَدُو» (19.1), where the second foot is مُفاعَلَتُن. Abd al-Aziz Atiq gives exactly this test for distinguishing a two-foot wafir half from hazaj. There is no single rawiy throughout. The early pairs move from lam to ba to dad, and later pairs adopt their own rhyme. The short ringing endings account for many elongated vowels in the transcripts. [Atiq, *Ilm al-arud wal-qafiya*, p. 69](https://shamela.ws/book/11298/67)

The paired translations preserve the directional changes built into «إلى»، «على» and «عن». Several jokes depend on sound alone. «مال» and «مالوا» have different roots, while «ذهب» and «ذهبوا» share consonants across noun and verb. At 11.2 *jadd* can denote fortune or wealth. At 20.2 «فعنه ... وردوا» stretches the ordinary use of the verb for arriving. At 21.2 «عازة» is a regional word for need. At 23.2 «لابة» most plausibly refers to the kin a person can count on, following «نفر» and «عدد». The regional field glossary in *al-Qawl al-maktub fi tarikh al-janub*, vol. 15, p. 220, entry 292, records this use in popular praise poetry. The *Atlas of Arabic Dialects* gives it alongside the older sense of a group of black camels. The word card preserves both. At 32.2 *hajj* supplies the idea of going to someone on purpose and echoes *haja*, a need. At 33.2 *khazz* is a costly cloth that could combine silk and wool. [Lisan al-Arab, «جدد», vol. 3, p. 108](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/1550) · [Al-Abbudi, regional «العازة»](https://shamela.ws/book/147374/389) · [Atlas of Arabic Dialects, «اللابة»](https://www.atlasallhajaat.com/public/word/7615) · [Taj al-arus, «لوب», vol. 4, p. 225](https://shamela.ws/book/7030/1810) · [Lisan al-Arab, «خزز», vol. 5, p. 345](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/2929)

## Audio verification

The unchanged order of the money, gold and silver pairs comes from the continuous audio. The subsequent pairs are likewise presented in their performed order. At 25.2 the full Cohere and Audar readings agree on «بمن ما عنده همّ», which one public transcript normalises to «بمن لا عنده همّ». At 31.2 both full passes give «لها حاجة». At 39.2 they recover the singular «لمشهورٍ ومشهورةٍ». The written witnesses in the comments disagree on some of these particulars. The source recording decides them.

At 14.1 the final noun matters. One public transcript takes the phrase as «أموالَه عَدّوا», “whose money they counted”. The isolated Cohere clip returns «ومن أمواله عد», without vowel marks or the plural verb’s final waw. Across the first aligned window the nominal reading scored −36.04 against −39.98 for the verbal phrase. With the revised word boundary at 71.38 s, the scores are −34.57 and −35.32. That 0.75-point gap leaves the whole-phrase comparison inconclusive. On «أمواله» alone the nominative leads by 2.32 in both passes. An independent phone trace recovers /l u h u/ at 70.29–70.55 s of the master, the same sequence as in the unambiguous nominative at 27.2. It gives /a/ before the final d, though the pharyngeal consonant makes the exact vowel less certain. The text retains the apparent performance. The likely sense is the noun «العِدّ», abundance, expressly glossed that way in *Lisan al-Arab* and the Cairo Academy’s *al-Mu'jam al-wasit*. The paired word card explains the difference between the recorded and dictionary vowels and gives the alternative verbal parsing. At 25.2 «ما» leads «لا» by 7.70, at 31.2 «لها» leads «لهم» by 13.25, and at 39.2 «لمشهورٍ» leads the dual by 33.18. [Lisan al-Arab, vol. 3, p. 282](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/1724) · [Cairo Academy, entry «العِدّ»](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/search_engine/roots/عدد)
