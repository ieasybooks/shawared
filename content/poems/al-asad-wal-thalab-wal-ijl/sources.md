# الأَسَدُ وَالثَّعلَبُ وَالعِجلُ

## Recording and extent

Alqimmah Studio published «حكايات أحمد شوقي» on 3 March 2023, crediting Osama Al-Waaedh with recitation and editing. The source lasts 966.844 seconds. Chapter 3 keeps **181.85–333.12 seconds of the original compilation** in one continuous cut. The source voice measurements place the first and last pitched sound of this chapter at approximately 182.39 and 331.78 seconds. The cut admits the entry and the decay of the final rhyme. [Compilation, chapter 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r6VpT0Lid0E&t=182s)

All 24 couplets are present, in order, with no second performance of a bayt. The fox’s elaborate address occupies bayts 6–18. “The minister” in 22 is the same calf whom the lion saw in 1. The last 14-second source window gives the fox’s boast and then the opening of the next chapter beyond the retained cut.

The first edition of *al-Shawqiyyat* (part I, Cairo, Matbaʿat al-Adab wa-l-Muʾayyad, 1898) provides an earlier text. Cairo has «أو ذاك الغزال» in 3.2. The earlier witness bears out the Beirut reading and the recording at the very word where the Hindawi printing has «الغزل». The heading and the first line both identify the animal in the story as a calf. [p. 169](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA169) · [p. 170](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA170)

Dar al-Awda prints 1–17 on p. 138 and 18–24 on p. 139. Hindawi divides after 13. There are two substantive differences in the second halves of 3 and 24. The address in 6 and the vocative in 10 are spaced as words for the reader. Arslan’s general discussion of speech in the animal fables provides background to the shift of register as the fox addresses the calf as a functionary. He does not quote this story there. The two supposed ancestors in 13 belong to the fox’s boast. Apis was the sacred bull at Memphis. The second reference is to the calf made during Moses’ absence, an episode also told in Qur’an 7.148 and Exodus 32.4. [Arslan, §8](https://www.safahat.org/books/96809026/24/) · [Qur’an 7.148](https://quran.com/7/148) · [Exodus 32](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.32.4)

The two printings transmit the same diwan tradition. The page reproductions and the word-by-word output from `scripts/collate.py` are in `media/research/al-asad-wal-thalab-wal-ijl/`. The studio captions are performance evidence. The glosses and bilingual commentary are editorial close readings of the cited text.

## Metre and rhyme

Full ramal in separately rhymed pairs, with the last foot shortened to فاعلن. The first half begins *na-ẓa-ral-lay | thu-i-lā-ʿij | lin-sa-mīn*, **⏑ ⏑ – – | ⏑ ⏑ – – | – ⏑ –**. Its answer *kā-na-bil-qur | bi-ʿa-lā-ghay | ṭin-a-mīn* gives **– ⏑ – – | ⏑ ⏑ – – | – ⏑ –**. In 11, *fa-ra-as-sul | ṭā-nu-fir-raʾ | sil-ka-bīr* gives **⏑ ⏑ – – | – ⏑ – – | – ⏑ –**. Its response *maw-ṭi-nal-ḥik | ma-ti-wal-ḥidh | qil-ka-thīr* gives **– ⏑ – – | ⏑ ⏑ – – | – ⏑ –**. These are فاعلاتن and its makhbun form فعلاتن. سمين/أمين, الرئيس/النفيس and الاحتيال/الغزال already give three different rhyme letters. A single rawiy would misdescribe the design.

## Readings against the performance

The 189–204-second passage and the full transcribers give «ذاك الغزال». Dar al-Awda prints the animal’s name, while Hindawi has «الغزل». The latter loses the long rhyme vowel of «الاحتيال» and supplies no suitable quarry. MMS represents both by the same tokens. At 15.2 both full transcripts suggest «العلي», but the card at 273 seconds, both diwans and the consonant/vowel sequence favour «العالي». The free phoneme trace in «العالي» has a long a before l and a following i, and the scored half leads by 11.83. At 24.2 Hindawi has «كبيرا», where the Beirut print ends «كبير». Both complete transcripts, the 330-second card and the genitive adjective after «رأسٍ» support Beirut. The reciter stops at r, matching «الصغير».
