# Abu Qays ibn al-Aslat · قالَت وَلَم تَقصِد لِقيلِ الخَنا

## Recording and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed recites all 24 bayts of no. 75 of the *Mufaddaliyyat* in their printed order. The original-clock interval is 89.57–290.94 s. The opening prose tells the story of the wife failing to recognise her husband. The edition begins when the verse begins and ends after «رهن بذي لونين خدّاع», before the closing credits. The video description numbers all 24 bayts, and cites the commentary transmitted by al-Anbari. [Recording and studio text](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q-GlG-Yc83Y)

The wording and order are generally those heard here. The short title line is outside the performance. - Abu Zayd al-Qurashi, *Jamharat ash'ar al-Arab*, pp. 522–527. Its 21 bayts rearrange the military descriptions and contract the camel episode. It has other readings in the woman’s speech and the arming scene. [pp. 522–524](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/469) · [pp. 525–527](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/472)
- Al-Anbari’s Arabic commentary in Lyall’s vol. I, no. LXXV, starts on printed p. 564 (scan leaf 370). The return from war and the wife’s reaction are on that page. The closing gloss carries onto p. 574 (leaf 360), where no. LXXVI begins. The book is scanned in reverse Arabic page order. [Arabic, p. 564](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n370/mode/2up) · [p. 574](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n360/mode/2up)
- C. J. Lyall, *The Mufaddaliyat*, vol. II, no. LXXV, pp. 224–228. The account attributed to al-Anbari connects the homecoming with the war of Hatib. The *Aghani* tradition connects it with Bu'ath. The introduction therefore describes the fighting of the Aws, and attributes the recognition story to al-Anbari. The commentary informs the interpretation of the ghoul (2), the helmet and short sleeps (4), the long coat of mail and scabbard (6–8), the tribal fighting and rallying point (13–16), and the cloths, pads and saddle of the riding camel (19–23). Lyall records a transmission lacking 22–23. Both are audible. The English halves and both sets of verse explanations were written for this recording. [Vol. II, pp. 224–228](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/224/mode/2up)
- The biography follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 3/211. The article gives Sayfi, the leadership of the Aws, the search for Abraham’s religion and the tradition about his death. [3/211](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/2553)

## Readings in the performance

The poem is in sari', with ayn as rawiy. Both the opening and the mail description sustain the short three-foot halves. Where older printings carry part of the article of the following word into the preceding half, the word remains intact on the side where the reciter gives it. At 10–12 several Shamela lines put complete words on the other side of that break. The continuous wording identifies the verse and the recorded pause identifies the displayed halves.

In 1.1 «لقيل» is the *Mufaddaliyyat* and the performance, beside «لقول» in the *Jamhara*. At 2.1 «أنكرتِهِ حين توسّمتِهِ» addresses the wife. The *Jamhara* gives a first-person reading and «حتى». The recording gives «غُمضاً» (4.2), «جُلّ بني مالك» (5.1, supported by the *Jamhara* against the digital «جبل»), «للأعداء … فضفاضة» (6), and «أحفزها … بذي رونق» (7.1). The *Jamhara* gives «نوماً»، «للهيجاء … مترصّة» and «أخفرها», with different wording for the blade. Read «قَرّاع» of the shield at 8.2, against the *Jamhara*’s «فزّاع». Al-Anbari’s Arabic p. 567 has the same shield with its epithet and explains how the low skirt of mail could be hooked up to the scabbard. At 10.1 the voice gives «الحزم والقوة», where the *Jamhara* gives «والكيس والقوة». [Shakir–Harun, pp. 284–285](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/258) · [Jamhara, pp. 523–525](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/470)

«كأنهم» at 14.1, «ولنا غاية» at 15.1 and «هلا سألت الخيل إذ قلصت» at 16.1 follow the reciter. At 14.1 the isolated Cohere text, both full recognisers, the studio description and the Arabic poem all give «كأنهم». The unvowelled acoustic comparison narrowly prefers «كأننا» by 5.7, but retesting just the fully vocalised word favours «كَأَنَّهُم» by 8.9. That agrees with the three transcripts. The *Jamhara* reads «كأننا»، «ولنا غابة» and «فسائل الأحلاف إذ قلصت». The banner or rallying point belongs to the collected men. At 19–23 the *Jamhara* has «ذات شقاشق» beside «ذات أساهيج», a different description of the camel in motion, and then passes directly to the closing bayt. The marked textiles and fluttering cloths heard here are present in the *Mufaddaliyyat*. [Shakir–Harun, pp. 285–286](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/259) · [Jamhara, pp. 525–527](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/472)

| 9.1 | «بزم ري مستبسل حذر» | «بزمر أم مستبسل حاذر» | The equipment just enumerated is «بزّ امرئ مستبسل حاذر». The wasl of «امرئ» after the geminate z makes a difficult word boundary. The boy’s or man’s gear and its vigilant owner are explained in al-Anbari. |
| 10.1 | «الحجم والقوه خير من» | «الحجم والقوة خير من» | «الحزم» means firmness of judgement and pairs with strength. The two recognisers often confuse it with «الحجم», size, which has no place among the virtues and failings in this comparison. The recorded sibilant and studio text support «الحزم». |
| 10.2 | «كالادهان والفكه والهاعي» | «من الإدهان والفكة والهاعي» | The preceding half ends «من», whose sound leaks across the cut. The noun is «الإدهان», showing weakness or accommodating others at one’s own expense. No extra comparison particle is needed. |
| 12.1 | «لا نالم القتل ونجزي بهم» | «لا نألم القتل ونجزي به» | The pronoun in «ونجزي به» refers to killing. Widened Audar restores the singular, and the following «الأعداء» is the object of recompense. |
| 14.2 | «ينهتن في غيل واجزاعيم» | No separate widened flag for this cut | The earlier crop adds a consonant to the end of «أجزاع», valley sides. The lions and their haunts are in the printed text and this final vowel carries the ayn rhyme. The revised run clears the half. |
| 19.2 | «فيه علي ادماءه الواعي» | «فيه على أدمى أهل واعي» | A pale she-camel («أدماء») is qualified by «هِلوَاع», swift or easily roused. Neither word takes the spurious boundary or an attached pronoun. The Arabic commentary treats them as two successive descriptions. |
