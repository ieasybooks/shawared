## The recording and its extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed, al-Shankabutiyya, 15 January 2024. The studio numbers its verses, displays each lexical pair with explanations and prints a transcription in the description. The original lasts 387.471 s. The opening gives the chain of transmission through al-Silafi to Ibn Durayd and the first grammatical heading. Thereafter al-Saeed interrupts the poem to announce changes of section and a reservation about attribution. The following eight keeps are on the original clock. Every numbered bayt is performed once, in this order. [Recording and studio text](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ap-W6LPvmYE)

The original-clock word alignments place all seven interior cuts in the gaps between poetry and speech. Together the eight spans give 307.90 s. The editorial count of 58 belongs to this numbered performance. Its final bayt is visible with the number ٥٨ at 384 s. The full Cohere transcript reaches it. The continuous Audar transcript stops after a heading near the end. The last studio card and the per-half check supply independent evidence for the close. [Closing card](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ap-W6LPvmYE&t=382s)

Clips from the edited recording were transcribed across all seven joins. Each goes directly from one verse to the next, including the transition from 45 to 46 across the reciter’s spoken caveat about attribution.

## Editions and vocabulary

- *Sharh al-maqsura al-Duraydiyya al-sughra*, published by Muhammad Zuhayr al-Shawish, Damascus, al-Maktab al-Islami. The opening on p. 5 gives the chain heard in the recording. The commentary runs through 45 on pp. 5–20. On p. 20 the editor identifies the break in his copy and expressly supplies the rest from the diwan. His supplement on pp. 20–21 ends with our 57. Its first three verses are the ones marked as doubtful by al-Saeed. [Arabic Collections Online, page images](https://hdl.handle.net/2333.1/2v6wwtwq)
- *Sharh al-maqsur wal-mamdud*, ed. Majid Hasan al-Dujayli and Salah Mahdi al-Bakri, Dar al-Fikr. This edition numbers 57 bayts, provides glosses for the supplement and compares manuscripts and printed witnesses. The separate entries for the two morning terms end the text on p. 48. Its notes on pp. 38–43 distinguish repeated words from different senses. The note on p. 40 gives both explanations of the water rope. - Shawqi Dayf, *Tarikh al-adab al-arabi*, IV, p. 253, counts 57 short words and 57 long partners, describes the sequence of changes in initial vowel and distinguishes the work from Ibn Durayd’s much longer *Maqsura*. For expressions with more than one dictionary sense, the sharh also uses the studio’s explanations. It renders both partners even where the same English noun is right for both. [Dayf](https://shamela.ws/book/11996/1725)
- Ibn Sida, *al-Muhkam*, III, p. 480, records the bat under both names in 56, with initial *a* and final alif in one and initial *i* and final hamza in the other. Al-Khalil, *Kitab al-ʿayn*, VIII, p. 311, defines the final رُواء as beauty of appearance. Al-Azhari, *Tahdhib al-lugha*, XV, p. 226, explains the rope in 41 as the one securing a load of water. ## Form and readings

The metre is truncated kamil, two متفاعلن feet per half. In 1.1 «لا تَرْكَنَنْ / نَ إِلَلْهَوَى» gives – – ⏑ – / ⏑ ⏑ – ⏑ –. In 34.1 «كَمْ مِنْ عِظَا / مِنْ بِلْلِوَى» gives the contracted form twice. The rhyme consonant is hamza, preceded by a long alif. The reciter sometimes prolongs its following kasra. A *maqsur* noun already ends in a long vowel. Its contrast with a *mamdud* noun is the latter’s final hamza after alif. Comparing the first vowels matters independently, particularly in 41, 47 and 58. Many words cross the caesura in this compact metre. For example, 6 divides ناظريـ / ـه, 21 البريـ / ـيّة and 57 عليـ / ـك.

- **1, 3, 16 and 20.** Shawish p. 5 has «واحذر» and reports «واذكر». The recording gives «واحذر», and in 3 has «حفير», a hollow dug beside the rim of a well. The electronic corpus offers «صغير». For 16 the studio explains الوَراء as descendants, specifically the children’s children, a meaning also recorded by Shawish pp. 10–11. In 20 السفا occurs twice. The studio describes two possible case readings for أهل. Its preferred nominative makes the light-headed people the ones driving dust towards dust. The critical edition p. 29 also knows a different wording with «ودّى». [Studio glosses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ap-W6LPvmYE&t=123s)
- **21.** Shawish p. 12 prints «لا تجيبك» and records «يؤذنونك» as well as a diwan reading with «الأحبة». The studio, continuous transcriptions and widened clips give «إن البرية يؤذنونك بالبراء». The people notify the child of dust that they disown him. The plural verb treats the collective as persons.
- **28, 30 and 36–38.** In 28 the studio and Shawish p. 15 take المَلاء as affluence. In 30 both distinguish the place بَدا from البَداء, a change of mind. Shawish p. 16 places Bada near Taif, while the studio identifies the modern locality near al-Wajh. The verse needs only a distant place. In 36 al-Dujayli and al-Bakri p. 38 and the studio give «ومناه في ماء الإناء». Shawish p. 18 has «والعمر في ماء الإناء» and reports «ومناه في ملء الإناء». In 37 the studio takes اللحاء as the bark or covering that is stripped away. Both printed commentaries also allow an exposure of men’s abusive wrangling. In 38 record «ذا السبق» with the numbered text on p. 38. Shawish p. 18 prints «والسيف» but reports the diwan’s «ذا السبق».
- **41–45.** The studio glosses الرِّواء in 41 as a rope, as does Shawish p. 19. Al-Dujayli and al-Bakri p. 40 print deliberation in their commentary, then give the rope evidence in their note. In 42 the numbered text p. 41 and the recording group البِلى and البَلاء under decay. Shawish p. 19 also gives trial for the long form. In 43 the studio explains both words as an hour of the night, also the explanation in Shawish p. 19. The critical commentary p. 41 takes both as the reaching of a term. In 44 record «مع الزمان» with the studio and Shawish, where the other edition has «على الزمان». In 45 the studio and the numbered edition explain سِوى and سَواء as “other”, while Shawish p. 20 also records “middle”. The man is succeeded by another and taken from among the others. The printed readings of the verb include initial ت and ي.
- **46–48.** The numbered edition pp. 42–43 and recording give «حب الفساد … بلا قلاء». The restored text in Shawish p. 20 has «حب النساء … مع القلاء». The recorded «للمحلّأ» in 47 is someone driven away from water. Shawish prints «للمجلّى». The numbered edition prints «للمحلّى» and reports further variation. The recorded participle makes access to the water of life the point. In 48 both names mean sunlight. Neither the rhyme nor the comparison refers to the passing days. These three appear in the reading under the reciter’s explicit attribution caveat. [Caveat and readings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ap-W6LPvmYE&t=291s)
- **53–58.** On p. 46 of the numbered edition «الجَرى والجِراء» are «نعمة الشباب ومتعته» and the studio glosses both as «نضارة الشباب». In 54 both printings give الغذى / الغذاء, nourishment. The recording and card give الفدى / الفداء, ransom, an attested pair that gives the question a different object. In 55 both names refer to pools and the contrast is between arriving and leaving. In 56 the studio gives «لا خير» and glosses both nouns as the bat. The printed «لا ضير» accompanies the contrasting glosses of a writing sheet and a bat or bark, p. 47. Dayf also reports a plant for the long form. In 57 the studio distinguishes the earlier الضُّحى from the later الضَّحاء. In 58 رُؤى is the plural of رؤيا, what one sees in sleep, and رُواء what another sees in one’s face or bearing. Neither inspected edition includes this bayt. The studio’s extra heading retains the same initial *u* in both nouns. The narrow pass flags 64 of 116 halves. The table compares the flagged clips with the independently widened Audar clips, the numbered studio text and the two editions. Restoring a complete word in recognition is common where the printed foot crosses the middle of a word. In the last foot a recogniser often turns a prolonged *-āʾi* into a different word. Original-clock frames, including the accompanying definitions, help distinguish those effects from genuine textual variants.
