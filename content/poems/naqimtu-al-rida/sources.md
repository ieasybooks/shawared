# Al-Ma'arri’s elegy for his father

## The full poem and its commentators

The Beirut *Saqt al-zand* (Dar Sadir and Dar Beirut, 1957), pp. 13–18, prints all 53 verses in the order of this recording. The Cairo *Shuruh Saqt al-zand*, second section, poem 41, pp. 907–942, prints separate comments by al-Tabrizi, al-Batalyawsi and al-Khwarazmi under a common verse text. Their individual glosses are identifiable, though the collected edition is one textual witness. It puts the fading lamp (our 38) before the grammatical lament «تئن ونصبي» (our 37). The recording follows the Beirut order. Al-Sa'id Ibada’s *Saqt al-zand wa-daw'uhu*, no. 41, begins at printed p. 354, archive leaf 501. Its heading identifies the poet’s father Abd Allah ibn Sulayman al-Tanukhi. The text itself identifies him as a master of rhyme at 26. [Beirut](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n12/mode/2up) · [Cairo commentaries](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n933/mode/2up) · [Ibada](https://archive.org/details/20211218_20211218_1732/page/n501/mode/2up)

Al-Jirawi, *al-Hamasa al-maghribiyya*, no. 494, II, pp. 882–883, selects 1, 4, 5, 9, 10, 18, 19 and 24–27. Al-Umari, *Masalik al-absar*, XV, pp. 444–445, selects 1, 2, 6, 8–10, 13, 24, 25, 32, 33, 44 and 45. His heading identifies this as an elegy for the poet’s father. Their different selections preserve the dark opening, the account of the world and the address to the dead in different proportions. [Al-Jirawi](https://shamela.ws/book/6902/824) · [Al-Umari](https://shamela.ws/book/11790/5670)

At 2 the smile draws the tooth out of the mouth as one draws a sword. Al-Tabrizi explains «شام السيف» and al-Khwarazmi makes «سني» its object and «تبسمي» its subject. At 3 the *sajn* to which the teeth are condemned is confinement. At 18 the studio’s accusative «ساعةً» makes the world company for an hour. Al-Tabrizi’s account also allows the hour itself to be the deadly companion, advancing the person’s death. At 20–21 the thirsty creatures are sandgrouse. At 22–23 the hooves and the journey by night belong to wild asses. At 33 the useless scabbard prepares the reader for 44, where the same noun «جفن» means an eyelid. At 37 the patient’s groan sounds like the grammatical particle governing the accusative. At 41 «يكني» means speaking indirectly. At 51 the same «لحن» names the song and the error from which its singing is free. These readings draw particularly on the glosses to the individual verses in the Cairo volume. [Shuruh, poem 41](https://archive.org/details/4034pdf/page/n933/mode/2up)

Ibn Manzur records «هبرزيّ» of a man of penetrating judgement, a newly struck coin and pure gold. Those senses explain the force of applying the adjective to minds at 16. [Lisan al-Arab, V, p. 423](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/3007)

## Recording, metre and division

Osama al-Nahari performs all 106 halves once, from «نقمت الرضا» to «فلا يهن». The continuous keep is 2.80–497.59 seconds of 502.561. The cut marker at app time 251.34 before 28.1 supplies an internal alignment boundary. Only the source margins fall outside the keep. The studio prints the verses as the performance advances. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqeC8w9lwB4)

Tawil, with nūn as the rhyme consonant. Verse 1 divides `naqimtur / riḍā ḥattā / ʿalā ḍā / ḥikil-muznī` and `falā jā / danī illā / ʿabūsun / minad-dajnī`. At 27 the poet turns the grave into a new home, `hanīʾan / lakal-baytul / jadīdu / muwassidan` and `yamīna / ka fīhi bis / saʿāda / ti wal-yumnī`. The verb and object cross the caesura. At 6 the mountain name scans contracted as «أُحد», explicitly vowelled on the studio card at 57 seconds.

## Variants against the recording

At **2.1** «فليت» (Beirut) scores 7.21 above Cairo’s «وليت», while «شام» scores 6.98 above al-Umari’s «شاء». The technical archaism explains the image of drawing a tooth. At **5.1** «والكرى» leads «والكرم» by 6.54. At **6.2** contracted «أُحد» leads ordinary «أُحُد» by 7.36. At **24.2** «وكلف» leads al-Jirawi’s «وعلّم» by 15.44. At **25.1** «روح» leads his «نفس» by 13.83. At **33.1** a word window gives «مضناك» a small lead of 2.40. Both printed diwans, al-Umari, and the description of the deserted home at 32 support «مغناك». The scabbard without a blade in the second half represents the house without its occupant. At **41.1** «ويكني» leads «ويسكني» by 7.16. At **46.2** the model gives «الضنين» a margin of 2.24 over «الظنين». The repeated root ض ن ن makes the speaker more jealous of the father he fears the weather will hurt. At **47.2** the studio and full Audar distinguish admiring «واهٍ» at the soft earth from painful «آهٍ» at the stones. The latter leads repeated «واهٍ» by 5.65. The Beirut text writes the same interjection «واه» in both positions. Cairo differentiates admiring «واه» and the lament «آه». All scores here use one-second emission context with a whole-second window fitted to the clip. The scored words and app-time positions are in `variant-windows.txt`.

The remaining comparisons between Beirut and Cairo are in `variant-witnesses.txt`. At **2.2** Beirut’s «تدمى» leads «يدمى» by 6.96. At **15.1** «المرء» leads «الميّت» by 7.86. At **16.2** the difference between «ولم يسلم» and Cairo’s «ولا يسلم» is only 0.38. Both full transcripts give «ولم يسلم». At **31.1** «لم يكن» leads «لم تكن» by 7.68, and at **43.2** «ما تحرّك» leads «لا يحرّك» by 22.57.

At **29.2** both full transcripts, Beirut and the studio card at 270 seconds give «سوى الظنّ». Cairo’s «سوى ظنّ» leads the mechanical comparison by 10.89. The scorer represents the definite form as `siwa alzani`, inserting a separate `al` where the initial vowel drops in liaison and the lām assimilates to ظ. The displayed text keeps the article. At **33.1** the card at 306 seconds explicitly reads «مغناك», confirming the reading of both complete editions.

## Half-verse verification

- **4.2, 5.1.** Narrow «رما حلمنايا» divides the construct «رماح المنايا». The wider transcript restores both. The narrow «والكرم» at 5.1 is «والكرى» in the wider clip, as in the prints. Sleep pairs with the following waking thoughts.
- **10.2, 32.2.** The same unrelated recogniser credit, «موسوعة النابلسي للعلوم الإسلامية», replaces two different verses in narrow clips. The expanded clips recover «محيا لها قامت له الشمس بالحسن» and «أمر من الإكرام بالحجر والركن». The latter also picks up «إنما» from the edge of the preceding «كأنما». The studio shows both expected halves. There is no additional spoken credit in the performed sequence.
- **14.2, 25.2.** The narrow «و العلم» separates the conjunction of «والعلم». The wider writes it joined and merely lengthens the rhyme on «المن». At 25.2 the narrow clip joins «وقد وعدا», and the wider «وقد عدا» loses the second wāw at the join. The two just named, Moses and Adam, are the subjects of the dual passive. The printed text and card agree.
- **37.2, 46.2.** The final «إنِّ» is the name of the grammatical particle adapted to the rhyme. Both clips spell its audible ending «إني» as if a first-person suffix had been added. At 46.2 the short «وما اشتاه» becomes «ومشتاه» in the wider clip, matching «مصيفه». Both clips write the wrong emphatic letter in the paired words of possessiveness. The prints, card and wordplay give «الضنين من الضن».
- **49.2, 51.2.** Short «بالعبد كالقني» becomes «بل عبده القني» in the wider clip. In the editions and card the mourner calls himself first «ابنك», then «عبدك القنّ», intensifying his filial loyalty. At 51.2 the narrow «البريعن» joins «البري عن». The wide clip writes the more usual hamzated «البريء عن». The printed and recited verse uses «البريّ», with the sense “free of”.
- The initial **44.1** added an article to «نزاهةً» and cleared with the revised boundaries. The edge tag at **43.1** adds «يا» before «يدٌ يدت الحسنى». The card and the two full texts give a hand and its cognate verb at the beginning of the verse. Their repeated yāʾ explains the recogniser’s extra syllable.

The final run found ten flagged halves in 106. The evidence includes the full and widened transcriptions, original verse cards and alignment against the media. The primary scans and their collation are preserved in the poem research directory.
