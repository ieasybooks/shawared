## Editions

- **al-Wahidi**, شرح ديوان المتنبي, ed. Friedrich Dieterici, *Mutanabbii carmina cum commentario Wahidii* (Berlin: Mittler, 1861), pp. 338–339: scan archive.org `carminacumcommen00muta`, leaves 562 and 561 (the scan runs backwards; page images read). Shamela 754, pp. 170–171, is the same text without vowels (shamela's own numbering, which it says does not follow the print; `wahidi.txt`, `wahidi.raw.txt`). All 9 abyat with commentary, partly voweled, under the heading «وقال وقد كُبِسَت انطاكية وقُتِلَ المهر والحِجر فقال» (shamela drops the و), after the rajaz poem on the horse. His text has «حَقيرٍ» in 2.1 and «مِثلَ» in 7.2; for 4.1 he gives Ibn Jinni's «قَرَبْنَ» and reports «قُرِينَ» and «قَرَيْنَ», voweled so.
- **al-Ukbari**, التبيان في شرح الديوان, attributed to al-Ukbari, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi (Dar al-Ma'rifa, Beirut, the reprint the scan's title page names; first printed by al-Babi al-Halabi, Cairo, 1936–38), vol. 4, pp. 119–120, poem 248: scan archive.org `20240104_20240104_1855`, leaves 1323–1324 (page images read; `ukbari.txt`), with the commentary also on shamela 6908. Fully voweled. Heading: «وقال: وقد كبست أنطاكية، فقتل مهره الذي وصفه والحِجر أمّه، وهي من الوافر والقافية من المتواتر». His text has «صَغيرٍ» (2.1), «مِثلُ» (7.2) and «القَريحَةِ» (9.2).
- **al-Barquqi**, شرح ديوان المتنبي (Hindawi reprint, 2014), p. 1253, with his notes 686–694 on pp. 1439–1440: scan archive.org `20201020_20201020_1638`, leaves 1252 and 1438–1439 (page images read; `barquqi.txt`). Fully voweled. Heading: «وقال، وقد كبست أنطاكية فقتل مهره الطخرور والحِجرُ أمه». His text has «صَغيرٍ» and «مِثلَ», and his notes give the three readings of 4.1 as Ibn Jinni's «قربن», «قُرين» (from القِرى) and «قَرين» (active).
- **al-Ma'arri**, اللامع العزيزي, ed. Muhammad Sa'id al-Mawlawi (2008), p. 1281 (shamela 29591/1273; `lami.txt`): abyat 1–4 and 9 with notes; meter «من الوافر الأول»; «حَقيرٍ». He passes on the report («يقال») that the poet lost a mare and a colt at Antioch in the days of his poem in ش for Abu al-Asha'ir.
- **معجز أحمد**, attributed to al-Ma'arri: shamela 753, pp. 192–193 (shamela's own numbering; `mujiz.txt`). All 9, heading «وكبست أنطاكية، فقتل المهر والحجرة فقال يندب مهره وفرسه»; «حَقيرٍ».
- **Anthologies:** al-Sahib ibn Abbad, الأمثال السائرة من شعر المتنبي, ed. Al Yasin (1965), pp. 34–35 (shamela 37460; `sahib.txt`), gives abyat 2 and 6–9 among the poet's proverbial lines, with «حَقيرٍ», «فَخرٌ» (6.1), «مِثلَ» (7.2) and «الفُهومِ» (9.2); al-Raghib al-Isfahani, محاضرات الأدباء, vol. 1, p. 524 (shamela 9078/520), gives abyat 1–2 with «صَغيرٍ» under «تحمّل المكاره في نيل المكارم».
- **Starting text:** set from al-Barquqi and al-Ukbari, put in the house style.

- **2.1** is a coin toss by score in all five renditions, whole half or last two words. Both transcribers hear «صغير» in every rendition they catch (each in five windows), and nowhere «حقير». The consonant settles it: a sibilant (ص) puts a burst of energy above 4.5 kHz at the word's start, a pharyngeal (ح) does not. Measured as the 4.5–7.8 kHz band against the 0.3–3 kHz band in 10 ms frames, the start of the word peaks at −3.0, −1.0, −3.7, −1.9 and +4.9 dB in the five renditions (at 25.08, 35.55, 103.35, 113.83 and 273.85 s, each within 0.03 s of the aligner's start of the word), as the sibilants of this recording do (ص of «صَفائِحُ» +5.1, of «صَحيحاً» +0.3 and −1.5; ش of «شَرَفٍ» −4.3), while ح of «الحَكيمِ» and خ of «خَديعَةُ» stay at −11 and −12, and the parallel rhyme word «عَظيمِ», sung on the same beat of the next half, at −17 to −23. So he sings «صَغيرٍ», which al-Ukbari and al-Barquqi print. A note gives the other reading.
- **4.1:** both transcribers hear «قربنا» (the stretched nun), and audar «قربت» at the window's edge; neither hears «قرين».
- **7.2:** al-Barquqi explains «مِثلَ» as the noun of لا النافية للجنس with its predicate left out; the second transcriber hears «ولا مثلا شجاعة».
- **2.2 at 115.6 s (the second refrain's last half):** the aligner held «عَظيمِ» to 125.5 s. At the end of every other sung half the spectral centroid falls below about 850 Hz and the spectral flatness to near zero within 0.3 s of the last word (31.7, 42.2 and 110.2 s), as the voice stops and only the instruments' chord is left; here that happens at 123.0–123.3 s, and the window transcript from 121.5 s hears only music. The half now ends at 123.0 s.
- **The end of the recording:** the same fall comes at 317.9–318.0 s of the download, 1.4 s after the aligner's end of the last «عَظيمِ» (316.46 s); the draft cut at 317.3 s, before it, with the music at −21 dB. The cut now follows the chord down to the silence at 319.6 s.

## Commentary drawn on

- **Glosses and meaning** follow al-Wahidi throughout (غامرت, القَرَب and the reversal in 4, the swords' tears in 3, the reading of 7, Abu Tammam's answer to Abu Sa'id al-Darir in 8, القريحة), with al-Ukbari (مروم, الشجو, الصفائح, الصياقل, الكلوم, «تغني من الغَناء لا من الغِنى», القريحة as the first water of a well) and al-Barquqi (مخلصات «خالصات من الخبث», الآفة «العاهة» and its pronoun for the saying, «لا مثلَ» with the predicate left out, شجوها as a verbal noun standing for a حال with its pronoun for the swords, the readings of 4.1, and in 7 the corollary that good sense is no substitute for courage, note 692).
- **3:** al-Ma'arri's second reading, that the poet means he will drive the horses into battle until they fall (al-Lami', p. 1281: «ولا يمتنع أن يريد أنه يحمل فرسه ومهره على الإقدام حتى يصيبهما القتل»).
- **9:** the Quranic parallel (al-Ahqaf 46:11) that al-Ukbari (on 8 and 9) and al-Barquqi (note 694) cite.
- **1.1 «غامرت»:** al-Ma'arri, «وأصل المغامرة أن تكون من اثنين فما زاد».
- **The colt's name:** al-Barquqi's heading («مهره الطخرور») and Ibn Furraja, *al-Fath ala Abi al-Fath*, p. 186 (shamela 37025/152, `furraja.raw.txt`: «يصف مهره الطخرور», on the rajaz poem before this one).
- **الحِجر, the mare:** al-Harbi, *Gharib al-Hadith*, vol. 1, p. 236 (shamela 13133/425, `harbi.raw.txt`), from Abu Ubayda: «الحِجْرُ: الأنثى من الخيل».
- **8:** al-Wahidi takes it as a saying on its own and traces it to Abu Tammam; the second reading, that the coward finds fault with courage because he does not know its worth, is the first of the two in معجز أحمد.
- **3:** شجوها in the accusative as a verbal noun (معجز أحمد).
- **The occasion** comes from the headings of al-Wahidi, al-Ukbari and al-Barquqi; the link to Abu al-Asha'ir from al-Lami' al-Azizi; the rest from al-Barquqi's life of the poet in his introduction: p. 35 (leaf 34), «وبارح شاعرنا الرملة سنة ٣٣٦ قاصدًا إنطاكية … فمدح فيها أبا العشائر»; p. 36 (leaf 35), «وأثناء إقامته في إنطاكية، أغار عليها بانس المؤنسي — قائد الأخشيديين — وفوجئ أبو العشائر فقاتل عن نفسه حتى بلغ حلب، فقال المتنبي قصيدته: إذا غامرت في شرف مروم», and «فقد كان أبو العشائر بن حمدان واليًا على إنطاكية من قبل سيف الدولة».

## Review

A second reader checked every half against the page images of al-Ukbari, al-Barquqi and al-Wahidi (Dieterici's print, found for this review), scanned every half, tested 2.1 again on all five renditions and on its consonant, checked the cut and the timeline against the audio, and checked the sharh, notes, intro and this file against the commentaries and a lexicon (2026-11-17). Fixed after the review:

- **Audio:** the file no longer stops dead in the final chord (cut at 319.6 s of the download, not 317.3 s); the second refrain's last 2.2 ends at 123.0 s, where the voice stops, not 2.5 s into the instrumental break.
- **Text:** none of the text changed. 2.1 «صَغيرٍ» is now settled by the consonant (a sibilant burst at the word's start in all five renditions, none at «عَظيمِ» on the same beat), not by the coin-toss scores; 7.2 «مِثلَ» is also al-Wahidi's and al-Sahib's.
- **Intro:** the occasion as al-Barquqi tells it (the Ikhshidid raid on Antioch while the poet stayed with Abu al-Asha'ir, 336 AH), in place of a governor's title set beside al-Ma'arri's report as if it were his.
- **Sharh:** 3 adds al-Ma'arri's second reading; 7 al-Barquqi's corollary; 9 the Quranic parallel al-Ukbari and al-Barquqi cite; 8 names Abu Sa'id al-Darir and gives the connected reading to معجز أحمد, not to al-Ma'arri; the English sharh of 4 says the blades came down to the fire as camels to water, not that they drank from it.
- **Notes:** 2.1.5 (the editions' «حَقيرٍ»), 7.2.5 (الحكيم, the wise man, not the colloquial doctor) and 8.1.1 (كم of number, not a question) added in Arabic, 2.1.5, 7.2.5 and 8.2.1 in English; 4.1.1 gives the third reading «قَرَينَ»; 3.1.2 gives al-Barquqi's parsing as his; 3.1.4 the colt's name in al-Barquqi and Ibn Furraja; heads put right (غامَرَ, شَجو, طَعم المَوت); the English 9.2.3 reworded.
- **sources.md:** al-Wahidi cited from Dieterici's print with printed pages; al-Ukbari's scan named as the Dar al-Ma'rifa reprint, and the attribution marked; who sings stated as not established (the pitch tracker finds no voice in the instrumental breaks either, so it cannot tell a chorus from a fuller arrangement); the sources for الحِجر and the colt's name added.
