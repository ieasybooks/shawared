# وَلِيُّ عَهدِ الأَسَدِ وَخُطبَةُ الحِمارِ

## Recording and extent

Alqimmah Studio published «حكايات أحمد شوقي» on 3 March 2023, crediting Osama Al-Waaedh with recitation and editing. The source lasts 966.844 seconds. Chapter 2 keeps **82.71–180.02 seconds of the original compilation** in one continuous cut. The source voice measurements place the first and last pitched sound of this chapter at approximately 83.23 and 178.56 seconds. The cut admits the entry and the decay of the final rhyme. [Compilation, chapter 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r6VpT0Lid0E&t=83s)

The complete 15-couplet fable occupies 30 performed halves in printed order, with each turn of courtly speech delivered once. The studio calls the chapter «حمار في الحفل». The diwan’s title identifies both the newborn and the donkey’s speech. The 99-second card prints «المرسوم بالأمان» and the 150-second card shows the invocation to the creator of barley. The ending in the separate source-edge clip is the fox’s funeral judgement «عاش حماراً ومضى حماراً».

- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, «الحكايات», Hindawi Foundation for Education and Culture, Cairo, deposit 19938/2012, ISBN 978-977-719-166-1. The page images and the electronic chapter were compared. [p. 876](https://archive.org/details/alshawkeyat/page/n876/mode/1up) · [p. 877](https://archive.org/details/alshawkeyat/page/n877/mode/1up) · [Electronic text](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/143/)

The first edition of *al-Shawqiyyat* (part I, Cairo, Matbaʿat al-Adab wa-l-Muʾayyad, 1898) provides an earlier text. The first printing titles the piece «ولي عهد الأسد وخطبة الحمار». The fox’s performance earns him the name of Jarir at the foot of p. 168. When the monkey takes the wine cup in the first couplet on p. 169, the guests hail him as Abu Nuwas. The donkey has yet to deliver his speech. All fifteen couplets occur in the recorded order. [p. 168](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA168) · [p. 169](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA169)

Both diwans run from «لمّا دعا داعي أبي الأشبال» to «عاش حماراً ومضى حمارا». Arslan’s remarks on everyday diction and reminiscences of La Fontaine apply to Shawqi’s animal fables generally. His examples in this section include a different story about a donkey made minister. The two poetic comparisons here identify Jarir with the fox’s recitation and Abu Nuwas with the cupbearer. The Arabic also pairs «العهد / المهد», bringing the newborn ruler down to the scale of a child, before it shifts the funeral speech from the prince to the donkey. [Arslan on the animal fables](https://www.safahat.org/books/96809026/24/)

The two printings transmit the same diwan tradition. The page reproductions and the word-by-word output from `scripts/collate.py` are in `media/research/wali-ahd-al-asad-wa-khutbat-al-himar/`. The studio captions are performance evidence. The glosses and bilingual commentary are editorial close readings of the cited text.

## Metre and rhyme

Full rajaz in rhymed couplets (مزدوج الرجز), with three feet to each half. «لمّا دعا | داعي أبي الـ | أشبالِ» scans **– – ⏑ – | – – ⏑ – | – – –**. «مبشّراً | بأوّل الـ | أنجالِ» answers **⏑ – ⏑ – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | – – –**. The last foot has qaṭʿ, the shortening that gives مستفعلْ. In bayt 8, *thum-ma-ta-lā | huth-thaʿ-la-bus | sa-fī-rū* and *yun-shi-du-ḥat | tā-qī-la-dhā | ja-rī-rū* both give **– ⏑ ⏑ – | – – ⏑ – | ⏑ – –**. The broken internal forms remain rajaz. Each bayt changes its rhyme, from الأشبال/الأنجال through الكاس/نواس to قرارا/حمارا, so there is no common rawiy to enter.

At 5.2 the Cairo Academy lists «المعيّة» under «مع» in *al-Muʿjam al-wasit*, in the sense of company or companionship. The lion’s herald addresses the courtiers in attendance. An Egyptian reader could also recognise the word from «ديوان المعيّة السنّيّة», the name on a series of court records listed by Raouf Abbas and Asim al-Disuqi. [Cairo Academy, مع](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/search_engine/roots/%D9%85%D8%B9) · [Egyptian National Archives holdings, as listed in *Kibar al-mullak wa-l-fallahin fi Misr*](https://www.safahat.org/books/61420307/0.4/)

## Readings against the performance

At 3.1 the isolated-vowel problem also appears in the chapter transcript’s «بالأماني». The full Audar take and the 99-second studio card have «بالأمان». The line accords safe conduct to all the king’s subjects, and «الداني» draws the ending of «الأمانِ» out into an ī. The aligner gives the spelling with yāʾ a 3.58 lead, too little to tell a lexical long vowel from a sustained rhyme here. Both printings read «الأمان». «الكاس» in 9.1 is the softened form of الكأس in both printings and rhymes with the poet’s name نواس.
