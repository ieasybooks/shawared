# منظومة أسماء الله الحسنى

## Authorial text and transmission

Muhammad Taqi al-Din ibn Abd al-Qadir al-Hilali al-Husayni introduces this poem in the first person. He names two predecessors, discusses their treatment of the names and dates his own composition to **10 Dhu al-Qa'da 1397 AH (1977 CE)**. The facsimile read for this edition is *Minhat al-kabir al-muta'ali fi shi'r wa-akhbar Muhammad Taqi al-Din al-Hilali*, edited and supplemented by his pupil Mashhur ibn Hasan Al Salman, Casablanca and Amman, 1431 AH (2010 CE), pp. 735–745. The introduction begins on p. 735, the date and first five bayts occupy p. 738, and the text ends on p. 745 before the heading for a different poem. The first-person introduction preserves the author's account. The collection appeared after his death. Its editor says on p. 29 that the poet apparently left this long and well-known piece out of his own collected diwan. [Introduction to the item, p. 735](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n734/mode/2up) · [Author's date and opening, p. 738](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n737/mode/2up) · [Ending, p. 745](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n744/mode/2up)

**The author-issued print can be located.** The editor records a separate sixteen-page booklet issued in 1405 AH (1985 CE), and on p. 29 specifies that al-Hilali had published it himself. The indexed catalogue entry of the Faculty of Sharia library in Fez lists *قصيدة اسماء الله الحسنى*, Muhammad Taqi al-Din al-Hilali, 1985, accession **12920**, shelf **س/1**, with no publisher supplied. A 2012 literary study also records the separate 1405 printing and the poet’s composition date. The wording of the present edition was checked on the collected text and the second witness below. A facsimile of the sixteen-page copy was unavailable. The editor also records a letter from Baghdad to the poet's pupil Ahmad Harun in Tetouan requesting the poem, and says that in Morocco pupils would read it to him after lessons. The editor gives no autograph shelfmark or certification of a later typesetting. [Editor's introduction, p. 29](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n28/mode/2up) · [Fez catalogue](https://biblio.chariaa.usmba.ac.ma/?page=931) · [Literary study, Rabitat al-Adab al-Islami](https://www.adabislami.org/magazine/2012/09/690/60)

The editor cites a posthumous republication in the Indian journal *Sawt al-Umma*, vol. 26, no. 5, Dhu al-Qa'da 1414 AH/May 1994. His item note on p. 735 gives pp. 23–29, his introduction on p. 29 gives pp. 23–24. The issue itself was unavailable. References below to its readings are explicitly through his apparatus. The introduction also gives 112 verses, while the item note gives 111. The consecutive text on pp. 738–745, the numbered second witness, and the studio performance give **111**. The earlier poem printed in *Sabil al-rashad* VI, pp. 228–230 of the Moroccan edition belongs to **Ahmad ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Hilali**. The footnote on p. 737 identifies that quotation. The similarly titled Cairo 2007 *Asma' Allah al-husna* is a prose work with a biographical introduction. It supplies biographical evidence here. [P. 737 and note](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n736/mode/2up) · [Cairo prose volume](https://archive.org/details/AAskZad-1106329)

## Second witness and interpretation

Bandar ibn Nafi' al-Abdali, *al-Bayan al-asma fi sharh manzumat asma' Allah al-husna*, first edition, 1448 AH (2026 CE), preface signed in Medina. The photographic pages give a numbered text at pp. 8–14 and a commentary on the verses at pp. 15–122. It is a separately edited witness with errors of its own. On p. 8 the first half of verse 9 is typeset twice. His explanation on p. 23, the diwan on p. 739 and the recitation restore the missing petition for healing. A 2016 typesetting by Abu Ahmad al-Shaydzmi was also collated as a transcription aid. [Al-Shaydzmi’s 2016 text and separate reading](https://shaydzmi.wordpress.com/2016/05/15/asmaa-al-hosna/) · [Al-Abdali, text and commentary](https://archive.org/details/aqidah-14853)

In his preface al-Hilali describes two names to a verse in al-Dimyati, and three or four together in his earlier namesake's poem. His own plan allows a principal invocation followed by a specific petition. Verses 1–5 announce the devotional and mnemonic purpose, 6–108 contain the addresses, reflections and petitions, and 109–111 close the prayer. Some verses express praise, a grievance or a condition without an explicit imperative. Al-Abdali, pp. 5–6, distinguishes the sound hadith about ninety-nine names from the disputed transmission of an enumerated list. He also distinguishes names from descriptions, for example at العدل (p. 50), منتقم (pp. 106–107), نور (p. 115) and رشيد (p. 119). The running count belongs to the poem. [Poet's explanation](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n737/mode/2up) · [Al-Abdali](https://archive.org/details/aqidah-14853)

The two commentaries and paired translations were written verse by verse. Among the distinctions supplied by al-Abdali are the source of mercy and its application to this repeatedly erring petitioner (7–8), bodily sight and insight (33, p. 49), a lawful livelihood and the willingness to share it (45, pp. 62–63), the two visits to water (48 and 96), God's reckoning and the deceiver caught in his own deceit (63, p. 82), resurrection and imitation of the Prophet (66), and pardon as restored favour (89, p. 107). The biographies draw on the Cairo prose volume, pp. 5–6, 8, 10 and 14, for his family and birthplace, study with his father, teaching in Lucknow, Baghdad and Medina, and death at home in Casablanca. The name in the poet record follows his self-identification in the verse preface. [Biographical volume](https://archive.org/details/AAskZad-1106329) · [Verse preface](https://archive.org/details/diwan_alhilali/page/n734/mode/2up)

## Recording, metre and boundaries

Khalid al-Sharafi, Alqimmah Studio, 30 January 2022. The original is 1122.406 seconds. The cut retains 4.65–1119.55, giving **1114.90 seconds**, −20.1 LUFS. All 111 numbered bayts appear once. The first voice onset aligns at app second 0.52 and the last word ends at 1112.34. The longer final release remains within the retained sound. The alignment uses 72, 70 and 80 half-lines. App-relative cuts were 363.55 and 714.10. At the first junction, 36.2 ends at 363.08 and 37.1 starts at 363.79. At the second, 71.2 ends at 713.67 and 72.1 starts at 714.30. The cuts are internal processing boundaries, and the listener hears one continuous recording. [Studio recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wJ68QFr6o-k)

The opening sadr scans متفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. At 56.1, أنت الشهيد على قوم طغوا وبغوا gives مستفعلن فَعِلُن مستفعلن فَعِلُن. The metre is basit and the rhyme consonant is lam. The diwan and the recorded delivery divide words across the two halves at 42 (ثمّ), 46 (رضاك), 50 (واحفظني), 61 (واجعل) and 107 (سواك). The displayed text keeps these metrical breaks, while translation and glosses restore the continuous sense.

The full PCM, word confidences, whole-take transcripts and numbered studio frames were available for review. Direct playback to the editor was unavailable.

## Final timing check

The final highlight for 111.2 ends at 1114.03 s on the app master. The 53 half-lines whose end times changed were transcribed again. The final per-half check flags 41 halves.

At 29.1 the revised short clip gives «ومنزيه». Heard with the surrounding halves, both transcriptions recover «ذكراً ومنزلة», the two things the poet asks God to raise. Both texts print the pair. At 99.1 both context transcriptions give «بمعرفة» without the possessive suffix that appeared in the first check. At 111.2 the short recogniser stops after «ما أطرب العيس». In the complete closing bayt both systems recover the driver and his camels, «حاد سار بالإبل». One extends the final kasra of «الإبل» to «بالإبلين». The diwan and the sustained rhyme support the collective noun «الإبل», camels.
