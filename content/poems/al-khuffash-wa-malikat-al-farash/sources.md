# حِكايَةُ الخُفّاشِ وَمَليكَةِ الفَراشِ

## Recording

Osama Al-Waaedh published this reading on 10 August 2022 under the title «الخفاش والفراشة» with a Salalah tag. The source runs 140.736 seconds. The app keeps 1.14–138.69 seconds of the original. The first words come at about 1.64 seconds. In the app audio the last vowel of «هلاك» remains voiced through about 136.99 seconds. The final half-verse closes at 137.02 seconds, leaving 0.53 seconds of the outdoor sound after it. There is one reciter throughout and no spoken introduction. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Nv4hOz0Muw)

All 31 printed couplets are present. Bayts 15 and 23 are each delivered twice in succession. In independent source clips, 57–64 and 65–71 seconds each recover the end of bayt 15. The clips at 93–100 and 101–108 seconds both recover bayt 23. Longer local Cohere clips (52–72 and 88–109 seconds) contain both complete pairs. Audar sometimes collapses immediate repetition in its transcript of a complete take. In addition, printed bayt 23 deliberately reproduces the opening bayt when the moth queen returns. The two narrative positions are retained in the poem. The app timeline contains 66 half-verse entries.

## Text and commentary

- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, Dar al-Awda, Beirut, 1988, part IV, pp. 144–146 (Internet Archive leaves 339–341). The title and all 31 pairs were read from the page images. The notes on pp. 144–145 identify the black friend as night, the prince as light, and Abu al-Misk as Kafur al-Ikhshidi. [Opening](https://archive.org/details/BBib-Alex-02915/page/n339/mode/1up) · [Notes](https://archive.org/details/BBib-Alex-02915/page/n340/mode/1up) · [Ending](https://archive.org/details/BBib-Alex-02915/page/n341/mode/1up)
- The Hindawi *Shawqiyyat*, section «الحكايات», gives the complete poem and the same identifications in notes 5–7. The printed pages of the Hindawi copy consulted are 881–882. It transmits the same underlying diwan tradition as the Dar al-Awda printing. [Hindawi text](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/143/) · [Hindawi page image](https://archive.org/details/alshawkeyat/page/n881/mode/1up)
- Shakib Arslan, *Shawqi: Sadaqat Arba'in Sana*, «أماثيل من شعر شوقي», section 8, «الحكايات في شعر شوقي». Shawqi’s contemporary quotes all 31 bayts, remarks on the light diction he chose for such stories and identifies the opposition between a handsome face and ugly conduct. Four word forms in this quotation differ from the printed diwan. The written collation is in `media/research/al-khuffash-wa-malikat-al-farash/collation.txt`. [Arslan’s quotation and commentary](https://www.safahat.org/books/96809026/24/)

The notes and both introductions draw on Arslan’s account. The dialogue itself fixes the distribution of night and light. The queen disparages the enslaved man’s price, and the bat answers by testing what a friend will give for another. The gloss of «أبو المسك» retains the historical reference supplied in the diwan’s footnote. «البُكا» at 25.2 is the shortened form of «البكاء», paired with the final alif of «مُنهَمِكا».

The short lines use two rajaz feet in each half. The opening «مَرَّت عَلى | الخُفّاشِ» gives *mustaf'ilun* followed by the shortened *mustaf'il* with a lengthened final vowel in rhyme. In 15.1 «إِن عُدَّ فيـ | مَن أَعرِفُ» both feet are complete *mustaf'ilun*. Each pair has its own rhyme sound, so no single rawiy is assigned to the poem.

## Readings against the recording

The two complete diwan texts agree. Four readings in Arslan’s quotation were scored against the words in the recorded halves. The first score in each pair is for the diwan. Cohere and Audar supplied independent full-take transcriptions. Where the scores are close, a second check uses a phoneme recogniser without prescribing an Arabic word.

## Half-verse verification

All 66 performances of a half-verse were transcribed individually. The first Cohere pass flagged eight clips. Once the full rhyme vowel was included, both performances of 23.2 gave «مليكة الفراش». Seven clips differ in the final pass. Times given above for the two repeated bayts refer to the source video.

- **1.2 and the first 23.2.** The clipped «الفراشية» adds a syllable to the rhymed «الفراشِ». The second 23.2 clip has «مليكة الفراش». Both whole takes give «الفراش» in the opening. The 99–112-second source window gives the full returning couplet, including «مَلِيكَةُ الْفَرَاشِ».
- **5.2 and 8.2.** The short clips have «الخاملة المجردة» and «كتمانه». The 9–22 and 27–40-second windows recover «الخامل المجرد» and «وسره كتمان». The two adjectives in 5 describe «الصديقَ الأسودَ». In 8 «كتمان» is the predicate, rhyming with «أمان».
- **13.1.** A final ذ strays into the short clip’s «الخصيذ» from the succeeding «ذو الثمن». The 45–58-second window places it correctly at the start of the second half.
- **15.1, both performances.** The verifier splits the normal spelling «فيمن» in both clips, and attaches a ه to «أعرف» in the first. The overlapping windows at 54–67 and 63–76 seconds give «في من أعرف» and carry the thought across into «أسمو به وأشرف». No pronoun follows «أعرف» in the printed witnesses.
- **31.1.** Both full transcribers, the 126–139-second source window and the half-line clip give «جِباله», “his mountains”. The competing scores are −28.12 for «جِبالُهُ أَشراكُ» and −32.13 for «حِبالُهُ أَشراكُ». An independent phoneme trace gives initial /dʒ/ at 132.63 app seconds, as it does in «جِواره» at 29.47 and «فجاءها» at 108.19. All three print witnesses have «حِباله», the cords from which a snare is made. Changing the consonant leaves the snares without their referent in the fable. The edition follows the printed word, and its bilingual card records the reciter’s substitution. The final sound in «أشراك» is the rhyming vowel /u/. The short clip writes «أشراكه».
