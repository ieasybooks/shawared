# طَرَقَ الخَيالُ وَلا كَلَيلَةِ مُدلِجِ

## Recording and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed for Mawqi' al-Shankabutiya, 6 September 2018. The keep is 28.00–107.55 of 122.369 s (79.55 s), after the prose announcement naming al-Harith and before the outro. Twenty halves are sung in order. The source description drops the sword-and-rain bayt, but both full-source transcripts and its own studio card at 87 s establish it as number 8. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pkCPR6jcoPM)

## Witnesses and commentary

- Al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, *al-Mufaddaliyyat*, ed. Ahmad Muhammad Shakir and Abd al-Salam Harun, no. 62, pp. 255–256. Its ten verses set the full count. The question to the phantom in 2, the double hawk image in 5–6, the sound of swords on the tent in 8 and the guest’s food in 10 are all present. This is a separate jim-rhyming poem of al-Harith. His Mu'allaqa begins «آذنتنا ببينها أسماء». [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/229) · [End](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/230)
- *Diwan al-Harith ibn Hilliza*, ed. Imil Badi' Ya'qub, no. 4, p. 107. It numbers twelve. Compare the crossed second halves of 5 and 6, «أحجمت» and «رعب» in 7, «وسمعت ... وقع السحابة» in 8, and «العوسج» for «العرفج» at the end of 9. Its two further bayts name Mu'attib and praise the cooking fire. Neither is recited. Reading Ya'qub’s 5–6 straight through leaves «لم تدرج» in the first dove clause and the boxthorn in the next. The performance has the coherent pairing in the *Mufaddaliyyat*, the doves taking cover in 5 and the struck dove falling still in 6. [Poem 4](https://shamela.ws/book/149875/41)
- Al-Anbari’s commentary in Charles J. Lyall’s *The Mufaddaliyat*, Arabic I, pp. 515–518, and Lyall’s English II, no. LXII, pp. 198–199. Lyall distinguishes 1–3 as an apparition’s visit, 4–6 as convivial and hunting memories, and 7–10 as the tribe’s boast. The long-backed hunting horse explains the singular pronoun after the plural white gazelles. The cold weather in 9 gives the milk camels a reason to seek the brushwood shelter. [Arabic commentary](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatant01mufauoft/page/n419/mode/1up) · [Introduction and notes](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/n235/mode/1up)
- Ibn Qutayba, *al-Ma'ani al-kabir*, III, p. 1159, quotes the last verse. «عمارة» is a substantial body of people, and the «مُدمَج» a well-shaped lot-arrow. When the milk runs short the group draws lots over a camel and slaughters it. This accounts for the turn from scarce milk to an implement instead of the name of a dish. Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, II, p. 154, supplies the poet’s affiliation with Bakr ibn Wa'il, his Iraqi desert background and the estimate of 570 CE. [Ibn Qutayba](https://shamela.ws/book/25837/1158) · [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/1385)

In 3 the travellers slacken and their mounts tire. The exception is the animal still moving quickly under the litter. In 4 the long verb construction leaves an understood «رُبَّ» to govern both the wine and the gazelles. The feminine address continues at «وحسبتِ» and «ألفيتِنا» from the initial apparition. In 5 «لآلئ» is the plural given by both textual witnesses and by both full transcriptions. The studio spells the plural «لآلٍ». Both plurals mean pearls, and the gathering of white gazelles gives the image its point.

The isolated MMS scores favour «إن» over «أنّى» by only 0.65 and the shorter pearl plural «لآلٍ» by 4.02. The interrogative is clear from the source card and the syntax. Both complete transcriptions give the longer pearl plural, which fills the metrical foot. At 4.2 MMS and Audar favour hyenas, «ضباع». The first full-source transcription gives gazelles, «ظباء», as do the card, the two editions and the description of the pale quarry as pearls in the next line. A separate phonetic decoder gives [ð] at the onset after the conjunction, consistent with the fricative ظ. At 5.2 the narrow and wide transcripts turn a hawk into a rock, but the scored consonants marginally favour «صقر» and the bird’s talons and wings are explicit in 6. At the repetition in 6.1 the narrow verification accepted the hawk, while the full Arabic transcripts again give a rock. An unprompted phone decoder resolves the middle consonant differently at the two occurrences, [f] and [x]. The word note distinguishes this acoustic difficulty from the identification of the bird. ## Metre and rhyme

Kamil, with jim as rawiy. Both halves of 1 give **u u – u – | u u – u – | u u – u –**. The divisions in the first fall after the long vowel of «الخيال» and the first syllable of «ليلة». The second scans *sa-di-kan-bi-ar | hu-li-na-wa-lam | ya-ta-'ar-ra-ji*. In 5 «فكأنهن لآلئ وكأنه» also has all three full feet. «صقر يلوذ حمامه بالعوسج» gives **– – u – | u u – u – | – – u –**, with iḍmar in the outer feet. The studio’s contracted pearl spelling does not by itself explain the five syllables of the central foot.

## Clip verification

The final verification on the master flagged 5 of 20 performed halves. The narrow transcriptions differed on 7 of 88 words (8.0%). The wider transcriptions are independent Audar decodes. The decisions compare these clips with the full-source transcriptions, the numbered studio cards and the literary witnesses.

| Half | Narrow clip | Wider clip | Editorial decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 | ليس دكا بارحلنا ولم يتعرجي (first pass «النبي») | جئ سديكا بأرح لنا ولم يتعرج | The earlier narrow clip produced the intrusive النبي. The revised short clip still splits سدكاً, while the wide clip approximates both سدكاً and بأرحلنا. The printed text and the card have a phantom clinging to the saddles. |
| 2.1 | ان اهتديت وكنت غير رجيله | أنه تديت وكنت غير رجيلة | Both recognisers contract أنى اهتديت, a question addressed to the phantom. The prints and studio have أنى. The question is what allowed it to reach a camp across such terrain. |
| 4.2 | وضباء محنيه ذعرت بسمحجي | وضباع محنية ذعرت بسمحجي | The wider transcription even turns the herd into hyenas. Both prints, the card and the first full transcription give ظباء. Their light colour motivates the pearls in 5. |
| 5.2 | صخر يلوذ حمامه بالعوسج | صخر يلود حمامه بالعوسج | Both clip engines and both full engines write صخر, “rock”. The studio says صقر, glosses it as the counterpart of the horse, and pictures the bird. All literary witnesses have a hawk. A predator pursuing doves, then hunting with its talon and wing, fixes the sense. |
| 9.2 | كالنعام الي كنيف العرفج | جنرتك النعامي إلى كنيف العرفجي | The wide clip restores the sound of رتك between material from the preceding half and النعام. The two complete literary texts share the ostrich’s gait. The anthology and card finish in العرفج, the plant used for the shelter. |
