# القصيدة العلوية — حسين أبو الفرج

Edition of Khalid al-Sharafi's recitation, 24 November 2026.

## Text, performance and numbering

Alqimmah Studio released the recording on 7 January 2023. The opening credits and the upload identify the author as **حسين بن وليد بن حسين أبو الفرج**, the reciter as **خالد الشرفي**, and the upload credits **رياض النعمان** for التعليق الصوتي. The second voice reads the title and section announcements. Only al-Sharafi's verse is in the app. [Studio recording, credits, chapters and dated upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWkmfcHTzC0)

The studio sets the credited author's text in numbered Arabic verse cards down the left side of the film. These supply the written witness, including vowels, half divisions, and the last visible number, ٢٥٢. The performance and cards belong to the same production. The studio's unpaginated text is cited here by its own bayt numbers. The research copy is `media/research/al-qasida-al-alawiyya/studio-720.mp4`, with 238 original-clock frames in `verse-cards/`. Consecutive frames overlap and many show more than one bayt. For any half, `media/research/al-qasida-al-alawiyya/card-locations.tsv` gives a source frame in seconds, a vertical pixel coordinate and a sheet row. The ordered contact sheets and the frame-second index are in `media/work/EWkmfcHTzC0/sheets/` and `card-line-index.json`. The studio description supplies credits and chapter times but gives no continuous verse text. None of the retrieved comments supplied a separate copy. I could not establish a separately paginated booklet or inspect this ode in the author's later collection *Al-Qala'id*. The collection is evidence for his career. Its 2026 reviews do not establish another reading of any of these lines.

The edition has all 252 numbered bayts in the order recited, 504 halves, with no repeated or omitted bayt. The verse card numbers survive the changes of chapter. At 46, 70 and 99 a word crosses the caesura (*tadwir*). The article or its first syllable belongs to the end of the first half on the card. The rest of the word opens the second half. In continuous speech the word is whole. The English likewise allows a clause to cross the division when the Arabic does.

The metre is *basit*. Scanning 1.1 gives أَختالُ فَخْ / رَنْ وَأَزْ / هُو فِلْوَرَى / تِيهَا, with quantities – – ⏑ – | – ⏑ – | – – ⏑ – | – –. The second half of the opening has the same pattern. At 126.1 the second and fourth feet are both ⏑ ⏑ –. The rhyming consonant is ه, preceded by long *i* and followed by *a*. It can belong to the word itself, as in تَنويها at 1, or to a suffix, as in نَواحيها at 47.

## Sources for the poet

*Al-Nahar*'s 1 February 2026 report on the Cairo recital identifies Abu al-Faraj as a Saudi academic, assistant professor of comparative literature and head of the Arabic Language and Literature department at King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah. It identifies his poems to Egypt and to al-Mutanabbi. The 3 January 2026 announcement of *Al-Qala'id* in Egypt's *Bawabat al-Dawla* gives the same full name as the studio, Husayn ibn Walid ibn Husayn Abu al-Faraj, and names Jadawel of Beirut as publisher. *Al-Jazirah*'s October review identifies Jadawel as publisher and discusses the collection and its introduction by Husayn Bafaqih. The profile uses the office as a role he has held. No birth year is established by these sources. [Cairo recital](https://www.alnaharegypt.com/1163310) · [*Bawabat al-Dawla*, collection announcement](https://www.aldawlanews.com/1327027) · [Review of *Al-Qala'id*](https://www.al-jazirah.com/2026/20261002/cm5.htm)

## Sources behind the explanations

The English is a new translation. Both commentaries and their word notes were written for this recording. The following works concern the traditions that Abu al-Faraj retells. A report his poem accepts, a later interpretation of it, and the poet's judgement about the protagonists sometimes give three different answers to a historical question.

- **11–49, Mecca and the genealogy.** Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq, *al-Sira al-nabawiyya*, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi, supplies the traditions of Zamzam, Abraha and the camels, Hashim's hospitality, and Qusayy's offices. The elephant sura does not give the camels episode. Qur'an 106 supplies the seasonal journeys. Al-Mus'ab al-Zubayri's *Nasab Quraysh*, ed. E. Lévi-Provençal, and Madelung's family trees distinguish relationships from stories about their bearers. Genealogists differ about whether Quraysh begins with Fihr or al-Nadr (42–43). Ka'b's priority in using أما بعد (38) is one attribution. At 11 Bukhari gives the dust episode behind *Abu Turab*. At 22 the poet takes the Sunni deathbed tradition about Abu Talib. Shi'i accounts remember his protection of Muhammad as an expression of faith. [Ibn Hisham](https://shamela.ws/book/23833) · [*Nasab Quraysh*](https://shamela.ws/book/2922) · [Qur'an 105](https://quran.com/105) · [Qur'an 106](https://quran.com/106) · [Bukhari 441](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:441) · [Bukhari 4772](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4772) · [Abu Talib and the traditions concerning his faith](https://www.islam.museum/en/person/abu-talib/)
- **50–73, the household.** Muslim gives Aisha's version of the cloak, Tirmidhi the title of Hasan and Husayn as leaders of the youths of Paradise, and Bukhari the tradition that Hasan would reconcile two Muslim parties. These explain 54–59. They do not supply Abu al-Faraj's agricultural images. At 67 Abu al-Faraj accepts the marriage of Ali and Fatima’s daughter Umm Kulthum to Umar. The Shi‘i scholar Ali Husayni al-Milani examines the chains and contents of the marriage reports. His conditional reading of reports in *al-Kafi* is that the contract took place under pressure. He accordingly rejects using it as evidence of friendship between Ali and Umar. The name list at 69–72 has an argumentative purpose. Naming a child cannot demonstrate allegiance on its own. [Muslim 2424](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2424) · [Tirmidhi 3768](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3768) · [Bukhari 2704](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2704) · [Al-Milani, *A Critical Assessment of Umm Kulthum’s Marriage to Umar*, trans. Jawid Akbari, pp. 76, 79–80](https://shiaword.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/A_Critical_Assessment_of_Umm_Kulthums_Marriage_to_Umar_en_9_17.pdf) · [Nasab Quraysh](https://shamela.ws/book/2922)
- **74–120, Ali and Muhammad.** Ismail K. Poonawala, “ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭāleb i. Life”, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, I/8, pp. 838–848, surveys early conversion, the migration, the campaigns and Ghadir. The order of the earliest converts varies with the transmitter and, often, the category being ranked. At 85–86 the Tabuk report compares Ali to Aaron in relation to Moses and reserves prophethood. At 87–90 the proclamation of Bara'a belongs to the pilgrimage led by Abu Bakr. The interpretations of Ghadir in Sunni and Shi'i tradition differ especially on the question of succession. For the Badr combatants, Khaybar, and Hunayn compare the corresponding *sira* narratives, the report on Qur'an 22:19 and Qur'an 9:25. [Poonawala](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ali-b-abi-taleb/ali-b-abi-taleb-i-life/) · [Muslim 2404a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2404a) · [Bukhari 4655](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4655) · [Ibn Hisham on Badr](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/629) · [Bukhari 3965](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3965) · [Ibn Hisham on the Trench](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/951) · [Khaybar and the standard, Bukhari 4210](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4210) · [Ibn Hisham on Hunayn](https://shamela.ws/book/23833/1174) · [Qur'an 9:25](https://quran.com/9/25)
- **121–142, the succession.** At 124 the speaker expressly attributes the early pledge by Ali and al-Zubayr to Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. Ibn Kathir gives the report through al-Bayhaqi in his account of the Saqifa. In Aisha's report in Bukhari, Fatima lives six months after Muhammad, and Ali pledges after her death. Some scholars understand the second pledge as a public renewal of the first. Madelung and Poonawala read the sources differently from the poet. The political sequence is distinct from the ranking by merit at 135. [Ibn Kathir on the Saqifa](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/البداية_والنهاية/الجزء_الخامس/قصة_سقيفة_بني_ساعدة) · [Bukhari 4240–41](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4240) · [Bukhari 3671](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3671) · [Wilferd Madelung, *The Succession to Muhammad*, Cambridge University Press, 1997, chapters 1–4](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Madelung%2C%20W%20-%20The%20succession.pdf)
- **143–178, Uthman's murder and the Camel.** Compare Poonawala and Madelung, chapter 4 and excursus 5. They discuss provincial grievances, reports of intercepted correspondence, allegiance to Ali, the Basran opposition, and the fighting. The attribution and even the precise letter at issue differ among the reports. At 152 Hudhayfa's broken door is Umar in the cited report. The poet recalls it during his account of Uthman. The oaths and former services of Ali's opponents explain his treatment of al-Zubayr, Talha and Aisha in 161–65. The reconciliation interrupted by instigators in 166–68 is one account of how fighting began. The number killed at Aisha's camel in 175 belongs to the poet's account. [Poonawala](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ali-b-abi-taleb/ali-b-abi-taleb-i-life/) · [Madelung](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Madelung%2C%20W%20-%20The%20succession.pdf) · [Hudhayfa's report, Bukhari 525](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:525) · [Talha's injured hand](https://dorar.net/h/RpMUxEhR/osoul)
- **179–220, Siffin, arbitration, and Nahrawan.** Abu al-Faraj credits both parties with motives he can honour while using Ammar's death to identify the transgressing side. Muslim preserves the saying about Ammar. The surviving versions of the arbitration agreement and the later stories about the arbitrators have distinct transmission histories. The terse agreement on consultation in 200 gives the poet's account. At 201 Abu al-Faraj echoes a watchword of Ali’s opponents. In Muslim, Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Rafi' reports their words as «لا حكم إلا لله». The poet opens with «الحكم لله» and adds that people are entrusted with administering the law. The one-third in 212 corresponds to the account of Ibn Abbas's debate in al-Nasa'i's *al-Sunan al-kubra*, where two thousand out of six thousand return. Al-Baladhuri also transmits an account in which five thousand initially depart under Ibn al-Kawwa. The poem’s invective and the speeches Abu al-Faraj gives the protagonists express his interpretation of the conflict. [Ammar, Muslim 2915a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2915a) · [The seceders’ watchword, Muslim 1066, in al-Nawawi’s commentary](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/53/3005/) · [Ibn Abbas's debate in al-Nasa'i](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/406/8826/) · [Al-Baladhuri, *Ansab al-ashraf*, on the Haruriyya](https://library.islamicportal360.com/ar/ansab-ashraf/3829/) · [Recitation and the throat, Bukhari 5058](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5058) · [“The Ṣiffīn Arbitration Agreement and Statecraft in Early Islamic Political Documents”, *Journal of Islamic Studies* 33 (2022), pp. 153–202](https://doi.org/10.1093/jis/etac001) · [Zein and El-Wakil’s abstract and publication details at Hamad Bin Khalifa University](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/the-siff%C4%ABn-arbitration-agreement-and-statecraft-in-early-islamic-/) · [Ibn Kathir on the Khawarij](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/59/853/) · [Poonawala](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ali-b-abi-taleb/ali-b-abi-taleb-i-life/)
- **221–252, death and epilogue.** At 224–26 Abu al-Faraj uses 17 Ramadan for the blow and allows Ali to live two further days. Other traditions put the attack on the 19th and death on the 21st. Counting Hasan's months completes the thirty-year caliphate in the Sunni account used at 228–29. The pilgrimage dispute (235–37), the burning and Ibn Abbas's objection (238), the sheet kept by Ali (240–42), Abu al-Hayyaj's commission (243–44), and Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya's question to his father (245–46) have identifiable hadith parallels. Bukhari's report of the burning gives no account of those people's divinisation of Ali. The conclusion about the imams' infallibility at 239 belongs to the poet's Sunni argument. At 104.2 the poet shortens the horseman’s name to «عمرو بن ودّ». Ibn Ishaq calls him «عمرو بن عبد ودّ» in Ibn Hisham’s recension, II, p. 225. The word notes supply the full name. [Ibn Hisham on the duel at the Trench](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/58/1142/)

At 38 Abu al-Faraj assigns the formula أما بعد to Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy. Taj al-Din al-Subki records three attributions on the cited page, to Quss ibn Sa'ida, Ka'b and David. At 225 Ibn Kathir records the verse attributed to Imran ibn Hittan with *taqi*, “pious”. Abu al-Faraj answers it with *shaqi*, “wretched”. The older line supplies the allusion, and the modern poet supplies the condemnation.
