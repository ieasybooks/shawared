# لا خَيلَ عِندَكَ تُهديها وَلا مالُ

## Recording and occasion

Osama al-Nahari, uploaded 18 December 2025. The original is 436.257 s. The edit keeps 5.73–435.53 s, retaining the opening «لا خيل» and the decay of «أشغال». All 46 bayts appear once in their diwan order, 92 halves in one continuous performance. Only the lead-in and tail fall outside the edit. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GIhYFJrqd38)

Al-Wahidi dates the poem to 348 AH in Egypt and names Abu Shuja' Fatik, nicknamed al-Majnun. He reports Ibn Jinni’s account of a single delivery to the poet worth a thousand dinars. From the question of a return gift come the neigh of a hobbled horse, a meadow capable of responding to rain and a name that can outlive its owner. At 4 al-Wahidi relates the hobbles to the difficulty of showing affection for Fatik openly under Kafur. That political application and the literal horse image both belong in the commentary. [Al-Wahidi, headnote and opening](https://shamela.ws/book/754/349) · [The gifts and the hobbles](https://shamela.ws/book/754/350)

## Witnesses

- Al-Wahidi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, pp. 349–352. The count begins with «لا خيل عندك» and ends at «ما قاته». The following «الحزن يقلق والتجمل يردع» starts the separate elegy on Fatik. Al-Wahidi’s distinctions between the different movements of a lance and a sword at 25, and between a man’s sustenance and his posthumous reputation at 46, inform the translations. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/754/349) · [The guests, the swords and the hours](https://shamela.ws/book/754/351) · [Close and boundary](https://shamela.ws/book/754/352)
- The commentary traditionally attributed to Abu al-Baqa' al-Ukbari, *al-Tibyan fi sharh al-diwan*, ed. Mustafa al-Saqqa, Ibrahim al-Abyari and Abd al-Hafiz Shalabi, III, pp. 276–288. Its numbered scholia identify the mounts and game, distinguish the two parties in the battle and explain why a single letter of *hamd* is beyond a rival’s reach. The commentary under 46 expressly prefers «قاته» with qaf and records the competing fa. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/1008) · [Middle](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/1014) · [Closing gloss](https://shamela.ws/book/6908/1020)
- Abd al-Rahman al-Barquqi, *Sharh Diwan al-Mutanabbi*, lam section, headnote 952 and verse notes 953–998. Its complete 46-bayt text is the other continuous collation witness. The text of the notes, the electronic display of the verse and the actual recording can disagree. The files in the research directory retain both the transcription and the downloaded chapter. [Al-Barquqi, lam section](https://adab.wiki/wiki/شرح_ديوان_المتنبي_(البرقوقي)/قافية_اللام#ftn.952)

At 10 «جهلت يمناه ما وهبت» describes the abundance of Fatik’s own gifts. Al-Wahidi and al-Barquqi explain «لا وارث» by his father’s generosity. He left his son no money. The verse sets a man who wins wealth at his own risk beside the hand that gives it away. [Al-Wahidi](https://shamela.ws/book/754/350) · [Al-Barquqi, note 962](https://adab.wiki/wiki/شرح_ديوان_المتنبي_(البرقوقي)/قافية_اللام#ftn.962)

At 6.1 the passive «أن يُجادَ لنا» is clear from the gift and the contrasting duty to give thanks. The electronic al-Wahidi’s spacing «أني جاد» is preserved in the collation. At 10.2 «سَأّال» uses the modern seat for the hamza. These spellings do not add a word to the performance.

## Metre

Basit. Each half has مستفعلن فاعلن مستفعلن followed by a shorter ending. The rhyme consonant is lam with a prolonged u. The opening is rhymed in both halves, «مالُ / الحالُ». In 43 the sadr ends with the ordinary two shorts and a long while the ajuz retains two longs. Vertical strokes mark the four feet.

| Bayt | Sadr | Ajuz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | lā-khay-la-ʿin ∣ da-ka-tuh ∣ dī-hā-wa-lā ∣ mā-lū | fal-yus-ʿi-din ∣ nuṭ-qu-ʾin ∣ lam-tus-ʿi-dil ∣ ḥā-lū |
| 23 | taj-rin-nu-fū ∣ su-ḥa-wā ∣ lay-hi-mu-khal ∣ la-ṭa-tan | min-hā-ʿu-dā ∣ tun-wa-ʾagh ∣ nā-mun-wa-ʾā ∣ bā-lū |
| 43 | law-lal-ma-shaq ∣ qa-tu-sā ∣ dan-nā-su-kul ∣ lu-hu-mū | al-jū-du-yuf ∣ qi-ru-wal ∣ ʾiq-dā-mu-qat ∣ tā-lū |

In 1 the boundary inside «عندك» joins the last two syllables of that word to the beginning of «تهديها». In 43 the division of «قتّال» between two feet preserves the doubled consonant that makes the blow of the final word so sharp.
