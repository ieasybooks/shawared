# أُغالِبُ فيكَ الشَّوقَ وَالشَّوقُ أَغلَبُ

Osama al-Nahari’s recording, published on 4 June 2026, gives all **47 bayts** in the order of the commentaries. Its opening is «أغالب فيك الشوق» and its last words «جدار معلّى أو خباء مطنّب». The continuous keep, **0.950–491.050 s** of the 500.669 s source, yields **490.100 s** of app audio. Each half occurs once. The final upright wall and tent on guy ropes are present in both full transcriptions and in verse 47 of the early texts. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLCN-oN68Xk) · [Al-Wahidi’s last page](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n233/mode/1up)

## Text, date and extent

- Ibn al-Iflili, *Sharh maʿani shiʿr al-Mutanabbi*, **al-sifr al-thani, I, pp. 119–130**. His rubric gives the recitation date as Thursday, two nights into Shawwal 347 AH (958 CE). He quotes all 47 bayts in order. The opening address to the absent beloved lasts into the journey. On p. 124 he explains that the poet has been singing his patron’s praises and would like a share in the cup. On p. 130 the wall and tent stand for the settled and the nomadic hearers to whom his verse travels. The rubric puts the poem in Shawwal 347 and says Kafur had sent the poet **six hundred dinars**. The printed numerals run from 1 to 47. Page 661 explicitly gives «وقاك ردى الأعداء تسري إليهم» as bayt **6**, and p. 667 starts a separate poem after 47. Page images resolve several corruptions in the electronic transcription. ## Readings and commentary

**2, 4, 6.** The performer has «تنائي», the days keeping an enemy far away. Al-Wahidi allows derivation from either ناءيتُ or أنأيتُ. Ibn al-Iflili writes «تُنئي». In 4 al-Nahari and al-Barquqi give «التي أتجنّب», the feminine form of the relative referring to the road. Al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Iflili give «الذي» and the language allows either gender for «طريق». In 6 the recording and both printed diwans have «تسري إليهم» where Ibn al-Iflili gives «تسري عليهم». The night conceals a raid in the first half and a visit from the beloved in the second. These are the two benefits darkness can confer in the argument about Mani and his followers. [Ibn al-Iflili, pp. 119–120](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/119) · [Al-Wahidi, pp. 660–661](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n239/mode/1up)

**8–13, 16–19.** The one star that remains after night is the horse’s blaze. A fold in its hide moves over its broad chest, and the animal still has speed in hand when the hunt ends. Al-Wahidi uses that reserve to explain the comparison between a horse and a friend who has been put to the test. The sudden address to «ابنة القوم» in 16 belongs to the language of love poetry. Kafur’s qualities then supply the verses as if by dictation, anticipating the complaint about who owns a poet’s words in 45. At 19 both continuous transcriptions give «ونادرةً», a singular feat even for an accomplished man. Ibn al-Iflili and Ibn Jinni, the latter quoted by al-Wahidi, have «وبادرةً», a sudden impulse. [Ibn al-Iflili, pp. 122–123](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/123) · [Al-Wahidi, pp. 662–663](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n237/mode/1up)

Al-Mutarrifi prints «وبادرةً إيّانَ» in 19.2. He explains that the manuscript itself has nūn in both verse and gloss, and supplies bāʾ from his interpretation of the gloss. The next note records «أحيان» in al-Fasr, al-Wahidi and the diwan. At 20.1 al-Jurjani gives «في الرّوع», in terror or battle, where the recording and the other complete printed witnesses give «في الحرب». His apparatus also records an alternative formulation of 23 that the poet is said to have recited to himself. The numbered poem has «كفّي زماننا … كفّيك» as in the recording. [Al-Jurjani, p. 624, nn. 4–5](https://library.ksaa.gov.sa/links/epubs/sharah_diwan_almutanabiy.pdf#page=617) · [p. 625](https://library.ksaa.gov.sa/links/epubs/sharah_diwan_almutanabiy.pdf#page=618) · [p. 627](https://library.ksaa.gov.sa/links/epubs/sharah_diwan_almutanabiy.pdf#page=620)

**22–30.** The Berlin print gives «وتشرب» at 22.2, like both continuous passes, al-Barquqi and Ibn al-Iflili. The erroneous electronic «ونشرب» would let the singer share the very cup he is asking for. Al-Wahidi’s own gloss distinguishes the singer from the drinker. At 24 the estate or governorship would yield a livelihood beyond what Kafur pays or what attendance at court costs. The feast of 25 joins the dated rubric to the poet’s homesickness. In 27 both diwans and the recording give «في فؤادي» where Ibn al-Iflili gives «في الفؤاد». At 29 the recording shares «الحسّاد» with al-Wahidi and al-Barquqi, against Ibn al-Iflili’s «الأعداء». At 30 al-Jurjani, Ibn al-Iflili and al-Wahidi read «إلى الشيب», into old age. Al-Nahari and al-Barquqi say «إلى الموت», into death. Death itself would be an escape from what stands between the enviers and their wish. Even a child could go grey at the sight. [Ibn al-Iflili, pp. 124–126](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/126) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 664](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n236/mode/1up) · [p. 665](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n235/mode/1up) · [Al-Barquqi, p. 185](https://archive.org/details/20201020_20201020_1638/page/n184/mode/1up)

**34–40.** The studio card at source 351 s has «ذا المُلكِ» with a kasra. The expression means the holder of the kingdom, the Ikhshid’s son whom Kafur raised. The same card puts a fatha above the ḍād of «مُرضَعاً», describing him as a child at the breast. The first-pass half expands this vowel to an alif in «مرضاعا», and the passive vowel leads by 9.14 in the acoustic comparison. Al-Jurjani also prints the passive participle. Al-Mutarrifi’s apparatus to al-Jurjani, p. 631 n. 1, explicitly assigns the genitive «المُلكِ» to *Muʿjiz Ahmad*, IV, p. 111, and *al-Tibyan*, I, p. 185. Ibn al-Iflili and al-Jurjani print the accusative, «ذا المُلكَ», so that the kingdom itself grows up in Kafur’s lap. Al-Jurjani takes the cub in 35 to be that whole kingdom. The recitation’s genitive sustains a different reference through 35–36, the real young ward whom Kafur protects. The lion’s claw is his Indian sword. In 39 the four occurrences of the same written shape alternate **بِيض**, white swords, with **بَيْض**, helmets. A sword flash comes true in a rain of blood. The gleam of the helmets gives no such rain and fails to protect their wearers. At 40 the preachers’ wood is the wood of pulpits, and prayer in a ruler’s name in the Friday sermon publicly declares his authority. [Al-Jurjani, pp. 631–633 and apparatus](https://library.ksaa.gov.sa/links/epubs/sharah_diwan_almutanabiy.pdf#page=624) · [Ibn al-Iflili, p. 127](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/127) · [pp. 128–129](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/128) · [Al-Wahidi, p. 666](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n234/mode/1up)

**42.1.** The three early commentators have «يستحقّك», asking which tribe could deserve to count Kafur among its own. A close view of the studio card at source 432 s shows **one dot**, «يستحفّك». The Hindawi setting of al-Barquqi likewise prints fāʾ on p. 185, although al-Barquqi himself explains the word with «يستحقّ» in n. 525 on p. 310. The continuous first pass and both half-line passes give fāʾ. The continuous Audar pass loses this stretch. On the aligned word at 430.62–432.42 s, fāʾ leads qāf by 7.44. The fāʾ verb is independently attested. Ahmad Rida gives «استحفّ أموالهم» for taking their property in its entirety, the same sense given by the Cairo Arabic Language Academy. In this performance a tribe’s standing would have to take in the whole man. This figurative use fits the question about ancestry and the metre. Both language layers keep the performed verb and explain the older reading. [Studio card at 432 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLCN-oN68Xk&t=432s) · [Al-Barquqi, p. 185](https://archive.org/details/20201020_20201020_1638/page/n184/mode/1up) · [his note 525, p. 310](https://archive.org/details/20201020_20201020_1638/page/n309/mode/1up) · [Al-Jurjani, p. 639](https://library.ksaa.gov.sa/links/epubs/sharah_diwan_almutanabiy.pdf#page=632) · [Ahmad Rida, *Muʿjam matn al-lugha*, II, p. 126](https://shamela.ws/book/29613/730) · [Cairo Academy, root حفف](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/search_engine/roots/%D8%AD%D9%81%D9%81)

**43–47.** Ibn al-Iflili reads the delight at the sight of Kafur in 43 as the pleasure of an anticipated welcome. Al-Wahidi, by contrast, reports Ibn Jinni putting a mocking interpretation to the poet and the poet laughing. The anecdote records an exchange and leaves the poem’s professions of earlier hopes intact. At 45 both continuous transcriptions and Ibn al-Iflili include the pronoun in «ولكنّه». «أُفتَّشُ» is passive. Earlier patrons sought out the poet for what he might say and carried off his words as plunder, one reason he offers for having praised others first. The readings in both language layers follow the early commentators here. In 46–47 his verse acquires a power his patron cannot confer. It goes wherever an audience lives. [Ibn al-Iflili, pp. 129–130](https://shamela.ws/book/37327/130) · [Al-Wahidi, pp. 666–667](https://archive.org/details/carminacumcommen00muta/page/n233/mode/1up)

An earlier pass used shorter clip endings. At 3.1 both isolated passes still lengthen «تئيّةً» to «تأياتا». At 29.1 the first clip gave «دون اللّه دافع». The earlier widened pass and the final clip give «ما اللّه دافع», including the short «ما» aligned at 300.06–300.14 s. Al-Jurjani supplies the understood object of «دافع», the harm God wards off. At 40.1 the earlier pair gave «هلامت … خطيب» and «حلامت … خاطب». The final clip restores the consonants of «علّمت» while spelling the last noun «خطيب». Al-Jurjani prints «خاطب» and the studio card shows the same spelling. The following half puts the man on the pulpit, praying and preaching. In the last foot, «خاطب» supplies the long syllable that «خطيب» would shorten. The verifier accepts their shared consonant skeleton.
