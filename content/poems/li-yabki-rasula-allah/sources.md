# لِيَبكِ رَسولَ اللَّهِ مَن كانَ باكِيا

## The recording and the poem

Osama Al-Waaedh recites the passage on the waterfront in Muharraq, Bahrain, according to his upload of 20 January 2023. The source runs 235.99 s. The edit keeps 2.94–231.12 s. The first clear voice onset is about 3.45 s and the last vowel tails off at about 230.60 s. The kept interval is continuous. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RpgYQw1XtF0)

The recording gives all twenty-six verses of Shukri Faisal’s **poem 450** once in their printed order, from «ليبك رسول الله» to «وخلفت من خلفته عنك ساليا». The Abbasid poet invokes the Prophet’s death long after the event. Lament and praise form the first seven verses. At «ركنّا إلى الدنيا» the speaking voice includes itself among those who have forgotten the example they mourn. The poem turns to dependence on others, the unassisted poor and the everyday sight of death, then addresses the builder who collects for a lifetime about to end. The last line imagines the survivors outliving even their grief. [Faisal, pp. 433–435](https://archive.org/details/0017pdf_202002/page/n473/mode/1up)

## Witnesses

- Shukri Faisal, *Abu al-Atahiya: Ash'aruhu wa-akhbaruhu*, Damascus University, 1965, no. 450, pp. 433–435. Read from the scan, leaves 473–475. A fresh heading «وقال أيضاً» and the metre introduce the poem midway down p. 433. No. 451, «لأبكينّ على نفسي», begins on p. 435 in basit after the twenty-sixth bayt. Faisal’s note on p. 433 says that his Beirut witness omits the first seven. Only the last line survives in his Zahiriyya witness at the top of a leaf whose predecessor is missing. The manuscript note does not say that the original poem had only one verse. ## Metre, rhyme and interpretation

Faisal marks tawil. The opening scans **u–u / u––– / u–– / u–u–** and **u–– / u––– / u–u / u–u–**, with the usual *qabd* in *fa'ulu* and the final *mafa'ilun*. The comparison with vipers at 14 scans **u–– / u––– / u–u / u–u–** and **u–– / u––– / u–– / u–u–**. The rhyme consonant is the pronounced y before the final long a. It is audible even in «كما هِيا», the pronoun *hiya* with a lengthened final vowel. An ordinary internal accusative such as «محمّداً» keeps its tanwin. The rhyme words follow their verse-boundary pronunciation.

The explanations follow the argument of no. 450, Cheikho’s heading at the turn to worldly life, and al-Kala'i’s context for the opening. «ثيابًا من التقى» at 11 recalls the garment of reverence in Qur’an 7.26. For 23.2 Ibn Manzur distinguishes the old funeral use of *is'ad*, joining in another’s lament. This supplies the sense obscured by the usual modern meaning of making someone happy. For 14.2 the Cairo Academy files *af'a* under ف ع و, and for 16.1 Ibn Manzur gives *kusa* as the plural of *kuswa*. [Qur’an 7.26](https://quran.com/7/26) · [Lisan al-Arab, سعد, vol. 3, pp. 216–217](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/1658) · [Cairo Academy, أفعى](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/محرك-البحث/أفعى) · [Lisan al-Arab, كسا](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3503/223)

Mahmud Ahmad al-Halhuli and Ibrahim Mustafa al-Duhun draw attention to the four calls of «يا موت» at 19–20, one in the middle of each half. Their printed quotation on p. 340 reads «وعلّمتَ» with m. The repetition informs the commentary on these two verses. [University of Sharjah Journal for Humanities and Social Sciences 17.2A (2020), pp. 329–356](https://doi.org/10.36394/jhss/17/2A/11)

Both complete-recording transcriptions and the overlapping windows from the original were compared with the central check of all 52 half-lines. It flagged six text differences and an initial clip edge. All six text flags received a second Audar pass with 0.35 s of padding. The corrected cut removed the edge flag. Two otherwise unflagged places affect the words a reader might understand. At 5.2 «شِعبًا» is a ravine or mountain pass. Faisal and Ibn Rajab mark the kasra. On its own the score slightly favours «شَعبًا», a people, by 0.74. Immediately after the sh at 41.86–41.90 s the vowel has a low first formant (371–413 Hz) and a second near 1950–1990 Hz, before the following pharyngeal raises the first formant and lowers the second. This supports the printed reading. At 23.1 the full takes and the overlapping window give f in «نرفي», while the isolated half gives «نرثي». The f candidate wins by 5.49, though the aligner represents ث as two letters, th. It also slightly prefers f in the known words «ثيابًا» and «ثوى», by 1.04 and 1.53 respectively. The weak frication before i at 194.88–194.96 s also resembles this speaker’s f in «كافيًا». The text keeps «نرثي لمعوِل», the idiom for feeling compassion for someone. The word cards indicate how close the consonant in the recording is to f. Ibn Manzur’s different verb «رفوته», I calmed him, takes a direct object and has a w in its stem. [Lisan al-Arab, رفا](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/122/3236/رفا)
