The attribution, occasion and first bayt stand at the bottom of p. 294. Page 295 has the remaining eight, followed by «وقال» and a poem in a different metre and rhyme. ## Exile, safe conduct and the poet

Ibn Shabba, 1, p. 294, explicitly says that Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr expelled the Umayyads from the Hijaz to Syria. In the _Aghani_, 1, p. 28, the report of Ahmad ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Ammar through Muhammad ibn Yunus ibn al-Walid places Abu Qatifa among the exiles from Medina. The narrative resumes on p. 29, with a parallel report through Ishaq al-Mawsili. The exiles’ poems have included a greeting to the poet’s people. Ibn al-Zubayr returns the greeting, declares Abu Qatifa safe and asks someone to tell him to return. He sets out and dies before reaching Medina. The narrative associates the pardon with Abu Qatifa’s poetry of longing. It does not single out the nun-rhyme recorded here as the poem that secured his return. The separate setting for the four-verse extract begins at the foot of p. 29. [Al-Aghani, vol. 1, pp. 28–30](https://archive.org/download/aghani2014/1.pdf#page=94)

The poet is Amr ibn al-Walid ibn Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt. Ibn Asakir gives his kunya as Abu al-Walid and Abu Qatifa as his usual byname. The _Aghani_ gives «القصر فالنخل فالجمّاء بينهما» immediately before the account of his descent. Al-Zirikli’s _al-A'lam_, vol. 5, p. 87, names him, places his exile in Damascus and gives **about** 70 AH (690 CE). Both biographies retain that qualification. The current numeric date field cannot express it. [Ibn Asakir, vol. 46, p. 446](https://shamela.ws/book/71/21572) · [Al-Aghani, vol. 1, pp. 11–12](https://archive.org/download/aghani2014/1.pdf#page=77) · [Al-Zirikli, vol. 5, p. 87](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4220)

## Metre and commentary

The metre is **tawil**, with nun as rawiy and damma in the rhyme. The opening sadr divides «أَلا لَيْ | تَ شِعْري هَلْ | تَغَيَّـ | ـرَ بَعْدَنا», giving ⏑–– | ⏑––– | ⏑–⏑ | ⏑–⏑–. The ajuz has the full third foot, «جَبوبُلْ | مُصَلّى أَمْ | كَعَهْدِلْ | قَرائِنُو». Both halves of the central verse 5 give ⏑–– | ⏑––– | ⏑–– | ⏑–⏑–. The second divides «وَلاكِنْ | نَهُو ما قَدْ | دَرَلْلا | هُ كائِنُو» in connected recitation. [Al-Aghani’s identification of the metre, 1, p. 30](https://archive.org/download/aghani2014/1.pdf#page=96)

- **The Medinan places.** Shaltut p. 294 n. 4 glosses the hard earth of the Musalla and cites the traditions about al-Qara'in. Al-Samhudi records three houses of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, alternatively three domed buildings. Abu al-Faraj, glossing another verse by the same poet, calls the Qara'in adjoining houses belonging to the family of Sa'id ibn al-As. Yaqut identifies the name as a site in Medina and places al-Balat on the paved ground between the Prophet’s Mosque and the market. [Al-Samhudi, _Wafa' al-Wafa_, vol. 4, p. 132](https://shamela.ws/book/23695/944) · [Al-Aghani, vol. 1, p. 11](https://archive.org/download/aghani2014/1.pdf#page=77) · [Yaqut, vol. 4, p. 319](https://shamela.ws/book/23735/1856) · [Al-Balat, vol. 1, p. 477](https://shamela.ws/book/23735/472)
- **Al-Jazira.** Yaqut places Jazirat Aqur between the Tigris and Euphrates, bordering Syria. The last verse sets a resident there beside a resident of Tayba. It gives no itinerary for the poet’s last journey. [Yaqut, vol. 2, p. 134](https://shamela.ws/book/23735/665)
- **Reading the images.** Ibn Manzur gives «رهن» for something that stays fixed, «تيامن» for taking the right-hand direction, and «شطنت الدار» for a dwelling lying far away. He describes the practice of drawing omens from the flight of birds. These senses underlie verses 3, 4, 7 and 8. [رهن, vol. 13, p. 189](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/6750) · [يمن, vol. 13, p. 461](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/7022) · [عيف, vol. 9, p. 261](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/4568) · [شطن, vol. 13, p. 238](https://shamela.ws/book/1687/6799)
