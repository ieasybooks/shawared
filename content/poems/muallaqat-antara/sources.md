## Attribution and title

- **Antara's:** Ibn al-Anbari (p. 293), al-A'lam al-Shantamari's recension of the diwan (Mawlawi, p. 181), Abu Zayd al-Qurashi's *Jamharat ash'ar al-Arab* (shamela 1487/309, p. 348, among the Mu'allaqat), al-Zawzani and al-Tibrizi all give the poem to Antara. Ibn al-Anbari quotes Ya'qub ibn al-Sikkit: Abu Amr al-Shaybani did not transmit the first bayt as Antara's until he heard Abu Hizam al-Ukli recite it as his (p. 294). Single abyat have their own transmission notes (below: 20, 36, 53, 56, 81–82, 86).
- **The occasion:** Ibn al-Anbari, pp. 293–294, and al-A'lam (Mawlawi, pp. 181–182): a man of Abs taunted Antara with his colour, his mother and his brothers and said he was the better poet; Antara answered «ستعلم ذلك», and this was the first poem he made, «وكان عنترة يومئذ لا يقول من الشعر إلا البيت والبيتين في الحرب» (Ibn al-Anbari). Al-A'lam adds «وكانت العرب تسمي هذه القصيدة المذهبة» (Mawlawi, p. 182). The story is Abu Ja'far Ahmad ibn Ubayd's, which Ibn al-Anbari quotes after Ya'qub's pedigree (p. 293); the intro names both. Ibn Hamdun, *al-Tadhkira al-Hamduniyya*, vol. 3, p. 423 (shamela 10646/1350), has the first-poem story and «والعرب تسميها المذهبة». The vowels of the name: al-Baghdadi, *Khizanat al-adab*, vol. 1, p. 125 (shamela 12732/123): «وكانت العرب تسميها المذهبة بصيغة اسم المفعول من الإذهاب أو التذهيب وهما بمعنى التمويه والتطلية بالذهب», so al-Mudhhaba or al-Mudhahhaba; the English intro gives the second.
- **Title:** the poem is called «معلقة عنترة» in the editors' notes to *Lisan al-Arab* (shamela 1687/2570, 2770, 3268, 3717, 4037: «في معلقة عنترة»), in a note to Ibn Ashur's collected articles (shamela 147597/605), in the heading of al-Zawzani's text in the Dar Ihya' al-Turath printing (shamela 11253/268, p. 245) and in al-Durra's *Fath al-Kabir al-Muta'al* (vol. 2, p. 123). The text sets it «مُعَلَّقَةُ عَنتَرَةَ» (the name is a diptote); English "The Mu'allaqa of Antara".

**The recited text** is a composite: in wording it mostly follows al-Zawzani and al-Tibrizi; it has the abyat that among the four only al-A'lam has (2, 3, 5, 17, 20, 83; 5 is also in J), al-A'lam's order for 81–83, 36 and 56, which al-Zawzani lacks, and one bayt from none of them (53). Every one of M's 85 abyat is recited, and all 79 of A's.

**`abyat_in_full` is 113**, al-Durra's count: his index is the only text found that holds all 86 recited abyat (M 85, A 79, Z 75; J's 108 lack 53). The 27 not recited are the additions al-Durra gathers from *Muntaha al-talab* and other collections, which Mawlawi gives in his notes (e.g. pp. 183, 185, 194, 196, 216, 217, 221) and many of which J and al-Tibrizi's «بعض الروايات» carry, so the recording does leave out abyat some transmissions have. The second reader weighed 86 (nothing in the text of A, T, Z or M is left out) against 113 and kept 113: "86 of 113" is true of the fullest gathering, while 86 would present a composite as the whole poem and hide what J has.
