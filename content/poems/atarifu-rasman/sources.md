# Qays ibn al-Khatim · مُذَهَّبَةُ قَيسِ بنِ الخَطيمِ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari recites 38 distinct bayts and 77 half-line occurrences. Keep 3.170–286.848 s on the original source clock. The first onset follows the silence ending at about 3.69 s. The original file ends at 286.848 s. The final rhyme and release extend past the aligner's last model word, so the cut takes the available tail. The app excerpt lasts 283.678 s. The opening sadr is recited twice before the first ajuz. The printed numbers and display addresses stay in the order of Nasir al-Din al-Asad's poem 4. Playback proceeds **1–15, 21, 16–20, 22–38**, with the two 1.1 occurrences at the very beginning. At 12 the voice substitutes the Aws contingent verse for the diwan's list. All other printed positions are represented. The two running transcriptions and the studio cards agree about the move from the spear splinters at 15 to the unhelmeted fighter of 21 and back to the towers of Muzahim at 16. ## Witnesses, occasion and commentary

- Nasir al-Din al-Asad, *Diwan Qays ibn al-Khatim*, no. 4, printed pp. 76–96. In this scan printed p. 76 is leaf 73. The heading places the poem in the war of Hatib. Its named encounters include the day of al-Hadiqa and Bu'ath. The old gloss and the editor's apparatus distinguish the voices and events. Al-Asad's pages explain the decoration on the abandoned ground, the glimpse at Mina, the mail rings and their comparison with locusts' eyes, the flood vocabulary, the sticks split by the mat-makers and the towers at Muzahim. Those explanations underlie the two original reader layers. [Opening, p. 76](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n73/mode/1up) · [Mail and contingents, pp. 81–83](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n79/mode/2up) · [Battle imagery, pp. 85–87](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n83/mode/2up)
- Abu Zayd al-Qurashi, *Jamharat ash'ar al-Arab*, ed. Ali Muhammad al-Bijawi, Nahdat Misr, prints Qays among the Mudhahhabat on pp. 507–514. Its 37 numbered bayts end with the wish about Suwayd. The transmitted closing notice counts 35. Both print and recitation begin «كاطّراد المذاهب». Note 3 assigns «كالطراز المذهب» to the Amiri printing (م), and the searchable text adopts that reading. The sun verse comes before «ديار التي كانت». The Aws verse occurs at printed 32. Ibn Sallam, *Tabaqat fuhul al-shu'ara'*, 1/228–229, gives nine verses, identifies Amra as bint Rawaha, and associates the poem with Bu'ath. Al-Bijawi's opening note, like the diwan, places it in the war of Hatib. The two introductions credit the distinct settings. The profile follows al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, 5/205. [Al-Bijawi, p. 507 and note 3](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n506/mode/1up) · [The Aws verse, p. 513](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n512/mode/1up) · [Ending and count, p. 514](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n513/mode/1up) · [Searchable opening](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/456) · [Ibn Sallam, 1/228–229](https://shamela.ws/book/6738/223) · [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4573)
- At 28 the older commentary identifies the man who swore off wine as Abu Qays ibn al-Aslat. Al-Asad argues for Hudayr al-Kata'ib, the Aws leader associated with a vow involving wine and the fort of Muzahim. The *Jamhara* has thirty years or pilgrimages, «ثلاثين حجة», against the thirty nights sung here. At 29 the old explanation defines «عازب الأموال» as outlying stock. Al-Asad finds the antecedent of «قعرها» unclear in this order and proposes understanding the strongholds mentioned in 30. The commentary treats that referent as a proposal. At 36 the old account identifies the absent Suwayd as Suwayd ibn al-Samit. [Al-Asad, pp. 91–93](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n89/mode/2up) · [p. 96](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n93/mode/1up)

Abu Ubayd's *al-Gharib al-musannaf* analyses «كاطّراد» as the assimilated form of the pattern افتعال from طرد. [3/940](https://shamela.ws/book/1750/891)

## Readings

At 2.1 «كانت» has running and clip support and a modest word-score advantage. The diwan's «كادت» makes the near-encounter explicit. At 3.1 the voice uses «تبدت». At 8 the obscure «أرِبت» is understood here as desiring to avert war. Al-Bijawi glosses it as having an «إربة», a need or object. At 9.1 the performance gives «الحرب». Al-Asad and al-Bijawi print «الموت». Al-Bijawi reports «الحرب» in the Amiri printing. At 11.1 «فضلها» is the surplus length of the linked armour that covers the fingertips. Ibn Sallam and al-Qurashi give «ريعها». [Al-Asad, pp. 77–81](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n77/mode/2up) · [Al-Bijawi, p. 508, notes 11–12](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n507/mode/1up) · [Mail and gloss, p. 509](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n508/mode/1up) · [Ibn Sallam, 1/229](https://shamela.ws/book/6738/224)

At 12.1 al-Nahari recites «أتت عُصَبٌ من الأوس تخطر بالقنا». The diwan's main text names particular contingents. Al-Bijawi prints that list as no. 11, then a parallel list as no. 13. Farther on, no. 32 reads «أتت عصبة للأوس تخطر بالقنا / كمشي الأسد في رشاش الأهاضب». Note 47 records plural «عُصَب» in أ and ج. Note 48 reports that ع omits the bayt. Al-Asad also gives the Aws verse among the supplementary pieces on p. 227. The spoken plural fits the word scores, and widened Audar gives «من الأوس». At 13 the recording and al-Asad give «إلى الموت … إليه». Al-Bijawi prints «إلى الحرب … إليه» and the electronic text has «إليها». The word comparison favours «إليها» by only 1.17. Isolated Cohere gives «إليه» and «الموت» supplies its masculine antecedent. At 16.2 and 17.1 the voice twice uses «بيضها». Al-Asad and al-Bijawi print «بيضنا», with «بيضها» recorded from م at the first occurrence. [Diwan text](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n79/mode/2up) · [Supplement, p. 227](https://shamela.ws/book/149873/143) · [Al-Bijawi, p. 509](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n508/mode/1up) · [Helmets, pp. 510–511](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n509/mode/2up) · [Aws and the plural witnesses, p. 513](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n512/mode/1up)

At 21.1 «وأضربهم يوم الحديقة حاسراً» gives the recorded verb and the place named by al-Asad, whose text begins «أجالدهم». Al-Bijawi prints «لقيتهم يوم الخنادق» and credits «لقيتكم» to the Amiri printing in note 40. At 26.1 the passing host is «شهباء», grey-white. The *Jamhara* has «بيضاء». At 27.1 the printed diwan and studio card contract the preposition to «مِ الأغرّ». The searchable diwan expands it to «من». At 36.1 the voice and the *Jamhara* give «خرّ منهم». Al-Asad prints «جُرّ منكم». The following half speaks of people driven along like livestock. [Al-Asad, p. 88](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n85/mode/1up) · [p. 91, including 27](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n88/mode/1up) · [p. 95](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n92/mode/1up) · [Al-Bijawi, p. 512 and note 40](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n511/mode/1up) · [Closing verses, p. 514](https://archive.org/details/20231218_20231218_0842/page/n513/mode/1up)

At 37.2 the one-word acoustic comparison favours printed «مَن» by 3.06. Both continuous transcriptions have «وما إن تركنا» and the card visible around 275 s writes «وما إن تركنا في بعاث بآيب». We retain the sung «إن» and explain the explicit relative «مَن» in the diwan and the *Jamhara*. The opposition is between those who returned and those left on the field. At 38.1 the conditional «ولو غبت عن قومي كفتني عشيرتي» occurs in al-Asad's p. 96 note 4, attributed to *Muntaha al-talab*. The main text «وغُيّبت عن يوم كَنَتني عشيرتي» and its old gloss say that Qays was in fact absent. His presence cannot be inferred from the first-person battle language alone. [Studio text, 275 s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7cnM_yCpQLc&t=275s) · [Al-Asad, p. 96 and apparatus](https://archive.org/details/diwan-qayes/page/n93/mode/1up)

## Metre and rhyme

Tawil. The rawiy is ب, with the sustained kasra of المذاهبِ، راكبِ، فنضاربِ. The repeated first sadr has the same four feet both times. The word «خُطانا» in 20.2 supplies the full فعولن. Foot divisions, including across word boundaries, follow below.
