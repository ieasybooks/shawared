# النَّملَةُ وَالمُقَطَّمُ

## Recording and extent

Alqimmah Studio published «حكايات أحمد شوقي» on 3 March 2023, crediting Osama Al-Waaedh with recitation and editing. The source lasts 966.844 seconds. Chapter 9 keeps **833.40–887.98 seconds of the original compilation** in one continuous cut. The source voice measurements place the first and last pitched sound of this chapter at approximately 833.94 and 886.50 seconds. The cut admits the entry and the decay of the final rhyme. [Compilation, chapter 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r6VpT0Lid0E&t=833s)

All twelve couplets occur once, giving 24 performed halves. The late aphorism «صاح لا تخش عظيماً» follows the ant’s own speech without an inserted narrator’s explanation. The studio card at 858 seconds places the running ant, her eyes and the height on separate visual rows; it is not an additional verse. The voice passes directly from the last m of «أعظم» into the decay before the lark chapter.

## Printed text and interpretation

- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, part IV, «الحكايات», Dar al-Awda, Beirut, 1988. The scanned leaves carry the original printed page numbers. [p. 148](https://archive.org/details/BBib-Alex-02915/page/n343/mode/1up)
- Ahmad Shawqi, *al-Shawqiyyat*, «الحكايات», Hindawi Foundation for Education and Culture, Cairo, deposit 19938/2012, ISBN 978-977-719-166-1. The page images and the electronic chapter were compared. [p. 883](https://archive.org/details/alshawkeyat/page/n883/mode/1up) · [Electronic text](https://www.safahat.org/books/70706142/143/)

The first edition of *al-Shawqiyyat* (part I, Cairo, Matbaʿat al-Adab wa-l-Muʾayyad, 1898) provides an earlier text. Cairo sets the two feet on either side of the central space even when it divides a word. On p. 176 the suffix «ها» in «وعيناها» starts the second half of verse 6, and «قِلُ» completes «فالعاقل» in 11. The catchword «صاح» at the foot of the page anticipates the single closing address on p. 177. [p. 176](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA176) · [p. 177](https://books.google.com/books?id=QIyl-Nj9R6UC&pg=RA1-PA177)

The Beirut and Hindawi pages agree through the final apostrophe. The name «المقطّم» brings a Cairo landmark into the fable. A human span provides the scale of the water while the noun «اليمّ» gives its scale to the ant. The ant has not seen the mountain move at all. The pronouns in 9 leave the reader to weigh her opposed wishes, while the turn to «صاحِ» in 12 puts the final counsel outside her quoted speech.

The two printings transmit the same diwan tradition. The page reproductions and the word-by-word output from `scripts/collate.py` are in `media/research/al-namla-wal-muqattam/`. The studio captions are performance evidence. The glosses and bilingual commentary are editorial close readings of the cited text.

## Metre and rhyme

The two feet in each half give short ramal. At 1, *kā-na-tin-nam | la-tu-tam-shī* scans **– ⏑ – – | ⏑ ⏑ – –**. *Mar-ra-tan-taḥ | tal-mu-qaṭ-ṭam* answers **– ⏑ – – | – ⏑ – –**. In 7, *sa-qa-ṭat-fī | shib-ri-mā-ʾin* gives **⏑ ⏑ – – | – ⏑ – –**, and *hu-wa-ʿin-dan | nam-li-kal-yamm* gives the same sequence. The shortened opening is khabn of فاعلاتن. Mīm is the rawiy in المقطّم/المعظّم/أعدم and throughout the second halves.

## Readings against the performance

In 6 and 11 the caesura falls inside a word (تدوير). The text follows the breaks «وعينا / ها» and «فالعا / قِلُ», visible already on p. 176 of Shawqi’s 1898 printing and again on Beirut p. 148 and Hindawi p. 883. In 6, *rī-wa-ʿay-nā* fills the second foot of the first half. The possessive *hā* opens the next foot. In 11, *lam-tu-fal-ʿā* fills the corresponding foot, and *qi-lu-man-khā* opens the next half. The word cards explain «وعيناها» and «فالعاقل» across the breaks.

In 5 the ant asks how she might «أَسلَم», come through safe. In 11 Beirut and Hindawi point the doubled lām in «سلّمتُ» and «فسلّم», yielding to what she cannot control. A free phoneme trace of the recording gives successive l sounds at 878.21 and 878.29 seconds in the first verb and at 881.13 and 881.29 in the second. The same root carries her wish to survive into her wish that she had yielded.
