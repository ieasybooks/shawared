# غير مجد في ملتي واعتقادي

## Recording, attribution and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed recites all **64 bayts**, once each, in the order of the two complete editions below. At 19.85–27.26 s of the source he speaks the attribution and says that al-Ma'arri is mourning a Hanafi jurist known as Abu Hamza. The keep is **28.72–494.36 s**. The first aligned word begins at 0.52 s of the cut audio. The numbered cards, continuous transcriptions by both Cohere and Audar, and the isolated half-lines were compared with the printed text. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=feT-tT6ZmJU)

Ibn al-Adim identifies **al-Hasan ibn Abi Husayn**, known as Abu Hamza, as a Hanafi jurist, transmitter of hadith and judge of Manbij, and quotes the opening of this very elegy. Taqi al-Din al-Tamimi gives his death as before 400 AH and names his brother Muhassin. These details agree with the rubric, Abu Hamza at 23, the transmission of hadith at 27, and the brother and nephews at 57–58. Al-Zirikli reports Yaqut's association of the elegy with a different man, the Baghdadi poet Abu al-Khattab al-Jubballi, and questions it in his note. Al-Jubballi exchanged other verses with al-Ma'arri. The dedication here follows the jurist's biographical entry. [Ibn al-Adim, *Bughyat al-talab*, X, p. 197](https://shamela.ws/book/1160/5650) · [Al-Tamimi, III, pp. 68–69](https://shamela.ws/book/322/794) · [Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, VI, p. 275](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/5777)

The paired English translation and both sets of explanations follow the commentaries on these bayts. At **23–27**, «قصد الدهر من أبي حمزة» takes the accusative portraits of the jurist, orator and transmitter, with the schools' dispute intervening at 25. Al-Tabrizi understands the reduction in disagreement as clarity achieved by the jurist. Al-Batalyawsi sees the Iraqis deprived of their advocate. In 27 «المعروفُ مِن صدقه» is the delayed subject of «يُحوِج», and the word المعروف itself crosses the caesura. In 29 «غُروب» can be the tips of the pens and the buckets one dips into a well. [*Shuruh*, III, p. 985 onward](https://archive.org/details/4035pdf/page/n15/mode/1up)

At **36** «الحزينَ» is the object and «جوى الحزن» the subject. At **37–41** the horses and the body on the throne appear in Qur'an 38, verses 31–34. Missing the prayer and striking the horses belong to the interpretation adopted in the poem. The wind rearing Solomon's son and returning him dead are details of an exegetical story, to which al-Batalyawsi objects. The distinction matters to the translations. At **44**, the first «معاد» means an ordinary return, the second the Resurrection. [*Shuruh*, III, p. 991 onward](https://archive.org/details/4035pdf/page/n21/mode/1up) · [Qur'an 38.31–34](https://quran.com/38/31-34)

At **45** the compact edition glosses هجد as «سهر». Al-Tabrizi says the attendants fell asleep once recovery was hopeless. Al-Batalyawsi glosses الهجود as sleep, and al-Khwarazmi spells out the inversion. Those who kept watch beside the sick man have at last fallen asleep. The translation follows this reading. [*Shuruh*, III, p. 995](https://archive.org/details/4035pdf/page/n25/mode/1up) · [Beirut, p. 11](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n10/mode/1up)

At **49–53**, the first companion is the friend's youth. «فاذهبا» sends both away and «ومراثٍ» is coordinated with the rain for which both are «حقيقين». At **57–58**, the wish for consolation extends from al-Muhassin to his brother's sons, «جرائحَ الأكباد» describing their condition. At **63–64**, al-Batalyawsi discusses life imparted to the body by the soul. Al-Khwarazmi discusses the living body raised again from remains. The compact Beirut note on p. 12 gives Adam made from earth as the example. Al-Zirikli's biography supplies the new poet profile's name, dates, childhood blindness, stay in Baghdad and practice of dictation. [*Shuruh*, III, p. 998 onward](https://archive.org/details/4035pdf/page/n28/mode/1up) · [Beirut, p. 12](https://archive.org/details/3487pdf_202001/page/n11/mode/1up) · [Al-Zirikli, I, p. 157](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/406) · [Continuation](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/407)

## Meter and divided words

The rubric specifies **al-khafif**. The rhyme is dal with a preceding long alif and a lengthened final kasra. At 1.1 «غير مجد في ملّتي واعتقادي» gives *ghay ru muj din | fī mil la tī | waʿ ti qā dī*, **– ⏑ – – | – – ⏑ – | – ⏑ – –**. At 1.2 «نوح باك ولا ترنّم شاد» gives *naw ḥu bā kin | wa lā ta ran | nu mu shā dī*, **– ⏑ – – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | ⏑ ⏑ – –**. Khabn shortens the middle and last feet of the second example. At 35.1, read in liaison, *ʾa sa fun ghay | ru nā fi ʿin | waj ti hā dun* gives **⏑ ⏑ – – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | – ⏑ – –**. The answer at 35.2, *lā yu ʾad dī | ʾi lā gha nā | ʾij ti hā dī*, gives **– ⏑ – – | ⏑ – ⏑ – | – ⏑ – –**.

Tadwir puts the caesura inside a word. The typographic fragments «قي / سَ», «غن / نَت», «للنُّع / مانِ» and «المَع / روفُ» must be read across it. At 31, 56 and 61 the article is on one side and the doubled sun consonant on the other. At 18 the two n sounds of «فأنتنّ» fall on opposite sides. Reassigning a whole word to either half would alter which syllables belong to the meter.

At 10–11 the two stars are Kochab and Pherkad in Ursa Minor. The Arabic pair al-Farqadan appears separately from al-Judayy (Polaris) in the navigators’ terminology. Both remain in the sky as one traveller after another passes beneath them. [Juan Acevedo and Inês Bénard, *The Stars of Indian Ocean Arab Navigation*, p. 21, Ursa Minor, entries 1–4](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.14758605)

At 37 the poem draws on one interpretation of Qur’an 38:33. Al-Tabari transmits the striking of the horses from several early authorities. He himself prefers the interpretation reported from Ibn Abbas that Solomon stroked their manes and hocks out of affection. The commentary attributes the first interpretation to the poem. [Al-Tabari, *Jamiʿ al-bayan*, commentary on 38:33](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura38-aya33.html)
