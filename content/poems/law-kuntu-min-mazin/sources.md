# لَو كُنتُ مِن مازِنٍ لَم تَستَبِح إِبِلي

## Recording and extent

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed recites Qurayt ibn Unayf on موقع الشنكبوتية, uploaded 3 June 2014. The original is 113.778 s. The cut keeps 45.25–98.30 s, a single 53.05 s passage. The spoken account of the loss of thirty camels and the recovery of a hundred ends before «لو كنت». The last sustained word is «وركبانا». The source-voice measurements place the opening at 45.76 s and the end of the last voiced group at 97.67 s. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DH1TD_5kxj0)

The eight recorded bayts are the eight of the opening selection of Abu Tammam’s *Hamasa*, in sequence, each once. The later poem returned by the incipit search quotes Qurayt. Its surrounding verses have no place in this performance.

## Witnesses and interpretation

- Al-Marzuqi, *Sharh Diwan al-Hamasa*, opening of باب الحماسة, pp. 20–26. The heading identifies a poet of Banu al-Anbar. Al-Marzuqi explains how a poet can praise a related clan and still be trying to rouse his own. At 5 the concession about numbers removes the tribe’s excuse. At 6–7 the speaker professes admiration for a piety which has cost him his herd. The commentary also quotes the ending early in its discussion. The collation puts the quoted bayt at 8. [Opening and heading](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/2) · [Close](https://shamela.ws/book/26536/8)
- Al-Khatib al-Tibrizi, *Sharh Diwan al-Hamasa*, I, pp. 3–5, names Qurayt and gives Abu Ubayda’s account of Banu Shayban, the thirty animals and Mazin’s intervention. On p. 4 it preserves Abu al-Nada’s alternative name for the raiders’ ancestress. On p. 5 it distinguishes horsemen from camel riders and gives both verbs for the raid. These glosses inform the two translations and word notes. [Occasion](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/2) · [Name](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/3) · [Closing verse](https://shamela.ws/book/6907/4)
- Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, V, p. 195, calls Qurayt a pre-Islamic Tamimi and remarks on how little is known of his life. The introduction in the Tibrizi edition calls him an Islamic poet. The profile gives both assessments. Neither source supplies dates. [Biographical entry](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4542)

The companion research directory contains the electronic pages, the eight verse lines extracted from each commentary, the output of `scripts/collate.py`, and the scores. Both commentary texts have «اللقيطة» in the main line. Abu al-Nada’s reading identifies al-Shaqiqa as the daughter of Abbad ibn Yazid of Dhuhl. Al-Tibrizi’s note identifies al-Laqita with the mother of Hisn of Fazara. The recording and its card give «الشقيقة». The equal-span acoustic scores are −28.20 for الشقيقة and −34.55 for اللقيطة. At 8.2 «شنّوا» scores −18.05 against −22.04 for «شدّوا». Al-Marzuqi uses شنوا and al-Tibrizi records it beside his main شدوا.

Basit, with nun as *rawiy* and the final alif its release. In the scans below, a dash marks a long syllable and u a short one. Each group is a foot.

At 1.2 the initial بَنُشْشَقيـ gives the shortened opening foot. The heavy last foot in «بانا» and «هانا» persists when the first hemistich ends in the lighter فَعِلُن.

## Recording verification

All 16 halves were checked by the repository verifier. **2.2** was its only differing half. It returned «من ذو لوثة لا نام», also listing «نام» as an extra word at the end. The widened Audar clip restores «إن» and draws the long cadence back into «لانا». The full take, the conditional construction in both commentaries, and the uploader’s card agree on «إن ذو لوثة لانا». The score for إن is −10.03 against −12.42 for من. There is one verb ending in the rhyme, with no further word before «قوم». The onset of the next half is separately aligned. This review uses the full-take and widened transcripts, the original card and the word alignment.
