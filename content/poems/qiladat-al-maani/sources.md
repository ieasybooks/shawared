# قلادة المعاني

## Recording and edition

- Osama Al-Waaedh, Alqimmah Studio, 26 May 2025. The description names Fawwaz ibn Hayif ibn Jurays al-Dawsari and links his own vocalised PDF, *Qiladat al-ma'ani, nazm kitab al-Zanjani*. The author dates its introduction 15 Shawwal 1446 AH, 13 April 2025. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uCW3G9kzAHY) and [author’s booklet](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1XUje6mE67rKKm2WMIN-wdD9pxYpmnnCl/view)
- The introduction, pp. 3–5, explains the choice to arrange rules by practical chapters rather than by the usual headings of legal theory. It also says that قيل generally signals the Hanafi position in this author’s comparison, without a judgement on its merit. The numbered verse occupies pp. 6–13. The printed opening has no. 1, the closing no. 106. On p. 11 a complete additional couplet in a footnote to no. 77 gives the poet’s own position on divine creation and human agency. The reciter sings it immediately there. Therefore the complete edition comprises 107 actual couplets. App 78 is the footnote, and app 79–107 correspond to print 78–106. Osama repeats the final couplet once. No numbered couplet was omitted.
- The complete audio was first transcribed in context. Eleven spoken chapter rubrics intervene before print 4, 14, 21, 23, 30, 31, 50, 57, 76, 86 and 94. The reader was not reciting the intervening smaller printed subheadings. Each spoken rubric, the verse ending immediately before and the verse opening immediately after were locally word-aligned. The cuts sit in the adjacent gaps. Retained source intervals in seconds were 3.37–25.80, 28.08–100.47, 102.73–152.66, 155.31–169.81, 172.12–221.70, 223.90–231.62, 233.88–364.97, 367.13–414.95, 417.30–545.68, 548.07–622.96, 627.22–681.19 and 683.54–780.55.
- The verse follows the author’s images and the recorded sequence. In 7 and 53 قيل crosses the division of the halves. The words حقيقة and ابتداء do likewise at 10 and 11. These are the booklet's metrical divisions. The metre is paired rajaz, with changing rhyme even though the poet also carries sense or rhyme across many halves. In 1.2 عَلَّمَنا مآخذَ الأحكامِ scans – ⏑ ⏑ – / ⏑ – ⏑ – / – – –. At 50.1 وَالعينُ لا تُملَكُ بالضمانِ, the feet are – – ⏑ – / – ⏑ ⏑ – / ⏑ – –.

## Sources for the explanations

- Abu al-Manaqib Mahmud ibn Ahmad al-Zanjani, *Takhrij al-furu' ala al-usul*, ed. Muhammad Adib Salih, 2nd ed., Mu'assasat al-Risala, Beirut, 1398 AH (1978 CE). The second witness here is the underlying prose book. It supplies the meanings, legal examples and positions, independently of the modern poet’s compression. No independent edited text of the 2025 poem was identified. The Arabic book and the author’s scanned pages were kept in `media/research/qiladat-al-maani/`. [Edition record and searchable text](https://app.turath.io/book/6298) and [catalogue of the individual questions](https://catalogue.univ-mosta.dz/fss/index.php?id=15000&lvl=notice_display)
- Print 4–13 track the questions in the chapters on purification and dry ablution, including the partitive مِن, wiping, isolated transmission and previous consensus. At 18–19 the second couplet completes al-Zanjani’s discussion of the follower’s prayer. The two together concern the relation between the imam’s and follower’s validity. At 22 the zakat of a child’s property illustrates the difference between a financial claim and a personal obligation to worship.
- Print 31–56 follow the book on sales, usury, pledges, agency, wrongful appropriation and leases. At 37 the poem describes the relevant food as stored («طعام مدخر»). Al-Zanjani’s actual statement in the second question on usury is «الطعم لا غير». The translation preserves the additional qualification in the verse and the commentary explains the difference. At 46 the printed spelling «مُثمَن» is retained in the sense of the priced object as distinct from the price.
- Print 57–75 follow the questions in the chapters on marriage, dower, divorce, resumption and maintenance. Print 76–85 follow the chapter on injuries, including questions about what general wording proves and when analogy can qualify scripture. In 77 and the footnote the poem’s claims about creation and human choice belong to its own theological account. Print 86–103 follow the chapters on penalties, expeditions, oaths, judgements, testimony and emancipation. The Arabic and English commentaries describe these as the positions presented by the two authors at their respective dates.
- The two dated booklets give the poet’s name and work. His author listing for *al-Nafahat al-atira* and *Wamadat fi kashf al-mughalatat* supports the biographical notice concerning logic and disputation. [Author’s bibliography](https://www.noor-book.com/u/فواز-الدوسري/books)

## Audio verification

- The clip verifier transcribed all 216 halves, including both performances of 107. It flagged 14 clips, covering 24 of 979 words. Every flagged half was also transcribed by Audar with a wider margin and checked against the booklet, the continuous transcript and the word alignment. Scores below compare whole halves against the same acoustic window. Higher scores are better.
- At 3.1 the wide clip has إلى أب المناقب and the continuous reading has أبي. Al-Zanjani’s attested kunyah follows the preposition إلى, requiring أبي. Scores for أبي and أب were −47.8 and −43.9. At 8.2 both recognisers repeatedly wrote فدر for the printed قدر (−34.1 and −41.6 respectively). The legal question concerns the amount an injunction requires. The sound resembling fa is recorded on the word card.
- At 7.1 and 53.1 the recognisers completed the split قيل. The lam has its own alignment at 48.00 and 371.58 seconds. At 10.1 and 10.2 the software distributed حقيقة differently across the two clips. The second syllable group at 69.50–69.78 fits the printed division. At 11.2 indiscriminate isolated transcription produced a different sentence, where the continuous reading recovered غاية فاتبع منارات الهدى. The last syllable of ابتداء is separately aligned at 76.82, غاية at 77.02 and منارات at 78.60. The respective word confidences were 0.28, 0.95 and 0.80. The singular منارة and plural منارات scored equally (−49.1).
- At 24.2 the short clip gave no usable sentence. The wide one recovered عن بعضه وقيل عنه and heard فلتفتح, which scored −42.1 against −48.4 for فالتفت. The uncut context gives the author’s imperative فالتفت, “pay attention”, with no additional ha (ح). In التفت the ta immediately after lam is ungeminated. The house normaliser mistakes its radical lam for the article and would double the following ta. The same root-letter distinction gives اِلتباس at 79.2.
- At 45.2 the short clip omitted the final pronoun of رجّحه and the wide clip lengthened the ending into رجّحا. The scores were −41.7 for رجّحه and −38.7 for رجّحا. The complete recitation and the grammatical object both support the booklet. At 55.1 the wide clip restored the apparent final h of سيده which the short clip lost. سيده and سيداً scored −57.8 and −61.7.
- At 71.2 the two recognisers heard an n at the start of آيه. Printed p. 11 shows the preceding word as قطعٍ with tanwin. Its n is sounded in liaison with آيه; the written division scored −36.0 against −38.8 for قطعِ نايه. At 73.2 the wide and short clips heard شرعاً. The complete transcript has the printed شرعه. The candidate scores of −38.6 for شرعه and −39.1 for شرعاً are inconclusive. The booklet has the noun شِرعة in rhyme with الرجعة.
- At 93.1 the short clip split قدّم ما as قد ما. The wide clip joined the m sounds, as in the printed imperative, while قد scored −34.3 against −38.4 for قدّم. The ensuing juxtaposition of two attributes calls for the instruction to give priority to one. At 106.1 the second recogniser recovered the two n sounds of فامنن, “grant”, as printed. It scored −47.8 against −51.6 for فآمن.
- The extra و noted at the left edges of 75.1 and 88.1 is the conjunction already attached to الحكم at both places. The recorded starts of the complete words are 516.82 and 605.96 seconds. At 98.2 the printed form المولى repeats the rhyme of 98.1. The reading المَولى scored −36.0, against −38.1 for المُولى and −42.4 for المُولي. The first half uses mawla for the Lord and the second for the human dependent whose action the jurists discuss. Al-Zanjani’s first question on oaths expressly distinguishes God's right from the character of the action.
