## Attribution

Every source read gives the poem to al-Khansa': Tha'lab's recension, the Beirut printing, al-Aghani, al-Mubarrad's *al-Ta'azi* and *al-Kamil*, Ibn Abd Rabbih's *al-Iqd*. Whom it mourns is disputed:

- **Mu'awiya ibn Amr.** Tha'lab's heading (p. 78): «وقالت لمعاوية أخيها، وقتله بنو مرّة على غدير قَلَهى», and his commentary names Mu'awiya in its glosses of bayt 2 and of his 13, our 23 («تريد أنّ معاوية كان ثقيلاً على الأرض»; «تقول: ما كان «معاوية» أدنى لها ممّن جناها»). Al-Aghani 15/57 quotes five of its abyat after Sakhr's burial at Asib, then: «قال السُّلَميّ: ليست هذه في صخر، هذه إنّما رثت بها معاويةَ أخاها، وبنو مُرّة قتلته». The poem itself, bayt 18: «فإن تكُ مُرّةُ أودت به»; and al-Bakri (*Mu'jam ma Ista'jam*, p. 1194, quoted in Abu Suwaylim's note, p. 82 n. 1) puts al-Mahw of bayt 11 in the land of Murra, where Hashim and Durayd, sons of Harmala, killed Mu'awiya. Al-Aghani 15/62–63 tells how they killed him when he raided Murra and Fazara.
- **Sakhr ibn Amr.** Abu Ubayda, as Abu Suwaylim reports him (p. 78 n. 5: «وزعم أبو عبيدة أنها قالت هذا الشعر في أخيها صخر لمّا دُفن بأرض بني سُليم عند جبل عسيب»); al-Aghani's placing of the five abyat; the Beirut printing's heading («قالت ترثي أخاها صخراً، لما مات ودفن في جبل عسيب … وقيل بل قالت هذا في أخيها معاوية لما قتله بنو مرة»); al-Mubarrad, *al-Ta'azi* p. 121 («وقالت أيضاً ترثي صخراً»); *al-Iqd* 3/224, whose second bayt reads «أمن بعد صخرٍ من آل الشريد». Abu Suwaylim adds (p. 107 n. 1) that bayt 15 «يزيدنا ثقة بأنّ هذه القصيدة قيلت في صخر؛ لأنه كان شاعراً».
- **Abyat given to others:** Abu Ubayda gave bayt 2 to Mayya bint Dirar al-Dabbiyya mourning her brother Qabisa (Abu Suwaylim p. 79, the note on that bayt, from al-Aghani, Dar al-Kutub, 13/143); bayt 11 is hers in Berlin 1 and, as a report, in al-Bakri (Abu Suwaylim pp. 82 n. 1, 83 n. 3); Ibn al-A'rabi gave bayt 13 to Amir ibn Juwayn al-Ta'i (Tha'lab p. 104); Abu Suwaylim's notes give al-Asma'i's version of 26 with a following bayt that some ascribe to Amir ibn Juwayn too (p. 93 n. 7).

The intro and sharh follow Tha'lab and al-Sulami (Mu'awiya), and give Abu Ubayda's view and al-Aghani's placing. The title is the first sadr: no source names the poem; «إما عليها وإما لها» is only the Beirut printing's heading.

## Editions

- **Tha'lab's recension:** ديوان الخنساء، شرح ثعلب أبي العباس أحمد بن يحيى، تحقيق أنور أبو سويلم (Dar Ammar, Amman, 1st ed. 1409/1988, with Mu'ta University): archive.org `KnSSa_TH` (the same PDF as `3318pdf_202001`), poem 4, pp. 78–109 (leaves 77–108; leaf = page − 1), read as page images (`thalab.md`, with the commentary taken down page by page). Voweled, 38 abyat, with Abu Suwaylim's apparatus: the Berlin manuscripts of Ibn al-Sikkit's recension (برلين ١، ٢), the Dar al-Kutub manuscript with al-Asimi's and al-Kirmani's marginalia (دار), بغ, and the printed «أنيس» (Cheikho, *Anis al-Julasa*), «م أنيس», «كرم» and «الحوفي». The recited text departs from it often (table below). Nine of its abyat are not in the Beirut printing, so not recited: 9 «فنفسي الفداء له من فقيد», 17–19, 30, 33, 34, 36 and 37 (Abu Suwaylim notes 17, 30, 34, 36 and 37 as missing from Karam and al-Hawfi, 17 also from Ibn al-Sikkit's recension, and «تقدّ الذؤابة» standing in Karam and al-Hawfi where Tha'lab has 9). Two recited abyat are not in Tha'lab's text: 8, which al-Aghani has, and 19, which Abu Suwaylim gives from Ibn al-Sikkit's recension, Karam and al-Hawfi (p. 109 n. 3).
- **The Beirut printing:** ديوان الخنساء (Dar Sadir, Beirut, n.d.; no editor named): archive.org `AAlexandrina-109874`, leaves 123–126, and the sharper scan of the same printing `diwan-al-khansa`, leaves 119–122; pp. 120–123, read as page images (`beirut.md`). Voweled, 31 abyat with short notes, headed «إما عليها وإما لها». Its pages and readings are the ones Abu Suwaylim cites as «كرم» («كرم: ص ١٢٠», «كرم: ص ١٢٢»), Karam al-Bustani's edition. **The reciter follows it**: its 31 abyat, its order, its wording, except 5.2 «يُجاري» (it prints «يُجازي») and the vowels discussed below.
- **al-Aghani:** Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani, *Kitab al-Aghani*, ed. Ihsan Abbas, Ibrahim al-Sa'afin and Bakr Abbas (Dar Sadir, Beirut, 3rd printing 1429/2008), vol. 15: archive.org `al-aghani-15`, section 282 «نسب الخنساء وخبرها وخبر مقتل أخويها صخر ومعاوية», pp. 54–63 (leaves 52–61), read as page images (`aghani.md`). Abyat 1, 2, 18, 7 and 8 at p. 57, voweled, with «لقد» in 1.2, «سأحمل نفسي على خُطّةٍ», and «غنّى فيه ابن سُرَيج خفيفَ رملٍ بالبنصر».
- **al-Mubarrad, *al-Ta'azi wa-l-Marathi*:** shamela 21554/83–84, pp. 121–122 (`taazi.txt`): 15 abyat with glosses, «ترثي صخراً».
- **al-Mubarrad, *al-Kamil*:** shamela 8505/1210, vol. 4, p. 43 (`kamil.txt`): six abyat with glosses («لعمر أبيه لنعم الفتى / إذا النفس أعجبها ما لها»).
- **Ibn Abd Rabbih, *al-Iqd al-Farid*:** shamela 23789/957, vol. 3, p. 224 (`iqd.txt`): five abyat («ألا ما لعيني ألا ما لها», «أمن بعد صخر», «فآليت آسى … وأسأل باكية», «وهمّت بنفسي كلّ الهموم», «سأحمل نفسي على خطّة»).
- **The Abu Dhabi encyclopedia text** (old shamela 4394, pp. 102–104, `encyclopedia.txt`): the Beirut printing's 31 abyat and order, with its own slips («الصُّيا» for الصياح, «تَطاعِنُها»); not an independent witness. It prints «بَيْنَهُ» in 24.1.

`abyat_in_full` is 31, the Beirut printing's count (Tha'lab has 38). The voweled text was set from the Beirut printing and Tha'lab and put in the house style (`scripts/tashkeel.ts --fix` dropped the kasra before the long ya of «العين»).

## Meter

Mutaqarib (فعولن ×4), rawiy ل (لَها throughout); the opening bayt is muṣarraʿ, its sadr ending on the rhyme («ما لَها»). `scan.py` passes 51 of 62 halves; the 11 it flags were scanned by hand:

- **2.1** «مِنَ آلِ»: the hamza of آل is dropped and its fatha moved to the nun (naql), «مِنالِ», as Tha'lab prints «مِنَ آلِ» and cohere hears «منال»: ʾa-baʿ-dab | ni-ʿam-rin | mi-nā-lish | sha-rī-di, u-- u-- u-- u-u.
- **Khurm at the head of an ajuz** (الخرم: the first letter of the first foot dropped; فعولن → عولن, the athlam, or فعولُ → عولُ, the athram), an old licence of the mutaqarib: 2.2 «حَلْ-لَت», 9.2 «يَو-مَل», 13.2 «تَر-مِس», 14.2 «نا-زَل», 15.2 «تَب-قى», 28.2 «غا-دَر» (athlam), 10.2 «با-لِ», 26.2 «تَك-شِ», 31.2 «آ-نَ» (athram). Most of these abyat are printed by Tha'lab as mudawwar, the word at the end of the sadr split across the halves («النفو/سِ», «الرجا/لِ», «الصبيـ/ـرِ», «السنا/نِ», «الصبا/حِ», «الإرا/خِ»), which removes the khurm; the house text keeps whole words in the sadr. 14.2, 28.2 and 2.2 (unless split «الشريـ/ـدِ» as in al-Aghani) have khurm in every layout. The audio confirms 26.2's «تَكشِفُ» against the regular «تُكَشِّفُ» (−13.4).
- **11.2** «المُغادَرِ»: the bayt is mudawwar (Tha'lab marks it «م»); the article's lam closes the sadr («الفتى ل» = فَتَل), so the ajuz scans mu-ghā-da | ri-bil-maḥ | wi-ʾadh-lā | la-hā.
- 6.1 «بِنَفسِيَ» and 6.2 «لِنَفسِيَ» take the fatha on the ya, as the Beirut printing prints it, without which neither half scans.
- 16.1 «يَذبُلٍ»: the mountain's name is a diptote (a name on the pattern of a verb), given tanwin as both editions print it; without the tanwin the sadr ends short (bu-la for bu-lin).

## Commentary drawn on

- **The sharh and notes** follow Tha'lab's commentary (with the glosses he reports from Abu Amr, Abu Ubayda, al-Asma'i, Ibn al-A'rabi, Ya'qub ibn al-Sikkit, al-Athram, Zaida, Mubtakir al-Tha'labi, Abus, Arram al-Sulami and al-Kirmani) and Abu Suwaylim's notes, with the Beirut printing's notes where it reads differently (1 سربال، 5 ذليق and المقارض, 12 بيض, 21 الحواضن and أحمال, 23 غالها, 27 المعملة and قاعداً and الأغفال, 28, 31) and al-Mubarrad's glosses (*al-Ta'azi*: حلت, تحش and أجذال, أتان الثميل, الخل; *al-Kamil*: الشوامخ, آلة, أولى لي).
- **Where the commentators are silent** and the sharh says so: 10.2 «حيث يُحلى لها» (*Lisan* vol. 14, p. 192: «وحَلِيَ بقلبي وعيني يَحلى … إذا أعجبك»), 12.2 «أمثالَها» (taken as specifying the doubled rings, after al-Asma'i's «التي تُنسج حلقتين حلقتين»), 29.2 «أُكلاً».
- ***Lisan al-Arab*** (shamela 1687, `lisan/`): قرض (vol. 7, p. 217: «القَرْضُ … ما يتجازى به الناس بينهم … من إحسان ومن إساءة»), جري (vol. 14, p. 141: «جاراه مجاراةً … أي جرى معه»), ألا (vol. 14, p. 40: «آلى يؤلي إيلاءً: حلف»), عمر (vol. 4, p. 601: «العَمْر والعُمُر والعُمْر: الحياة»), هون (vol. 13, p. 439), صبح (vol. 2, p. 505: «ويوم الصباح: يوم الغارة … لأنهم أكثر ما يغيرون عند الصباح»), غول (vol. 11, p. 507: «غاله الشيء غولاً … أهلكه وأخذه من حيث لم يدر»), أكل (vol. 11, p. 21), شبل (vol. 11, p. 352: «الشبل: ولد الأسد»), حلا (vol. 14, p. 192).
- **The occasion and the brothers' deaths:** al-Aghani 15/54 (her nasab, her name Tumadir, Durayd ibn al-Simma's proposal), 15/55–57 (Sakhr speared by Rabi'a ibn Thawr al-Asadi in a raid on Asad, dying of it years later, buried near Asib), 15/62–63 (Mu'awiya killed by Hashim or Durayd, sons of Harmala al-Murri). The Quran: 19.2 echoes al-Zalzala 99:1; 2.2's «أثقالها» as the dead, al-Zalzala 99:2, after al-Mubarrad.
- **The poet's life** (bio): al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, vol. 2, p. 86 (shamela 12286/1183–1184, `alam.txt`): name, Sulaym of Qays Aylan, Najd, most of her life before Islam, her conversion and visit to the Prophet with Sulaym («فكان رسول الله يستنشدها ويعجبه شعرها»), her elegies for Mu'awiya and Sakhr, her four sons at al-Qadisiyya in 16 AH, and her death in 24 AH (645; al-Zirikli prints «٦٤٥ هـ», a slip for م); Tha'lab's title page also gives «ت ٢٤هـ». Al-Aghani 15/54–63 for the rest.

Fixed in the review:

- **Text:** 1.1 «لِعَينِكِ» and 15.1 «مِثلِ» kept on a lean and recorded; 20.1 and 28.1 added to the table with their scores; 16.1's diptote «يَذبُلٍ» recorded as a licence.
- **Timeline:** 16.1/16.2 (above); 29.1 moved off the silence before it.
- **Meaning and sharh:** 2 now gives Tha'lab's preference for Abu Amr's reading; 22 says that Ibn Amr took the calamity on himself, not that he "sufficed" his people; 18 no longer says that al-Sulami argued from this bayt, only that he names Murra as the killers; 1 no longer asks a question; 20 deepened.
- **Notes:** the English sharh of 24 and 28 no longer repeat their notes; 21.2 «تُبين» glossed from the Beirut note, not from an unsourced derivation; 26.1 no longer calls the women «حرائر»; heads in the house style («حَلّى»).
- **Intro and bio:** the recited text is described as the Beirut printing's order, not its every word (5.2); her conversion is placed before her visit to the Prophet, as al-Zirikli has it; Ibn Surayj's setting added as the reason the poem was sung.
