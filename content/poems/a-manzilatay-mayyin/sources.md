# أَمَنزِلَتَي مَيٍّ سَلامٌ عَلَيكُما

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari, 18 April 2024. Keep **0.00–580.42 s** of the 581.869 s original. The first 0.458 s give the lead-in to the address. The closing rhyme «تسبح» runs into the final quiet from 579.768 s. The source cards and the two independent full-take transcriptions give **66 bayts once each**, in the sequence of poem 39 in al-Bahili. In 11 and 36 the poet supplies different openings for the same second half, «فياف لطرف العين فيهن مطرح». The distant speaker strains to see Mayy in 11. The desert defeats his claim to be drawing near in 36. Both are present on their own cards, at 93 and 312 s. The alignment divides at 290.36 s, in the pause before 34.1. The recording plays straight through this pause. [Recitation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ExzN5R95KN8)

- Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, V, p. 124, identifies Ghaylan ibn Uqba al-Adawi, Abu al-Harith, gives his years as 77–117 AH (696–735 CE), and names Mayya al-Minqariyya. He describes the desert poet’s visits to al-Yamama and Basra and records both Isfahan and the desert as traditions for his place of death. The tawil has ḥāʾ (ح) for its rhyme consonant, with damma. The opening «أَمَنزِ | لَتَي مَيٍّ | سَلامٌ | عَلَيكُما» gives فعولُ مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. At 33.2 «إِلى بَيـ | ـتِ مَيٍّ آ | خِرَ اللَّيـ | ـلِ طُلَّحُ» gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. In 26–28 the object of «تجلو» is held back. She polishes the teeth likened to the white heads of daisies. The original caesuras allow the arak twig, the evening dew and finally the teeth to appear in that order. ## Commentary and vocabulary

- Al-Bahili explains the opening rain by the gravel it turns over, and the woman’s two names occur side by side in these verses. His gloss of «لعهدها به» places the gazelle’s fawn exactly where she last saw it. Her approach and retreat show how she watches both the riders and her young. The sharh uses his identifications of the anklets, ivory, milkweed and the sand ridges packed by heavy rain. Under «المجن» he understands Mayy’s hanging sash. Macartney’s gloss describes a sash free to move at the waist and cites silver among its ornaments. [Rain](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1179) · [Gazelle](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1187) · [Jewellery and sash](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1190) · [Macartney, p. 82](https://archive.org/details/dwnshirdhalrumma00dhal/page/n87/mode/1up)
- Al-Bahili gives two explanations for the marks across the hard earth in 30. Al-Asma'i takes the subject to be fierce winds that fall short of her camp. An alternative takes it to be wild animals. At 31–32 the calls of ravens sound like the lament of bereaved Nubian women, and the staff borers give a physical meaning to the fear of parting. At 45–46 al-Bahili describes the mirage as silk that seems to split and then be stitched whole again over the hills. Macartney also records a comparison with the pattern of a sword blade. [Winds and ravens](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1195) · [Staff of parting](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1196) · [Mirage](https://shamela.ws/book/16545/1202) · [Macartney, p. 86](https://archive.org/details/dwnshirdhalrumma00dhal/page/n91/mode/1up)
- A parallel in the poet’s ayn ode repeats 42.1. Al-Bahili explicitly identifies Atlal there as the poet’s she-camel and «أزى الظل» as shadow contracting to the foot of the wall. The two ropes in 48 belong to a well bucket. At 52–58 al-Bahili identifies the smooth part of the camel’s neck behind the ear, the leather of the Yemeni sandal, the lower leg, the pale mark on the forehead of dawn, the call «عاج» to a she-camel, and a raised tail mimicking pregnancy. The comparison at 59 changes the animal in view to a scarred male wild ass. Its mares are the subject of «رعت» in 61, the eggs in 62 are ostrich eggs, and a single flock poised on the mares’ heads figures their stillness in 63. The first candidate comparisons used a whole-bayt window located by its studio card. Their times in the research file are **original source seconds**. The two full transcribers (Cohere and Audar) and the separately cut half supply independent checks. A second comparison uses just the aligned half in app seconds wherever the wider window was indecisive or disagreed with the transcripts. Each comparison keeps the surrounding words fixed.

## Verification of individual halves

The 132 isolated halves initially gave 29 flags. After clip ends were extended, 28 still differed from the reference supplied to the transcriber. The two full takes, widened Audar clips, studio cards and printed witnesses inform the following decisions.

| Half | Clip evidence and decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1.1, 5.2 | Both clip engines run the vocative «أمنزلتي ميٍّ» together. The dual address governs «عليكما» here and in 2. At 5 both hear لذاتي. The card and the glosses have لداتي, his age-mates, the people who have grown up at the same pace as the speaker. |
| 6.1, 6.2, 8.2, 9.1, 11.1 | The wide 6.1 restores «إذا غيّر النأي المحبين لم يكد». In 6.2, 8.2 and 9.1 both engines repeatedly break up Mayya’s name as من يت or ميتة. In the wide 11.1 Mayy’s vocative is distinct. The following أنّا introduces what she is asked to know. The cards distinguish مَيّ and مَيّة throughout. |
| 14.1, 16.1, 17.2 | The contracted verb at 14 is discussed above. Widening 16.1 restores أدماء, the doe’s pale colour, and widening 17.2 restores طلاً, her fawn. Those two readings agree with the cards and both commentaries. |
| 21.1, 22.1–2 | Wide 21.1 restores عيجت, curved or bent, and expands the unfamiliar البُرى into البراء. Wide 22.1 restores singular فوقه with the dune as antecedent. In 22.2 both engines divide «لبّدن الهذاليل نُضّح» at the wrong places. The three graphic words at 189 s and the gloss on p. 1201 distinguish the rainstorms, their action on the small ridges, and the abundance of their water. |
| 27.2, 28.1, 31.2, 32.1 | رامة is a place, whose final ة is heard as t in connected recitation before the following adjective, which led to رمت. هِجان describes the whiteness of the front teeth. Neither رجال nor إجى explains them. At 31 the wide engine restores مثاكيل and النوب but loses the rhyme نوّح. At 32 the initial short clip converted نيةٍ to نيتي. The longer ending and the 279 s card restore the noun. |
| 33.2, 34.1, 36.1 | The wide 33.2 gives «إلى بيت ميّ آخر الليل طُلّح» with unrelated material at the left edge. The 288 s card starts the half with إلى. In 34 the recognisers merge the imperative فمت and the vocative بعل ميّ. In 36 they merge ميّة with اغبرّ. The paired cards and al-Bahili distinguish the husband in 34 from the dusty wastes in 36. |
| 41.1, 43.2, 45.2, 46.2 | Both engines read وليقة for وديقة. The card at 357 s and al-Bahili p. 1211 give وديقة, oppressive midday heat. Wide 43.2 resolves فللموت, introducing the prospect of relief from love. At 45 the wide clip restores يمصح, the mirage carrying away sight. At 46 it separates ذرى قورها and gives ينصح, sewing split fabric back together, as al-Bahili explains. |
| 51.2, 52.2, 53.1, 55.2, 59.2 | The wide 51.2 restores Saydah’s name from صيد حور. In 52 the construct «مرآة الغريبة» accounts for the t attached to the bride’s mirror. At 53 both clips misdivide أحمّ الروق, the oryx with a dark horn. At 55 wide Audar restores أقرح but gives السماوات. The two forms tie in the isolated acoustic comparison. Al-Bahili explicitly glosses the singular السماوة as the visible outline of dawn, and the card at 480 s has the singular. In 59 both engines misplace the t of الموماة before the hamza of جأب, the robust wild ass. |

The additional left-edge strings «النبي» at 33.2, «والله» at 40.1 and «قال» at 53.2 have no counterpart on the respective cards or in al-Bahili. The openings are إلى, أبيت and كسبت. The refined clip ends at 4.1, 7.1, 9.1, 16.1, 17.1, 19.1, 32.1, 41.1 and 66.2 preserve the 132 consecutive halves.
