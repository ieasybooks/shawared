# حائِيَّةُ اِبنِ أَبي داوُدَ

## Recording and recension

Osama Al-Waaedh posted «حائية ابن أبي داود» on his own channel on 21 June 2019. He performs all 33 bayts of the recension printed by al-Ajurri and al-Dhahabi. The first bayt is repeated in full and the final half twice sung. Overlapping clips of the opening and close, followed by a check of all 69 half-verses, establish the sequence. The two occurrences of 1.1 begin at 0.48 and 11.50 seconds on the app clock, and those of 33.2 at 347.44 and 352.42. The second performance of 33.2 ends with a sustained final vowel. Its highlight extends to 357.30 seconds. The app retains 3.35–361.18 seconds of the 365.145-second source, removing opening silence and the end of the decay. The first clear signal follows 3.85 seconds and the trailing silence begins at 360.662. The resulting 357.83 seconds play at −20.1 LUFS integrated and −7.0 dBTP, with −5.6 dB gain. [Recording, displayed verse and uploader’s transcription](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gSty6-XZEJM)

- **Al-Ajurri, الشريعة**, vol. 5, pp. 2563–2565. He places himself among the listeners who took the poem down from Ibn Abi Dawud in the Rusafa Mosque on a Friday, five days before the end of Sha‘ban 309 AH. The 33 verses follow, then the poet’s statement that he holds these beliefs with his father, Ahmad ibn Hanbal and scholars whose teaching had reached him. This eyewitness testimony supplies the authorship and occasion in both introductions. [Text and postscript](https://shamela.ws/book/13035/2385)
- **Al-Dhahabi, العرش**, vol. 2, pp. 390–393, prints 33 numbered bayts in performance order. The editor’s apparatus records omissions and variant readings in named witnesses. Al-Dhahabi also quotes the poem in **سير أعلام النبلاء**, vol. 13, in his biography of Ibn Abi Dawud. The verse-by-verse collation used الشريعة and العرش beside the two commentaries below. [Al-Arsh, opening](https://shamela.ws/book/7350/742) · [Middle](https://shamela.ws/book/7350/743) · [Close](https://shamela.ws/book/7350/744) · [Siyar](https://shamela.ws/book/10906/8352)
- **Abd al-Razzaq ibn Abd al-Muhsin al-Badr, التحفة السنية شرح منظومة ابن أبي داود الحائية**, pp. 9–10, also gives 33 bayts. At the opening of the electronic text the halves of 2 and 3 have run across columns. For the electronic collation their four halves were paired against al-Ajurri and al-Dhahabi. His commentary on the Companions, the life to come and faith supplies the referents of the condensed names, images and polemical terms. **Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Aql, نثر الورود شرح حائية ابن أبي داود**, fourth edition, 1439 AH / 2018, offers a further vocalised witness, particularly at 5 and 24. - **Abd al-Rahman ibn Nasir al-Barrak, توضيح المقصود من حائية ابن أبي داود**, verse text pp. 9–15. The editors collate al-Shatti and Muhammad Abd al-Salam, among others. Their expanded version has 40 bayts. Its 19–25 name the Prophet’s family, further Companions, Successors and jurists. The reciter goes directly from al-Zubayr (18) to «وقل خير قول في الصحابة» (19), as do al-Ajurri, al-Dhahabi’s العرش and al-Badr. The catalogue count of 33 follows the complete shorter recension. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/30039/5) · [Additional bayts and their sources](https://shamela.ws/book/30039/7) · [Continuation](https://shamela.ws/book/30039/8) · [Closing variants](https://shamela.ws/book/30039/10)
- **Al-Zirikli, الأعلام**, vol. 4, p. 91, gives the name, Sijistan birthplace, travels with his father, Baghdad teaching, loss of sight and death, 230–316 AH / 844–929 CE. Al-Dhahabi supplies fuller biographical testimony. [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/3239) · [Al-Dhahabi](https://shamela.ws/book/10906/8352)

The metre is tawil with ḥāʾ as rhyme consonant. The opening half scans فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعلن (⏑ – – / ⏑ – – – / ⏑ – ⏑ / ⏑ – ⏑ –). At 24.1 the third foot is full, فعولن. The commentary identifies the Quranic rope at 1 (3:103), the language of sura 112 at 7 and the passages in al-Fath concerning the Companions at 20 (48:18, 29). Jarir’s report of the full moon is in al-Bukhari 554, the nightly descent in al-Bukhari 1145, the ten named Companions in al-Tirmidhi 3747 and the charcoal and seeds in Muslim 185. The interpretation of a disputed claim is identified in the sharh as the poet’s or his commentators’. ### The long vowel at 16.1

Both whole-track transcriptions give «البريّات». Both the isolated and expanded windows reproduce the plural. The graphic and the verse witnesses (al-Ajurri, al-Dhahabi, al-Badr and al-Barrak) have «البريّة», a collective meaning creation. Both readings would scan. With البريّة the third foot is فعولُ, with البريّات it is فعولن. The forced aligner gives identical scores after normalising both forms.

The same reciter’s «في الصحابةِ كلّهم» at 19.1 places the vowel before t in the same position at the end of the third foot. Both short-window recognisers turn this collective into «الصحابات», despite the graphic, the books and the ten men named immediately beforehand. A separate phoneme pass places the vowel-to-t interval at 173.84–174.46 in البريّة (about 620 ms) and at 204.70–205.28 in الصحابة (580 ms). In the next bayt’s للصحابة it is about 560 ms, as it is in دعامة at 21.2. The following عَقدِ at 21.2, the creed or bond of belief, scores 3.89 above عِقدِ, a necklace. These are approximate landmarks in a melodic performance. The sustained sound alone does not settle lexical vowel length. The text keeps the attested collective, while its bilingual word card explains why the plural can be heard.
