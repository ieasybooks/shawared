# لَعَمري وَما دَهري بِتَأبينِ هالِكٍ

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari, 8 April 2024. Keep **1.37–597.18 s** of the 597.891 s source, giving **595.81 s** of continuous audio. The initial quiet ends at 1.876 s. The closing rhyme «فودّعا» dies into the quiet beginning at 596.462 s. The changing verse cards, Cohere’s full-source transcript, the independent Audar take and 102 aligned halves give the 51 bayts of *al-Mufaddaliyyat* 67 in order, once each. The alignment split at app 300.80 s falls before 27.1, where Mutammim explains why he wishes rain upon that particular stretch of country. The entire interval is retained. ## Text and recensions

- Al-Mufaddal al-Dabbi, *al-Mufaddaliyyat*, ed. and explained by Ahmad Muhammad Shakir and Abd al-Salam Muhammad Harun, Cairo, Dar al-Ma'arif, sixth edition, **poem 67, pp. 265–270**. The base count and sequence are its 51, from the denial that lament is the poet’s habit to the departure of Malik’s enemy. Its 20 begins «فلمّا تفرّقنا» and 21 «وكنّا كندماني جذيمة». The source plates show the two in this order. Its 45–51 turn to the conduct of the man who brought the news. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/239) · [The companions](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/241) · [Al-Mushaqqar and the witness’s hand](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/243) · [Last eleven](https://shamela.ws/book/6904/244)
- Abu Abd Allah al-Yazidi, *al-Amali*, Hyderabad, **pp. 18–26**. He heard his uncle Ubayd Allah read the poem to Muhammad ibn Habib. His text places the gaming verses early, records competing readings of the broken shelter and the divided meat, and supplies several lines beyond the recorded sequence. His account makes al-Minhal the man who found Malik fallen and laid his cloak over him. On pp. 24–25 he identifies al-Muhill, distinguishes the rag from the small herdsman’s saddle and explains the unencumbered courier. Pp. 25–26 give the report about Mutammim reciting for Umar. [Transmission and the cloak](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/18) · [Gaming](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/19) · [Captive and child](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/21) · [Foster-camels](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/24) · [Messenger](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/25)
- Abu Zayd al-Qurashi, *Jamharat ash'ar al-Arab*, ed. Ali Muhammad al-Bijadi, **pp. 594–601**. The local collation restores four caesuras omitted from the electronic transcription. It gives 44 verses. Its 8 rewrites the scene of sharing meat and its 39 rewrites the announcement of death. The literal collation flags both as unmatched, although they correspond in subject and much wording to our 16 and 44. Its 13 «فتى كان مخذاماً إلى الروع ركضه» and 29 «فتى كان أحيا من فتاة حيية» are unperformed additions. It places the gaming arrows at 6, the companions of Jadhima and the brothers’ parting after the prayer for rain, and al-Mushaqqar just before its close. It ends on Mutali' and the peak of Salma (our 40). This arrangement is a distinct witness to the elegy. [Opening](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/533) · [Additions and reordered passages](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/536) · [Close](https://shamela.ws/book/1487/540)
- C. J. Lyall, *The Mufaddaliyat*, poem LXVII, **vol. I, Arabic text with al-Anbari’s commentary, pp. 526–544**, and **vol. II, pp. 205–212**. The Arabic opening «بتأبين مالك» carries «هالك» as a variant and the last three verses occupy the top of p. 544, immediately before LXVIII. The Arabic pages explain the terms of hospitality, the prayer over the grave, the four names at al-Mushaqqar and the return of the messenger. Lyall explicitly transposes 20 and 21 in English on the authority of other witnesses. He also adopts a different sequence in part of the al-Mushaqqar passage and questions the identification of its Malik with the poet’s brother. The words «عنهم» at 38 point back to several men. The sharh preserves the dispute. His p. 212 describes several old she-camels fostering a single calf, the role of the padded saddle cloth and an alternative reading «سميع» at 44. Our text and rendering keep بصير. - Al-Zirikli, *al-A'lam*, V, p. 274, gives Mutammim’s genealogy, kunyah Abu Nahshal, membership of Yarbu and residence in Medina during Umar’s caliphate. It dates his death **around 30 AH (650 CE)**. The two biographies preserve the approximation. His entry also gives the personal names attached to Jadhima’s two proverbial companions and reports a different, stellar identification in a note. [Biographical entry](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/4779)

The metre is tawil. Ayn (ع) bears the rhyme, followed by the alif prolonging its fatha. The opening «لَعَمري | وَما دَهري | بِتَأبيـ | ـنِ هالِكٍ» gives فعولن مفاعيلن فعولن مفاعلن. The place-names retain their caesura at 26.2, «فَرَوّى | جِبالَ القَرْ | يَتَينِ | فَضَلفَعا», فعولن مفاعيلن فعولُ مفاعلن. There are two separate uses of «مما أصاب فأوجعا», at 1 and 39. In 1 جزعٍ follows the genitive of the preceding half. In 39 جزعاً answers فرحاً in the same verse. The repetition marks the gap between Mutammim’s claim at the opening and the grief he can now describe.

## Commentary and vocabulary

The *Amali* distinguishes the man who sits out the gaming contributions (بَرَم) from the men who take shares, and explains how a piece of old hide can rattle in the cold. Among Malik’s dependants are the captive whose rawhide bond has stiffened his limbs and the widow’s poorly fed child, whose head resembles a bustard chick. In the rain prayer it separates the low cloud beneath the storm, the showers, the watercourses and the soft young vegetation. The images change from a rider seeking food and a hide roof that will scarcely withstand winter, to a brother wishing renewal on the earth that now covers Malik. [Winter shelter](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/18) · [The captive and the chick](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/21) · [Rain and new growth](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/22)

At 41–44 a single comparison occupies four bayts. The three foster-camels find a drag mark and the place where their one calf fell. One calls, the others answer, then the whole kneeling herd joins in. Only «بأوجد مني» measures their grief against the moment a human voice made Malik’s death public. In 47 the rag and the saddle pad turn the opening cloak inside out as an ethical question. Al-Minhal once stopped and used what he had to cover Malik. The poet asks what al-Muhill did with what he had at hand. Al-Yazidi and Lyall supply the particular uses of أظآر, شارف, برك, هدم, سوية and مقزّع behind the notes and translations. [Al-Yazidi, pp. 24–25](https://shamela.ws/book/8196/24) · [Lyall, p. 212](https://archive.org/details/mufaddaliyatanth00mufauoft/page/212/mode/1up)

| 13.2, 16.2, 19.2 | ثوى في is joined or respelled as ثوافي in 13. The syntax determines where the captive was kept, in the rawhide strap. Both recognisers struggle at «الفرث ... أن يتوزّعا» in 16. | 36.2, 38.1–2 | At 36 wide Audar restores «ورزء بزوار القرائب أخضع» in its consonants. The text’s ورزءاً continues the object «نكبةً», and أخضعا ends the rhyme. For 38 see the two separate entries above. The plural hearing belongs to the word for death, and the two hands sometimes inferred from the unvowelled writing are excluded by the printed vocalisation. |
| 40.1–2, 47.1 | At 40 the engines attach أنّ to the relative ما, and turn the ل of the conditional answer «لتضعضعا» into a separate negative. The catastrophe would shake either mountain. At 47 the first engine drops the interrogative and writes the final ت of the saddle pad as a verb. The wider clip restores the question’s opening double vowel and the noun سويّة. |
| 50.2, 51.2 | The first 50.2 clip yielded nothing. Wide Audar supplies «لآواه مجموعاً له أو ممزّع», the answer to the condition in 50.1. On the extended last half Cohere transcribes «فقد آب شانيه إياباً فودّعا» in full. The card, the final rhyme and the 51-verse base agree. |
