# أتاني أبيت اللعن أنك لمتني

## Recording and extent

Osama al-Nahari recites the twelve-bayt apology to al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir. The retained passage is 3.83–107.83 s on the original clock. The opening matter and silence before the first verse end at 3.83 s. The closing rhyme is retained through its decay. The continuous transcripts and 24 aligned halves give one pass through 1–12. An overlapping continuous segment begins again with «ملوك» at 6.1. The adjoining segments and the independent transcription place only one utterance there. [Original recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5XTwkmxoGEk)

## Text and commentary

- Muhammad Abu al-Fadl Ibrahim, ed., *Diwan al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani*, Dar al-Ma'arif, poem 8, pp. 72–74. The heading calls it praise of al-Nu'man and an apology to him. All twelve bayts are present in the order recited. The gloss on p. 72 connects the visitors and the bed of thorns with the poet's distress. Page 73 explains patronage, the scabby camel and the king's rank. Page 74 explains the sun, the repair of a friend's faults and the granting of satisfaction to a complainant. These passages underpin the two explanations. [Opening and commentary](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n71/mode/1up) · [End of poem 8](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n73/mode/1up)
- Al-A'lam al-Shantamari, *Ash'ar al-shu'ara' al-sitta al-jahiliyyin*, al-Nabigha, no. 8, electronic section 36. It gives all twelve, with the sun before the tarred camel and the king's rank. Its sequence for our 8–10 is 10, 8, 9. The two witnesses agree on «فرشنني»، «ويقشب» and «دونها». For 10.1 the reciter uses «فإنك», as in al-Shantamari and al-Suyuti. Ibrahim's verse text has «بأنك» and the gloss below the verse begins «فإنك شمس». [Poem and sequence](https://shamela.ws/book/5449/36)
- Al-Suyuti, *Sharh shawahid al-Mughni*, 1:223–225. He explicitly calls 12 the end of the poem in the diwan he saw in al-Asma'i's transmission. He then describes *Muntaha al-talab*. Its beginning is «أرسماً جديداً من سعاد تجنّب», it reaches the oath after the eight opening bayts, and drops the bed of thorns. It moves «ولست بمستبق» before «ألم تر» and puts the line cited as the grammatical witness, «فلا تتركنّي بالوعيد», at the end after «فمثلك يعتب». His short text has the digital readings «فرسنني»، «ويهشب» and «حولها» at 2 and 9. The Arabic and English introductions identify the independent apology. [Independent poem and readings](https://shamela.ws/book/17724/221) · [Longer recension](https://shamela.ws/book/17724/222) · [Final glosses](https://shamela.ws/book/17724/223)
- Ibn Sa'id al-Maghribi, *Nashwat al-tarab*, pp. 568–569, quotes another apology that rhymes in ʿayn and also begins a bayt «أتاني أبيت اللعن أنك لمتني». That bayt ends «وتلك التي تستك منها المسامع». It cannot supply missing narrative or a prelude to this bāʾ poem. [Juxtaposed texts](https://shamela.ws/book/28174/487)

The local collation compares three separately prepared twelve-bayt witnesses. The base count of twelve is Ibrahim's and al-Asma'i's. The recorded opening and closing agree with them. No introductory bayt from the other recension is restored.

## Metre

The rhyme consonant is bāʾ. In the following scans u is a short syllable and – a long one. The ṭawīl opening scans `u–– | u––– | u–u | u–u–` and `u–– | u––– | u–– | u–u–`. At 6 («ملوك وإخوان») the two halves scan `u–– | u––– | u–– | u–u–` and `u–u | u––– | u–u | u–u–`. The shorter third foot in 1.1 and the first foot of 6.2 are instances of qabḍ.

## Acoustic readings and verification

Each comparison retains the same word count and uses the aligned half with 0.30 s of context on each side. Scores are summed CTC log likelihoods. Positive differences favour the alternative.

The scores use the MMS model and romanisation of the repository’s variants tool. The alternatives include readings in the editions, spellings produced by the recognisers and tests of pronunciation. A difference under two points has little discriminating value. The last column gives the reading used in the verse.

The familiar greeting in 1.1 interrupts the clause “word reached me that you blamed me”. The regular verb لام gives لُمتَني. The small difference between vowel scores cannot establish لِمتَني. The diwan and the two other witnesses agree on the episode of the thorn-strewn bed, although the online text of al-Suyuti changes consonants in its two verbs at 2. For the high rank in 9 the reciter and Ibrahim have دونها. Al-Suyuti’s surrounding kings, حولها, are a genuine alternative image.

At 10.1 both continuous transcriptions have فإنك. Independent recognition of the opening sound gives f, and the metrical phrase in 11.1 has two successive m articulations around edited 88.71 and 88.85 s. The doubled m belongs to لَمَّ, to draw together or put to rights. Ibrahim’s commentary and al-Suyuti’s explanation of شعث both give the sense of repairing a friend’s faults. A score favouring a spelling with one m cannot carry the sense of the verse. [Ibrahim, pp. 73–74](https://archive.org/details/3409pdf_202001/page/n73/mode/1up) · [Al-Suyuti, 1:225](https://shamela.ws/book/17724/223)

Widened Audar transcriptions are shown where available. The lead’s timing review extended 5 half endings to preserve their voiced tails.
