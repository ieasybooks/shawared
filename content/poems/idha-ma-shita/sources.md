# إذا ما شئت أن تصنع

## Recording and text

Abd al-Rahman al-Saeed, published by al-Shankabutiyya on 2 August 2014. Original clock 22.76–51.00 s. The opening slate, «قال العباس بن الأحنف» and the trailing silence are outside the edition. The introductory voice ends at 22.62 s, the verse begins at 23.22 s, and the last voice dies away at about 50.68 s. All six bayts occur once, in diwan order. [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tz4bM14-SXc)

Atika al-Khazraji, ed., *Diwan al-Abbas ibn al-Ahnaf*, Cairo, Dar al-Kutub al-Misriyya, 1373 AH, poem 323, p. 164. The scanned page gives all twelve halves, the metre and manuscript and anthology variants underneath. The verb in 1 crosses the gap as «تَصنَ / عَ». The English carries the complete verb in its first half. The title similarly completes the word. In 2 retain the passive «معشوق» of the printed text and recording. The apparatus also records «عاشق». In 3 «هاهنا» is one adverb, opposed to «ثمّ». In 4 read «وقس» and «فإن زدت» with the diwan. In 5 «يدنوا» is dual, from يَدْنُوانِ, with the n dropped by لم. The Arabic spelling of the rhymes follows the recitation, including the unhamzated كاسا، باسا، راسا. [Al-Khazraji, p. 164](https://archive.org/details/3368pdf_202001/page/n179/mode/1up)

Al-Zajjaji, *Majalis al-ulamaʾ*, ed. Abd al-Salam Harun, meeting 12, pp. 22–23, quotes 1 and 3–6 in an account attributed to al-Asma‘i. He has «ودع بينهما شبراً، وإن زدت» and omits the cup verse. Al-Qifti, *Inbah al-ruwat*, II, p. 204, also divides تصنع after ن. Ibn al-Jarrah, *al-Waraqa*, p. 7, preserves a further telling with «أن تبصر» and an explicit attribution to Abbas. These witnesses illuminate the circulation of the picture conceit and its shifting imperatives. [Al-Zajjaji](https://shamela.ws/book/26676/21) · [Al-Qifti](https://shamela.ws/book/10525/606) · [Ibn al-Jarrah](https://shamela.ws/book/618/7)

## Metre, language and biography

Hazaj, two مفاعيلن feet per half, with the usual shortening. In 1.1 «إِذا ما شِئْ / تَ أَن تَصنَـ» gives ⏑ – – – / ⏑ – – ⏑. Moving the final عَ to that half would add a syllable. In 3.2 «وَصَوِّر ثَمْ / مَ عَبّاسا» gives two full feet. Sin is the rhyme consonant. The final alif of الناسا and عبّاسا prolongs the rhyme. The closing «قاست / قاسى» inflects the same verb for the two lovers and recalls the unrelated «قِس». Equal-window fits favour the recorded readings over the variants. At 2.1 «كيف معشوق» leads «كيف عاشق» by 10.26. At 4.1 «وقس بينهما» leads «ودع بينهما» by 24.82, and at 4.2 «فإن زدت» leads «وإن زدت» by 7.17. The app-clock windows and raw log scores are preserved in the research directory.

For the poet, al-Zirikli, *al-Aʿlam*, III, p. 259, supplies his name, family connections, upbringing, alternative death places and the library dates 192 AH / 808 CE. Ibn Khallikan, *Wafayat al-aʿyan*, no. 319, III, pp. 20–25, preserves accounts of his love verse and court reception. Fawz is introduced as she appears in the poems. [Al-Zirikli](https://shamela.ws/book/12286/2694) · [Ibn Khallikan](https://shamela.ws/book/1000/1031)

## Per-half verification

Both full-take transcribers recover the six-bayt sequence. The clipped Cohere check flags six halves. These decisions compare each with the widened Audar clip and the printed page.

| Half | Evidence and decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1.1, 1.2 | The first clip completes تصنع and the narrow second clip is empty. The independent second clip reads «تصنع شيئا يعجب الناس». Both halves of the reported word and the entire following phrase are accounted for. Keep the division on the printed page. |
| 2.1 | Narrow «معشوقهم». The wider result separates «معشوق» from the onset of تحسّى. Neither the diwan nor the continuous take gives a possessive suffix. |
| 2.2 | Narrow «كاساد» adds a consonant at the rhyme. Wider Audar ends in «كاسا», as do the page and continuous reading. |
| 3.1 | Both machine passes distribute ها and هنا differently. The picture and its location are distinct words in the printed phrase «فصوّر هاهنا فوزاً». |
| 5.1 | Narrow «يدنوها» inserts a pronoun. Widening restores «فإن لم يدنوا حتى» verbatim. The two heads in the next half confirm the dual subject. |
